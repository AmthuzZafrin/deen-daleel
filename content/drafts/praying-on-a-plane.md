# How do I pray on a plane?

## Answer

**Pray in your seat, at the time, facing the qibla at the start if you can.** A
prayer prayed seated in its time is better than one delayed until you land.

**The rule you are relying on** is the sick-person's ladder, which is about
capacity rather than illness. Al-Marghinani: **when the sick person is unable to
stand, he prays sitting, bowing and prostrating — because of his words to Imran ibn
Husayn: "pray standing; if you cannot, then sitting; if you cannot, then on your
side, gesturing" — and because obedience is according to capacity.** [1]

*Obedience is according to capacity.* On a full aircraft with the seatbelt sign on,
your capacity is a seat.

**How to pray in your seat**

1. **Make wudu** in the lavatory before the time comes, or do tayammum if that is
   impossible — there is a separate answer here on it. Better still, board in wudu
   and wear socks you can wipe over.
2. **Face the qibla for the opening takbir** if you can work it out — many aircraft
   moving-maps let you. Then face forward for the rest. Do not contort yourself.
3. **Pray seated.** Recite normally, quietly.
4. **Gesture for bowing and prostration** — a small dip of the head for ruku', a
   deeper one for sujud. **Make the prostration lower than the bowing, because it
   stands in place of them and so takes their ruling.** [2]
5. **Do not raise a tray table to your forehead.** **If you are able to prostrate on
   the ground, prostrate; and if not, gesture with your head** [3] — bringing an
   object up to the face does not count, while lowering the head does.

**If there is floor space** — a galley area, a bulkhead, an empty row — and the
crew are content, pray standing and prostrate properly. Many do. But do not create
a disturbance or ignore the crew: the seated prayer is fully valid and asking is
not always the better move.

**Shorten and combine.** A flight is travel, so:

- **Dhuhr, Asr and Isha become two rak'ahs.** Fajr and Maghrib stay as they are.
- **Combine Dhuhr with Asr, and Maghrib with Isha** — at either time. There are
  separate answers here on shortening and on combining.

Between them these usually solve the whole problem. Pray Dhuhr and Asr together
before boarding or after landing, and there is nothing left to do in the air.

**On working out the times.** Use the flight's local-time display or an app with
the aircraft's position; the times move fast on an eastbound flight. If you
genuinely cannot establish them, pray when you reasonably judge the time has come
— the qibla rules apply by analogy, and there is a separate answer here on
estimating direction and being wrong.

**Crossing time zones and the far north.** Long flights can compress or skip a
prayer window entirely. If a window genuinely does not occur, nothing is owed for
it. If it passes while you sleep, pray it on waking — there is a separate answer
here on that.

**The one thing not to do** is decide the flight is too difficult and pray nothing.
Every element above — seated, gesturing, shortened, combined, direction estimated —
is a recognised concession, and using all of them at once still leaves you with a
valid prayer.

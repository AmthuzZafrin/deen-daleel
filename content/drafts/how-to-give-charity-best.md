# What's the best way to give charity?

## Answer

Someone asked the Prophet, peace be upon him, this exact question, and got an answer that is not about how much or to whom. Abu Hurayra narrates the man asking which charity is greatest:

> **that you give charity while you are healthy and grasping, fearing poverty and hoping for wealth — and do not delay until, when it reaches your throat, you say: for so-and-so this, and for so-and-so that, when it has already become so-and-so's** [1]

The whole answer is in the timing. Not the amount. Charity given while you still want the money, still feel the loss of it, still have plans for it, is worth more than charity given when you are dying and it is leaving you anyway.

Al-Nawawi relays al-Khattabi's gloss: **the meaning of the hadith is that grasping predominates in the state of health, so if a man is open-handed in that state and gives charity, he is more truthful in his intention and it is greater in his reward** [2]. The difficulty is the point. A gift that costs you nothing has proved nothing.

**Four further principles the sources give.**

**Start with the people you are responsible for.** Al-Nasa'i records Hakim b. Hizam: **the best charity is what comes out of a surplus; the upper hand is better than the lower hand; and begin with those you support** [3]. Three rules in one sentence — give from what you can spare rather than crippling yourself, aim to end up as the giver rather than the receiver, and do not send money abroad while your own household goes without. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr reports the Prophet's warning to Sa'd, who wanted to will away most of his wealth: **that you leave your heirs wealthy is better than that you leave them destitute, begging from people** [4].

**Give quietly, and never remind them.** Al-Qurtubi lists the marks of the best giving: **that he conceal his charity, for God's words "and if you hide it and give it to the poor, that is better for you"; and that he not reproach, for God's words "do not nullify your charity by reproach and injury"** [5]. Reminding someone of what you gave them undoes it — the Qur'an says so in those terms.

**Give what you would rather keep.** Al-Qurtubi again: **that he think little of the great amount he gives, because the world in its entirety is little; and that it be from the wealth he loves most** [6]. The bag of clothes you were going to throw out is not charity in the sense meant here. The thing you would rather have kept is.

**Cancel a debt rather than extending it.** Ibn al-'Arabi gives a specific and unusually practical ruling: **charity to an insolvent debtor is an act of devotion, and that is better before God than granting him time until he is able to pay** [7]. If someone owes you money and cannot pay, writing it off is a higher act than patience with them — and it is charity, countable as such.

**What this adds up to.** The best charity is not the largest cheque or the most photographed campaign. It is: given now while it hurts a little; from surplus rather than from what your family needs; quietly; without ever mentioning it again; of something you actually valued; and — if there is a debt you are owed by someone who cannot pay — simply let go of.

Where to send it is genuinely secondary to all of this, and the sources treat it that way. A struggling relative, a neighbour, a local family, a famine appeal: all of these are sound. What they will not let you do is give badly and make up for it with the size of the amount.

# What voluntary fasts are recommended?

## Answer

There are a lot, and you are not meant to do all of them. The clearest single source is a long hadith in Muslim where the Prophet is asked about fast after fast and answers each in turn — it works almost as a menu.

**Three days of every month.** Of these he said: **fasting three days of every month, and Ramadan to Ramadan, is the fast of a lifetime** [1]. The Hanafi manuals specify which three: **it is recommended to fast the white days, the thirteenth, the fourteenth and the fifteenth** [2] — the nights of the full moon.

**Mondays and Thursdays.** Asked about Monday, he said: **that is a day I was born on, and the day I was sent** [3]. Al-Nawawi records that **our colleagues and others agreed on the recommendedness of fasting Monday and Thursday** [4].

**The day of Arafa** — the ninth of Dhu al-Hijja, for those not on Hajj. Asked about it he said it **expiates the year past and the year to come** [5].

**Ashura**, the tenth of Muharram: it **expiates the year past** [6].

**The six days of Shawwal**, after Eid. Ibn Qudama: **fasting six days of Shawwal is recommended in the view of many of the people of knowledge** [7], on the strength of Abu Ayyub's report that **whoever fasts Ramadan and then follows it with six from Shawwal, it is as though he fasted a lifetime** [8].

**The sacred months**, especially Muharram. Al-Nawawi: **among the recommended fasts is fasting the sacred months — Dhu al-Qa'da, Dhu al-Hijja, Muharram and Rajab — and the best of them is Muharram** [9]. Asked which fast is best after Ramadan, the Prophet said: **the month of God which you call Muharram** [10]. Al-Nawawi also notes **fasting Sha'ban is from the sunna, and among it the nine days from the beginning of Dhu al-Hijja** [11].

Now the limit, which matters as much as the list. Asked about fasting perpetually, the Prophet said of such a person: **he has neither fasted nor broken his fast** [12]. Ibn Taymiyya explains the reasoning: it becomes mere habit, and it crowds out things that have a better claim on you. The most he endorsed was every other day — **that is the fast of my brother David** [13]. So the shape of the sunna is rhythm, not accumulation.

One practical clearance: the Hanafi books state that **voluntary fasting is not disliked for someone who owes days from Ramadan** [14], though other authorities prefer you clear the debt first.

Pick one or two and keep them. That is closer to the sunna than attempting the whole list for a fortnight and stopping.

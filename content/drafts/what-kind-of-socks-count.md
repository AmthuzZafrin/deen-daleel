# Do thin socks count for wiping?

## Answer

**This is where the schools genuinely part company**, and you should know that
before you decide, because the honest answer is not one rule but a range.

The permission was given for the *khuff* — a leather sock or soft boot covering
the ankle. Nobody disputes that. The question is how far it stretches toward what
is in your drawer.

**The three positions on cloth socks (jawrabayn)**

| | |
|---|---|
| Abu Hanifa, al-Shafi'i | only if leather-faced or leather-soled |
| Abu Yusuf, Muhammad, al-Thawri | yes, if thick enough |
| — | and from this the later latitude follows |

Ibn Abd al-Barr states the strict side: **wiping over cloth socks is not permitted
according to Abu Hanifa and al-Shafi'i unless the two are leather-covered** [1].
And immediately the other: **this follows their position on wiping over cloth
socks when the two are thick, which is the view of al-Thawri, Abu Yusuf and
Muhammad** [2].

Note that Abu Hanifa's own two leading students went the other way. That is
significant — it is not a stray opinion, it is the Hanafi school's own senior
disagreement, and it is the source of the position most people follow today.

**The working test, drawn from what the disputants actually argue about.** Across
all the positions, the concerns are the same three:

1. **Does it cover the ankle?** This is the shape requirement, and it is less
   demanding than it sounds. Al-Sarakhsi: **the shaft of the khuff is not a
   consideration, so that even if he wore a khuff with no shaft, wiping over it
   is permitted so long as the ankle is covered.** [3]
2. **Can you walk in it?** The classical phrasing is about tears, but the standard
   it sets is general — Ibn Khuwayz Mindad explains Malik's leniency about a small
   hole to mean **that the tear does not prevent it being used and worn, and that
   someone like him could walk in it.** [4]
3. **Is it thick enough that it isn't really a bare foot?** This is the crux, and
   the thing the schools weigh differently.

**On holes and thinness**, the range is wide and worth seeing, because it shows
nobody demanded perfection. **Malik permits wiping over a khuff even if there is
a small tear in it** [5]. Ibn Abd al-Barr: **the jurists differed over the torn
khuff, whether one may wipe over it; Malik and his colleagues said he may wipe if
the tear is slight and the foot does not show through it** [6]. Abu Hanifa set a
number: **if what shows of the foot is less than three fingers, he may wipe; and
he may not wipe if three show** [7].

**Practically, then**

- **Leather boots, khuff-style leather socks** — agreed by everyone.
- **Thick wool or heavy cotton socks that stay up on their own, that you could
  walk a distance in, and through which the skin does not show** — permitted on
  the position of Abu Yusuf, Muhammad and al-Thawri, which is the mainstream
  practice today.
- **Thin nylon or dress socks, ankle socks that slip down, anything you can see
  skin through** — outside the permission on every reckoning. Wash your feet.

**If you follow a school, follow its answer.** If you do not yet, take the middle
position and apply it honestly rather than generously: the questions are whether
it covers the ankle, whether it stays on, and whether it is a real covering or a
formality. You will know.

**Two things that apply whichever position you take.** You must put them on in
full wudu, and the permission has a time limit — **when we were with the Messenger
of Allah on a journey he commanded us not to take them off for three days, except
from janaba, but from stool, urine and the like** [8]. There is a separate answer
here on the timing, and another on wiping generally.

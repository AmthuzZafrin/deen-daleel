# What are the rules for honest trade?

## Answer

**The rule is one sentence, and the tradition treats it as a matter of belonging rather than of etiquette: `من حمل علينا السلاح فليس منا، ومن غشنا فليس منا` [1] — whoever bears arms against us is not of us, and whoever cheats us is not of us.**

Deception is placed beside taking up weapons. That is the register the whole subject sits in.

**The founding case is small, and that is the point.** The Prophet passed a heap of grain, put his hand inside, and found it damp: `من طعام فأدخل يده فيها، فنالت أصابعه بللا، فقال: يا صاحب الطعام، ما هذا؟ قال: أصابته السماء يا رسول الله! قال: أفلا جعلته فوق الطعام حتى يراه الناس؟ ثم قال: من غش فليس منا` [2] — *rain caught it, Messenger of God.* — **why did you not put it on top of the grain so people could see it?** Then: whoever cheats is not of us.

Nothing false was said. The seller had simply arranged the goods so the flaw sat where no one would look. That is the standard: not "did I lie" but **would the buyer have seen it if I had not arranged things this way.**

Ibn Qudama gives the concrete forms: `ولا يحل لبائع الصبرة أن يغشها ; بأن يجعلها على دكة، أو ربوة، أو حجر ينقصها، أو يجعل الرديء في باطنها أو المبلول` [3] — it is not lawful to cheat over a heap: setting it on a platform, a mound, or a stone that reduces it, or putting the poor or the damp goods **inside** it. Piling it to look larger; hiding the bad stock beneath the good. Every modern version of this is recognisable in that list.

**Misrepresentation by arrangement counts as much as by speech.** Ibn Hajar states the principle behind the rulings: `لان البائع يظهر صفة المبيع تارة بقوله وتارة بفعله فإذا أظهر المشترى على صفة فبأن الامر بخلافها كان قد دلس عليه فشرع له الخيار` [4] — the seller conveys the quality of the goods **sometimes by his words and sometimes by his conduct**; so if he presented them to the buyer as having a quality and the matter turns out otherwise, he has deceived him, and the buyer is given the option to undo the sale.

And the reason: `فان المشترى انما بدل ماله بناء على الصفة التي أظهرها له البائع` [5] — the buyer only parted with his money **on the basis of the quality the seller displayed to him.**

That is a general theory of misrepresentation, and it covers a photograph taken at a flattering angle, a demonstration run under conditions the customer will never have, and a headline price that omits what will be added later.

**The remedy is practical rather than punitive.** `فإذا وجد ذلك، ولم يكن المشتري علم به، فله الخيار بين الفسخ، وأخذ تفاوت ما بينهما ; لأنه عيب` [6] — where this is found and the buyer did not know of it, he has the choice between cancelling and taking the difference in value, because it is a defect. The wronged party gets to unwind the deal or be compensated. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr treats the whole area as resting on this: `قال ابن عبد البر هذا الحديث أصل في النهى عن الغش وأصل في ثبوت الخيار لمن دلس عليه بعيب` [7] — a foundation for the prohibition of cheating and for the option given to one deceived about a defect.

**Disclosure is an affirmative duty, not merely a ban on lying.** Al-Nawawi on the report that the two parties to a sale have an option until they part: `البيعان بالخيار ما لم يتفرقا فإن صدقا وبينا بورك لهما في بيعهما) أي بين كل واحد لصاحبه ما يحتاج إلى بيانه من عيب ونحوه في السلعة والثمن وصدق في ذلك وفي الاخبار بالثمن وما يتعلق بالعوضين` [8] — if they are **truthful and make things clear**, they are blessed in their sale; meaning each discloses to the other what needs disclosing — of defect and the like, in the goods **and in the price** — and is truthful in that and in stating the price.

Note that the price is included. Misrepresenting what you paid, or what something is worth, is on the same footing as hiding a fault.

And the consequence of the alternative: `ومعنى محقت بركة بيعهما أي ذهبت بركته وهي زيادته ونماؤه` [9] — the blessing of their sale is erased; its increase and growth go out of it.

**Exploiting an information gap is cheating even when nothing is concealed.** The clearest illustration is the rule on meeting caravans before they reach the market: `وقد أثبت الشارع خيار للركبان إذا تلقوا واشترى منهم قبل أن يهبطوا إلى السوق ويعلموا السعر وليس هناك عيب ولا خلف في شرط ولكن لما فيه من الغش والتدليس` [10] — the sellers are given an option where they were met and bought from before reaching the market and learning the going price; **there is no defect here and no broken condition** — only that the transaction involves cheating and deception.

No lie, no hidden fault, no broken promise. Simply one party using the other's ignorance of the market. That is enough.

**And there is a remedy for the person who keeps being taken advantage of.** A man who was repeatedly deceived in his dealings was told: `من بايعت فقل لا خلابة` [11] — whoever you deal with, say: *no deception.* A stipulation entitling him to withdraw. If you know you are a poor negotiator, saying so and reserving the right to reconsider is a prophetic instruction rather than a weakness.

**What this comes to in practice.**

- **Disclose defects without being asked.** The grain seller was not questioned; he was faulted for the arrangement.
- **Do not present things in a way that creates a false impression**, even if every statement is literally true. The test is what the buyer will conclude, not what you technically said.
- **Be truthful about the price**, including what you paid and what will be added.
- **Do not trade on the other party's ignorance of what things are worth**, especially where they have no way to find out.
- **If you have been deceived, you are entitled to unwind the deal or take the difference.** That is a right the law gives you, not a favour to request.

The tradition does not treat commercial honesty as a soft virtue that yields to competitive pressure. It puts the person who cheats outside the community in the same breath as the one who takes up arms against it — and it gives the person cheated a concrete remedy rather than only sympathy.

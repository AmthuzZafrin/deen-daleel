# What is ihram and what can't I do in it?

## Answer

Ihram is the state you enter to perform Hajj or 'Umra — not the two white cloths, though those are the
visible part of it. It is a consecration: from the moment you enter it, a set of ordinary permitted
things becomes forbidden to you until the rites release you.

Al-Fatawa al-Hindiyya puts the essence in one line: **"the pillar is that there occur from him an act
belonging distinctively to the Hajj"** [1] — a saying or a deed that commits you.

### Where it begins

There are fixed boundary points around Mecca, and you may not cross them heading in without entering
ihram. **"The stations that a person may not pass except in ihram are five: for the people of Medina,
Dhu al-Hulayfa; for the people of Iraq, Dhat 'Irq; for the people of Syria, al-Juhfa; for the people of
Najd, Qarn; and for the people of Yemen, Yalamlam"** [2]. They are geographic, not tribal: **"each one
of these stations is the station for its own people and for whoever passes it who is not of its
people"** [3].

Travelling by a route the classical books did not know does not exempt you. The same work already
handles arrival by sea: **"whoever performs Hajj by sea, his station is when he draws level with a place
on land that he may not pass except in ihram"** [4]. Flying is the same problem with the same answer,
which is why airlines announce the crossing.

The rule holds even if you are not intending pilgrimage: **"it is not permissible for one coming from
outside to enter Mecca without ihram, whether he intended a rite or not"** [5] — a Hanafi position,
disputed by others.

### How you enter it

You bathe, put on the garments, pray two units, and then say the talbiya. That last is what commits you.
Qadi Khan gives the wording: **"the form of the talbiya is that he says: *Labbayk Allahumma labbayk, la
sharika laka labbayk, inna al-hamda wa'l-ni'mata laka wa'l-mulk, la sharika lak*"** [6].

Intention alone is not enough in the Hanafi school: **"one does not become muhrim with us by the
intention alone unless the talbiya is joined to it, or he drives a sacrificial animal; and if he says
the talbiya without intending, he does not become muhrim according to the apparent narrations"** [7].
Ibn Taymiyya makes the same structural point from the other direction — **"rather he made the talbiya
in the Hajj like the takbir in the prayer"** [8] — and, on that basis, that **"it is not legislated for
anyone to say anything before the talbiya"** [9]: no spoken formula of intention.

Then you keep saying it. **"The muhrim says the talbiya frequently: after the prayers, in the last part
of the night, whenever he meets riders, or ascends a height, or descends into a valley; and he raises
his voice with the talbiya"** [10].

### What is forbidden while you are in it

Al-Kasani sorts the restrictions before listing them: **"the prohibitions of ihram are in origin of two
kinds — a kind that does not invalidate the Hajj, and a kind that invalidates it"** [11]. Only marital
intimacy falls in the second.

**Sewn clothing, for men.** The hadith in Sahih Muslim: **"do not wear shirts, nor turbans, nor
trousers, nor hooded cloaks, nor leather socks — except someone who cannot find sandals, let him wear
the socks and cut them below the ankles; and do not wear any garment touched by saffron or
wars"** [12]. The concession is repeated for the lower garment too: **"trousers, for one who cannot find
a waist-wrapper; and leather socks, for one who cannot find sandals"** [13]. What is banned is wearing
such things in the normal way — **"what is forbidden of wearing sewn clothing is the customary
wearing"** [14] — so a stitched bag or belt is not the issue.

**For women** the dress is ordinary clothing, with two exclusions: **"a woman does not wear the face-veil
and does not wear gloves"** [15]. The face is left uncovered, not covered by a fitted veil.

**Perfume.** The Prophet ﷺ told a man who had entered 'Umra in a scented cloak: **"as for the perfume on
you, wash it three times; and as for the cloak, take it off — then do in your 'Umra what you would do in
your Hajj"** [16]. Applying it beforehand is a different matter: **"they agreed that it is permissible to
apply perfume before ihram with what does not remain in substance after the ihram, even if its scent
remains"** [17].

**Hunting, and conduct.** Qadi Khan's summary: **"he guards against the prohibitions of his ihram, which
are lewdness, disobedience, quarrelling, intercourse, and interfering with game — by taking it, or
pointing it out, or directing to it, or assisting"** [18]. Note how far the hunting ban reaches: even
pointing at the animal. The first three come straight from the Qur'an: **"there is (to be) no lewdness
nor abuse nor angry conversation on the pilgrimage"** [19].

### What is not forbidden

More than people assume. Washing, bathing, and sheltering are all fine — **"there is no harm in his
taking shade under a tent"** [20], which by extension covers an umbrella, a bus, a shaded walkway. Ihram
is a restriction on adornment, comfort-seeking through scent, and the taking of life. It is not a
mortification.

What breaks it, and what a breach costs, is a separate question.

# Are there manners for using the toilet?

## Answer

Yes — and they are more detailed than you would expect, because the jurists treated this as a matter of both purity and dignity. Most of it reduces to a short routine you can learn in a day.

**The short version.**

Going in: say *bismillah, allahumma inni a'udhu bika min al-khubuthi wa'l-khaba'ith*, and step in with your left foot. Inside: don't talk, don't take Allah's name in there, don't linger. Coming out: right foot first, and say *ghufranak*. Then clean yourself with water, using the left hand.

**Entering.**

Anas reported that the Prophet ﷺ "when he entered the lavatory would say: *O Allah, I seek refuge in You from the male and female devils*. This is a *hasan sahih* hadith" [1].

Al-Nawawi makes two clarifications. First, "entered" means *intended to enter*: "the *khala'*, the *kanif* and the *mirhad* are all the place of relieving oneself; and his saying *when he entered* means when he intended to enter — and so it came explicitly in al-Bukhari's narration: he used to say it when he intended to enter" [2]. So say it before you go in, at the door. Second, on *al-khubuth wa'l-khaba'ith*: al-Khattabi "meant the males of the devils and their females" [3].

Al-Nawawi adds: "this etiquette is agreed upon as recommended, and there is no difference in it between buildings and open country" [4]. A modern bathroom counts.

Ibn 'Abidin gives the fullest sequence: "when he wants to enter the lavatory he should get up before what is coming overcomes him, and not have with him anything bearing a venerated name, nor with his head uncovered… When he reaches the door he begins with the *basmala* — it is said the supplication, which is the correct view — so he says: *Bismillah, allahumma inni a'udhu bika min al-khubuthi wa'l-khaba'ith*; then he enters with the left foot" [5].

On the "venerated name": Al-Fatawa al-Hindiyya, "it is disliked that he enter the lavatory having with him a ring bearing the name of Allah or something of the Qur'an" [6]. The practical modern form of this is your phone. Nobody in the seventh century carried a Qur'an app into the toilet; you can.

**Inside.**

Ibn 'Abidin: "he does not return a greeting, nor answer the caller to prayer; and if he sneezes he praises Allah in his heart. He does not look at his private parts nor at what comes out of him, nor spit into the urine, nor prolong his sitting — for that generates haemorrhoids — nor blow his nose, nor clear his throat, nor turn about much, nor fiddle with his body, nor raise his gaze to the sky" [7].

Two of those are worth pulling out. **Don't linger** — "he does not prolong his sitting, for that generates haemorrhoids" [8], which is medically sound advice from roughly 1830 and an argument against reading your phone in there. And **don't speak the name of Allah aloud**: hence no returning salam, no answering the adhan, and a silent *alhamdulillah* if you sneeze. This is the reason it is bad manners to greet someone through a bathroom door.

**The qibla.**

Do not face the qibla or turn your back to it while relieving yourself. Al-Nawawi treats that as settled and addresses the follow-up question: "if he avoided facing the qibla and turning his back to it during the exit of urine and stool, and then wished to face it or turn his back to it during *istinja'* — that is permitted" [9]. And Jerusalem is a separate case: "it is not forbidden to face Bayt al-Maqdis or to turn one's back to it while urinating or defecating, but it is disliked" [10].

In a fixed modern bathroom you often have no choice about which way the fixture points. The rule is about your orientation, and where you genuinely cannot help it, you have done what you can.

**Coming out.**

Right foot first — Al-Fatawa al-Hindiyya: "it is recommended for him on entering the lavatory to say *allahumma inni a'udhu bika min al-khubuthi wa'l-khaba'ith*, and he puts his left foot forward; and on leaving he puts the right forward" [11].

And the supplication on exit, per Ibn 'Abidin: "then he goes out with his right foot and says: *Ghufranak* — praise be to Allah who has removed from me what harms me and retained for me what benefits me" [12]. *Ghufranak* — "[I ask] Your forgiveness" — is the one to learn.

**Cleaning yourself.**

Water is better, stones are a licence. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr: "the jurists today are agreed that *istinja'* with water is purer and cleaner, and that stones are a dispensation and a widening, and that *istinja'* with them is permitted in travel and at home" [13]. So the water is the norm; tissue alone is the concession, and it is a valid one.

If you are using something dry, how many wipes? A real disagreement. Al-Nawawi: the hadith forbidding fewer than three stones "is explicit and sound that completing three wipes is obligatory and indispensable… and this is the position of Ahmad b. Hanbal, Ishaq b. Rahawayh and Abu Thawr" [14]; whereas "Malik and Dawud said what is obligatory is cleanliness — so if it is achieved with one stone, that suffices him" [15]. Either way, three is safe and clean is the goal.

**Use your left hand.** Al-Nawawi: "the scholars have agreed that one is forbidden from *istinja'* with the right hand; then the majority hold that it is a prohibition of dislike and manners, not a prohibition of forbiddance" [16]. And more broadly: "our companions said it is recommended that he not use the right hand in anything of the affairs of *istinja'*, except for a necessity" [17]. Pour with the right, clean with the left.

**A useful classification.** Al-Fatawa al-Hindiyya sorts *istinja'* into five levels: two obligatory — "washing the impurity of the outlet in the *ghusl* from major impurity, menstruation and postnatal bleeding, so that it does not spread over his body"; and where the impurity has passed beyond its outlet, obligatory according to Muhammad "little or much, and that is the more cautious", while the other two require it when it exceeds the size of a dirham. "The third is *sunna*, and that is when the impurity has not passed beyond its outlet. The fourth is recommended, and that is when he urinates and does not defecate, he washes his front. The fifth is an innovation, and that is *istinja'* from breaking wind" [18].

That last one settles a question people genuinely ask: passing wind does not require washing. It breaks your wudu; it does not make you dirty.

**Where not to relieve yourself.**

Al-Fatawa al-Hindiyya: "it is disliked to urinate or defecate in water, whether flowing or standing; and it is disliked on the bank of a river, or a well, or a basin, or a spring, or under a fruit-bearing tree, or in a crop, or in shade that people benefit from sitting in; and it is disliked beside mosques and the '*Id* prayer-ground, and in graveyards, and among riding animals, and in the paths of the Muslims" [19].

The thread running through that list is other people. Not water they will drink, not shade they will sit in, not a path they will walk.

**Standing.** "It is disliked to urinate standing, or lying down, or stripped of one's garment, without an excuse — and if it is for an excuse, there is no harm in it" [20]. Disliked, not forbidden, and excused by need.

**In short.** *Bismillah, a'udhu billahi min al-khubuthi wa'l-khaba'ith* — left foot in — silence, and be quick — right foot out — *ghufranak* — then water, left hand.

If you are new to Islam and this is the first time anyone has told you the religion has an opinion about your bathroom: this is not fussiness. It is the same principle running through wudu and the prayer — that the small, private, unwatched parts of the day are also part of the life you are living for Allah.

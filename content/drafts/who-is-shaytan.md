# Who is Shaytan and how does he work?

## Answer

**Short answer: Iblis is a creature who refused a command out of pride, was cast
out, and now works by suggestion rather than force.** The most useful thing in
the sources is not his biography but the description of his *method*, which is
oddly specific and not what most people expect.

**The refusal.** Qur'an 20:116: **"And when We said unto the angels: Fall
prostrate before Adam, they fell prostrate (all) save Iblis; he refused."** [1].
That is the origin of the enmity — a refusal, and specifically a refusal rooted
in thinking himself better.

**What he is.** Ibn Hajar, discussing the chapter heading "the description of
Iblis and his troops" [2], reports that the question of his nature was
disputed: **"واختلف هل كان من الملائكة ثم مسخ لما طرد أو لم يكن منهم أصلا على
قولين مشهورين"** — *they differed over whether he was of the angels and was
transformed when he was expelled, or was never among them at all — two well-known
positions* [3]. So even that much is not settled, and it is worth noticing that
the tradition is comfortable saying so.

**The instruction the Qur'an gives about him.** 35:6: **"Lo! the devil is an
enemy for you, so treat him as an enemy. He only summoneth his faction to be
owners of the flaming Fire."** [4]. Note the form: not "fear him" but *treat him
as an enemy* — which is a description of a posture, not of a terror.

**Now the method, which is the useful part.** Muslim heads a chapter for it —
"Shaytan's inciting of discord, his dispatching of his raiding parties to test
people, and that with every human there is a companion" [5]. Under it, from
Jabir: **"إن الشيطان قد أيس أن يعبده المصلون في جزيرة العرب، ولكن في التحريش
بينهم"** — *Shaytan has despaired of those who pray in the Arabian Peninsula ever
worshipping him — but [his hope is] in setting them against one another* [6].

Read that carefully. The stated aim is not to make you an idolater. He has given
that up. The aim is **discord between believers**.

And the ranking of his agents makes the same point: **"إن إبليس يضع عرشه على
الماء، ثم يبعث سراياه، فأدناهم منه منزلة أعظمهم فتنة"** — *Iblis places his
throne upon the water, then sends out his raiding parties; the nearest of them to
him in rank is the one who causes the greatest trial* [7]. The one who wins the
prize in that report is not the one who caused a murder or an apostasy:
**"ما تركته حتى فرقت بينه وبين امرأته، قال: فيدنيه منه ويقول: نعم أنت"** — *"I
did not leave him until I separated him from his wife." So he draws him near and
says: "Yes — you."* [8].

Breaking up a marriage. That is the achievement the report singles out. On this
material, the ordinary damage — a family split, believers turned against each
other — is the actual project, not a side effect.

**How he works, and how he does not.** By suggestion. 7:200: **"And if a slander
from the devil wound thee, then seek refuge in Allah. Lo! He is Hearer,
Knower."** [9]. The prescribed response is not a ritual of combat; it is turning
to Allah. And 16:98: **"And when thou recitest the Qur'an, seek refuge in Allah
from Satan the outcast."** [10] — which is why the *a'udhu billah* precedes
recitation.

**So, practically.** Take the description at [6] and [8] seriously and check it
against your own life: the places you are most likely to be doing his work are
arguments with other Muslims, a marriage going bad through accumulated
resentment, and a family fracturing. Those are named. What is *not* prescribed
anywhere above is fear of him as a presence, or specialists, or protections
bought from anyone. The response given is seeking refuge in Allah [9][10], and it
is available to anyone.

Two things follow from that which are worth saying plainly. Blaming Shaytan does
not remove your responsibility — he is described as suggesting, not compelling.
And the pride at [1] is the original of the thing; on the sources' own telling,
the refusal that started all of it was someone who could not bear to be told he
was not the better creature.

**On the language above:** "Iblis" is his proper name and "al-shaytan" a
description meaning the rebel or the distant one; "tahrish" is inciting discord
[5][6]; "qarin" is the companion the chapter heading mentions; "isti'adha" is
seeking refuge, and "a'udhu billahi min al-shaytan al-rajim" is the formula at
[10].

**A note on scope.** The sources contain much more — his names, his lineage, the
question of his troops, the reports about eavesdropping on the heavens — some of
it firmly transmitted and some of it not; Ibn Hajar's discussion at [2]–[3] shows
the level of caution the scholars applied. This answer keeps to what is
established and to what actually bears on how a person lives.

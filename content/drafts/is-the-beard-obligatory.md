# Do I have to grow a beard?

## Answer

The command is not in doubt. What its violation amounts to — sin, or something
less — and whether you may trim, are two separate questions, and the sources on
this file answer the second at length and the first more cautiously than modern
discussions usually do.

**The command, in five wordings.** Malik, from Nafi', from Ibn 'Umar: "the
Messenger of Allah ﷺ ordered the clipping of the moustaches and the letting-grow
of the beards" [1]. Al-Nawawi collects the variants: "and it came in al-Bukhari's
narration *wafiru al-liha*, so there are five narrations — *a'fu*, *awfu*,
*arkhu*, *arju*, *waffiru* — and the meaning of all of them is leaving it as it
is" [2]. It is also among the practices of the *fitra*: "the *fitra* is five —
circumcision, shaving the pubic hair, trimming the moustache, clipping the
nails..." [3].

**The reason the sources give.** Al-Khattabi, as al-Nawawi reports him:
letting-grow "is leaving it in full without clipping — clipping it was made
disliked for us, as the practice of the non-Arabs; and he said: it was of the
style of Chosroes to clip the beards and let the moustaches grow" [4]. Note the
shape: the Persian fashion was short beard, full moustache, and the instruction
is the exact reverse of it, item for item. The command is a mark of distinction,
which is why it comes paired with the moustache and not on its own.

**What these particular texts say about shaving.** Qadi 'Iyad, quoted by both
al-Nawawi and Ibn Hajar: "shaving it, clipping it and thinning it are disliked;
as for taking from its length and its breadth, that is good — and being
conspicuous by making it large is disliked, just as it is disliked in clipping
and shearing it" [5]. Al-Nawawi disputes him — but only on the second half:
"the chosen view is to leave the beard as it is, and not to touch it with any
shortening at all" [6]. On shaving, they agree, and the word both use is
*disliked*.

Be aware of the limit here. These commentaries frame the matter in the language
of *karaha*; they do not say in these passages that shaving is sinful, and they
do not say it is merely optional either. Scholars after them have argued both
harder positions. What this library shows you is a firmly established command, a
stated reason for it, and commentators who treat departing from it as a real
fault without settling its exact grade. If you need the grade settled for your
own situation, that is a question for a scholar in your school.

**The trimming disagreement is where the material is richest.** Al-Tabari's
survey, via Ibn Hajar: "a group went to the apparent sense of the hadith and
disliked taking anything from the beard, of its length or its breadth; and a
group said: if it exceeds a fistful, the excess is taken — then he traced it by
his chain to Ibn 'Umar, that he did this" [7]. Al-Ghazali's version of the same:
"the salaf differed over what grows long of the beard. It was said: there is no
harm in grasping it and clipping what is below the fist — Ibn 'Umar did it, then
a group of the Successors; and al-Sha'bi and Ibn Sirin approved of it" [8].

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr's argument for the trimming view is the strongest single point
on the file: "this is Ibn 'Umar — he narrated *let the beards grow*, and he
understood the meaning, and so he used to do what we have described; and a
group of the scholars held it, in hajj and outside hajj" [9]. The narrator of
the hadith is also its earliest interpreter.

The generation after him was split in every direction: "Qatada disliked taking
from his beard except in hajj or 'umrah, and he used to take from his sideburns;
al-Hasan used to take from the length of his beard; and Ibn Sirin saw no harm in
it" [10]. Al-Tabari himself chose 'Ata's position, with a practical reason: "he
chose 'Ata's view and said that if a man left his beard and did not touch it
until its length and breadth became grotesque, he would have exposed himself to
whoever would mock him" [11].

Al-Nawawi holds the other line and disposes of the counter-evidence honestly:
"the sound view is that taking from it is disliked absolutely; rather he leaves
it as it is however it may be, on account of the authentic hadith *let the
beards grow*. As for the hadith of 'Amr b. Shu'ayb from his father from his
grandfather, that the Prophet ﷺ used to take from his beard, from its breadth
and its length — al-Tirmidhi narrated it with a weak chain that is not used as
proof" [12].

**So, practically.** Grow it: that much is a clear prophetic command with a
clear stated purpose, and the sources treat neglecting it as a fault rather than
a matter of taste. Trimming: a live disagreement with Ibn 'Umar's own practice
on one side and al-Nawawi's reading of the text on the other — taking what
exceeds a fistful is a well-attested position and you are not obliged to
apologise for it. Shaping the neckline and cheeks, keeping it clean and not
letting it become grotesque: al-Tabari and 'Ata' explicitly had that in view.

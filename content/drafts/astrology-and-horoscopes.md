# What does Islam say about horoscopes?

## Answer

Horoscopes are forbidden, and unusually for a modern question, the scholars name the practice directly rather than leaving it to be inferred. The *munajjim* — the astrologer — is listed by name in the classical definitions of the very thing the Prophet ﷺ prohibited consulting.

**The prohibition.**

Two reports. The first, from a wife of the Prophet ﷺ, transmitted by Ahmad: "whoever comes to a fortune-teller and believes him in what he says — no prayer of his will be accepted for forty days" [1]. Al-Qurtubi cites the same report to Sahih Muslim, in a wording that turns on the asking rather than the believing: "what came in Sahih Muslim from one of the wives of the Prophet ﷺ, that the Prophet ﷺ said: whoever comes to a fortune-teller and asks about a thing, no prayer of his will be accepted for forty nights" [2]. The second, from Abu Hurayra: "whoever comes to a soothsayer and believes him in what he says… has disavowed what was revealed to Muhammad" [3].

Be aware that the second report is criticised. Al-Nawawi records the hadith critics on it: "al-Tirmidhi said: we know it only from the hadith of Abu Tamima from Abu Hurayra. Al-Bukhari said: no hearing by Abu Tamima from Abu Hurayra is known. Al-Bazzar said: this is a *munkar* hadith — and in the chain also is Hakim al-Athram, of whom al-Bazzar said: he is not used as proof, and what he alone transmits is nothing" [4]. Ibn Hajar answers that it has corroborating witnesses "from the hadith of Jabir and 'Imran b. Husayn, both of which al-Bazzar transmitted with two good chains" [5]. So the severe wording is contested; the forty-days report is not, and it is enough.

**Why astrology is what these reports are about.**

Ibn Hajar defines the offence: *kahana* is "the claim to knowledge of the unseen, such as reporting what will occur on the earth, with reliance upon some cause" [6]. That last clause is the whole of astrology — a claimed method that licenses a claim about the future.

And he says who the word covers: "*kahin* is a word applied to the *'arraf*, and to the one who casts pebbles, and to the astrologer" [7]. Al-Khattabi, quoted by al-Nawawi, is more explicit still: "and among them is one called the astrologer, a soothsayer. He said: and the hadith comprises the prohibition of coming to all of these, and of referring to their statement, and of believing them in what they claim" [8]. Al-Qurtubi: "the *'arraf* is the diviner and the astrologer who claims knowledge of the unseen… and he is the one who infers matters by causes and premises he claims to know" [9].

Ibn Taymiyya closes the definitional escape route in advance: "*'arraf*: it has been said that it is a general name for the soothsayer, the astrologer, the sand-diviner and their like, among those who speak about advance knowledge by these paths; and even if it were said that in the language it is a name for some of these types, the rest enter into it by way of general meaning" [10]. Whether your particular practice was named in the seventh century does not matter; the category is defined by what it claims to do.

Ibn Hajar's fourth type of soothsaying describes the newspaper column exactly: "what rests upon experience and custom, so that one infers the coming event from what occurred before it — and of this last category is what resembles sorcery; some of them reinforce it with omen-taking, divining-lines and the stars, and all of that is blameworthy in the law" [11].

**"But it's sometimes right."**

That is the argument *against* it, not for it. Al-Nawawi: the prohibition on going to soothsayers is "because they speak about hidden things, some of which may happen to be correct — so a trial is feared for a person on that account, since they confuse people greatly in the matter of the revealed law" [12]. Occasional accuracy is precisely what gives a false system its grip.

The Prophet ﷺ's own assessment, as al-Qurtubi cites from Muslim: "people asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ about the soothsayers, and he said: They are nothing" [13].

**"But I only read it for fun."**

The reports condition the penalty on *believing* — "and believes him in what he says." Reading it while giving it no credence is not the same act as consulting it. But two things are worth weighing before resting on that. The wording al-Qurtubi cites to Muslim turns on the asking, not the believing [2] — on that report, consulting is enough by itself. And the honest question is whether "just for fun" survives contact with a description of your character and your coming month that you find flattering. Most people do not read horoscopes with genuine indifference to whether they are true.

**Payment is forbidden by consensus — including yours.**

Al-Nawawi transmits from al-Baghawi: "the people of knowledge have agreed upon the prohibition of the soothsayer's fee — and it is what the soothsayer takes for his soothsaying — because the act of soothsaying is void, and it is not permissible to take a wage for it" [14]. Al-Mawardi placed it under public enforcement: "the *muhtasib* prevents people from earning by soothsaying and idle amusement, and both the taker and the giver are disciplined for it" [15]. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr lists it among the earnings agreed to be forbidden: "of the earnings agreed upon as forbidden: usury, the dowries of prostitutes, illicit gain, bribes, taking a wage for wailing and for singing, and for soothsaying, claiming the unseen, and tidings of the heavens" [16].

So paid astrology apps, subscription readings and birth-chart services are on both sides of a forbidden transaction. Al-Qurtubi thought the problem acute in his own day too: "many of those who attach themselves to jurisprudence and religion have been deceived, and have come to these soothsayers and fortune-tellers, who dazzled them with absurdities and extracted their wealth from them" [17] — and he adds, of the forty-days report, "so what of one who takes them on and spends upon them, depending upon their statements?" [18].

**What is not forbidden: the stars themselves.**

None of this touches astronomy. Al-Qurtubi draws the line at the point of method: "as for one who reports on the eclipse of the sun and moon — our scholars said: he is disciplined but not imprisoned. As for his not being declared a disbeliever, it is because a group said: it is a matter grasped by calculation and the reckoning of the lunar mansions" [19]. Calculation is knowledge; divination is a claim about the unseen. The reason he gives for even the light discipline is candid and dated: "they introduce doubt upon the common people, since these do not grasp the difference between this and other than it, so they disturb their beliefs and cause them to abandon their foundations in certainty" [20]. That is a worry about public confusion in his century, not a verdict on the science.

Navigation by the stars is Qur'anic. Ibn 'Abbas on "and landmarks; and by the star they find their way": "the landmarks are the markers of the roads by day — that is, He made for the roads markers by which guidance is obtained; *and by the star they find their way* means by night" [21]. And the Qur'an itself speaks of the *buruj* in the sky; al-Jalalayn glosses them as the familiar twelve — "Aries, Taurus, Gemini, Cancer, Leo, Virgo, Libra, Scorpio, Sagittarius, Capricorn, Aquarius and Pisces" [22]. Naming the constellations is not the offence. Reading your fate in them is.

Ibn Hajar marks where the gravest verdict actually falls: "only the one who attributes origination to them disbelieves; but as for one who takes them as a sign of some event occurring on the earth, no" [23]. Note what that does and does not do. It means an ordinary Muslim who checks a horoscope is not thereby outside Islam — nobody should be told otherwise. It does not make the practice permitted; the reports on going to soothsayers stand on their own.

**In short.** Delete the app, stop the subscription, and don't pay for a reading. If someone tells you your star sign explains your temper or your marriage prospects, they are describing you from a system the Prophet ﷺ called nothing. Look up at the sky, know the names of what is there, use it to find the qibla or the season, and leave the future to the One who has it.

```
Fath al-Bari 6:442

Arabic:
الاستسقاء والمراد منه وقوع إجابة الدعاء في الحال وقد تقدم شرحه في الاستسقاء وأورده هنا من طريقين لحماد بن زيد فقوله وعن يونس هو ابن عبيد وهو معطوف على قوله عن عبد العزيز بن صهيب وحاصله أن حمادا سمعه عن أنس عاليا ونازلا وذلك لأنه سمع من ثابت وحدث عنه هنا بواسطة وذكر البزار ان حمادا تفرد بطريق يونس بن عبيد هذه (قوله وغيره يقول فعرفنا) وهو من العرافة وكذا اختلفت الرواة عند مسلم هل قال فرقنا أو عرفنا وفي رواية الإسماعيلي فعرفنا من العرافة وجها واحدا وسمي العريف عريفا لأنه يعرف الامام أحوال العسكر وزعم الكرماني ان فيه حذفا تقديره فرجعنا إلى المدينة فعرفنا (قلت) ولا يتعين ذلك لجواز أن يكون تعريفهم وارسالهم قبل الرجوع إلى المدينة (قوله هلكت الكراع) بضم أوله وحكى عن رواية الأصيلي كسرها وخطئ والمراد به الخيل وقد يطلق على غيرها من الحيوان لكن المراد به هنا الحقيقة لأنه عطف عليه بعد ذلك غيره (قوله كمثل الزجاجة) أي من شدة الصفاء ليس فيها شئ من السحاب (قوله فهاجت ريح أنشأت سحابا)
```

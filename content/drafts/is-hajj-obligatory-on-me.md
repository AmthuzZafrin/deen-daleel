# Do I have to do Hajj, and when?

## Answer

Hajj is obligatory once in your lifetime, and only if you can actually manage it. Both halves of that
sentence matter, and the second is where most people's real question sits.

Al-Nawawi states the rule in its usual form: **"know that the Hajj is an individual obligation upon
every legally responsible, free, Muslim person who is able"** [1]. Four conditions, and the fourth —
*istita'a*, being able — is the one the jurists spend their pages on. He also records that the once-only
limit is not disputed: **"they agreed that neither the Hajj nor the 'Umra is obligatory in a person's
lifetime except one time"** [2]. If you have done it, you have done it. Everything after that is
voluntary.

### What "able" means

The verse says God has a right over people, **"the pilgrimage to the House, upon whoever is able to
find a way to it"** [3]. The Prophet ﷺ was asked what that way is, and answered: **"the way to the Hajj
is provision and a mount"** [4] — that is, the cost of the journey and the transport. Ibn 'Abbas glossed
it a little more fully: **"the way is that the servant's body be sound, and that he have the price of
provision and a mount, without anyone barring him"** [5].

So three things, in the language of today: your health, your money, and a road that is actually open.

But the jurists are emphatic that the hadith is an illustration, not a closed list. Al-Kasani:
**"do you not see that if between him and Mecca there is a swelling sea with no ship there, or an enemy
standing between him and reaching the House, the Hajj is not obligatory on him despite the presence of
provision and mount"** [6]. Al-Jassas makes the same point from the other direction — the sick and
fearful, the old man who cannot stay seated on a mount, the immobilised, **"and everyone for whom
reaching it has become impossible, he is not able to find the way to the Hajj even if he has provision
and a mount"** [7]. The reason given is that obligating such people **"would be a manifest hardship and
a severe difficulty"** [8].

That is worth reading carefully if you are anxious about this. The classical rule is not "find a way at
any cost." It is that God did not oblige you to what you cannot bear.

### If money is the obstacle

Two positions worth knowing, because they are still argued today.

Al-Qurtubi records Malik's view that a person who can walk and has provision owes the Hajj even without
a mount: **"if he is able to walk and finds provision, then the obligation of Hajj is upon him"** [9] —
and the Hanafis reject that, holding both provision and transport to be conditions. But everyone rules
out one route in particular: **"as for if he can obtain provision by begging from people on the road,
it is disliked for him to perform Hajj, because he becomes a burden on people"** [10]. Going into debt
or dependence to get there is not what the verse asks of you.

Note also that the standard is relative to distance. Al-Kasani: **"as for the people of Mecca and those
around them, the Hajj is obligatory upon the strong among them"** [11] — the mount is a condition
because of the journey, so where there is no journey, there is no such condition.

### When, once you are able

Here the schools genuinely differ, and it affects whether delaying is a sin.

Ibn Qudama, for the Hanbalis: **"the sum of it is that whoever the Hajj has become obligatory upon, and
who is able to perform it, it is obligatory upon him immediately, and it is not permitted for him to
delay it"** [12] — citing the hadith **"whoever intends the Hajj, let him hasten"** [13]. Against that,
he reports al-Shafi'i's position: **"the Hajj is obligatory as an expansive obligation, and he may delay
it"** [14].

Within the Hanafi school the same split appears internally: **"as for its being immediate, that is the
position of Abu Yusuf and the sounder of the two narrations from Abu Hanifa; and according to Muhammad
it is obligatory with delay permitted, though hastening is better"** [15]. And Ibn Nujaym adds the
consolation that follows: **"if he performs Hajj at the end of his life, the sin is lifted by
agreement"** [16].

### In practice

Ask yourself three questions. Am I responsible and Muslim — yes. Can I fund the journey and my
dependants' upkeep while I am gone, without borrowing or begging. Am I physically able to travel, and
is the road open. If all three are yes, the obligation is on you now, and the majority view is not to
put it off. If any is no, it is not yet on you, and no sin attaches to waiting for it to become so.

One further condition applies to women in the Hanafi and Hanbali schools — al-Jassas: **"in our view
the presence of a mahram for a woman is among the conditions of the Hajj"** [17]. That is contested and
is its own question.

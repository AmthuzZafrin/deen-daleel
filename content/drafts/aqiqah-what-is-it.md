# What is aqiqah and do I have to do it?

## Answer

**What it is.** Ibn Qudama gives both usages: "the *'aqiqa* is the animal slaughtered on account of a newborn; and it is said: it is the food prepared and invited to on account of the newborn" [1]. Behind the word there is a small dispute about etymology — Abu 'Ubayd traced it to the hair on a newborn's head, while Ahmad rejected that reading and held that "the *'aqiqa* is the slaughtering itself", from *'aqq*, to cut.

**Do you have to do it? Three positions, and the majority answer is no.** Ibn Qudama: "the *'aqiqa* is a *sunna* in the view of the general body of the people of knowledge — among them Ibn 'Abbas, Ibn 'Umar, 'A'isha, the jurists of the Successors and the imams of the great cities — except the people of *ra'y*, who said it is not a *sunna* and it is a matter of the age of ignorance" [2]. At the other end: "al-Hasan and Dawud said it is obligatory; and it is reported from Burayda that people will be presented for it as they are presented for the five prayers" [3]. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr sets out the middle position and lists who held it: "Malik used to say it is a confirmed *sunna* which must be acted upon, and that is the position of al-Shafi'i, Ahmad b. Hanbal, Ishaq, Abu Thawr and al-Tabari" [4].

His argument against obligation is the kind you can check yourself. The Prophet's wording was: *whoever has a child born to him and wishes to make an offering for his child, let him do so.* Ibn 'Abd al-Barr's comment: this is "evidence that the *'aqiqa* is not obligatory, **because the obligatory is not spoken of as 'whoever wishes, let him do it'**" [5]. The grammar of the permission is the argument.

The obligation view leans on a different hadith — *every boy is pledged by his 'aqiqa* — and Ibn Hajar shows how much that phrase can carry: "it is said its meaning is that the *'aqiqa* is binding, indispensable, so the newborn is likened, in its being binding on him and his not being free of it, to a pledge in the hand of the pledge-holder — and this strengthens the view of those who held it obligatory. And it is said the meaning is that he is pledged by the harm of his hair" [6]. Four readings of one word, and the ruling shifts with each.

**How much.** The standard is two sheep for a boy, one for a girl: "for a boy two comparable sheep, and for a girl one sheep" [7]. The same is reported through 'A'isha, and al-Tirmidhi grades it *hasan sahih* [8]. Ibn Qudama reports Ibn 'Umar holding one sheep each for boy and girl, on the report that the Prophet made an *'aqiqa* of one sheep each for al-Hasan and al-Husayn. And there is a minority view worth naming and rejecting: al-Hasan and Qatada "said an *'aqiqa* is made for a boy and not for a girl — and the majority disagreed with them, saying an *'aqiqa* is made for a girl too, their evidence being the reports that explicitly mention the girl" [9]. The reason the two of them gave, in Ibn Qudama's report, was that a girl brings no joy. The majority did not accept it.

**When.** "It is slaughtered on the seventh day; if that passes, then on the fourteenth; if that passes, then on the twenty-first. This is reported from 'A'isha ... and we know of no disagreement among the people of knowledge who hold it prescribed about the desirability of slaughtering it on the seventh day" [10]. Malik was stricter — Ibn Hajar reports that on his view it is tied to the seventh day, "and he also said that if the child dies before the seventh, the *'aqiqa* lapses; and in Ibn Wahb's narration from Malik, that whoever was not given an *'aqiqa* on the first seventh, an *'aqiqa* is made for him on the second seventh. Ibn Wahb said: there is no harm in an *'aqiqa* for him on the third seventh" [11]. Ibn Qudama is the most relaxed: slaughtering before or after "discharges it, because the intended purpose is achieved".

**If it was never done for you.** This matters for anyone who came to Islam as an adult, and the sources split. Ibn Qudama: "if no *'aqiqa* was made at all, and the boy reaches maturity and earns, there is no *'aqiqa* upon him. Ahmad was asked about this question and said: that is upon the father — meaning he does not make an *'aqiqa* for himself, because the *sunna* is in respect of another. But 'Ata' and al-Hasan said: he makes an *'aqiqa* for himself, because it is prescribed on his account, and because he is pledged by it, so it is fitting that his own release be prescribed for him" [12]. Two defensible positions; neither is obligatory on the other's terms.

**What goes with it.** "It is recommended to shave the child's head on the seventh day and to name him ... and if he gives in charity the weight of his hair in silver, that is good" [13]. Al-Nawawi adds the handling of the meat: "it is recommended that its limbs be separated and its bones not broken ... and one eats and feeds and gives in charity, and that is on the seventh day; and because it is his first slaughtered animal, it was thought preferable that no bone be broken, as a good omen for the soundness of his limbs; and it is recommended that its meat be cooked as a sweet dish, as a good omen for the sweetness of his character" [14].

**Two things to drop.** One is smearing the baby's head with the blood. Al-Nawawi records the replacement explicitly: "in the age of ignorance they used to put a piece of cotton in the blood of the *'aqiqa* and place it on the newborn's head, so the Prophet commanded them to put perfume in place of the blood" [15]. The other is a textual point, and it is a good example of how carefully these books were edited. Abu Dawud carries a version of the Samura hadith reading *and he is smeared with blood* — and then flatly annotates his own narration: "Hammam was contradicted in this wording; it is an error from Hammam" [16], and "this is not to be acted upon" [17]. The correct word was *yusamma*, he is named.

**So, in short:** an offering made by the parent for a newborn — two sheep for a boy, one for a girl — slaughtered ideally on the seventh day, with the head shaved, the child named, the weight of the hair given in silver, the meat eaten, gifted and given away. It is a strongly recommended *sunna* in the majority view and not a duty; a minority held it obligatory and one school did not treat it as a *sunna* at all. If it was never done for you, one respected position says the moment has passed and it was your father's to do, and another says you may do it for yourself.

ليس بمخير إلا في الضرورة لأن الله تعالى قال   : فمن كان منكم مريضا أو به أذى من رأسه فإذا حلق رأسه عامدا أو لبس عامدا لغير عذر فليس بمخير وعليه دم لا غير السابعة واختلفوا فيمن فعل ذلك ناسيا فقال مالك رحمه الله : العامد والناسي في ذلك سواء في وجوب الفدية وهو قول أبي حنيفة والثوري والليث وللشافعي في هذه المسألة قولان : أحدهما لا فدية عليه وهو قول داؤد وإسحاق والثاني عليه الفدية وأكثر العلماء يوجبون الفدية على المحرم بلبس المخيط وتغطية الرأس أو بعضه ولبس الخفين وتقليم الأظافر ومس الطيب وإماطة الأذى وكذلك إذا حلق شعر جسده أو أطلى أو حلق مواضع المحاجم والمرأة كالرجل في ذلك وعليها الفدية في الكحل وإن لم يكن فيه طيب وللرجل أن يكتحل بما لا طيب فيه وعلى المرأة الفدية إذا غطت وجهها أو لبست القفازين والعمد والسهو والجهل في ذلك سواء وبعضهم يجعل عليهما دما في كل شيء من ذلك وقال داؤد : لاشئ عليهما في حلق شعر الجسد الثامنة واختلف العلماء في موضع الفدية المذكورة فقال عطاء : ما كان من دم فبمكة وما كان من طعام أو صيام فحيث شاء وبنحو ذلك قال أصحاب الرأي وعن الحسن أن الدم بمكة وقال طاوس والشافعي : الإطعام والدم لا يكونان إلا بمكة والصوم حيث شاء لأن الصيام لا منفعة فيه لأهل الحرم وقد قال الله سبحانه هديا بالغ الكعبة رفقا لمساكين جيران بيته فالإطعام فيه منفعة بخلاف الصيام والله أعلم وقال مالك : يفعل ذلك أين شاء وهو الصحيح من القول وهو قول مجاهد والذبح هنا عند مالك نسك وليس بهدي لنص القرآن والسنة والنسك يكون حيث شاء والهدي لا يكون إلا بمكة ومن حجته أيضا ما رواه عن يحيى بن سعيد في موطئه وفيه : فأمر علي بن أبي طالب ( رضي الله عنه ) برأسه يعني رأس حسين فحلق ثم نسك عنه بالسقيا فنحر عنه بعيرا قال مالك قال يحيى بن سعيد : وكان حسين خرج مع عثمان في سفره ذلك إلى مكة ففي هذا

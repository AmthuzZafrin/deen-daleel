# Which parts of wudu are compulsory and which are just recommended?

## Answer

Four things are obligatory. Everything else in the wudu you were taught is
recommended.

Al-Qurtubi derives it straight from the verse: **God mentioned four limbs — the
face, whose obligation is washing; the hands likewise; the head, whose obligation
is wiping, by agreement; and the feet, over which there is disagreement. He
mentioned nothing besides them, and that shows that whatever is beyond them is
manners and sunnas.** [1]

So the compulsory core is:

1. **Wash the face.**
2. **Wash the arms** to the elbows.
3. **Wipe the head.**
4. **Wash the feet** to the ankles.

And the recommended additions — the ones people most often think are required:
washing the hands first, rinsing the mouth, rinsing the nose, wiping the ears,
doing everything three times, starting with the right. Al-Nawawi lists the
basmala and washing the hands among **the sunnas — and that is the school's
position.** [2]

**Two honest complications, because "four" is the summary and not the whole
truth.**

*The feet.* Al-Qurtubi says the disagreement over them is coming, and it is the
old question of whether the verse commands washing them or wiping them. In
practice every school you are likely to be following has you washing them.

*Rinsing the mouth and nose.* This is the one place where the sunna/obligation
line is genuinely contested. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr sets out the argument of those who
hold them merely recommended: **God did not mention them in His Book, His
Messenger did not make them obligatory, and there was no consensus on them — and
obligations are established only by those routes.** [3] Against that, he reports
a position that splits the two: **Abu Thawr and Abu 'Ubayd did not differ that
rinsing the mouth is a sunna and drawing water into the nose obligatory — they
said that whoever omits the nose-rinsing and prays must repeat, and whoever omits
the mouth-rinsing need not.** [4]

**What this is actually for.** Not so you can do less. The four are what makes
the difference between a wudu that is valid and one that has to be repeated —
which matters when you have already prayed, or when you are at work with two
minutes and a cold tap. Do the full form when you can. Know the four for when
you cannot.

The step-by-step complete wudu is a separate answer in this collection, and the
order of the four is another, since the schools differ on whether that order is
itself required.

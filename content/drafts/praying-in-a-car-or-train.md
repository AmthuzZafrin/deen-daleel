# Can I pray sitting in a car or train?

## Answer

**Voluntary prayers, yes — seated, facing wherever the vehicle faces. The five
obligatory prayers, no, unless you genuinely cannot get out.**

**The distinction is old and explicit.** Ibn Qudama: **we know of no disagreement
among the People of Knowledge on the permissibility of voluntary prayer on a mount
during a long journey.** [1] Ibn Abd al-Barr states it as consensus: **they agreed
that it is permitted for everyone who travels a journey in which the prayer is
shortened to pray voluntary prayers on his mount wherever it faces, gesturing for
the bowing and the prostration, making the prostration lower than the bowing.** [2]

That is exactly how you pray in a car or on a train: seated, facing forward, a dip
of the head for ruku' and a deeper one for sujud.

But the same reports carve out the obligatory prayers. The Prophet **used to pray
voluntary prayers on the back of his mount, facing wherever he was facing,
gesturing with his head** [3] — and Muslim's wording adds the limit: **except that
he would not pray the obligatory prayer on it.** [4]

**And it is not restricted to long journeys.** The Hanafis and most others extend
it. Al-Fatawa al-Hindiyya: **voluntary prayer on a mount outside the city is
permitted, and he gestures wherever the mount faces** [5] — and, **the sound view
is that the traveller and the non-traveller are alike in this, so long as he is
outside the city** [6]. So a commute counts, if you are outside the town.

**So, for the five daily prayers**

1. **Stop, if you can.** A service station, a layby, a platform, a car park. This
   is the answer whenever it is possible.
2. **If you genuinely cannot** — a coach that will not stop, a train with hours to
   run and the prayer time expiring, being driven by someone who will not pull over
   — pray seated in the vehicle, facing the qibla at the start if you can manage it,
   gesturing for bowing and prostration.
3. **A prayer prayed in its time seated is better than one prayed after its time
   standing.** That principle is set out in the separate answer here on praying with
   najasa you cannot remove, and it applies here identically.
4. **Consider combining instead.** On a journey you may pray Dhuhr and Asr together
   at either time, and Maghrib with Isha — which usually removes the problem
   entirely. There is a separate answer here on it.

**Practically**

| | |
|---|---|
| Nafl, dhikr, witr on a long drive or train | **yes**, seated, any direction |
| Fard, and you can stop | **stop and pray properly** |
| Fard, moving, time about to expire, cannot stop | pray seated; valid |
| Fard, and you could have combined earlier | plan better next time; pray it now |
| **Driving yourself** | never pray while driving — stop first |

**On the qibla in a vehicle.** For voluntary prayer, wherever you are facing is
fine, by the consensus above. For an obligatory prayer prayed out of necessity,
face the qibla for the opening takbir if you can, then carry on facing forward.

**A note on planes**, which is a related but slightly different case with its own
answer here.

لا نعلم خلافا بين أهل العلم في إباحة التطوع على الراحلة في السفر الطويل. قال الترمذي: هذا عند عامة أهل العلم. وقال ابن عبد البر: أجمعوا على أنه جائز لكل من سافر سفرا يقصر فيه الصلاة أن يتطوع على دابته حيثما توجهت، يومئ بالركوع والسجود، يجعل السجود أخفض من الركوع. وأما السفر القصير وهو ما

# Is vaping haram?

> **This question is marked `expect: decline`.** The answer must explain
> why it needs a person — a mufti, a doctor, a lawyer — and must not give
> a ruling. Cite only to show what the question touches on.

## Answer

No text names it, and the honest answer distinguishes two cases that usually get collapsed into one: a non-smoker taking it up, and a smoker using it to quit.

**The rule that decides it.**

The same principle that governs smoking. Ibn Taymiyya: "**everything that benefits is *tayyib*, and everything that harms is *khabith*** — and the correspondence, evident to everyone with a mind, is that benefit corresponds to permission and harm corresponds to prohibition. And the concomitance [is clear]: for prohibition turns with harms — present in carrion, blood, the flesh of swine, animals with fangs and talons, wine and others of what harms people's selves" [1].

He puts it more briefly elsewhere: "the *tayyib* is what benefits a person, and He forbade the foul, which is what harms him" [2]; and in his tafsir: "the good things He permitted are the foods beneficial to intellects and characters, and the foul things are those harmful to intellects and characters — just as wine is the mother of foul things, because it corrupts intellects and characters" [3].

The Qur'an frames the pair the same way: "Say: The evil and the good are not alike even though the plenty of the evil attract thee" (5:100) [4].

**Applying it is harder here than with cigarettes, and pretending otherwise is not useful.**

With tobacco, the harm is settled beyond argument, so the rule bites immediately. With vaping the medical picture is younger and still moving. What is not in dispute: nicotine is addictive; the aerosol is not inert; and the products are heavily marketed to people who have never smoked. What is disputed is *how much* harm, and over what timescale.

That difference in evidence produces a difference in verdict between the two cases:

**For someone who does not smoke** — and especially a teenager or young adult — this is straightforward on the rule above. You would be taking on an addictive substance that offers no benefit, at some level of harm that is established but not yet fully measured. There is nothing on the *tayyib* side of the ledger for it to weigh against. Avoid it.

And note the corrosive argument to watch for: *it's safer than cigarettes*. That is a comparison, not a permission. Something can be less harmful than a harmful thing and still be harmful — the rule turns on benefit and harm, not on ranking against a worse option.

**For an existing smoker using it to stop** — the comparison genuinely changes, because the alternative is not "nothing", it is continuing to smoke. On the same harm calculus, moving from a settled severe harm to a lesser one is a move in the right direction, and scholars who permit it in this case are applying the identical rule to different facts. Two conditions make that reasoning honest rather than a rationalisation: that it is genuinely a means of quitting, with an end in view — not a permanent substitute or a way to smoke where smoking is banned; and that you would say the same thing to someone who asked you why you were doing it.

If you are a smoker and vaping is what your doctor or a cessation programme recommends, taking that route with the intention of coming off both is defensible. If a year later you are still vaping and no longer trying to stop, the case that justified it has expired.

**A caution about the verse people usually cite.**

The proof most often quoted for "don't harm yourself" is *and be not cast by your own hands to ruin* (2:195) [5]. It is worth knowing that a Companion is on record narrowing it. Abu Ayyub al-Ansari explained that it came down about the Ansar: "we said to one another privately: Allah has honoured Islam and its helpers have become many, so let us stay with our properties and put them in order — and Allah sent down, rejecting that upon us: *and spend in the way of Allah and be not cast by your own hands to ruin*… so *the ruin* was our staying behind with our properties" [6]. When people applied it in the opposite direction — calling a man who charged the enemy self-destructive — Abu Ayyub objected: "People, you are interpreting this verse in this way, but it was only revealed concerning us" [7].

So do not rest the case on that verse. The stronger ground is the *tayyib*/*khabith* rule, which is stated as a rule and applies directly.

**Money.** Ibn 'Abd al-Barr: "the Messenger of Allah ﷺ forbade the squandering of wealth" [8]. A recurring spend on something with no benefit is the clear case.

**Fasting.** Vaping during a fast should be avoided, and for most schools it breaks it. But the reasoning is worth knowing, because it is not uniform. The excuse for smoke reaching the throat is grounded in *unavoidability* — al-Jassas: "it resembles dust and smoke entering his throat, so it does not break his fast" [9] — which plainly does not cover something you deliberately inhale. Ibn Taymiyya, however, argues from a different criterion: "what is forbidden is only what reaches the stomach and turns into blood and is distributed through the body — and we make this a sixth aspect: so we measure kohl and the enema and the like against incense and oil and the like, by the common factor between them" [10]. On that reasoning, a vapour that does not nourish would be treated differently.

The practical upshot: this is a real disagreement in principle, but it does not give you a licence. Do not vape while fasting.

**In short.** If you don't vape, don't start — the rule the tradition supplies has nothing to put on the other side of the scale. If you smoke and are using it to quit, that is a defensible use of the same rule, on the condition that quitting is actually the plan. Either way, an addiction is a thing to get out of, not a thing to relocate.

```
وقوله تعالى: {وما علمتم من الجوارح مكلبين} أي: أحل لكم الذبائح التي ذكر اسم الله عليها والطيبات من الرزق، وأحل لكم ما اصطدتموه (7) بالجوارح، وهي من الكلاب والفهود والصقور وأشباه ذلك، كما هو مذهب الجمهور من الصحابة والتابعين والأئمة، وممن قال ذلك: علي بن أبي طلحة، عن ابن عباس في قوله: {وما علمتم من الجوارح مكلبين} وهن (8) الكلاب المعلمة (9) والبازي، وكل طير يعلم للصيد (10) والجوارح: يعني الكلاب الضواري والفهود والصقور وأشباهها.

رواه ابن أبي حاتم، ثم قال: وروي عن خيثمة، وطاوس، ومجاهد، ومكحول، ويحيى بن أبي كثير، نحو ذلك. وروي عن الحسن أنه قال: الباز والصقر من الجوارح. وروي عن علي بن الحسين مثله. ثم روي عن مجاهد أنه كره صيد الطير كله، وقرأ قول الله [عز وجل] (11) {وما علمتم من الجوارح مكلبين} قال: وروي عن سعيد بن جبير نحو ذلك.

ونقله ابن جرير عن الضحاك والسدي، ثم قال: حدثنا هناد، حدثنا ابن أبي زائدة، أخبرنا ابن جريج، عن نافع، عن ابن عمر قال: أما ما صاد من الطير البزاة وغيرها من الطير،   فما أدركت فهو لك، وإلا فلا تطعمه.
```

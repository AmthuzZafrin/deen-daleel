# I prayed facing the wrong way — do I repeat it?

## Answer

**No. If you made a reasonable effort to find the direction, your prayer stands —
even if you later discover you were wrong.**

**The ruling, in a worked case.** Al-Fatawa al-Hindiyya: **a man prayed in the
mosque on a dark night by estimation, and it became clear that he had prayed
towards other than the qibla — his prayer is valid, because it is not upon him to
knock on people's doors to ask about the qibla.** [1]

That last clause is the whole principle. You are obliged to make a *reasonable*
effort, not an exhaustive one. Having done that, the outcome is not held against
you.

**Another case, closer to the modern one.** Someone confused about the direction
*in Mecca itself*, unable to ask anyone: **a man to whom the qibla was unclear in
Mecca, in that he was confined and there was no one present to ask, so he prayed by
estimation and then it became clear he had erred — it is narrated from Muhammad
that there is no repetition upon him, and that is the more analogically sound
view.** [2]

And the general statement: **they did not inform him, so he estimated and prayed —
it is valid even if it becomes clear that he erred.** [3]

**What "reasonable effort" means**

| | |
|---|---|
| Used a qibla app, or followed a mosque's mihrab | effort made — prayer stands |
| Asked someone nearby | effort made — prayer stands |
| Worked it out from the sun, or a map | effort made — prayer stands |
| Simply guessed without trying, when you easily could have checked | this is the case where repeating is required |

The line is between *estimating after trying* and *not bothering*.

**If you realise mid-prayer.** Turn to the correct direction and continue — you do
not restart. The Hindiyya's discussion of someone whose judgement shifts between
rak'ahs assumes exactly this: he continues in the new direction rather than
beginning again, and there is a recorded position that even **praying four rak'ahs
towards four directions** [2] is valid where the judgement genuinely changed each
time.

**If you realise afterwards.** Nothing is owed. Do not make up the prayer, and do
not carry it around.

**Two situations this covers directly**

*You've been praying the wrong way for months.* It happens — a flat map read
wrongly, a badly-oriented flat, a compass thrown off by a steel building. All the
prayers you prayed in good faith are valid. Correct the direction going forward
and leave the past alone.

*The mosque and your app disagree.* Follow the mosque. Ibn Qudama's reason is that
established qiblas **are set up by people of expertise and knowledge**, and that
is a stronger source than a phone compass indoors, which is easily deflected.

**The exception, for completeness.** If you are *looking at* the Ka'ba, you must
face it precisely; there is no estimation for someone who can see it. That applies
in the Haram and nowhere else.

**And a note on proportion.** Facing the qibla is a condition of prayer, but the
condition is written for people who cannot see Mecca and never will. The entire
tier system exists to make sure their prayers count. If you tried, yours counted.

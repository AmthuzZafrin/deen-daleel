# There's no water where I work — can I do tayammum?

## Answer

**Only if there is genuinely no usable water. A sink in the building is water,
even if using it is awkward.** That is the hard part of this question, and it is
worth being straight about it before the encouraging part.

**Being at home rather than travelling is not the obstacle.** The verse mentions
journeys because that is where the problem usually arises, not as a condition.
Ibn Abd al-Barr: **the scholars differed over tayammum while resident when there
is no water; Malik and his colleagues held that tayammum while travelling and
while resident are the same, when water is absent or its use is impossible on
account of illness or severe fear.** [1] The hadith is framed in general terms —
**good clean earth is the purifier of the Muslim, even if he does not find water
for ten years** [2], and **the earth has been made for me a mosque and a
purifier** [3].

**But awkwardness is not absence.** This is where the ruling is strict, and the
strictness has a reason. Al-Jassas: **whenever he is one who has water, he must
use it, whether he fears the time will run out or does not** [4] — the presence of
water ends the permission, full stop. He allows only one exception, Malik's:
**Malik said tayammum suffices him if he fears the time will pass** [5]. The
Shafi'is are narrower still, permitting it to a healthy resident only where there
is real danger.

So the honest answer for most workplaces is: **there is water, and you must use
it.** A bathroom sink is water. A kitchen tap is water. That washing your feet
there is uncomfortable is not, in the classical reckoning, an absence.

**What to do instead, and it solves the problem completely**

- **Come to work in wudu.** Make it at home. It lasts until something breaks it.
- **Wipe over socks.** This is the actual answer, and it is why the concession
  exists. Make full wudu at home, put socks on, and from then on you wipe over
  them instead of washing your feet — for a full day and night as a resident.
  Renewing wudu at work then means face, arms, head and a wipe over the socks,
  all at a sink, with nothing to take off. There are separate answers here on
  this and on which socks qualify.
- **Use a bottle** if the sink is unworkable, or the accessible washroom if there
  is one. There is a separate answer here on making wudu in a public bathroom.

**When tayammum genuinely does apply at work**

- **The water is shut off**, or there is none in the building.
- **You cannot reach it** — locked out, a site with no facilities, on the road.
- **Using it would harm you** — a skin condition, a wound, a cast. There is a
  separate answer here on that, and the ruling is much more generous.
- **Malik's case**: the prayer time is about to expire and you cannot reach water
  before it does. Not every school allows this, but it is a real position of an
  imam.

**How to do it there:** a wall, a stone windowsill, the ground outside — anything
of the earth, clean. There is a separate answer here on the method.

**One thing to hold onto if you do end up praying with tayammum.** Malik's ruling
on this is one of the most generous sentences in the chapter: **whoever rises for
prayer and does not find water, and acts on what Allah commanded him with regard
to tayammum, has obeyed Allah — and the one who found water is no purer than he
is, nor is his prayer more complete, because both were commanded, and each acted
on what Allah commanded him with.** [6]

That is not a consolation prize. It is a statement that the two are equal in the
sight of the law.

**A practical note:** on Malik's reckoning a fresh tayammum is needed for each
prayer — **rather, he performs tayammum for each prayer, because he is obliged to
seek water for each prayer; and whoever seeks water and does not find it performs
tayammum** [7]. The Hanafis are easier here. Either way, it takes ten seconds.

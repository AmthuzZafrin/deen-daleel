# How do I pray, step by step?

## Answer

**Start with what is obligatory, then add the rest.** If you learn only the first
list you can pray validly today; the second list is what makes it complete, and
you can pick it up over weeks.

**The obligatory core, from the Prophet's own teaching**

A man prayed badly in the mosque and the Prophet sent him back — **go back and
pray, for you have not prayed** — three times over. [1] When the man admitted he
knew no better and asked to be taught, this is what he was given:

**When you rise for prayer, say *Allahu akbar*; then recite what is easy for you
of the Qur'an; then bow until you are settled in bowing; then rise until you are
standing straight; then prostrate until you are settled in prostration; then rise
until you are settled sitting — then do that throughout your whole prayer.** [2]
In another wording: **when you rise for prayer, complete the wudu, then face the
qibla, and say the takbir.** [3]

Al-Nawawi notes what that is doing: **this hadith contains many benefits, and it
should first be known that it is understood as a statement of the obligations,
not of the sunnas.** [4] That is your floor.

**The Hanafi statement of the same floor.** Al-Quduri: **the obligations of the
prayer are six** [5] — **the opening takbir** [6], **standing, recitation, bowing,
prostration, and the final sitting for the length of the tashahhud** [7]. And
then, decisively: **whatever is beyond that is sunna.** [8]

**Now the full prayer, in order**

1. **Face the qibla, and intend the prayer** — in the heart, nothing spoken.
2. **Say *Allahu akbar*, raising your hands.** Al-Quduri: **when a man enters the
   prayer he says the takbir and raises his hands with it, until his thumbs are
   level with his earlobes.** [8]
3. **Place your right hand on your left**, and say the opening du'a: **Glory be to
   You, O Allah, and Yours is the praise; blessed is Your name and exalted is Your
   majesty, and there is no god but You** — then **seek refuge in Allah from the
   accursed Shaytan.** [9]
4. **Recite al-Fatiha, then a passage after it.** **Then he recites the Opening of
   the Book and a sura with it, or three verses from whatever sura he wishes.** [10]
5. **Say *Allahu akbar* and bow.** Hands on the knees, fingers spread, back
   flat — **he supports himself with his hands on his knees, spreads his fingers,
   and flattens his back; he neither raises his head nor lowers it.** [11] Say
   **Glory to my Lord the Magnificent** three times [12].
6. **Rise**, saying *sami' Allahu liman hamidah*, then *rabbana laka'l-hamd*.
7. **Say *Allahu akbar* and prostrate.** Forehead and nose on the ground. Say
   **Glory to my Lord the Most High** three times [13].
8. **Sit up**, saying *Allahu akbar*.
9. **Prostrate again**, the same way. That completes one **rak'ah**.
10. **Stand for the next rak'ah** and repeat from step 4 — but without the opening
    du'a and without seeking refuge again.
11. **After the second rak'ah, sit** for the tashahhud.
12. **In a three- or four-rak'ah prayer**, stand and complete the remaining
    rak'ahs, reciting only al-Fatiha in them.
13. **In the final sitting**, say the tashahhud, then the salawat on the Prophet,
    then a du'a of your choosing.
14. **Turn your head right and say *as-salamu alaykum wa rahmatullah*, then left
    and say it again.** The prayer is over.

**What to do about the Arabic if you don't have it yet.** Note that the Prophet's
instruction was **recite what is easy for you of the Qur'an** — not a fixed
minimum. There is a separate answer here on the smallest amount you need to start,
and another on how long it takes to learn al-Fatiha. Neither is a reason to delay
praying.

**How to actually learn this.** Not from a page. Stand next to someone in a mosque
and follow. Or watch one video through, then pray one rak'ah aloud with the page
open. The physical sequence takes an afternoon; the Arabic takes longer, and that
is fine.

**And the one thing the hadith above is really about**: *tuma'nina* — settling.
The man's error was speed. Bow until your body is still. Rise until you are
straight. Sit until you are settled. That is the difference between the prayer he
was sent back from and the prayer he was taught.

# Is forex or day trading allowed?

> **This question is marked `expect: decline`.** The answer must explain
> why it needs a person — a mufti, a doctor, a lawyer — and must not give
> a ruling. Cite only to show what the question touches on.

## Answer

**Exchanging one currency for another is lawful and ordinary — that is `صرف`, a named contract. But it carries one requirement that retail forex platforms are built to avoid: both sides must actually change hands before the parties part. Leveraged margin trading, overnight rollover, and cash-settled positions each fail that requirement on its own.**

**The contract and its condition.** Ibn Qudama: `الصرف: بيع الأثمان بعضها ببعض. والقبض في المجلس شرط لصحته بغير خلاف. قال ابن المنذر: أجمع كل من نحفظ عنه من أهل العلم على أن المتصارفين إذا افترقا قبل أن يتقابضا، أن الصرف فاسد` [1] — currency exchange is the sale of monies for one another; **taking possession in the session is a condition of its validity, without disagreement**; Ibn al-Mundhir reports the agreement of everyone whose view is preserved that if the two parties separate before taking possession, **the exchange is void.**

The underlying text: `لا تبيعوا الذهب بالذهب، إلا مثلا بمثل، ولا تشفوا بعضها على بعض، ولا تبيعوا الورق بالورق، إلا مثلا بمثل، ولا تشفوا بعضها على بعض، ولا تبيعوا منها غائبا بناجز` [2] — do not sell gold for gold except like for like, do not give one an advantage over the other, and **do not sell what is absent of them for what is present.**

Al-Nawawi records where the schools stand: `والتفرق قبل التقابض حرام كذلك عندنا وعند المالكية والحنبلية خلافا للحنفية فيما عدا الصرف` [3] — separating before mutual possession is forbidden in our school and among the Malikis and Hanbalis; the Hanafis differ **only in matters other than currency exchange**. On `صرف` itself there is no exit.

**How strictly this was applied.** The reported case is worth having in full, because it shows the tolerance the rule allows — which is none. Talha b. 'Ubaydullah exchanged a hundred dinars, took the gold, turned it over in his hand, and said *until my treasurer comes from the Ghaba.* 'Umar was listening and intervened: `والله لا تفارقه حتى تأخذ منه` [4] — **by God, you will not part from him until you take it from him.**

And 'Umar's own instruction: `وان استنظرك حتى يلج بيته فلا تنظره الا يدا بيد هات وهذا إني أخشى عليك الربا` [5] — **if he asks you to wait while he steps into his house, do not wait for him**; only hand to hand, *give and take* — I fear usury for you.

Sufyan b. 'Uyayna's assessment: `قال سفيان بن عيينة هذا أصح حديث روى عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم في هذا يعني في الصرف` [6] — the soundest report transmitted on this subject.

A short walk indoors was too long a delay. That is the standard.

**Applying it to what forex platforms actually offer.**

- **Leverage.** Trading a position many times your deposit means the broker advances the rest. That advance is a loan, and the position exists only because of it — bringing in both the loan rules and the requirement that you own what you sell.
- **Overnight rollover or swap.** A charge or credit for holding a position past a cut-off is, in substance, a payment for time on a currency balance. That is the increase the whole chapter is about; and a "swap-free" account does not by itself cure the other problems.
- **No delivery.** In most retail contracts nobody ever receives any currency. Positions are opened and closed and the difference is settled in your account balance. On the material above, the essential act — mutual, actual taking of both sides — never occurs at all. That is not a technicality about timing; it is the absence of the contract.
- **Short selling a currency you do not hold** runs into the prohibition on selling what you do not have, set out on the car-finance page.

Taken together, the standard retail product fails the condition that these books describe as agreed upon without dissent.

**What remains clearly permitted.**

- **Changing money for a real purpose** — travel, paying a supplier, moving savings between currencies — at a bureau or bank, where both sides are handed over then and there.
- **A spot conversion in a bank account** where both legs settle immediately and you genuinely own the resulting balance. Contemporary scholars generally treat crediting to your account as possession for this purpose; the settlement delays built into interbank practice are debated, and a scholar can tell you how their school treats them.
- **Holding a foreign currency and later converting it back**, which is two separate spot exchanges rather than a position.

**What the corpus does not decide.** It has nothing on electronic settlement, on whether a ledger entry counts as `قبض`, on standard settlement conventions, or on regulated brokers. Those are real questions on which qualified scholars differ, and someone converting money through a bank is in a genuinely different position from someone opening a leveraged position — the first has a live debate about the mechanics of possession, the second has no possession to debate.

**What to do.**

If you are converting currency for a real need, do it and do not overthink it — take both sides at the same time. If you are considering trading as a way to make money, the features that make it profitable to the platform are precisely the ones the material above rules out, and the honest summary is that the standard product is not a currency exchange at all. Take the specific contract to a scholar if you think yours is different, and ask them the one question that matters: **does each side actually receive the other's currency, and when?**

# How do I pray witr?

## Answer

**An odd-numbered prayer at the end of the night. One rak'ah, or three, or more —
the schools differ, and all the forms are sound.**

**How many rak'ahs?**

Al-Marghinani gives the Hanafi position: **witr is three rak'ahs, not separated
between them by a salam — because of what Aisha reported, that the Prophet used to
pray witr with three.** [1] Al-Nasafi states it as settled: **witr is obligatory**
[2], and **it is three rak'ahs with one salam** [3].

The other schools allow more forms: **and in one position he prays witr with two
salams, and that is the position of Malik** [4] — two rak'ahs, salam, then a single
rak'ah. And a single rak'ah on its own is valid on the Shafi'i and Hanbali schools.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hanafi** | 3 rak'ahs, one salam, qunut in the third |
| **Shafi'i, Hanbali** | 1, 3, 5, 7 or more — odd; 3 usually as 2 + 1 |
| **Maliki** | 2 + 1 |

**Is it obligatory?** The Hanafis say *wajib* — a rank below fard but binding.
Everyone else calls it a strongly emphasised sunna. Either way: pray it. Nobody
treats it as optional in practice, and the Prophet never left it.

**How to pray it (Hanafi form, three rak'ahs)**

1. **Intend witr**, say the takbir.
2. **First rak'ah**: al-Fatiha and a sura. **He recites in every rak'ah of the witr
   the Opening of the Book and a sura** [5] — including the third, which is unusual.
3. **Second rak'ah**: al-Fatiha and a sura, then sit for the first tashahhud, then
   stand.
4. **Third rak'ah**: al-Fatiha and a sura. Then, **before bowing**, raise your hands,
   say *Allahu akbar*, and recite the **qunut** du'a: **he makes qunut in the third
   before the bowing, always** [6].
5. Bow, prostrate, sit, and give the salam.

**Two-plus-one form (the other schools)**: pray two rak'ahs and give salam; then
stand and pray one rak'ah, with qunut after the bowing on the Shafi'i reckoning.

**On the qunut, which is the main visible difference.** Al-Marghinani: **he makes
qunut in the third before the bowing — and al-Shafi'i said after it** [7]. The
Hanafis also make it year-round, where the Shafi'is confine it to the last half of
Ramadan. Both are transmitted from the Prophet, and this is one of those
disagreements to follow rather than resolve.

**When to pray it**

- **Any time after Isha until dawn.** It is the last prayer of the night.
- **Best at the end of the night** if you expect to wake — after tahajjud. There is
  a separate answer here on that.
- **If you doubt you will wake**, pray it before you sleep. This is the standard
  advice and the practical one.
- **Once per night.** If you prayed witr and then wake and pray more, do not repeat
  the witr.

**If you don't know the qunut yet.** Pray the three rak'ahs and make du'a in your
own words in the third, standing, before bowing. The prayer is valid. Learn the
transmitted du'a — *Allahumma inna nasta'inuka...* — when you can.

**And a note on scale.** Witr can be one rak'ah. On a night when you are exhausted,
one rak'ah after Isha discharges it on three of the four schools, and three quick
ones on the fourth. It is designed to be the easiest thing you do all day.

وروي أنه كان يصلي بعد العشاء ركعتين ويصلي من الليل تسعا منها الوتر وكان   ينام أول الليل ويحيي آخره وما ألفاه السحر إلا عند أهله قائما وكان يوتر في آخر الليل حتى انتهى وتره إلى السحر وما قرأ القرآن كله قط في ليلة ولا صلى ليلة إلى الصبح وكان إذا فاته قيام الليل من وجع أو غيره صلى من النهار اثنتي عشرة ركعة وكان يقول الوتر ركعة من آخر الليل ويقول أوتروا قبل أن تصبحوا وقال صلاة آخر الليل مشهودة وذلك أفضل

وهذا كله صحيح في الصحيح وقد بينا في شرح الحديث الجمع بين اختلاف الروايات في عدد صلاته فإنه كان يصلي إحدى عشرة ركعة وهي كانت وظيفته الدائمة وكان يفتتح صلاة الليل بركعتين خفيفتين فهذه ثلاث عشرة ركعة

وكان يصلي إذا طلع الفجر ركعتين ثم يخرج إلى صلاة الصبح فهذا تأويل قول من روى أنه كان يصلي خمس عشرة ركعة

وقد روت عائشة في الصحيح أن النبي كان يصلي تسع ركعات فيها الوتر ولعل ذلك كان حين ضعف وأسن وحطمه البأس أو كان لألم والله أعلم

# Can I shake hands with the opposite gender?

## Answer

The majority position is no, with a non-mahram. The evidence is a single, well-known statement, and it is
worth seeing exactly what it says and what the scholars built on it.

### The report

Women came to give the Prophet ﷺ their pledge of allegiance, and he said: **"I do not shake hands with
women"** [1].

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr draws the ruling: **"in his saying 'I do not shake hands with women' is evidence that it
is not permitted for a man to have physical contact with a woman who is not lawful to him, nor touch her
with his hand, nor shake hands with her"** [2].

And he draws a second inference, which is the part that shows the statement was specific rather than
general: **"in his ﷺ saying 'I do not shake hands with women' is evidence that he used to shake hands with
men, at the pledge and otherwise"** [3]. Handshaking itself is a sunna — Ibn Hajar: **"the basis of
handshaking is a sunna"** [4] — and this is an exception carved out of it: **"excepted from the general
command to shake hands is the unrelated woman"** [5].

That is how the scholars read it. Not a general aversion to touch, but a specific exclusion.

### The variant reports

The material is not uniform, and the honest presentation includes that. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr also records
'Ata': **"the Prophet ﷺ did not shake hands with women"** [6], and Qays b. Abi Hazim: **"when the Prophet
ﷺ took the pledge, he did not shake hands with women except with a cloth on his hand"** [7].

The second wording is the basis of the practice, in some places, of offering a covered hand. It is a
minority route and not the majority's ruling, but it is in the sources rather than invented.

### What this means in a Western workplace

The ruling stands, and so does the difficulty. A refused handshake reads as an insult to someone who does
not know why, and the tradition takes the manner of a refusal seriously even where it upholds the refusal
itself.

What tends to work, and what nothing above forbids: put your right hand on your chest and greet the
person warmly by name; say plainly and without apology that you don't shake hands across genders as a
religious practice; get in first, before the hand is extended. The awkwardness is almost always in the
surprise rather than the rule.

### Where the schools differ

Some scholars have permitted it where there is no desire and no alternative, and some contemporary
authorities extend that to a professional setting where refusal would cause real harm. Others hold the
prohibition without exception. The Hanafi and majority position is the one set out above.

If this is a live problem in your work — a job interview, a client, a superior who will take offence —
that is a question to put to a scholar who will hear the specifics, not one to resolve from a general
page. What you should not conclude from this answer is that the situation has no solution.

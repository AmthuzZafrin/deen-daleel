# What are the five pillars?

## Answer

**The five are: the testimony of faith, the prayer, zakat, fasting Ramadan, and
Hajj if you are able.** They come from a single hadith, and the image in it is
worth keeping — a building, not a checklist.

Muslim heads the chapter with the wording: **"باب قول النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم
بني الإسلام على خمس"** — "the Prophet's ﷺ saying: Islam is built upon five" [1].
The report from Ibn 'Umar: **"بني الإسلام على خمس، شهادة أن لا إله إلا الله، وأن
محمدا عبده ورسوله، وإقام الصلاة، وإيتاء الزكاة، وحج البيت، وصوم رمضان"** —
*Islam is built upon five: testifying that there is no god but Allah and that
Muhammad is His servant and Messenger, establishing the prayer, giving zakat,
Hajj to the House, and fasting Ramadan* [2].

**The first pillar is described in more than one way, and the variants are
instructive.** One wording gives it as **"بني الإسلام على خمسة، على أن يوحد
الله، وإقام الصلاة، وإيتاء الزكاة، وصيام رمضان، والحج"** — *built upon five: that
Allah be declared one, establishing the prayer, giving zakat, fasting Ramadan,
and Hajj* [3]. Another is sharper still: **"بني الإسلام على خمس، على أن يعبد
الله، ويكفر بما دونه"** — *that Allah be worshipped and that what is besides Him
be rejected* [4]. All three are the same first pillar, put three ways: the
testimony, declaring Him one, and worshipping Him while rejecting the rest.

**In brief, what each one is.**

- **Shahada** — testifying that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is
  His Messenger [2]. Saying it sincerely is what makes a person Muslim.
- **Salah** — the five daily prayers [2].
- **Zakat** — the yearly obligatory portion of accumulated wealth given to those
  entitled to it, distinct from voluntary charity [2].
- **Sawm** — fasting the month of Ramadan [2].
- **Hajj** — the pilgrimage to Mecca, and the Jibril hadith attaches the
  condition explicitly: **"وتحج البيت إن استطعت إليه سبيلا"** — *and that you
  make Hajj to the House if you are able to find a way to it* [5]. It is owed
  once in a lifetime, by those who can.

**Where the five sit in the wider picture.** Ibn Taymiyya notes that the Prophet
ﷺ distinguished three things in the hadith of Jibril: **"قد فرق النبي في حديث
جبريل عليه السلام بين مسمى الإسلام ومسمى الإيمان ومسمى الإحسان"** — *he
distinguished between what is named Islam, what is named iman, and what is named
ihsan* [6] — and observed that **"اعلم أن الإيمان والإسلام يجتمع فيهما الدين
كله"** — *iman and islam together comprise the whole of the religion* [7]. So the
five pillars are the *islam* half: the outward frame. The six articles of faith
are the *iman* half. Neither is the whole thing on its own.

The building image in the hadith carries the same sense [1]: pillars are what
holds a structure up, not the whole of it. Honesty, prayerfulness, kindness to
parents and the rest of what Islam asks are not among the five, and are not
thereby optional.

**If you have just become Muslim.** You are already a Muslim by the first pillar
[2]. The prayer is the one that begins immediately and shapes the day. Zakat only
applies once you hold wealth above the threshold for a year, and Hajj only when
you are actually able [5] — so for most new Muslims those two are not yet live
questions. Fasting arrives with Ramadan. Nobody is expected to arrive with all
five running.

**On the language above:** "shahada" is the testimony; "salah" the prescribed
prayer; "zakat" the obligatory alms; "sawm" fasting; "Hajj" the pilgrimage.
"Islam" here has a narrow technical sense — the outward acts — alongside its
ordinary broad sense of the religion as a whole [6].

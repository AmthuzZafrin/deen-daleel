# Are there sunnah acts before sleeping?

## Answer

Yes, and they are unusually well-documented — Muslim devotes a whole chapter to what the Prophet ﷺ said on lying down. None of it is obligatory. Al-Nawawi is explicit that the central hadith contains "three important recommended *sunnas*, not obligatory ones" [1].

**The core hadith.**

Al-Bara' b. 'Azib reported the Prophet ﷺ saying: "When you take to your bed, perform your ablution as you would for prayer; then lie down on your right side; then say: *O Allah, I have submitted my face to You, and entrusted my affair to You, and turned my back to You in hope and in fear of You. There is no refuge and no escape from You except to You. I have believed in Your Book which You revealed, and in Your Prophet whom You sent.* And make them the last of your speech — for if you die that night of yours, you die upon the *fitra*" [2].

Al-Nawawi names the three sunnas in it: "the first is ablution when intending sleep — and if he is already in ablution, that ablution suffices him, since the aim is to sleep in a state of purity, for fear that he may die during his night, and so that his dream may be truer and further from Shaytan's playing with him in his sleep and frightening him. The second is sleeping on the right side, because the Prophet ﷺ loved starting on the right, and because it is quicker to wake. The third is the remembrance of Allah, so that it is the seal of his deeds" [1].

He also glosses the words: "*and I have turned my back to You* means: I have relied upon You and depended on You in all my affairs, as a person leans with his back upon what supports him. His words *in hope and in fear* mean: coveting Your reward and fearing Your punishment" [3].

**A detail worth noticing.** Al-Bara' repeated the words back to memorise them and said "*I have believed in Your Messenger whom You sent*" — and the Prophet ﷺ corrected him: "Say: *I have believed in Your Prophet whom You sent*" [4][5]. Al-Nawawi reports al-Mazari's explanation: "the reason for the objection is that this is a remembrance and a supplication, so one should confine oneself in it to the transmitted wording with its letters — and the recompense may be attached to those letters; and perhaps he ﷺ was given these words by revelation, so their delivery with their exact letters is required" [6].

The lesson: with a transmitted *du'a*, say it as it was given. Your own words are welcome too — but a *sunna* formula is not a paraphrase.

**The shorter ones, if that is too much at once.**

Al-Bara' also reported: "when he took to his bed he would say: *O Allah, in Your name I live and in Your name I die*; and when he woke he would say: *Praise be to Allah who gave us life after He caused us to die, and to Him is the resurrection*" [7]. Those two are short, paired, and easy to learn first.

Anas reported another: "when he took to his bed he would say: *Praise be to Allah who fed us and gave us drink, and sufficed us and gave us shelter — for how many are there who have none to suffice them and none to shelter them*" [8].

**Dust the bed first.**

Abu Hurayra reported: "When one of you goes to his bed, let him take the inner edge of his lower garment and dust his bed with it, and let him mention Allah's name — for he does not know what came onto his bed after him. Then when he wishes to lie down, let him lie on his right side, and say: *Glory be to You, my Lord; by You I have laid down my side and by You I raise it. If You take my soul, forgive it; and if You release it, protect it with what You protect Your righteous servants*" [9].

The original reason was scorpions and the like. The instruction survives the reason: a shake of the sheet and *bismillah* is what it asks of you.

**Why the right side.**

Ibn Hajar: "the right was specified for benefits — among them that it is quicker to wake; and among them that the heart is attached toward the right side, so it is not weighed down with sleep; and among them, Ibn al-Jawzi said: the physicians have stated that this posture is more wholesome for the body" [10]. He passes on their reasoning: "they said one begins by lying on the right side for a while, then turns to the left, because the first causes food to descend, and sleeping on the left digests, since the liver encloses the stomach" [11].

Nobody is asking you to hold a position all night. Start on your right.

**On sleeping in wudu.**

The point is not that sleep requires purity — it does not. Ibn Hajar gives the benefits, including "that his dream may be truer and further from Shaytan's playing with him. Al-Tirmidhi said: there is no mention of ablution at sleeping in the hadiths except in this hadith" [12]. Ibn Hajar also cites Mu'adh's report on the same theme: "there is no Muslim who spends the night in remembrance and purity, then stirs in the night and asks Allah for good of this world and the next, but that He gives it to him" [13].

If you are in a state of major impurity (*janaba*), you may still sleep. Al-Nawawi: "the upshot of all the hadiths is that it is permissible for the one in *janaba* to sleep, eat, drink and have intercourse before bathing, and this is agreed upon" [14] — but making wudu first is recommended: "our companions stated that sleeping, eating, drinking and intercourse before ablution is disliked, and these hadiths indicate it; and there is no disagreement among us that this ablution is not obligatory — and this is the position of Malik and the majority" [15].

**Waking up.**

Two small things. Use the *siwak* — 'A'isha reported that the Prophet ﷺ "never slept by night or by day and then woke but that he used the tooth-stick before performing ablution" [16]. A toothbrush serves. And washing the face: al-Nawawi notes "the wisdom in washing the face is the removal of drowsiness and the traces of sleep" [17].

**If you want a longer one.**

Abu Hurayra transmitted a fuller supplication the Prophet ﷺ taught: "*O Allah, Lord of the heavens and Lord of the earth and Lord of the mighty Throne; our Lord and the Lord of everything; Splitter of the grain and the date-stone; Revealer of the Torah, the Gospel and the Criterion — I seek refuge in You from the evil of every thing You seize by its forelock. O Allah, You are the First, so there is nothing before You; and You are the Last, so there is nothing after You; and You are the Manifest, so there is nothing above You; and You are the Hidden, so there is nothing beneath You. Settle our debt for us and enrich us out of poverty*" [18].

**In practice.** If you are starting from nothing tonight: make wudu, shake out the sheet, lie on your right side, say *bismika allahumma ahya wa bismika amut*, and when you wake say *alhamdulillah alladhi ahyana ba'da ma amatana wa ilayhi'n-nushur*. Add the longer supplication of al-Bara' when you have learned it.

And note what al-Nawawi says the third *sunna* is *for*: that the remembrance of Allah be "the seal of his deeds" [1]. That is the point of the whole practice. You do not know that you will wake up. This is how a Muslim goes to sleep anyway — settled, in wudu, with Allah's name as the last thing said.

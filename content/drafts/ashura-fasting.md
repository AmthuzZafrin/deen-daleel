# What is the fast of Ashura?

## Answer

It is a voluntary fast on the tenth of Muharram, the first month of the Islamic year. Al-Nawawi states the date and the accompanying one: **our colleagues say Ashura is the tenth day of Muharram, and Tasu'a is the ninth of it; this is our school, and the majority of scholars said the same** [1].

What is promised for it is in a hadith of Abu Qatada: **the fast of the day of Ashura — I reckon with God that it expiates the year before it** [2]. Al-Tirmidhi notes this wording is unique to Abu Qatada's report, and that Ahmad and Ishaq acted on it.

It has an unusual history. Aisha reports that **the day of Ashura was fasted by Quraysh in the age of ignorance, and the Messenger of God used to fast it** [3] — so it predates Islam as an Arabian custom, and he kept it and ordered it in Medina. Then Ramadan was made obligatory and its status changed: **when Ramadan was made obligatory the day of Ashura was left, so whoever wished fasted it and whoever wished left it** [4]. Al-Nawawi records the school-level dispute about whether it was ever strictly obligatory, and then the settled outcome: **the Muslims are agreed that today it is not obligatory, and that it is a sunna** [5].

The standard recommendation is to fast the ninth alongside the tenth. **Our colleagues and others agreed on the recommendedness of fasting Ashura and Tasu'a** [6], and al-Nawawi gives the reason from a report in Ahmad: **fast the day of Ashura and differ from the Jews — fast a day before it and a day after it** [7]. The principle behind it, as he puts it, is **that what is intended is differing from the Jews in their restricting themselves to the tenth** [8]. He offers two further explanations — joining an extra day to it as one does with Friday, and caution in case the month was miscounted and your "tenth" is really the ninth.

So in practice: fast the ninth and tenth of Muharram if you can, or the tenth and eleventh, or just the tenth. Nothing is owed if you miss it.

# Are chess and card games allowed?

## Answer

One thing is agreed and settles most of it: **money on the outcome makes it forbidden, full stop.** Beyond that the sources divide, and chess divides them differently from dice.

**The agreed part.**

Ibn Taymiyya: "the scholars are agreed that playing *nard* and chess is forbidden when it is for a stake — and that is the gambling and *maysir* which God has forbidden" [1]. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr says the same of chess: "**the scholars do not disagree** that gambling on it and consuming the stake by it is not lawful, and that it is of the forbidden *maysir*; and one known for doing so is a fool whose testimony is not accepted" [2].

That is one of the very few places in this whole area where consensus is real and undisputed. Any card game for money, any online game with a wager, any "friendly" pot — that is *maysir*, and it is the Qur'an's own word.

And the reach of *maysir* is wide. Ta'us and 'Ata': "**every gambling is of *maysir* — even children playing with knucklebones and walnuts**" [3]. The amount does not matter; the structure does.

**Dice: the strict case.**

Buraydah reported the Prophet ﷺ: "whoever plays *nardashir* is **as though he had dipped his hand in the flesh of a pig and its blood**" [4]. And in another wording: "whoever plays *nard* **has disobeyed God and His Messenger**" [5].

Ibn Taymiyya: that hadith is authentic, "and playing *nard* is forbidden **even if it is not for a stake**, according to the vast majority of scholars; and with a stake it is forbidden by consensus" [6].

There is a minority the other way, and Ibn 'Abd al-Barr reports it honestly rather than burying it: "I do not know of anyone who gave licence to play it except what has come from 'Abdullah b. Mughaffal — and 'Ikrima, al-Sha'bi and Sa'id b. al-Musayyab. For Shu'ba narrated... *I entered upon 'Abdullah b. al-Mughaffal and he was playing with his wife al-Khudayra' at al-qisab* — meaning *nardashir*; and it is narrated of 'Ikrima and al-Sha'bi that they used to play *nard*. And Ibn Qutayba mentioned... Sa'id b. al-Musayyab was asked about playing *nard* and said: **if it is not for gambling, there is no harm in it**" [7].

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr does not follow them — he holds the hadith decisive and takes the general reading as "better and more prudent". But he reports them, and so should anyone answering this question.

**Chess: a genuinely different picture.**

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr is explicit that this is not the same file: "as for chess, the scholars' disagreement about playing it is unlike their disagreement about playing *nard*, **because many of them permitted playing chess so long as it was not for gambling**" — and he names them: "Sa'id b. al-Musayyab, Sa'id b. Jubayr, Muhammad b. Sirin, Muhammad b. al-Munkadir, 'Urwa b. al-Zubayr and his son Hisham, Sulayman b. Yasar, Abu Wa'il, al-Sha'bi, al-Hasan al-Basri, 'Ali b. al-Husayn b. 'Ali, Ja'far b. Muhammad, Ibn Shihab, Rabi'a and 'Ata' — **all of these permit playing it other than for gambling**" [8].

Fifteen names, most of them among the most cited Successors in the fiqh of Medina and Kufa. That is not a fringe.

**The Maliki position, stated as a practical rule — and its reasoning is the most useful thing here.**

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr: "the upshot of the position of Malik and the majority of the jurists regarding chess is that whoever does not gamble with it, and plays with his household in his house discreetly, **once in a month or a year**, unobserved and unknown — that is pardoned for him: not forbidden to him, nor even disliked. And that if he becomes absorbed in it and reckless about it, his propriety and integrity lapse and his testimony is rejected. **And this shows you that it is not forbidden in itself and in its essence — because if it were, its little and its much would be equal in prohibition**" [9].

That last sentence is a piece of legal reasoning worth carrying with you, well beyond chess. When a ruling changes with quantity, the object is not what is being prohibited. The behaviour is.

**The strict view, stated fairly.**

It is real and it is the majority. Ibn Taymiyya: "playing chess is forbidden according to the vast majority of the scholars of the community and its imams, like *nard*" [10]. Malik: asked about playing chess, he said "**there is no good in it and it is nothing; it is of the futile — and all play is of the futile**" [11]. And the Companions' reports: "Ibn 'Umar was asked about chess and said: **it is worse than *nard***; and Abu Musa al-Ash'ari said: **none plays chess but a sinner**; and Abu Ja'far was asked about chess and said: **spare us this Magianism**" [12].

Even inside the strict camp there is disagreement about severity: "they disputed which of the two is worse — Malik and others said chess is worse than *nard*, and Ahmad and others said chess is lighter than *nard*" [13].

And one enjoyable exchange, since the "chess trains you for war" argument is as old as chess. Al-Turtushi, replying to a scholar who made it in al-Masjid al-Aqsa: "**rather it corrupts the management of war** — because the aim of war is the kingship and seizing it, whereas in chess you say *check* — *[meaning] O king, get out of my way*. And those present burst out laughing" [14].

**The criterion that cuts across all of it.**

Al-Qasim b. Muhammad was asked whether chess is *maysir*. He answered without answering: "**everything that distracts from the remembrance of God and from prayer is *maysir***" [15].

That is the most portable sentence in this file, and it is by one of the seven jurists of Medina. It says: stop asking about the board and ask about the hours.

**Practically.**

- **Any stake makes it forbidden.** Cards for money, a pot among friends, a wager on the outcome — this is the agreed case, and no school gives you a way round it.
- **Dice games are forbidden by the great majority even without stakes**, on an explicit and sound hadith. Backgammon, Ludo, Snakes and Ladders, any game whose engine is thrown dice — this is the ruling to expect, and a small minority of the *salaf* differed.
- **Chess and card games without stakes are genuinely disputed.** The majority prohibit; a long roster of Successors permitted them. If you follow a school, take its ruling.
- **On the Maliki working rule**, occasional private play without stakes is pardoned; habitual absorption is what costs you your standing. The reasoning — that a rule which varies with quantity is not about the object — is worth understanding rather than just applying.
- **Al-Qasim's test governs everything else.** A game that eats your prayers is condemned by every party here, whatever it is played with. A quarter of an hour with your children is not what any of these men were describing.

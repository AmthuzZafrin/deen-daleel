# Is Mother's Day allowed?

## Answer

**Nothing in this corpus discusses Mother's Day** — it is a twentieth-century invention and no jurist here had occasion to rule on it. But the underlying subject is one the sources treat at length, and what they say about it is worth far more than a ruling on a date.

**The core hadith.**

A man asked the Prophet ﷺ: *who among people has the most right to my good company?* He said: "**your mother.** Then who? He said: **then your mother.** Then who? He said: **then your mother.** Then who? He said: **then your father, then the next nearest to you, and the next**" [1].

Al-Qurtubi draws out the arithmetic and then the reason: "**this hadith indicates that love for the mother and compassion toward her ought to be three times the love for the father** — because the Prophet ﷺ mentioned the mother three times and mentioned the father only in the fourth. And when this meaning is reached, direct experience testifies to it: the hardship of pregnancy, the hardship of delivery, and the hardship of suckling and rearing **belong to the mother apart from the father — three stations the father is free of**" [2].

The jurists then argued about the proportion, which tells you how seriously they took it: "it is related of Malik that a man said to him: *my father is in the land of the Sudan and has written to me to come to him, and my mother prevents me from that.* He said to him: **obey your father and do not disobey your mother** — so Malik's statement indicates that kindness to the two is equal in his view. And al-Layth was asked about this question and ordered obedience to the mother, holding that **she has two thirds** of the kindness. And Abu Hurayra's hadith indicates that **she has three quarters**... and al-Muhasibi claimed in his *Kitab al-Ri'aya* that **there is no disagreement among the scholars that the mother has three quarters of the kindness and the father a quarter**" [3].

Ibn al-'Arabi puts it at the level of structure: "**kindness to parents is a pillar among the pillars of the religion, among the obligatory things**" [4].

**And the Prophet ﷺ ranked it above what a young man most wanted to do.**

A man came asking permission to go and fight. "He said: **are your parents alive?** He said yes. He said: **then strive in [serving] them**" [5].

Another: "*I pledge to you emigration and struggle, seeking the reward from God.* He said: **is either of your parents alive?** He said: yes, both of them. He said: **and you seek the reward from God?** He said yes. He said: **then go back to your parents and keep them the best company**" [6].

He turned a volunteer away from the front line and sent him home to his mother, and called that the reward he was looking for.

**This does not depend on their religion.**

Al-Qurtubi is explicit: "**kindness to parents is not restricted to their being Muslims** — rather if the two are disbelievers he shows them kindness and does good to them when they have a covenant" [7], citing *"Allah forbiddeth you not those who warred not against you on account of religion..."*

So if your mother is not Muslim, none of the above is reduced by a single degree.

**So — the day itself.**

The two things the sources do prohibit around dates are joining another religion's festival, and inventing an act of worship tied to a day. Mother's Day is neither. It is not a rite of any religion, and buying flowers is not a devotion.

If you follow the position that any recurring annual celebration is an added *'id* and should be left, that is a real view held by serious people, and following it costs your mother nothing — provided you do the thing the sources actually demand.

**Which is the real point.**

Nothing here makes one day in the year the measure. What the hadith describes is a standing obligation with a stated proportion, and the honest question this material puts to you is not whether you may send a card in March. It is:

- **When did you last ring her?** Not on the day everyone rings.
- **Does she have what she needs** — money, help, company, someone to take her to appointments?
- **Do you speak to her the way you would want your children to speak to you?**
- **If she is not Muslim, does she experience your religion as something that took her child away, or as the reason you are the one who shows up?**

Take the day if you like it. It is a good prompt and there is nothing here forbidding it. But a son who marks Mother's Day and is otherwise absent has the wrong end of this file, and a son who never marks it and calls every week has the right one.

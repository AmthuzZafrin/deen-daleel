# I didn't know about zakat for years — do I owe it all?

## Answer

Yes, in principle you owe the years you were above the threshold — and no, this is not a disaster. Take the second part as seriously as the first, because the panic this question usually comes with does more harm than the debt does.

**Why the years do not simply vanish.** Zakat is a right that attaches to wealth, and the jurists treat it as surviving the owner's ignorance of it. Ibn Qudama, working through a case where a man learns only later that the ground of an obligation had been there all along, reasons: **if years have passed over him and then he learns he was alive, he is obliged to pay for what has passed** [1] — **because the existence of the cause of obligation in past time has become clear to him, so paying for what has passed became obligatory on him** [2]. The obligation was not created by your discovering it. It was there, and you have now discovered it.

Al-Nawawi puts the same principle at its sharpest edge: even someone who **left Islam and then returned is obliged to pay what became due during his Islam and his apostasy** [3]. If the obligation survives that, it survives not having known.

And the sources are severe about withheld zakat as a category. Al-Jassas: **it is established that kanz is a name for that whose obligatory zakat has not been paid** [4] — the hoarded gold of Surah al-Tawba. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr reports Ibn 'Umar being asked what kanz is: **it is wealth from which zakat is not paid** [5].

**Now read that carefully, because it does not describe you.** What the texts condemn is *withholding* — knowing the obligation and refusing it. A person who did not know zakat existed, or did not know it applied to them, is not a hoarder. They are someone who has just learned something. The debt is real; the blame is not.

**What to actually do.**

1. **Work out which years you were above the nisab at all.** Most people who ask this have fewer qualifying years than they fear. Years as a student, years in debt, years below the threshold: nothing owed. If you never had savings above the nisab value, you owe nothing at all, and you can stop here.

2. **Estimate rather than agonise.** You will not have exact balances from eight years ago. Make an honest, slightly generous estimate for each qualifying year. The jurists did not require records that do not exist.

3. **Pay it as you can.** There is no requirement to settle it all at once. Treat it as a debt you are working down — a standing monthly payment alongside your current year's zakat is a perfectly sound way to discharge it, and far better than a lump sum you cannot manage and therefore never start.

4. **Set your date going forward** so this does not recur.

**If you became Muslim as an adult,** the years before that are not in question at all. Zakat is an obligation of the Muslim community on its members; it does not run retroactively into a life before Islam. Your count starts from your shahada — and realistically from when you first held a nisab after it.

**One point worth putting to a scholar** if your situation is large or complicated: the schools differ on whether missed zakat is a debt owed to the poor that must be discharged in full regardless of your current means, or whether present hardship can reduce it. If back-paying honestly would leave you unable to meet your own obligations, that is exactly the question to ask, and it is asked from a position of good faith, not evasion.

# Does getting changed break my wudu?

## Answer

**No. Changing your clothes does not break wudu** — not taking a shirt off, not
changing into pyjamas, not getting undressed to shower, not being naked. You can
change and pray without repeating anything.

The reason is worth understanding, because it settles a whole family of
questions like this one.

**Wudu is broken by events, not by states.** The hadath is a specific thing that
happens to your body. Bukhari records a man from Hadramawt asking Abu Hurayra
that exact question — what *is* a hadath? — and the answer is as concrete as it
gets: **no prayer is accepted from one who has broken his wudu until he performs
it; a man from Hadramawt said: what is the hadath, Abu Hurayra? He said: breaking
wind, silently or audibly.** [1] Being undressed is not an event. Nothing left
your body.

**And the list of breakers is a closed, argued-over set.** This is the strongest
evidence, and it is indirect: read what the scholars actually fought about. Ibn
Taymiyya surveys the disputes and they are all about bodily discharges and
contact — **the scholars disputed over impurity leaving other than the two
passages, such as a wound, bloodletting, cupping, nosebleed and vomiting** [2],
and **they disputed over touching women and touching the penis, whether it
breaks wudu** [3]. Centuries of disagreement, school against school, and not one
of them raises clothing. If uncovering yourself broke wudu, it would be the most
frequently triggered nullifier in existence and there would be a chapter on it.
There is no chapter on it, because it is not one.

So the default holds. Al-Nawawi states it in the middle of the sleep dispute, and
it is the governing rule for anything not on the list: **the default is that
purity remains.** [4] Whoever claims some further thing breaks wudu has to prove
it.

**Practical cases this covers:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Getting changed after work | wudu intact |
| Undressing to shower — without a full bath being due | wudu intact |
| Changing a baby's clothes | wudu intact (touching najasa is washed off, not re-wudu'd) |
| Sleeping in your clothes, then changing | the *sleep* is the question, not the change |

Note the last row. If something else happened while you were changing — you slept,
used the toilet, broke wind — then *that* broke your wudu, and there are separate
answers here on each. Do not let the two get confused: it is easy to feel that
the whole episode broke your wudu when only one part of it did.

**May I renew anyway?** Yes, and there is reward in it, but understand what you
are doing. Al-Jassas gathers the reports on renewing wudu for each prayer without
being in a state of hadath and concludes: **all of this indicates the desirability
of wudu at every prayer, even if he is not in a state of hadath.** [5] The
Prophet said, **wudu upon wudu is light upon light.** [6] Ibn Taymiyya says the
same about the contested cases: **the most apparent view in all of these
categories is that they do not break wudu, but wudu is recommended from them.**
[7] That is a *virtue* — an optional extra. It is not a repair, and treating it
as one is the road to washing yourself into misery over a clean shirt.

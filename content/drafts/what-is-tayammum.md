# What is tayammum and when can I use it?

## Answer

**Tayammum is purification with clean earth instead of water.** You touch the
ground, wipe your face and your hands, and you may pray. It stands in for both
wudu and ghusl when water is unavailable or would harm you.

**The Qur'an authorises it in the same breath as wudu**, which tells you how
ordinary it is meant to be: **and if ye are sick or on a journey, or one of you
cometh from the closet, or ye have had contact with women, and ye find not water,
then go to clean, high ground and rub your faces and your hands with some of it**
[1]. The verse is repeated almost word for word at 4:43 — **and if ye be ill, or
on a journey, or one of you cometh from the closet, or ye have touched women, and
ye find not water, then go to high clean soil and rub your faces and your hands
(therewith)** [2].

The word itself just means *to head for something*. Ibn Abd al-Barr: **its meaning
in the Shari'a is heading for the earth specifically, for purification, when water
is absent.** [3]

Al-Nawawi places it beyond dispute and notes what it signifies: **know that
tayammum is established by the Book, the Sunna and the consensus of the Umma, and
it is a distinction with which Allah singled out this Umma.** [4] Earlier
communities had no such provision — the hadith says **the earth has been made a
purifier for me** [5].

**When you may use it**

- **No water**, or not enough for a full purification.
- **Water is present but out of reach** — you cannot get to it, or reaching it
  would put you in danger.
- **Illness or injury**, where using water would make things worse. There is a
  separate answer here on that case.
- **Severe cold** with no means of warming water, where washing would harm you.

**Travelling is not a requirement.** The verse mentions journeys because that is
the common case, not as a condition. Ibn Abd al-Barr on the disagreement: **the
scholars differed over tayammum while resident when there is no water; Malik and
his colleagues held that tayammum while travelling and while resident are the
same, when water is absent or its use is impossible on account of illness or
severe fear** [6]. If there is genuinely no usable water, where you are standing
does not change the ruling. There is a separate answer here on doing tayammum at
work.

**What it does and does not do**

This is the part that determines everything downstream. Ibn Abd al-Barr: **the
scholars are agreed that the purification of tayammum does not lift janaba, nor
minor impurity, once water is found.** [7]

Tayammum is not a lesser wash. It is a *permission to pray in a state that would
otherwise bar you* — a licence with a condition attached, and the condition is the
absence of water. The moment water becomes available, the underlying state
returns: if you were in janaba, you are in janaba again and must bathe; if you
merely lacked wudu, you make wudu.

**Practical consequences:**

- **One tayammum works for both wudu and ghusl** — the procedure is identical
  whichever you are substituting for.
- **You do not repeat prayers** you prayed with a valid tayammum once water
  arrives. They were valid.
- **Water appears before you start praying** → the tayammum is finished; use the
  water.
- **Water appears during the prayer** → genuinely disputed, and there is a
  separate answer here on the mechanics.

**What you can use.** Earth, sand, stone, dust — anything of the ground, clean.
The Hanafis are the broadest here and the Shafi'is the narrowest, but every school
permits ordinary soil and sand, and none requires that you find loose dust
specifically. A wall or a dusty stone will do.

There is a separate answer here on exactly how to perform it, and one on doing it
when illness rather than absence of water is the reason.

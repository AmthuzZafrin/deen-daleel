# How does divorce actually work in Islam?

## Answer

Divorce in Islam is designed to be slow. The Qur'an sets it out as a staged process with two chances to
stop: **"Divorce must be pronounced twice and then (a woman) must be retained in honour or released in
kindness"** [1].

Al-Qurtubi glosses the second half: **"the meaning of His saying 'or released in kindness' is that he
leaves her without taking her back until her waiting period expires"** [2]. So the design is: pronounce
once, wait; the marriage stays alive through the waiting period and he may take her back. Twice. Only on
the third does it become final.

### The correct way to do it

Al-Quduri sorts the forms: **"divorce is of three modes: the best divorce, the sunna divorce, and the
innovated divorce"** [3].

**The best.** **"The best divorce is that a man divorce his wife with a single pronouncement, in a period
of purity in which he has not had relations with her, and leave her until her waiting period
expires"** [4]. One pronouncement. Then nothing — no repetition, no confirming it, no announcing it three
times to make it stick. The waiting period does the rest.

**The sunna form**, if more than one is used: **"the sunna divorce is that he divorce a woman with whom
the marriage has been consummated three times in three periods of purity"** [5] — spaced across three
cycles, months apart, with every opportunity to reconsider.

**The innovated form**, which is what most people actually do: **"the innovated divorce is that he divorce
her three times with a single word, or three times in one period of purity. If he does that, the divorce
takes effect and she becomes irrevocably separated from him, and he is sinful"** [6]. Al-Qurtubi adds
divorcing during menstruation or post-natal bleeding to the same category [7].

Note the two halves of [6]: sinful, *and* effective. In the Hanafi position, doing it wrongly does not
undo it.

Al-Qurtubi explains why the concentrated form is condemned: **"the falling of three at once is not good,
because in it is the abandonment of the latitude God has widened and drawn attention to"** [8]. The
staging exists to give you room. Using it up in one sentence throws that away.

### If it was said in the wrong state

Al-Quduri: **"if a man divorces his wife during menstruation, the divorce takes effect, and it is
recommended that he take her back"** [9]. Take her back, wait for a clean cycle, and then decide
properly — which usually means not divorcing at all.

### What counts as a divorce

Two categories. **"Divorce is of two kinds: explicit and allusive. The explicit is his saying 'you are
divorced', 'divorced woman', 'I have divorced you' — by this a revocable divorce takes effect, and only
one takes effect by it, even if he intended more than that"** [10]. Note the last clause: in the Hanafi
school, one explicit pronouncement is one divorce regardless of what was in his head.

Allusive wording — "go back to your family", "you are free of me" — is different: **"the second kind is
the allusive expressions, and divorce does not take effect by them except with evidence or a contextual
indication"** [11]. Intent has to be established.

And capacity is required: **"the divorce of every husband takes effect if he is sane and adult; the
divorce of a child, an insane person, or a sleeping person does not take effect"** [12]. Whether extreme
anger or intoxication falls under this is a live dispute and its own question.

### The three states afterwards

Ibn Taymiyya: **"divorce is of three kinds by the agreement of the Muslims. The revocable divorce, which
is the one in which he can take her back without her choosing"** [13] — the marriage is not over; she is
still in the house, still maintained, still inheriting from him if he dies.

**"The irrevocable divorce, by which he remains one of her suitors, and she is not lawful to him except
by a new contract"** [14] — over, but they may remarry, with a new nikah and a new mahr.

And the third, the one that requires an intervening marriage, which applies **"where he has divorced her
three pronouncements, as God and His Messenger permitted"** [15] — meaning three *separate* divorces
across three periods, not one outburst.

### The "three at once" dispute

This is where the classical positions diverge sharply, and it matters enormously to people who have said
it in anger.

Al-Qurtubi records: **"'Ali b. Abi Talib and Ibn Mas'ud said: one divorce is binding on him — and Ibn
'Abbas said the same"** [16]. Ibn 'Abbas's argument: **"his saying 'three' has no meaning, because he did
not divorce three times"** [17] — the verse says divorce is *twice*, i.e. successively, so saying a
number does not multiply an act performed once.

The four schools' dominant position is the opposite: three said at once count as three. But the minority
view is held by major Companions, and Ibn Taymiyya is the best-known later authority for it.

**If this has happened to you, do not conclude anything from this page.** Which position applies depends
on your school, your wording, your state at the time, and — in many countries — the civil law. Take it to
a qualified scholar or a shari'a council, in person. That is genuinely the answer, not a deflection.

### What comes first

Nothing above is a first step. The Qur'an's own sequence puts arbitration before any of it, and the
waiting period exists because reconciliation is expected to happen inside it. If you are at the point of
asking how divorce works, the more useful questions are what a waiting period is, whether a wife can
initiate separation, and what mediation the law requires first.

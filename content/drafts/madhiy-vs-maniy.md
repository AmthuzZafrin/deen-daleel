# What's the difference between the discharges, and which needs ghusl?

## Answer

**Three different fluids, three different rulings.** Getting them apart is worth
five minutes, because the difference is between needing a full bath and needing
nothing at all.

| | what it is | ruling |
|---|---|---|
| **Maniy** (semen) | thick, whitish, with a distinct smell; released with desire, and the arousal subsides after it | **ghusl** — a full bath |
| **Madhy** (pre-seminal fluid) | thin, clear, sticky; appears with arousal, often unnoticed | **wudu**, and wash the area |
| **Wady** | thick and white, comes out *after* urinating, nothing to do with arousal | **wudu**, and wash the area |

**The definitions, from Ibn Qudama.** **As for wady, it is a white water that
comes out after urine, thick — and its ruling is the ruling of urine exactly,
because it comes out of the outlet of urine and runs its course.** [1] And: **as
for madhy, it is a viscous, thin water that comes out following desire, at the tip
of the penis; and the apparent position of the school is that it is impure.** [2]

All three are impure and must be washed off clothing — **what comes out of the two
outlets, such as urine, faeces, madhy, wady, blood and other things: we know of no
disagreement about its impurity** [3]. But only semen puts you in janaba.

**The rule that decides it: no emission, no bath.** This is the practical heart
of the question. Al-Nawawi: **if he kisses a woman and feels the semen moving and
descending, and holds his penis so that nothing comes out at the time, and he does
not know of it coming out afterwards — then there is no ghusl upon him in our
view.** [4]

Arousal is not the trigger. Sensation is not the trigger. *Emission* is. He gives
the parallel that makes it obvious: **the scholars are agreed that whoever feels
the hadath — such as a rumbling or wind — and nothing comes out of him, owes no
wudu; so likewise here.** [5]

**Applied to the ordinary cases**

- **Aroused, wet patch, no release** → almost certainly madhy. **Wudu**, wash the
  spot on your body and the mark on your clothes. No bath.
- **Woke up with dampness, no dream** → the fiqh has a rule for this, and there is
  a separate answer here on wet dreams. Short version: if your penis was at rest
  when you slept, treat it as semen and bathe.
- **Dampness after urinating** → wady. Nothing but wudu, and it is not a sign of
  anything.
- **Release** → ghusl.

**How to tell them apart in practice.** Semen is thick and has a smell you will
recognise; the arousal ends with it. Madhy is thin and clear and the arousal
continues. Wady comes after urination and has no connection to arousal at all. If
you genuinely cannot tell and you were not aroused, the schools differ — there is
a separate answer here that sets out the disagreement.

**A note on scruple.** Madhy is extremely common, and for some people constant. It
requires washing the affected area and a fresh wudu — not a shower, not a change
of everything, and not a bath before every prayer. If it is continuous, you fall
under the ruling for people with a persistent discharge, which has its own answer
here and is far easier than repeating.

**A note for reverts.** This distinction is not obvious and nobody is born knowing
it. Prayers prayed before you knew the difference are not something to go back
over. From here on, apply the table.

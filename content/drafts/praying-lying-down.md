# How do I pray if I can't get out of bed?

## Answer

**You pray lying down, gesturing with your head. The prayer is not waived, and it
is not deficient.**

**The rule comes from the Prophet's instruction to Imran ibn Husayn**, who had
haemorrhoids and asked how to pray. Al-Marghinani gives it: **when the sick person
is unable to stand, he prays sitting, bowing and prostrating — because of his
words to Imran ibn Husayn: "pray standing; if you cannot, then sitting; if you
cannot, then on your side, gesturing" — and because obedience is according to
capacity.** [1]

*Obedience is according to capacity.* That is the whole principle, and everything
below is its application.

**The full ladder, from the same passage.** The Prophet's instruction runs down
through every level of capacity: **the sick person prays standing; if he cannot,
then sitting; if he cannot, then on his back, gesturing — and if he cannot, then
Allah is the most worthy to accept his excuse.** [2]

Note where it ends. Not with the prayer being cancelled, but with an
acknowledgement that you have done what was in you.

**How to do it**

1. **Lie on your back with your feet towards the qibla**, if you can. Al-Marghinani:
   **if he is unable to sit, he lies on his back and puts his feet towards the
   qibla, and gestures for the bowing and the prostration.** [3]
2. **Or lie on your side facing the qibla** — also valid: **and if he lies on his
   side with his face towards the qibla and gestures, it is permitted** [4], though
   the Hanafis prefer the first position.
3. **Gesture with your head.** A small dip for the bowing, a deeper one for the
   prostration — **he makes his prostration lower than his bowing, because it
   stands in place of them and so takes their ruling.** [5]
4. **Do not raise anything to your forehead.** **If you are able to prostrate on
   the ground, prostrate; and if not, gesture with your head** [6] — a pillow or a
   book brought up to the face is not a prostration, and the fuqaha are explicit
   that it does not count while a head movement does.
5. **Recite as you normally would**, quietly.

**If you cannot even move your head.** The Hanafi position is that the prayer is
*postponed*, not cancelled: **if he is unable to gesture with his head, the prayer
is postponed from him — and he does not gesture with his eyes, nor with his heart,
nor with his eyebrows** [7]. And the postponement is not a cancellation: **his
saying "it is postponed from him" indicates that the prayer does not drop from
him** [8], provided he is lucid. You wait until you can, and pray then.

**What you can drop, and what you cannot**

- **Wudu** — if water would harm you, tayammum; if that is impossible too, there
  is a separate answer here on illness and purification. Someone else may help you.
- **Facing the qibla** — do it if you can be turned; if not, pray as you are.
- **Cleanliness of clothes** — small amounts are excused, and there is a separate
  answer here on praying with najasa you cannot remove.
- **The prayer itself** — not dropped, at any level of this ladder.

**Practical notes for hospital and bed**

- **Ask a nurse to help you turn** towards the qibla if it is easy; do not make it
  a struggle.
- **Someone can find the direction for you** on a phone.
- **A short prayer is a complete prayer.** There is no requirement of length.
- **Prayers missed while unconscious** are a different question, and there is a
  separate answer here on missed prayers.

**And the thing worth holding onto.** The chapter this comes from was written by
people who visited the sick and knew exactly how little a person in bed can manage.
Its whole structure is a descent through diminishing capacity in which the prayer
is never lost — and its last line, for someone who can do nothing at all, is not a
ruling but a reassurance: **Allah is the most worthy to accept his excuse.** [2]

# Is there anything Allah won't forgive?

## Answer

One thing, and only while a person holds to it: *shirk* — associating a partner
with Allah. Everything else is either forgiven or subject to His will. And the
sources are emphatic that even *shirk* is forgiven to whoever leaves it.

**The verse people take as the answer.** "Lo! Allah forgiveth not that a partner
should be ascribed unto Him. He forgiveth (all) save that to whom He will"
(4:48) [1].

**And the verse people forget to read alongside it.** "Say: O My slaves who have
been prodigal to their own hurt! Despair not of the mercy of Allah, **Who
forgiveth all sins**" (39:53) [2].

The two look contradictory. Ibn Taymiyya's reconciliation is the clearest thing
in this file, and it is the whole answer.

**How they fit.** He rules out the easy reading first: "He informed us that He
does not forgive *shirk*, and informed us that He forgives what is less than it
for whom He wills. And it is not permissible that the repentant be intended by
that, as some of the Mu'tazila say — because **Allah forgives *shirk* to whoever
repents**, and Allah likewise forgives what is less than *shirk* to the
repentant; so it would have nothing to do with His will" [3].

The logic: if 4:48 were about repentant people, the exception would be pointless,
because the repentant are forgiven *shirk* too. So 4:48 must be about the
unrepentant — and among the unrepentant, *shirk* is the one thing not covered.

He states it directly: "**Allah forgives the servant any sin he repents of — so
whoever repents of *shirk*, Allah forgives him**" [4]. And the two verses divide
the ground between them: "in the verse of repentance He generalised and left it
unrestricted; and in that verse He specified and made it conditional — so He
singled out *shirk* as what He does not forgive, and made what is other than it
conditional on His will" [5].

**Who 39:53 was revealed about.** This matters, because the verse is often quoted
as though addressed to Muslims with ordinary sins. Al-Tabari records the
occasion, from Ibn 'Abbas: "the people of Mecca said: Muhammad claims that whoever
worshipped idols, and called on another god beside Allah, and killed the soul
Allah made inviolable, will not be forgiven — so how can we emigrate and accept
Islam, when we have worshipped the gods and killed the soul Allah made
inviolable, and we are people of *shirk*?" And Allah revealed: "**do not despair
of My mercy — indeed Allah forgives all sins**" [6].

Al-Nasa'i has the same from Ibn 'Abbas: "some people from the idolaters had killed
and killed much, then came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and said: what you say and
call to is good — if only you could tell us that there is an expiation for what
we have done. So there was revealed *and those who do not call upon another god
with Allah, nor kill...*; and there was revealed *O My slaves who have been
prodigal against themselves*" [7].

The verse was sent down for murderers and idolaters, asked in exactly your tone of
voice, and its answer to them was *do not despair*. Whatever is on your account,
it was addressed over your head to people carrying more.

**And the warning is pointed the other way from where people expect.** Not at the
sinner, but at whoever writes the sinner off. Muslim: the Prophet ﷺ related "that a
man said: by Allah, Allah will not forgive so-and-so. And Allah, Exalted, said:
**who is this who swears by Me that I will not forgive so-and-so? I have forgiven
so-and-so, and I have nullified your deeds**" [8].

Abu Hurayra taught it as a rule of speech: "**do not ever say to a man: by Allah,
Allah will not forgive you** — or, will never admit you to Paradise." He was told
it was just something people say to a friend in anger, and answered: "do not say
it" [9]. The story behind it is of two men of the Children of Israel, one devout
and one a persistent sinner, and the devout one finally swore that oath. When
both died: "He said to the sinner: go, enter Paradise by My mercy. And He said to
the other: were you knowing about Me? Were you able to do what is in My hand?
Take him to the Fire" [10].

**So, to answer plainly.** There is no sin in your past that has closed the door,
provided you are not now holding to *shirk* — and if you were, leaving it removes
even that. What the sources treat as genuinely dangerous is not the size of the
sin but the despair itself, and the presumption of announcing on Allah's behalf
who is beyond forgiving. Both of those are decisions about Allah rather than
about the sin, which is why they are the ones the texts single out.

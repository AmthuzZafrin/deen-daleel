# Can I invest in the stock market?

> **This question is marked `expect: decline`.** The answer must explain
> why it needs a person — a mufti, a doctor, a lawyer — and must not give
> a ruling. Cite only to show what the question touches on.

## Answer

**Owning a share is owning an undivided fraction of a real business, and both halves of that — the fractional ownership and the profit-sharing partnership — are among the contracts these books permit most readily. The difficulty is not the share; it is what the company does and how it is financed. On that, the corpus supplies a working principle that is recognisably the basis of modern screening.**

**Fractional ownership is settled.** Ibn Taymiyya: `يجوز بيع المشاع باتفاق المسلمين كما مضت بذلك سنة رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم` [1] — the sale of an undivided share is permitted **by the agreement of the Muslims**, as the Sunna established. Owning a thousandth of a company without owning any identifiable part of its warehouse is not a modern novelty; it is the ordinary law of co-ownership.

**Partnership for profit is a named contract with worked-out rules.** Al-Sarakhsi on `شركة العنان`: `فهو ان يشترك الرجلان برأس مال يحضره كل واحد منهما ولا بد من ذلك اما عند العقد أو عند الشراء حتى أن الشركة لا تجوز برأس مال غائب أو دين` [2] — two men contribute capital each of them brings, at contract or at purchase; a partnership on absent capital or on a debt is not valid. And what the contract actually does: `وعندنا موجب شركة العقد الوكالة على معنى ان كل واحد منهما يكون وكيل صاحبه في الشراء بالمال الذي عينه` [3] — each becomes his partner's agent for buying with the specified capital.

The rule that makes equity different in kind from lending is in the `مضاربة` chapter: `وإنما كانت الوضيعة على رب المال لأنه لا ضمان على المبضع والمضارب في البضاعة والمضاربة` [4] — **the loss falls on the owner of the capital**, because the working partner does not guarantee it.

That is exactly what a shareholder does. You put money in, you take an agreed share of profit if there is profit, and if the business fails you lose your capital. Nobody owes you a fixed return. That is the structure the tradition prefers, and it is the opposite of the arrangement the prohibition of usury is aimed at.

**So the real question is the company, and here the corpus gives a principle rather than a percentage.**

Ibn Taymiyya, asked about dealing with people whose wealth mixes lawful and unlawful: `إذا كان في أموالهم حلال وحرام ففي معاملتهم شبهة لا يحكم بالتحريم إلا إذا عرف أنه يعطيه ما يحرم اعطاؤه` [5] — there is a **doubt** in dealing with them; it is not ruled forbidden unless it is known that what he is giving is what may not be given.

Then the operative rule: `فان كان الحلال هو الأغلب لم يحكم بتحريم المعاملة وإن كان الحرام هو الأغلب قيل بحل المعاملة وقيل بل هي محرمة` [6] — if the lawful **predominates**, the dealing is not ruled forbidden; if the unlawful predominates, some permitted the dealing and some forbade it.

And then the sentence that matters most for a modern portfolio: `وإذا كان في ماله حلال وحرام واختلط لم يحرم الحلال بل له أن يأخذ قدر الحلال` [7] — where lawful and unlawful are mixed in a man's wealth, **the mixing does not make the lawful forbidden**; he may take the measure of the lawful. He states the remedy plainly: `وكذلك من اختلط بماله الحلال والحرام أخرج قدر الحرام والباقي حلال له` [8] — **he takes out the measure of the unlawful, and the rest is lawful to him.**

That is purification, described in a fatwa written centuries before anyone computed a screening ratio. The modern practice of estimating the impermissible portion of a company's income and giving that share away is an application of it, not an invention.

Al-Sarakhsi gives the same principle from the other end, and it cuts both ways: `لان الحلال هو الغالب والحكم للغالب` [9] — the lawful predominates and **the ruling follows the predominant**. But equally: `أن الحكم للغالب وإذا كان الغالب هو الحرام كان الكل حراما في وجوب الاجتناب عنها في حالة الاختيار` [10] — where the unlawful predominates, the whole is treated as forbidden as far as the duty of avoidance goes, **where one has a choice**.

Note that last clause. Where alternatives exist, the standard tightens. In a market with thousands of listed companies, you have a choice.

**Where this leaves the actual categories.**

- **A company whose business is itself forbidden** — a brewery, a casino, a conventional bank or insurer, a pornography business. The predominant is the forbidden, and you have alternatives. Nothing above gives room here.
- **A company doing lawful business that happens to hold interest-bearing deposits or debt** — which is nearly every listed company. This is precisely the mixed case, and Ibn Taymiyya's answer applies: not ruled forbidden where the lawful predominates, with the tainted measure taken out.
- **A company where the forbidden portion is substantial but not the main business.** This is the genuinely contested middle. Some jurists in the passage above permitted dealing even where the unlawful predominated; others forbade it. That disagreement is the ancestor of the differing thresholds you will see quoted today.

There is also the prudential counsel, which is not a ruling but is in the books: `اجعلوا بينكم وبين الحرام سترة من الحلال من فعل ذلك استبرأ لعرضه ودينه` [11] — put a screen of the clearly lawful between yourselves and the forbidden; whoever does so has protected his honour and his religion.

**What the corpus does not supply.**

It gives no numbers. The specific ratios used by modern screening bodies — caps on interest income, on debt relative to market value, on liquid assets — are contemporary judgements about how to apply `الغالب` to audited accounts. They are reasonable applications, but do not let anyone present a particular threshold as though it came from these books, because it did not.

Nor does anything here address a shareholder's lack of control over the company, index funds that buy an entire market indiscriminately, the difference between buying newly issued shares and buying from another investor, or derivatives — which are a different question and raise the objections set out on the pages about uncertainty and gambling.

**What to do.**

Own shares in businesses doing lawful things, and treat that as the main filter rather than an afterthought. Where a company is lawful in its business but has some interest income, follow the rule above: estimate the tainted portion and give it away — not as charity you take reward for, but as disposal, which is the subject of its own page here. Use a recognised screening service or a scholar to set the threshold, and understand that you are adopting someone's applied judgement rather than a text.

And if you are being sold a product that promises a fixed return regardless of the venture's performance, that is not equity, whatever it is labelled. The test in these books is simple and worth carrying: does the loss fall on the capital, or has someone guaranteed you a gain?

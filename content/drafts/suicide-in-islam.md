# What does Islam say about suicide?

> Reviewer note: **Lead with help, not with a ruling.** Someone reaching this may be in danger now. Direct them to immediate support first, in plain words. The ruling may be mentioned but must never be the opening, and must never be framed in a way that adds shame to someone already suffering.


## Answer

**If you are thinking about ending your life, please reach out to someone right
now — before reading anything else on this page.**

- Call your local emergency number, or go to your nearest emergency department.
- Contact a crisis line in your country. Most are free, open all night, and
  answered by someone who will not judge you. Searching "suicide helpline" with
  your country's name will find one in seconds.
- If you can, tell one person you trust today. Not the whole story. Just that
  you are struggling and you do not want to be alone with it.

You are not required to have the right words, or to be composed, or to have
tried hard enough first. You just have to tell someone.

---

**On the question itself.** Yes, Islam prohibits taking one's own life. But if
you came here carrying that as one more accusation against yourself, please
read what follows carefully, because the same sources are far gentler with you
than you may be being with yourself.

**You are not held to more than you can bear.** God does not burden a soul
beyond its capacity. [1] That is not a slogan. It means that whatever you are
carrying, the One who allowed it does not consider you obliged to carry it
perfectly, or silently, or alone.

**Despair itself is what the Qur'an argues against, repeatedly.** To those who
have wronged themselves — the verse names them that way, and still calls them
*My slaves* — it says: do not despair of the mercy of God, who forgives all
sins. [2] Elsewhere, despairing of God's mercy is described as the condition of
someone who has lost their way, not of someone beyond saving. [3] The Qur'an
treats hopelessness as the error, not as the verdict.

**And the promise attached to hardship is stated twice, in consecutive verses:**
with hardship comes ease. [4] Not after you have earned it. With it.

**What this means practically.** Feeling this way is not a sign that your faith
has failed. Pain of this kind is an illness and an affliction, not a
disqualification, and seeking treatment for it is no different from seeking
treatment for anything else that is hurting you. Talking to a doctor is not a
religious failure. Neither is medication, and neither is therapy.

If someone has told you otherwise, they were wrong, and they were speaking
outside their competence.

**Please tell someone today.** A doctor, a crisis line, an imam you trust, a
friend, a family member. Whatever you believe about yourself right now, you are
worth the phone call. Make it before you decide anything else.

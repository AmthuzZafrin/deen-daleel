# Can I eat food cooked by non-Muslims?

## Answer

**Short answer: yes. Food is not made unlawful by who cooked it — the exception
is meat that required slaughtering, not food in general — and there is an
explicit instruction about using their cooking pots: wash them and cook in
them.** The question of the pan is answered more directly in the sources than
almost anything else in this area.

**The starting point.** **"This day are (all) good things made lawful for you.
The food of those who have received the Scripture is lawful for you, and your
food is lawful for them."** [1] And the list of what is actually forbidden does
not include a category for the cook: **"Say: I find not in that which is revealed
unto me aught prohibited to an eater that he eat thereof, except it be carrion,
or blood poured forth, or swineflesh - for that verily is foul - or the
abomination which was immolated to the name of other than Allah."** [2]

Ibn Qudama states the ruling and the vessels together: **"فأهل الكتاب يباح أكل
طعامهم وشرابهم، والأكل في آنيتهم، ما لم يتحقق نجاستها. قال ابن عقيل: لا تختلف
الرواية في أنه لا يحرم استعمال أوانيهم"** — *as for the People of the Scripture,
eating their food and drink is permitted, and eating from their vessels, so long
as their impurity is not established. Ibn 'Aqil said: the narration does not
differ that using their vessels is not forbidden* [3].

**And it is not limited to Jews and Christians.** Al-Sarakhsi: **"ولا بأس بطعام
المجوس وأهل الشرك ما خلا الذبائح"** — *there is no harm in the food of Magians
and idolaters, except what they slaughter* [4]. Al-Qurtubi puts the same boundary
precisely: **"طعام من لا كتاب له كالمشركين وعبدة الأوثان ما لم يكن من ذبائحهم
ولم يحتج إلى ذكاة إلا الجبن لما فيه من إنفحة الميتة"** — *the food of one who has
no scripture, such as idolaters and image-worshippers [is permitted], as long as
it is not from what they slaughtered and does not require slaughtering — except
cheese, because of the rennet of carrion in it* [5].

Read the exception carefully. **The line is drawn around slaughtered meat**, not
around the cook. Bread, rice, vegetables, sweets, a stew with no meat in it —
nothing in the sources puts those in question because of who made them.

**Now the pans.** Al-Tirmidhi records: **"سئل رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم عن
قدور المجوس فقال: أنقوها غسلا، واطبخوا فيها"** — *the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was
asked about the pots of the Magians. He said: clean them by washing, and cook in
them* [6]. Ibn Qudama cites a second wording of the same questioner's report:
**"إن وجدتم غيرها فلا تأكلوا فيها، وإن لم تجدوا غيرها فاغسلوها وكلوا فيها"** —
*if you find others, do not eat from them; and if you do not find others, wash
them and eat from them* [7].

Between those two: **using another pan is preferable; washing the one you have is
sufficient.** For a shared kitchen where a flatmate cooks pork, that is a
complete answer.

**A point Ibn al-'Arabi makes that is worth pausing on.** He notes that the
permission was not granted in ignorance of the situation: **"وكان حالهم يقتضي
ألا يؤكل طعامهم لقلة احتراسهم عن النجاسات لكن الشرع سمح في ذلك"** — *their
condition would have implied that their food should not be eaten, because of how
little they guard against impurities — but the law was lenient in that* [8].

The leniency is deliberate and informed. He grades the washing: **"وغسل آنية
المجوس فرض وغسل آنية أهل الكتاب فضل وندب"** — *washing the vessels of the Magians
is obligatory, and washing the vessels of the People of the Scripture is a merit
and recommended* [9]. And he refuses to let the permission be narrowed into a
grudging exception: **"رخصة أكل طعامهم حل تأصل في الشريعة واستقر"** — *the
dispensation of eating their food is a permission rooted in the law and settled
in it* [10] — not a concession that expires when circumstances ease.

**How it was practised.** Ibn Taymiyya cites the Prophet ﷺ accepting an
invitation: **"فان رسول الله اجاب دعوة يهودى إلى خبز شعير واهالة سنخة"** — *the
Messenger of Allah ﷺ accepted the invitation of a Jew to barley bread and rancid
fat* [11] — and reasons from the fat, which comes from the vessels they
habitually cook in. And on the scale of it: **"فقد استفاض ان اصحاب رسول الله لما
فتحوا الشام والعراق ومصر كانوا يأكلون من ذبائح أهل الكتاب اليهود والنصارى وانما
امتنعوا من ذبائح المجوس"** — *it is widely transmitted that the Companions of the
Messenger of Allah, when they opened Syria, Iraq and Egypt, used to eat from the
slaughtered meat of the People of the Scripture, the Jews and the Christians, and
only abstained from the slaughter of the Magians* [12].

**The scrupulous counter-position, stated fairly.** Al-Qurtubi raises a physical
worry about porous cookware: **"وربما سرت النجاسات في أجزاء قدور الفخار فإذا طبخ
فيها بعد ذلك توقع مخالطة تلك الأجزاء النجسة للمطبوخ في القدر ثانية فاقتضى الورع
الكف عنها"** — *and perhaps impurities penetrate into the substance of earthenware
pots, so that when one cooks in them afterwards, mixing of those impure particles
with what is cooked a second time is to be expected; so scrupulousness required
refraining from them* [13]. Note the word he uses: *wara'*, scrupulousness — a
personal caution, not the ruling. And he immediately limits it: **"فأما ما
يستعملونه لغير الطبخ فلا بأس باستعماله من غير غسل"** — *as for what they use for
other than cooking, there is no harm in using it without washing* [14]. Plates,
cups, cutlery.

**One thing that is genuinely disputed: cheese.** The rennet question splits the
schools. Ibn Taymiyya: **"فأبو حنيفة يقول بطهارتها ومالك والشافعى يقولان بنجاستها
وعن أحمد روايتان"** — *Abu Hanifa holds it pure, Malik and al-Shafi'i hold it
impure, and from Ahmad there are two narrations* [15]. If you have been told
there is one answer on cheese, there is not.

**So, practically.**

- **Food cooked by a non-Muslim is not forbidden for that reason.** The rule is
  about ingredients, not hands.
- **The exception is meat that needed slaughtering** — which is a separate
  question with its own answers on this site.
- **A pan that has cooked pork: wash it and use it.** That is the explicit
  instruction, not a lenient reading of one.
- **If a clean pan is available, prefer it.** One wording expresses that
  preference; neither makes the washed pan unlawful.
- **Plates, glasses and cutlery need no special treatment**, on al-Qurtubi's own
  distinction.
- **Cheese and rennet-derived products are a real disagreement**, not a settled
  prohibition or a settled permission.

**Where this stops.** These passages do not settle whether a specific piece of
meat was validly slaughtered, and they do not address modern processing where the
ingredient list is unknown. Those turn on facts rather than on this ruling.

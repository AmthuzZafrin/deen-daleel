# What is the prostration of forgetfulness and when do I do it?

## Answer

**Two extra prostrations that repair a prayer you got wrong by mistake.** They are
the reason a forgetful slip does not cost you the prayer.

**When you do them**

- You **added** something — an extra rak'ah, an extra bowing or prostration.
- You **left out** something obligatory but not a pillar — the first sitting, the
  tashahhud, saying the qunut.
- You **doubted** how many rak'ahs you had prayed. There is a separate answer here
  on that case specifically.

**You do not do them** for missing a *sunna*, or for a wandering mind, or for
mispronouncing something. Nor do they repair a missed pillar — if you left out a
bowing or a prostration entirely, you must supply it, not prostrate for it.

**How you do them**

Two prostrations, exactly like the ones in the prayer — say *Allahu akbar*, go
down, say *subhana rabbiya'l-a'la*, sit up, prostrate again — then the tashahhud
and the salam.

**Before or after the salam? The schools genuinely differ, and all of them work.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hanafi** | after the salam, always |
| **Shafi'i, Maliki** | before the salam (Maliki: after, for an addition) |
| **Hanbali** | before for an omission, after for an addition |

Ibn Qudama reports the Hanafi position and its authority: **the People of Opinion
said the prostration of forgetfulness is all after the salam, and he may perform
them before it — the like of that is narrated from Ali, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, Ibn
Mas'ud, Ammar, Ibn Abbas, Ibn al-Zubayr, Anas, al-Hasan, al-Nakha'i and Ibn Abi
Layla** [1] — citing **for every forgetfulness, two prostrations after the
salam, without distinguishing between addition and omission** [2].

And his own school's reason for splitting the two: **it is established from the
Prophet that he prostrated before the salam and after it, in authentic hadith
agreed upon — so what we have said acts on all the hadith and reconciles them,
without discarding any of them.** [3]

Al-Kasani gives the elegant argument for *before*: **the prostration is only
brought in order to repair a deficiency lodged inside the prayer** [4] — and a
repair belongs inside the thing it is repairing, not after it has ended.

**Which should you do?** Follow your school if you have one. If you do not, do
them **before the salam** — it is the majority practice and it also covers you if
you later follow a school that requires it, since a prostration before the salam
is inside the prayer either way.

**Two things worth knowing**

*If you realise after the salam*, do them then — that is precisely the case the
"after" position is built around, and no school makes you repeat the prayer.

*If you are following an imam*, you do not make your own. You follow his — even if
you were the one who lost count.

**And the point of the whole institution.** Al-Kasani's phrase for it is the best
short description: the prostration exists **to repair a deficiency lodged inside
the prayer** [4]. An ordinary human mistake in prayer has a fixed, cheap remedy,
and the remedy is itself worship. The prayer stands either way — which is the
whole reason this chapter exists, and a good reason not to repeat prayers over
slips.

# What does it mean to trust in God?

## Answer

*Tawakkul* is reliance on Allah — and the hadith most often quoted for it is
usually quoted with the second half left off, which reverses its meaning.

**The hadith, in full.** 'Umar b. al-Khattab: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said,
"if you were to rely on Allah with the reliance He deserves, He would provide
for you as He provides for the birds — **they go out hungry in the morning and
return full in the evening**" [1]. Al-Tirmidhi: "this is a *hasan sahih* hadith;
we do not know it except from this route" [2].

The birds are not fed in the nest. They go out. The hadith is an argument *for*
effort, framed as an argument for reliance — the point being that the effort is
not what feeds them.

**The Qur'an makes the same sequence explicit.** "And consult with them upon the
conduct of affairs. And when thou art resolved, then put thy trust in Allah. Lo!
Allah loveth those who put their trust (in Him)" (3:159) [3]. Consult, resolve,
*then* rely. Reliance comes after the deciding, not instead of it. And as a
standing instruction: "And put thy trust in Allah, for Allah is sufficient as
Trustee" (33:3) [4]; "Allah! There is no god save Him. In Allah, therefore, let
believers put their trust" (64:13) [5].

**Where it actually lives.** Ibn Hajar describes the working state: the believer
acts "in seeking provision, of what he cannot do without in food and drink, and
in guarding against an enemy by preparing weapons and locking the door and the
like — and along with that his heart does not rest upon the means; rather he
believes that they do not of themselves bring benefit nor repel harm, but that
the cause and the effect are the doing of Allah, and all of it is by His will"
[6].

Al-Qushayri states it as a rule: "*tawakkul*'s place is the heart, and outward
movement does not contradict it — once the servant has realised that all of it
is from Allah: so if something is made easy, it is by His easing, and if it is
made hard, it is by His decree" [7].

And the sources are explicit that earning is legislated, not merely tolerated:
"among the proofs for the legitimacy of earning is what preceded in the Book of
Sales, from the hadith of Abu Hurayra: *the best thing a man eats is from his own
earning* — and Dawud used to eat from his own earning" [8]. The prophet who was
a king made armour for a living.

**The strongest statement against the misreading.** Al-Tabari, reporting the
extreme position and then rejecting it: "it is said that none deserves the name
of reliance except one whose heart is not mixed with fear of anything at all —
not even the ravening beast or the attacking enemy — nor one who exerts himself
in seeking provision or in treating a pain" [9]. His answer: "**the truth is
that whoever trusts in Allah and is certain that His decree upon him will come to
pass, his taking up of the means does not impair his reliance** — following his
own practice and the practice of His Messenger" [10].

Seeking work, taking medicine, locking your door, buying insurance against a
risk you can foresee: none of these is a failure of trust. The Prophet ﷺ did all
the equivalent things.

**One honest qualification.** There is a recognised higher station, and the
sources do not pretend otherwise: "the station of contentment and submission is
higher than taking up the means — al-Khattabi and those who followed him
inclined to this" [11]. That is a real position. But note what it is: a
description of the spiritually advanced, offered alongside al-Tabari's ruling
that the ordinary believer who works and takes precautions is fully within
*tawakkul*. It is not a standard you are failing.

**So, practically.** Do the work. Make the plan, take the medicine, apply for
the job, prepare properly. Then hand the outcome over, and mean it — because the
outcome was never in your hands, and the effort was never what produced it. That
is the whole doctrine, and the bird that leaves the nest each morning is its
image.

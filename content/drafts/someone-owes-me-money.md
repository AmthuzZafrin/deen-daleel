# Someone owes me money and won't pay — what can I do?

## Answer

**You are in the right, and the tradition says so in strong terms — but what you may do next depends entirely on one fact: whether they can pay and won't, or genuinely cannot. Those are two different cases with opposite answers, and most disputes go wrong because the creditor has not established which one he is in.**

**If they can pay and are stalling, that is named as oppression.** `مطل الغنى ظلم واذا اتبع احدكم على مليء فليتبع` [1] — **the delay of one who has the means is oppression**; and if one of you is referred to a solvent payer, let him follow it.

Ibn Taymiyya draws out how strong that is: `فجعل مجرد المطل الذى هو تأخير الاداء مع القدرة ظلما فكيف بالترك رأسا` [2] — **mere delay**, which is postponing payment while able, is called oppression; **what then of abandoning payment altogether?**

He places it in his general classification of wrongdoing: `والظلم نوعان تفريط في الحق وتعد للحد فالأول ترك ما يجب للغير مثل ترك قضاء الديون وسائر الأمانات` [3] — wrongdoing is of two kinds, falling short in a duty and transgressing a limit; the first is failing in what is owed to another, **such as failing to repay debts** and other trusts.

So this is not a matter of personal disappointment. In these books an unpaid debt someone can afford is `ظلم` — the same word used for the graver wrongs — and you are entitled to say so.

**The second clause of that hadith is practical advice you may be able to use.** `واذا اتبع احدكم على مليء فليتبع` — if your debtor transfers the debt to someone solvent who owes *him*, accept it and pursue that person. And the transfer is final: al-Shafi'i, following Malik, holds that `ان الرجل إذا أحال الرجل على الرجل بحق له، ثم أفلس المحال عليه أو مات لم يرجع المحال على المحيل أبدا` [4] — once transferred, if the new debtor then becomes insolvent or dies, **the creditor never reverts to the original debtor.**

Know that before agreeing. A transfer settles your claim against the original party permanently, so it is worth taking only against someone genuinely able to pay.

**If they genuinely cannot pay, the picture changes completely.**

There is a formal remedy, and it is a court's to apply, not yours. Ibn Hajar: `وذهب الجمهور إلى أن من ظهر فلسه فعلى الحاكم الحجر عليه في ماله حتى يبيعه عليه ويقسمه بين غرمائه على نسبة ديونهم` [5] — the majority hold that where a person's insolvency is established, **the judge restrains him in his property, sells it, and divides it among his creditors in proportion to their debts.**

And then the limit, stated to creditors directly: `خذوا ما وجدتم وليس لكم إلا ذلك` [6] — **take what you find, and you have nothing beyond that.**

That is the end of the matter. A creditor's right runs to the debtor's assets, not to the debtor. There is no licence to pursue someone who has nothing, and no entitlement to more than a proportional share alongside everyone else owed money.

**What this means you should actually do.**

1. **Establish which case you are in before doing anything else.** Ask directly and specifically: not "when will you pay" but "what is your situation." A person avoiding you may be ashamed rather than dishonest, and the ruling turns on the answer.
2. **If they can pay, ask plainly and in writing.** You are entitled to. Say what is owed and when it was due. Nothing in the material above asks you to absorb the loss quietly to keep the peace — the delay is the wrong, not your asking about it.
3. **If they cannot pay, give time.** That is the corresponding duty on you, and it is the express command of scripture regarding one in difficulty. Pressing someone with nothing is not enforcing your right; it is going past it.
4. **Consider taking part payment or a schedule.** Half recovered by agreement beats a whole claim that destroys a relationship and yields nothing.
5. **Use the courts rather than self-help.** The insolvency procedure above is a judge's function precisely because a creditor is a poor judge of his own case. Taking property yourself, or applying pressure through others, is not what the texts authorise — they authorise a claim, adjudicated.
6. **Write the next one down.** Most of these situations trace back to a loan with no written record and no agreed term, which is the specific thing the longest verse in the Qur'an tells you to avoid.

If the amount is large or the person is a relative, get a third party both of you trust involved early. And if it becomes clear you will not recover it, forgiving the debt of someone genuinely unable to pay is treated in this tradition as a considerable act — not a defeat.

# When exactly can I break my fast?

## Answer

**The moment the sun sets — which is your timetable's Maghrib time. Do not add a
safety margin; hurrying is the sunna, and delaying it is the thing the hadith warns
against.**

**Hasten it.**

Sahl ibn Sa'd reports: **my community will not cease to be in good so long as they
hasten the breaking of the fast** [1]. Al-Nawawi cites the same in al-Majmu' —
**people will not cease to be in good** [2] so long as they hasten it — and Abu
Dharr's wording, which pairs the two ends of the day: **my community will not cease
to be in good so long as they hasten the breaking of the fast and delay the pre-dawn
meal** [3].

Ibn Qudama has the strongest form, a hadith qudsi: **God says: the most beloved of
My servants to Me are the quickest of them to break the fast** [4].

Al-Nawawi and Ibn Qudama both report the same episode from Aisha. Two Companions
were described to her, one who hurried both the iftar and the Maghrib prayer and one
who delayed both; she asked which was which, was told the first was Ibn Mas'ud, and
answered that this was how the Messenger of God did it.

**So the practice is: break at Maghrib, not five or ten minutes after.** A margin
added "to be safe" is moving away from the sunna, not toward it. If your timetable
is reliable, it is the timetable.

**What to break it with.**

Ibn Qudama: **it is recommended that he break the fast on fresh dates; if there are
none, then dried dates; if there are none, then water** [5] — from Anas's report of
the Prophet's own practice, which he graded via al-Tirmidhi as hasan gharib.

**Break first, pray after.** Anas again, in Ibn Qudama: **I never saw the Messenger
of God pray until he had broken his fast, even if only with a drink of water** [6].

So the order is: sunset → dates and water → Maghrib → the meal. Not: Maghrib →
meal. This is worth knowing because many people, arriving at a mosque, wait for the
prayer before taking anything.

**Practically.**

- **Maghrib in your prayer app or local mosque timetable is the time.** Sunset, not
  civil dusk.
- **A date and water is enough to have broken it.** The meal can follow the prayer.
- **If you are travelling across time zones**, you follow the sunset where you
  actually are — see the answer on very long days for the harder version of this.
- **If you break slightly early by mistake**, believing the sun had set when it had
  not, that day is made up. If you break slightly late, nothing is owed; you have
  just missed a sunna.

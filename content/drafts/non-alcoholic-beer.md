# Can I drink non-alcoholic beer?

## Answer

**Short answer: on the substance question the sources are unusually direct,
because the classical jurists had the same drink — a fermented barley or raisin
brew called *fuqqa'* — and ruled on it by name: if it does not intoxicate, there
is no harm in it.** The remaining considerations are about you and your company,
not about the liquid.

**The classical case.** Ibn Qudama, in the chapter on drinks: **"ولا بأس بالفقاع
وبه قال إسحاق، وابن المنذر"** — *there is no harm in fuqqa'; Ishaq and Ibn
al-Mundhir said so too* [1]. Ibn Hajar reports Malik being asked about it
directly and answering: **"فقال إذا لم يسكر فلا بأس به"** — *he said: if it does
not intoxicate, there is no harm in it* [2] — and gives the general rule the case
falls under: **"وحكمه حكم سائر الأنبذة ما دام طريا يجوز شربه مالم يشتد"** — *its
ruling is the ruling of the other steeped drinks: as long as it is fresh, drinking
it is permitted so long as it has not turned strong* [3].

**The underlying principle, stated plainly.** Ibn Qudama again: **"وما طبخ من
العصير والنبيذ قبل غليانه، حتى صار غير مسكر، كالدبس، ورب الخرنوب، وغيرهما من
المربيات والسكر، فهو مباح ; لأن التحريم إنما ثبت في المسكر، ففيما عداه يبقى على
أصل الإباحة"** — *what is cooked of juice and nabidh before it ferments, so that
it becomes non-intoxicating — like date syrup, carob molasses, and other preserves
and sugars — is permitted; because the prohibition was established only regarding
the intoxicant, so what lies outside it remains on the default of permission* [4].

**The prohibition attaches to the intoxicating property, and everything else
returns to the default.** That is the whole answer to the substance question.

He also defines the category: **"والنبيذ: ما يلقى فيه تمر أو زبيب أو نحوهما ;
ليحلو به الماء، وتذهب ملوحته، فلا بأس به ما لم يغل، أو تأتي عليه ثلاثة أيام"** —
*nabidh is what has dates, raisins or the like put in it to sweeten the water and
remove its brackishness; there is no harm in it so long as it has not fermented,
or three days have not passed over it* [5]. Two markers, both about the state of
the drink.

**And the test is not a laboratory one.** Ibn Hajar: **"لا يتعين الاختبار بالشرب
بل يقع بغير الشرب مثل أن يصير شديد الغليان أو يقذف بالزبد"** — *the test is not
confined to drinking; it may be made without drinking, such as its becoming
strongly fermented or throwing up froth* [6]. The jurists were asking whether the
drink had *turned*, by observable signs — not whether any ethanol molecule was
present, which was not a question available to them and is not the question the
ruling was built on.

**Ahmad's exchange is worth having.** Asked about reduced grape syrup, and told
that people said it intoxicates: **"قيل لأحمد: إنهم يقولون: إنه يسكر. قال: لا
يسكر، ولو كان يسكر ما أحله عمر"** — *it was said to Ahmad: they say it
intoxicates. He said: it does not intoxicate; and had it intoxicated, 'Umar would
not have permitted it* [7]. The claim was tested against the effect, not accepted
because it sounded cautious.

**Two things that are not the substance question.**

*The dregs.* Al-Fatawa al-Hindiyya: **"ويكره شرب دردي الخمر والانتفاع به ولو شرب
منه ولم يسكر فلا حد عليه عندنا"** — *drinking the lees of wine and making use of
them is disliked; and if one drinks of it and does not become intoxicated, there
is no prescribed penalty upon him in our view* [8]. Note the two-level answer:
disliked, but not a punishable offence. The jurists were willing to hold both at
once, and so should you.

*The company.* The report most often quoted about this is: **"نهى رسول الله -صلى
الله عليه وسلم- عن مطعمين: عن الجلوس على مائدة يشرب عليها الخمر"** — *the
Messenger of Allah ﷺ forbade two kinds of eating: sitting at a table on which
wine is drunk...* [9]. But Abu Dawud, who records it, immediately grades it:
**"هذا الحديث لم يسمعه جعفر من الزهري، وهو منكر"** — *Ja'far did not hear this
hadith from al-Zuhri, and it is munkar* [10]. That does not settle the question of
sitting with people who are drinking — other evidence bears on it — but it does
mean that this particular text should not be used as though it were decisive.

**Practically.**

- **A drink that cannot intoxicate anyone is not khamr**, on Ibn Qudama's
  principle and Malik's direct answer.
- **The relevant question for a "0.5%" product is whether a large quantity of it
  would intoxicate**, since the rule is that if much intoxicates, little is
  forbidden. For products where drinking a great deal of it would do nothing, that
  rule is not engaged.
- **Resemblance is a separate concern**, and a real one to some scholars, but it
  is a matter of *makruh* reasoning rather than the prohibition of khamr — the
  dregs ruling shows the jurists using exactly that register.
- **If you are recovering from drinking, this is not a fiqh question.** Whether a
  familiar taste and a familiar bottle are good for you is something you know
  better than any ruling does, and that consideration outranks the permissibility.
- **You are not obliged to drink it**, and nobody needs a reason not to.

**Where this stops.** Whether any particular product actually contains a
negligible or a significant amount of alcohol is a fact about that product, not
something this site can rule on. And the broader question of being at a table
where alcohol is served is not settled by the passages above.

# Can I eat at a normal restaurant?

## Answer

**Short answer: yes, with the meat as the only real question — and the sources
are far less demanding about uncertainty than the anxiety around this suggests.**
Ordering a vegetarian or fish dish at a normal restaurant needs no ruling at all.
The interesting material is about what happens when you cannot verify something,
and it consistently points away from investigation.

**The baseline.** The forbidden list is short and specific: **"Forbidden unto you
(for food) are carrion and blood and swineflesh, and that which hath been
dedicated unto any other than Allah"** [1]. And the general instruction is
positive: **"So eat of the lawful and good food which Allah hath provided for
you, and thank the bounty of your Lord if it is Him ye serve."** [2]

**What the sources do with uncertainty.** Al-Nasa'i records people asking the
Prophet ﷺ about exactly this shape of problem: **"إن ناسا من الأعراب يأتونا بلحم
ولا ندري أذكروا اسم الله عليه أم لا فقال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم اذكروا اسم
الله عز وجل عليه وكلوا"** — *some bedouins bring us meat and we do not know
whether they mentioned the name of Allah over it or not. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ
said: mention the name of Allah over it, and eat* [3].

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr draws out the principle: **"أن ما ذبحه المسلم ولم يعرف هل سمى
الله عليه أم لا أنه لا بأس بأكله وهو محمول على أنه قد سمى والمؤمن لا يظن به إلا
الخير وذبيحته وصيده أبدا محمول على السلامة حتى يصح فيه غير ذلك"** — *what a
Muslim slaughtered, where it is not known whether he said the name of Allah over
it, there is no harm in eating it; it is presumed that he did. Nothing but good
is assumed of a believer, and his slaughtering and hunting are always presumed
sound until something else is established* [4].

**"But they do not even follow their own rules."** This objection has an answer
that is older than the objection. Al-Nawawi, on the fat taken at Khaybar: **"وفي
هذا الحديث دليل لجواز أكل شحوم ذبائح اليهود وإن كانت شحومها محرمة عليهم وهو مذهب
مالك وأبي حنيفة والشافعي وجماهير العلماء"** — *in this hadith is evidence for the
permissibility of eating the fat of the slaughter of the Jews, even though their
fat is forbidden **to them** — and that is the school of Malik, Abu Hanifa,
al-Shafi'i and the great majority of the scholars* [5]. His reasoning from the
verse: **"قال المفسرون المراد به الذبائح ولم يستثن منها شيئا لا لحما ولا شحما ولا
غيره"** — *the exegetes said what is meant by it is their slaughtered meat, and
nothing was excepted from it — not meat, not fat, not anything else* [6].

So even a case where the People of the Scripture are eating something *their own*
law forbids does not remove the permission. That is a good deal more generous
than most restaurant advice you will encounter.

**Shared fryers, shared grills, the same pan.** Al-Jassas records a case put to
Abu Hanifa — a bird falls into a cooking pot on the fire and dies in it — and
lists the positions of five scholars on it. Ibn 'Abbas's answer, which Abu Hanifa
endorsed with a refinement: **"فذكروا له عن ابن عباس أن اللحم يؤكل بعد ما يغسل
ويهراق المرق"** — *they mentioned to him from Ibn 'Abbas that the meat is eaten
after being washed, and the broth is poured away* [7]. Al-Awza'i and al-Layth
also permitted it with washing: **"وقال الأوزاعي يغسل اللحم ويؤكل وقال الليث بن
سعد لا يؤكل ذلك اللحم حتى يغسل مرارا ويغلي على النار حتى يذهب كل ما كان فيه"** —
*al-Awza'i said the meat is washed and eaten; al-Layth ibn Sa'd said that meat is
not eaten until it is washed repeatedly and boiled on the fire until everything
in it is gone* [8]. Malik went the other way: **"وقال ابن وهب عن مالك في الدجاجة
تقع في قدر اللحم وهي تطبخ فتموت فيها قال لا أرى أن آكل تلك القدر لأن الميتة قد
اختلطت بما كان في القدر"** — *Ibn Wahb reported from Malik, regarding a hen that
falls into a pot of meat being cooked and dies in it: I do not consider that I
should eat that pot, because the carrion has mixed with what was in the pot* [9].

Note the shape of that: **a spectrum of considered positions**, most of them
resolving with washing, none of them treating the situation as beyond
discussion. A shared fryer is a version of this question, not a category the law
never anticipated.

**Practically, at a normal restaurant.**

- **Vegetarian, vegan and fish dishes are not in question.** Nothing in the
  forbidden list reaches them, and no ruling is needed to order one.
- **Pork and alcohol dishes you decline**, and you do not need to explain
  yourself at length to do it.
- **Meat with an unknown slaughter is the real question**, and it is answered
  separately under *Can I eat meat from a regular supermarket?* and *Is meat
  slaughtered by Christians or Jews permissible?* — where you land depends on the
  country and the school you follow.
- **You are not required to interrogate the kitchen.** The hadith above is the
  closest thing in the sources to that instinct, and it says the opposite.
- **Say bismillah.** That is what you were actually told to do.
- **On shared equipment, washing is the majority remedy**, and scholars differed
  about how far it needs to go. Treating it as an absolute barrier goes beyond
  what the sources support.

**Where this stops.** These passages do not settle any specific establishment's
supply chain, and they do not address whether to be at a table where alcohol is
being served — a different question about company rather than food. If a
particular situation is difficult, that is worth asking someone who knows the
place and knows you.

# Is it normal for my faith to go up and down?

## Answer

**Yes — and the classical sources treat it as the normal condition of a
believer, not as a warning sign.** That faith rises and falls is a settled
position among the jurists and hadith scholars, stated in exactly those words.

**The position.** Ibn Hajar transmits al-Shafi'i: **"سمعت الشافعي يقول الايمان
قول وعمل يزيد وينقص"** — *I heard al-Shafi'i say: faith is speech and action; it
increases and decreases* [1] — with the fuller wording **"وزاد يزيد بالطاعة
وينقص بالمعصية"** — *it increases with obedience and decreases with disobedience*
[2]. He adds the logical point: **"فان كل قابل للزيادة قابل للنقصان ضرورة"** —
*whatever admits of increase necessarily admits of decrease* [3].

Ibn Taymiyya reports it as the broad consensus of the jurists — naming Malik,
al-Layth, al-Thawri, al-Awza'i, al-Shafi'i, Ahmad, Ishaq and others —
**"والايمان يزيد بالطاعات وينقص بالمعاصى وأهل الذنوب عندهم مؤمنون غير مستكملى
الايمان من أجل ذنوبهم"** — *faith increases with acts of obedience and decreases
with acts of disobedience, and in their view people who sin are believers whose
faith is not complete on account of their sins* [4]. And: **"وعلى أن الايمان يزيد
وينقص يزيد بالطاعة وينقص بالمعصية وعليه جماعة أهل الآثار والفقهاء من أهل الفتيا
في الأمصار"** — *this is the position of the scholars of hadith and the jurists
who give fatwa across the lands* [5]. Al-Nawawi says the same of the texts:
**"فهي متظاهرة متطابقة على كون الايمان يزيد وينقص وهذا مذهب السلف والمحدثين"** —
*they are mutually reinforcing and agreed that faith increases and decreases;
this is the position of the early generations and the hadith scholars* [6].

**The Qur'anic basis.** 9:124: **"As for those who believe, it hath increased
them in faith and they rejoice"** [7]. If faith can be increased, it has levels.

**The part that should settle the anxiety.** Read [4] again. Someone whose faith
has dipped is described there as a **believer whose faith is not complete** —
not as someone who has lost it. Ibn Taymiyya states the consequence directly:
**"وقال الايمان مراتب بعضها فوق بعض فليس ناقص الايمان ككامل الايمان"** — *faith
has degrees, one above another; one whose faith is deficient is not the same as
one whose faith is complete* [8]. Degrees, not a switch.

Ibn Hajar even locates the mechanism in something obviously variable:
**"الايمان يزيد وينقص لان الحب والبغض يتفاوتان"** — *faith increases and
decreases because love and aversion vary* [9]. Love varies in intensity from
week to week in every human relationship. On this reading, of course faith does
too.

**And it happened to a prophet.** On Ibrahim's request — *but so that my heart
may be at rest* — Ibn Hajar reports Mujahid's gloss: **"وعن مجاهد قال لأزداد
ايمانا إلى ايمانى"** — *so that I may increase in faith upon my faith* [10]. A
prophet asking for more faith than he already had. Whatever a dip means, it does
not mean you are uniquely defective.

**What to do when it is low.** The sources give the mechanism at [2] and [4], so
the remedy is implied by it rather than needing separate evidence: faith
increases with acts of obedience. Practically, that means the movement runs from
action to feeling, not the other way round. Waiting to feel it before you pray is
the wrong order; praying is one of the things that raises it.

Concretely: hold the five prayers even when they feel mechanical — they are the
scaffolding; keep some Qur'an in the week, in a translation you actually follow;
keep company with people whose faith is steady, since the effect is real and
noticed by everyone who has tried it; and make du'a about the dip itself, in your
own words. If the low is not a spiritual dip but exhaustion, isolation or
depression, treat it as that — faith is not the tool for fixing sleep deprivation,
and a doctor is not a religious failure.

**A word if you are new to Islam.** The first months often carry an intensity
that does not last, and its passing is commonly mistaken for something going
wrong. On the material above it is the ordinary shape of a believing life
[4][8][9]. What matters is the direction over years, not the reading on any given
week.

**On the language above:** "iman" is faith; "ta'a" is obedience, "ma'siya"
disobedience; "mustakmil al-iman" means one whose faith is complete — the phrase
at [4] that distinguishes deficient faith from absent faith.

**A note on scope.** Whether faith increases and decreases — and whether actions
are part of faith or only evidence of it — was one of the most contested
questions in early Islamic theology, and the passages quoted here are engaged in
that dispute. This answer gives the position of the jurists and hadith scholars
as those sources report it; some theologians took a different view [6]. If you
want the argument rather than the conclusion, ask someone who can walk you
through it.

هذا حديث حسن صحيح. وهكذا روى سهيل بن أبي صالح، عن عبد الله بن دينار، عن أبي صالح، عن أبي هريرة.

وروى عمارة بن غزية هذا الحديث عن أبي صالح، عن أبي هريرة، عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم قال الإيمان أربعة وستون بابا.

٢٦١٤

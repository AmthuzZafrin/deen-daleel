# I work in a shop that sells pork and alcohol — is my income halal?

## Answer

**Your wage is not made unlawful by the fact that the shop also sells pork and alcohol. That belief is addressed head-on in these books and rejected. What does need attention is whether *you personally* handle the sale of those items — which is a different question, and usually a more solvable one.**

**First, the part that is settled against you, so you know where the line is.** Selling these things is forbidden, and the prohibition covers the price as well as the substance: `إن الله حرم الخمر وثمنها وحرم الخنزير وثمنه` [1] — God forbade wine **and its price**, and forbade swine **and its price**. And the general statement: `ان الله حرم بيع الخمر والميتة والخنزير والأصنام` [2].

The most instructive report is of a man who brought a skin of wine as a gift after its prohibition, not knowing, and was told to take it away; he instructed his servant to sell it, and was stopped: `شعرت أن الذي حرم شربها حرم بيعها` [3] — **do you not know that the One who forbade drinking it forbade selling it?**

So the act of selling is what the texts name. Not being present in a building where it is sold.

**Second, the part almost everyone gets wrong.** Al-Nawawi states it as flatly as anything in this collection: `ما يقوله العوام اختلاط الحلال بالحرام يحرمه فباطل لا أصل له` [4] — **what ordinary people say, that the mixing of the lawful with the unlawful makes it forbidden, is baseless and has no foundation.**

He gives the method instead: `طريقه أن يفصل قدر الحرام فيصرفه إلى الجهة التي يجب صرفه فيها ويبقى الباقي له يتصرف فيه بما أراد` [5] — the way is to separate out the measure of the unlawful and direct it where it must go, and **the remainder stays his, to dispose of as he wishes.**

Ibn Taymiyya applies the same reasoning to a seller from whom something was wrongly taken: `فان هذا المال المأخوذ ظلما سواء أخذ من البائع أو من المشتري لا يوجب وقوع الشبهة فيما بقى من المال وكما لو ظلم الرجل وأخذ بعض ماله فان ذلك لا يوجب وقوع الشبهة فيما بقى من ماله` [6] — a wrongful portion does not cast doubt over what remains, just as a man who is wronged in part of his wealth does not thereby have the rest of it clouded.

Taint does not spread by contact. It is measured, separated, and dealt with — the rest is clean.

And where the lawful is the larger part, Ibn Taymiyya's rule applies directly: `فان كان الحلال هو الأغلب لم يحكم بتحريم المعاملة` [7] — where the lawful predominates, the dealing is not ruled forbidden.

**Third, for what you have already earned while unaware.** Ibn Taymiyya notes that when the prohibition of usury came, `فامرهم بترك ما بقى في الذمم من الربا ولم يأمرهم برد ما قبضوه` [8] — they were ordered to abandon what remained outstanding and **were not ordered to return what they had already received**. He extends the principle to a Muslim who transacted holding a view he thought sound: `ولو أنه تبين له فيما بعد رجحان التحريم لم يكن عليه اخراج المال الذي كسبه` [9] — even if it later becomes clear to him that prohibition is the stronger view, **he is not obliged to divest what he earned.**

If you have been doing this job without knowing there was a question, that applies to you. The obligation runs forward from now, not backwards over your rent and your groceries.

**So where does that leave your actual job?**

Sort your duties by whether you personally effect the sale:

- **Scanning pork or alcohol at a till, stocking those specific shelves, ordering that stock, serving at an alcohol counter.** This is the act the texts name. It is the part to address.
- **Everything else** — other departments, deliveries of other goods, cleaning, shelf-filling elsewhere, admin, security, customer service. Nothing above touches this, and the rule that mixing does not taint means your wage for it is not in question.

**What to do.**

1. **Ask to be moved.** In practice this is the whole solution for most people. Many shops will keep a worker off the alcohol counter or the relevant tills if asked, and this is a common enough request that it is rarely treated as strange. Ask before assuming refusal.
2. **If you cannot avoid occasional handling** — a mixed basket at a general till — that is a much smaller portion of your work than the anxiety suggests, and the proportionality principle above applies. Some scholars will permit it outright given the predominance of the lawful; others will ask you to estimate the corresponding share of your wage and dispose of it, following the procedure on the page about unlawful income.
3. **Do not treat your whole income as tainted**, and do not treat your household as living on forbidden money. Al-Nawawi's sentence is the answer to that, and it is unusually blunt for a reason.
4. **If the forbidden items are the main business** — an off-licence, a bar, a butcher selling mainly pork — the calculus is different and the wage question is genuinely hard. That situation, rather than a supermarket, is the one worth putting to a scholar with real urgency.

Take the specific split of your duties to someone qualified if you are unsure. But the underlying fear — that working near forbidden goods makes everything you earn forbidden — is one these books explicitly say has no basis.

# Does being sick break my fast?

## Answer

**If it came up on its own, no — your fast is intact. If you made yourself vomit,
yes, and you make the day up.**

The whole ruling turns on that one distinction, and it is one of the most settled
points in the chapter.

**The hadith.**

Abu Hurayra reports: **whoever is overcome by vomiting, there is no make-up upon
him** [1] — **and whoever makes himself vomit deliberately, let him make it up** [2].

Ibn Qudama defines the two words: **the meaning of *istaqa'* is to vomit while
seeking to vomit; and *dhara'ahu* is coming out without any choice on his part** [3].

**How settled it is.**

Ibn Qudama quotes two of the great surveyors of scholarly opinion. Al-Khattabi:
**I do not know of any disagreement among the people of knowledge in it** [4]. And
Ibn al-Mundhir: **the people of knowledge are agreed on the invalidity of the fast
of one who makes himself vomit deliberately** [5].

Al-Tirmidhi reports the same practice: **the practice among the people of knowledge
is on the hadith of Abu Hurayra from the Prophet — that the fasting person, when
vomiting overcomes him, has no make-up upon him; and when he makes himself vomit
deliberately, let him make it up** [6], naming al-Thawri, al-Shafi'i, Ahmad and
Ishaq.

**A note on the hadith's grade, since al-Tirmidhi raises it himself.**

He calls it **a hasan gharib hadith** [7] and records that al-Bukhari did not think
it well-preserved through that chain. Ibn Qudama, on the other side, notes that the
contrary reports — that vomiting does not break the fast at all — come through a
weak narrator. The jurists acted on Abu Hurayra's hadith; the grading discussion is
worth knowing rather than worth worrying about.

**The principle behind it.**

Ibn Qudama gives the argument the other side used, which is also the general rule of
the chapter: **breaking the fast is by what enters, not by what exits** [8]. Vomit
exits. On that reasoning alone, no vomiting would break the fast.

The jurists accepted the principle and made deliberate vomiting an exception on the
strength of the text — and because in inducing it you have acted on your own fast.

**What is owed.**

A make-up day only. No expiation. Ibn Nujaym: **that is, he returned the vomit, or
vomited deliberately, and swallowed what is not normally taken as nourishment or as
medicine — his fast is invalidated and a make-up is required of him, and there is no
expiation upon him** [9].

**Two edge cases.**

**How much?** Ibn Qudama reports a difference within his own school: the apparent
position is that **a little vomit and a lot are the same**, while a second narration
holds **it does not break the fast except by a mouthful** [10]. So on one view a
small involuntary retch is definitively nothing.

**Swallowing it back.** This is the case that does break the fast even when the
vomiting was involuntary — Ibn Nujaym's ruling above covers the person who
**returned the vomit**. If it came up and you swallowed it back deliberately, that
counts as something entering.

**In practice:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Sick from illness, travel, pregnancy, a smell | Fast intact. Rinse your mouth, continue |
| Made yourself sick | Fast broken. Make the day up. No expiation |
| Vomited involuntarily, then swallowed it back on purpose | Make the day up |
| Nausea, retching, acid reflux with nothing coming up | Nothing at all |

**If you are vomiting from illness**, the more relevant question is whether you
should be fasting at all — see the answer on who must fast. The books treat pushing
through real illness as a fault, not a virtue.

وكلاهما صحيح متقارب المعنى قوله صلى الله عليه وسلم (ما يصيب المؤمن من وصب ولا نصب ولا سقم ولا حزن حتى الهم يهمه إلا كفر الله به من سيئاته) الوصب الوجع اللازم ومنه قوله تعالى ولهم عذاب واصب أي لازم ثابت والنصب التعب وقد نصب ينصب نصبا كفرح يفرح فرحا ونصبه غيره وأنصبه لغتان والسقم بضم السين واسكان القاف وفتحهما لغتان وكذلك الحزن والحزن فيه اللغتان ويهمه قال القاضي هو بضم الياء وفتح الهاء على ما لم يسم فاعله وضبطه غيره يهمه بفتح الياء وضم الهاء أي يغمه وكلاهما صحيح قوله (عن ابن محيصن شيخ من قريش قال مسلم هو عمر بن عبد الرحمن بن محيصن) وهكذا هو في معظم نسخ بلادنا أن مسلما قال هو عمر بن عبد الرحمن وفي بعضها هو عبد الرحمن وكذا نقله القاضي عن بعض الرواة وهو غلط والصواب الأول ومحيصن بالنون في آخره ووقع في بعض نسخ المغاربة بحذفها وهو تصحيف قوله صلى الله عليه وسلم (قاربوا) أي اقتصدوا فلا تغلوا ولا تقصروا بل توسطوا (وسددوا) أي اقصدوا السداد وهو الصواب قوله صلى الله عليه وسلم (حتى النكبة ينكبها) وهي مثل العثرة يعثرها برجله

# Can I brush my teeth while fasting?

## Answer

**Brushing is fine. The tooth-stick is explicitly permitted while fasting; the only
live disagreement is about the afternoon, and it is a preference, not a
prohibition. Toothpaste is not addressed by these sources — the principle is that
you must not swallow it.**

**The siwak is permitted, and it is sunna.**

Ibn Qudama: **Ahmad said: there is no harm in the tooth-stick for the fasting
person** [1] — citing Amir ibn Rabi'a: **I saw the Prophet, more times than I can
count, using the tooth-stick while fasting** [2], graded hasan by al-Tirmidhi.

Al-Sarakhsi states the Hanafi position and the Shafi'i objection together: **the
fasting person may use the tooth-stick at the beginning of the day and at its end;
and al-Shafi'i disliked the tooth-stick for the fasting person at the end of the
day** [3]. Ibn Umar's practice is quoted in al-Bukhari's own chapter heading: **Ibn
Umar said: he uses the tooth-stick at the beginning of the day and at its end** [4].

**The disagreement about the afternoon.**

The objection is about the smell. Ibn Qudama: **Ahmad and Ishaq recommended leaving
the tooth-stick in the late afternoon** [5] — the reasoning being the hadith that
the breath of the fasting person is better with God than the scent of musk, and
brushing removes it.

Al-Sarakhsi answers that argument head-on: the tooth-stick **does not remove the
*khuluf* — rather it increases it; it only removes an unpleasant odour**, and the
Prophet's words were about the fasting person's rank, not about the smell as such.

Either way this is a question of what is *better*, not of what is permitted. Nobody
in these passages says brushing invalidates the fast.

**A wet or fresh brush.**

Ibn Qudama reports both narrations from Ahmad: **the transmission from him differed
regarding using a fresh twig, and dislike was transmitted from him** [6] — the worry
being that particles could dissolve and reach the throat — and **it was transmitted
from him that it is not disliked; and this is what al-Thawri, al-Awza'i and Abu
Hanifa said** [7].

Al-Sarakhsi is unequivocal: **the tooth-stick, wet and dry, are the same in this**
[8], and he cites Aisha: **the Messenger of God used to use a fresh tooth-stick while
fasting** [9].

**Toothpaste.**

Toothpaste did not exist, so no ruling on it exists. What the sources give is the
principle, and it is clear enough to apply. Ibn Qudama: **he breaks the fast by
everything he introduces into his interior, or into a cavity in his body such as his
brain or his throat, and the like of that which passes to his stomach — if it
reaches there by his choice, and was something he could have guarded against** [10].
Al-Sarakhsi puts it more tersely: **the consideration is what arrives, not the
route** [11].

So the question is not "is toothpaste allowed" but "did any of it go down". Toothpaste
in the mouth and spat out has not reached the interior. Toothpaste swallowed has.

**In practice:**

- **Brush.** Use a small amount, don't tilt your head back, and spit thoroughly.
- **Rinse with plain water afterwards** — carefully, not vigorously. See the answer
  on mouthwash for why the vigour matters.
- **If you accidentally swallow a little**, that falls under what could not be
  guarded against, on the same reasoning as saliva; it does not break your fast. If
  you swallow it deliberately, it does.
- **If you are anxious about it**, brush at suhoor and after iftar and use a plain
  wet brush or a siwak during the day. That is a comfortable position that nobody
  disputes — but it is a precaution, not a requirement.

Bad breath while fasting is real, and nothing in these sources asks you to endure it
as a form of worship.

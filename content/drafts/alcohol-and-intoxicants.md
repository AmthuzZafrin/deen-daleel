# What does Islam say about alcohol and intoxicants?

## Answer

**Short answer: intoxicants are forbidden, the prohibition is not limited to
wine or to any particular ingredient, and the majority position is that the
amount does not matter — if a large quantity of a thing intoxicates, a small
quantity of it is forbidden too.** There is one genuine historical disagreement
about non-grape drinks, and it is set out below rather than papered over.

**How the Qur'an brings it in.** First as a weighing: **"They question thee about
strong drink and games of chance. Say: In both is great sin, and (some) utility
for men; but the sin of them is greater than their usefulness."** [1]. Then
decisively — Ibn Taymiyya cites the verse the prohibition rests on: **"إنما الخمر
والميسر والأنصاب والأزلام رجس من عمل الشيطان فاجتنبوه"** — *wine, gambling, idols
and divining arrows are only an abomination of Satan's handiwork, so avoid it*
[2].

Sahih Muslim preserves the moment it landed. Anas was serving his elders a drink
of unripe and ripe dates: **"فجاء رجل، فقال: «إنها قد حرمت الخمر»، فقالوا: اكفئها
يا أنس، فكفأتها"** — *a man came and said: wine has been forbidden. They said:
pour it out, Anas. So I poured it out* [3]. He adds that this mixture *was* their
wine at the time — which is the first clue that the word never meant grapes only.

**The word covers whatever intoxicates.** Muslim's own chapter heading says so:
**"باب تحريم الخمر، وبيان أنها تكون من عصير العنب، ومن التمر والبسر والزبيب،
وغيرها مما يسكر"** — *chapter on the prohibition of khamr, and the clarification
that it may be from grape juice, from dates, unripe dates, raisins, and other
things that intoxicate* [4].

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr supplies the evidence from those who spoke the language. Anas:
**"قال أنس الخمر من العنب والتمر والعسل والحنطة والشعير والذرة فما خمرت من ذلك
فهو الخمر"** — *khamr is from grapes, dates, honey, wheat, barley and millet;
whatever of that ferments is khamr* [5]. And Abu Musa, preaching: **"خمر المدينة
من البسر والتمر وخمر أهل فارس من العنب وخمر أهل اليمن من البتع وهو من العسل وخمر
الحبش السكركة من الذرة"** — *the wine of Medina is from unripe and ripe dates,
the wine of the Persians from grapes, the wine of the Yemenis is bit', which is
from honey, and the wine of the Abyssinians is sukurka, from millet* [6].

Six ingredients, four regions, one ruling. The category was defined by **effect**,
not by recipe, from the beginning.

The hadith state it directly — **"كل مسكر حرام وكل مسكر خمر"** — *every
intoxicant is forbidden and every intoxicant is khamr* [7] — and extend it past
the point of intoxication: **"ما أسكر كثيره فقليله حرام"** — *whatever a large
amount of intoxicates, a small amount of it is forbidden* [8].

**Where the scholars actually differ.** Ibn Taymiyya lays out both sides, and it
is worth reading in full because the popular versions of this argument
misrepresent both. The majority: **"أما الأشربة المسكرة فمذهب جمهور علماء
المسلمين من الصحابة والتابعين لهم باحسان وسائر العلماء أن كل مسكر خمر وكل خمر
حرام وما أسكر كثيره فقليله حرام وهذا مذهب مالك وأصحابه والشافعى وأصحابه وأحمد بن
حنبل وأصحابه"** — *as for intoxicating drinks, the position of the great majority
of the scholars of the Muslims, from the Companions, the Successors who followed
them well, and the rest of the scholars, is that every intoxicant is khamr, every
khamr is forbidden, and whatever a large amount of intoxicates, a small amount is
forbidden. This is the school of Malik and his companions, al-Shafi'i and his
companions, and Ahmad ibn Hanbal and his companions* [9] — and he adds that it is
**"وهو أحد القولين في مذهب أبى حنيفة وهو اختيار محمد بن الحسن صاحب أبى حنيفة
واختيار طائفة من المشايخ مثل أبى الليث السمر قندى وغيره"** — *also one of the two
positions within Abu Hanifa's school, and the choice of Muhammad ibn al-Hasan,
Abu Hanifa's companion, and of a group of the shaykhs such as Abu'l-Layth
al-Samarqandi and others* [10].

The minority: **"وذهب طائفة من العلماء من أهل الكوفة كالنخعى والشعبى وأبى حنيفة
وشريك وغيرهم إلى أن ما أسكر من غير الشجرتين النخل والعنب كنبيذ الحنطة والشعير
والذرة والعسل ولبن الخيل وغير ذلك فانما يحرم منه القدر الذى يسكر وأما القليل الذى
لا يسكر فلا يحرم"** — *a group of scholars of Kufa, such as al-Nakha'i,
al-Sha'bi, Abu Hanifa and Sharik, held that what intoxicates from other than the
two trees — the palm and the vine — such as nabidh of wheat, barley, millet,
honey, mare's milk and so on, only the intoxicating quantity of it is forbidden;
as for the small quantity that does not intoxicate, it is not forbidden* [11].

**And note what both sides agree on:** **"وأما عصير العنب الذى إذا غلا واشتد
وقذف بالزبد فهو خمر يحرم قليله وكثيره باجماع المسلمين"** — *as for grape juice
which, when it ferments, becomes strong and throws up froth, that is khamr, and
little and much of it are forbidden by the consensus of the Muslims* [12]. **Wine
in any amount is agreed.** The disagreement was about a narrow class of
non-grape, non-date preparations, and it was already the minority position in the
classical period.

**What counts as an intoxicant beyond drink.** Ibn Taymiyya generalises from the
mechanism rather than the container: **"وكل ما كانت فيه هذه الشدة المطربة فهو خمر
من أى مادة كان من الحبوب والثمار وغير ذلك وسواء كان نيئا أو مطبوخا"** — *and
everything in which this exhilarating potency is found is khamr, from whatever
material it may be, from grains, fruits or otherwise, and whether raw or cooked*
[13]. He applies it to a non-drinkable substance directly — **"وكذلك ( الحشيشة )
المسكرة يجب فيها الحد"** — *and likewise intoxicating hashish, the prescribed
penalty is due for it* [14] — arguing at length against those who claimed it
merely clouded the mind without intoxicating.

The reasoning transfers to substances that did not exist then. What is being
regulated is a state, not a beverage. He also grounds the prohibition in
consequences the verse itself names: potency **"التى تصد عن ذكر الله وعن الصلاة
وتوقع العداوة والبغضاء"** — *which turns one away from the remembrance of Allah
and from prayer, and brings about enmity and hatred* [15].

**Practically.**

- **Alcoholic drinks are out, in any quantity.** The majority position, and on the
  grape-derived ones there is no disagreement at all.
- **A drink is not permissible because it is weak.** That is exactly what "if much
  intoxicates, little is forbidden" rules out.
- **Recreational intoxicants beyond drink fall under the same reasoning**, on the
  argument above, since the category is defined by what a substance does.
- **Medical use, cooking, and trace amounts are separate questions** — they turn
  on different evidence and have their own answers on this site.
- **If you are trying to stop**, the Qur'an's own sequence is worth noticing: it
  named the harm and the benefit honestly before it forbade.

**Where this stops.** These passages establish the category and the ruling on
drinking. They do not settle what happens when alcohol is a solvent, a residue,
or a cooking ingredient, nor questions of addiction, treatment, or someone else's
drinking — each of which needs its own evidence.

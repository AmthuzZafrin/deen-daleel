# What do I do if my period starts during Hajj?

## Answer

Your Hajj continues. Menstruation stops one act — the circling of the Ka'ba — and nothing else.

This was settled on the pilgrimage itself. 'A'isha began menstruating at Sarif on the way to Mecca and
wept, saying she wished she had not come that year. The Prophet ﷺ told her: **"that is a thing God has
decreed upon the daughters of Adam. So do what the pilgrim does, except do not circle the House until
you become pure"** [1]. Jabir describes the outcome: **"'A'isha menstruated, and she performed all the
rites except the circling of the House, and she did not pray"** [2]. Al-Bukhari titles the chapter with
the rule itself: **"the menstruating woman performs all the rites except the circling of the
House"** [3].

Ibn Taymiyya reports that as settled: **"the menstruating woman performs all the rites while
menstruating, except the tawaf — by the Sunna of the Messenger of God ﷺ established from him, and by the
agreement of the imams"** [4].

### What you still do

Everything. Enter ihram, say the talbiya, stand at 'Arafa, spend the night at Muzdalifa, stone the
pillars, offer the sacrifice, cut your hair. **"So the woman who arrives in Mecca menstruating before
the standing does not circle the House, but she does stand at 'Arafa"** [5]. The reason is structural:
**"there is nothing among the rites for which purity is required except the tawaf"** [6].

That includes the sa'y, on the majority view: **"as for the going between al-Safa and al-Marwa, there is
dispute in it, and the majority hold that purity is not required for it"** [7]. So if you have completed
the tawaf and then begin bleeding, you may still do the sa'y.

The farewell tawaf is waived outright: **"the Prophet ﷺ dropped the farewell tawaf from the menstruating
woman"** [8]. When told that Safiyya was menstruating, he asked, **"'is she going to detain us?' It was
said to him that she had already performed the tawaf of ifada. He said: 'then no'"** [9] — the departure
circuit simply falls away.

### What if it is only spotting?

Not everything counts. Ibn Taymiyya's preferred position on brownish and yellowish discharge: **"if it
occurs within the habitual period along with black and red blood, then it is menstruation; otherwise
not"** [10], and he cites Umm 'Atiyya: **"we did not consider the yellowish and the brownish after
purification to be anything"** [11]. Discharge outside your normal days, after you have become pure, is
not a period, and does not stop you circling.

### The real problem: the group leaves

This is the modern version of the question, and Ibn Taymiyya answered its exact form. The starting point
is to try: **"the menstruating woman performs all the rites except the circling of the House — so she
should strive not to perform the tawaf except in a state of purity"** [12]. But then:

**"If she is unable to do that, and it is not possible for her to stay behind from the caravan until she
becomes pure and can perform the tawaf, then if she performs the tawaf of ziyara while menstruating, it
suffices her according to one of the two positions of the scholars"** [13].

His reasoning is worth having in full, because it is the principle the answer rests on: **"God does not
burden a soul beyond its capacity"** [14]. He argues from the greater case — a person who cannot meet the
conditions of prayer prays as they are — to the lesser. And on whether a sacrifice is owed: **"if she
offers a sacrifice it is more cautious; and if not, it is not clear that anything is upon her"** [15],
since **"one who leaves an obligation out of incapacity is not like one who leaves it for some other
reason"** [16].

He adds a practical instruction for that case: **"it is proper for the menstruating woman, when she
performs tawaf, to bathe and to secure herself"** [17] — wash and use protection, as one does when
entering ihram.

### In practice

Try to wait; that is everyone's first answer, and it follows directly from [12]. Ask your group leader
— delaying the ifada tawaf until the last days is normal. Whether to postpone the period medically is a question to put to a doctor and to a scholar before
you travel, not one to settle in Mina; the sources here do not address it.

If none of that is available and the flight is leaving, the position above exists, was given by a major
jurist, and is applied by muftis today. Take it to a scholar you trust rather than deciding alone under
pressure — but know that the tradition did not leave you without an answer, and that the first thing the
Prophet ﷺ said to a woman weeping over this was that it was not her fault.

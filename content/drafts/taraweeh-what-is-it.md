# What is taraweeh and do I have to pray it?

## Answer

Taraweeh is the long night prayer prayed in congregation through Ramadan, after the isha prayer. The name is descriptive: **taraweeh is the plural of tarwiha, originally a verbal noun meaning a rest** [1] — because after every four units the congregation sits and rests.

It is a strongly recommended sunna, not an obligation. Ibn Qudama: **it is a strongly emphasised sunna, and the first to make it a sunna was the Messenger of God** [2]. Ibn Nujaym reports the same conclusion in the Hanafi tradition — Abu Yusuf asked Abu Hanifa about it and about what Umar did, and he answered: **taraweeh is a strongly emphasised sunna** [3]; **more than one has related consensus on its being a sunna** [4]. So nobody sins by not praying it.

Why the Prophet did not simply make it a congregational fixture is preserved in Aisha's report. He prayed in the mosque one night and people prayed with him; the next night more came; on the third or fourth he did not come out, and in the morning he explained: **nothing prevented me from coming out to you except that I feared it would be made obligatory upon you** [5]. What he did do was encourage it in general terms — **whoever stands in prayer through Ramadan out of faith and seeking reward, what has gone before of his sins is forgiven** [6].

The congregational form we have now goes back to Umar. Abd al-Rahman ibn Abd al-Qari went out with him one night in Ramadan and found people scattered, each praying alone or in small clusters, and Umar said: **I think that if I gathered these behind a single reciter, it would be better** — **then he resolved on it and gathered them behind Ubayy ibn Ka'b** [7]. Coming out on a later night and seeing them all praying together, Umar said: **what an excellent innovation this is** [8]. That famous line is why you sometimes hear taraweeh called an innovation; in context it is praise, and both Ibn Qudama and Ibn Nujaym read it as the recovery of something the Prophet had already established.

Two practical points. On length, Aisha reported that the Prophet **did not exceed eleven units in Ramadan or outside it** [9], while the Hanbali and Hanafi manuals both transmit twenty as the received practice — so mosques differ, and both numbers have a pedigree. On staying to the end, there is a report from Abu Dharr worth knowing: **when a man prays with the imam until he departs, standing the whole night is written for him** [10].

So: go if you can, go for part of it if that is what you can manage, and do not treat it as a test you are failing. It is voluntary prayer, and voluntary prayer is measured by what you sustain.

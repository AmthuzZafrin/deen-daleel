# How do I greet non-Muslims?

## Answer

Two things are settled, one is disputed, and the disputed one has more room in it than you have probably been told.

**Settled: you always return a greeting.**

*"When ye are greeted with a greeting, greet ye with a better than it or return it. Lo! Allah taketh count of all things"* (4:86) [1].

Al-Nawawi: "**the scholars are agreed on returning [the greeting] to the People of the Book when they give salam** — except that one does not say to them *wa 'alaykum al-salam*, but says only *'alaykum*, or *wa 'alaykum*" [2].

**Why the reply has that particular form — and it is not what people assume.**

The wording comes from a specific incident. Some Jews of Medina used to say *al-sam 'alaykum* — *death be upon you* — as a pun on *al-salam*. The instruction *wa 'alaykum* is the reply to *that*.

'A'isha heard it and answered them: *rather, death and the curse be upon you*. "And the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: **'A'isha, God loves gentleness in all matters.** She said: did you not hear what they said? He said: **I said — and upon you**" [3].

He corrected *her*, not them. That is the tone the whole question should be read in.

**Disputed: whether you may greet first — and this is where the range is wide.**

The hadith people cite: "**do not initiate the salam to the Jews and the Christians; and when you meet one of them on a road, force them to its narrowest part**" [4]. Ahmad b. Hanbal held that "going to this hadith is preferable to what contradicts it" [5].

But Ibn 'Abd al-Barr opens his treatment by saying that "**the *salaf* and those after them disagreed** about initiating the People of the Covenant with the salam" [5] — and then names who did it:

"**Abu Umama al-Bahili never passed a Muslim, a Jew or a Christian without initiating the salam to him.** And it is related of **Ibn Mas'ud, Abu al-Darda' and Fadala b. 'Ubayd** that they used to initiate the salam to the People of the Covenant. And of Ibn Mas'ud, that he wrote to a man of the People of the Book: *peace be upon you*; and of him also that he said: **if Pharaoh said something good to me, I would return the like to him**" [6].

And Abu Umama's own reason, which is worth memorising: 'Urwa b. Ruwaym "saw Abu Umama al-Bahili giving salam to everyone he met, Muslim or protected person, saying: **it is a greeting for the people of our religion, and a safeguard for the people under our protection, and a name of the names of God which we spread among ourselves**" [7].

The disagreement reached the caliphs. "It was said to Muhammad b. Ka'b al-Qurazi that 'Umar b. 'Abd al-'Aziz, asked about initiating the People of the Covenant, said: *we return to them and do not initiate to them.* He said: **as for me, I see no harm in initiating them with the salam.** He was asked: why? He said: because of God's words *so turn away from them and say: peace — for they will come to know*. **And Malik's position in this is like the position of 'Umar b. 'Abd al-'Aziz, and Ibn Wahb permitted it**" [8].

**How the two sides are reconciled — three attempts, all useful.**

*Ibn 'Abd al-Barr's own:* "it may bear, in my view, that the meaning of *do not initiate them* is **that it is not incumbent upon you to initiate them as you do with the Muslims** — and if it is carried on this, **the disagreement is lifted**" [9].

*Al-Tabari's:* the Prophet ﷺ passed a mixed gathering of Muslims, idolaters and Jews, and gave salam to them. "Al-Tabari said: what Usama narrated does not conflict with Abu Hurayra's hadith, for there is no contradiction between them — **Abu Hurayra's hadith comes out as general, and Usama's report clarifies that its meaning is specific**" [10].

*Al-Nakha'i's, which is the practical one:* "**if you have a need with a Jew or a Christian, initiate the salam to him.** So it becomes clear by this that Abu Hurayra's hadith — *do not initiate them with salam* — is where there is **no reason** calling you to initiate them: discharging an obligation, a need that arises for you with them, **or a right of companionship, or of neighbourliness, or of travel**" [11].

And the case that shows it in life: Ibn Mas'ud greeted a Persian landholder who had travelled with him. "'Alqama said: I said to him, *Abu 'Abd al-Rahman, is it not disliked to initiate them with the salam?* He said: **yes — but the right of companionship**" [12].

The man who is transmitted as *both* initiating the salam to the People of the Book and being asked why, answered with the right of the person in front of him.

Al-Awza'i's answer to the same question is the most generous thing in the file: "he was asked about a Muslim who passed a disbeliever and greeted him. He said: **if you greet, righteous men before you greeted; and if you leave it, righteous men before you left it**" [13].

**Two practical rulings from al-Nawawi that settle most modern cases.**

*A mixed room.* "If he passes a gathering in which there are disbelievers and Muslims, or one Muslim, **it is recommended that he greet them**, intending the Muslims" [14] — on the same hadith of Usama.

*Another greeting altogether.* "If he wishes to greet a protected person with other than the salam, al-Mutawalli and al-Rafi'i said he may — such as saying **may God guide you**, or **may God make your morning pleasant**" [15].

That is the answer to almost every practical case: *good morning*, *how are you*, *hope you're well* are not the *salam*, and no part of this dispute touches them.

And the Prophet's own letter to Heraclius shows the form for writing to someone who is not Muslim: "**From Muhammad, servant of God and His Messenger, to Heraclius the great one of the Romans: peace be upon whoever follows guidance**" [16].

**Practically.**

- **Always return a greeting.** That is the Qur'an's command and there is consensus on it.
- **Ordinary courtesies are not the *salam* at all.** *Good morning, hello, how are you, take care* — these are outside the dispute, and al-Nawawi names them as permitted explicitly.
- **"As-salamu 'alaykum" to a colleague or friend:** there is a real disagreement. Ahmad's position is not to initiate it; Malik's and 'Umar b. 'Abd al-'Aziz's is that there is no harm; and Ibn Mas'ud, Abu al-Darda', Fadala and Abu Umama did it.
- **Where there is a relationship, the permissive position is stronger.** Companionship, neighbourliness, travel, a need — al-Nakha'i and Ibn Mas'ud both make the tie the reason.
- **A mixed group: greet them**, intending the Muslims. That is al-Nawawi's explicit ruling and it fits every workplace.
- **Never use it as a weapon.** The one person corrected in these reports is the Muslim who answered rudeness with rudeness, and what she was told was that God loves gentleness in all matters.

Translation (Marmaduke Pickthall):
And if they argue with thee, (O Muhammad), say: I have surrendered my purpose to Allah and (so have) those who follow me. ```
Qur'an 3:64 — Surah Aal-i-Imraan, verse 64 of chapter 3

Translation (Marmaduke Pickthall):
Say: O People of the Scripture! Come to an agreement between us and you: that we shall worship none but Allah, and that we shall ascribe no partner unto Him, and that none of us shall take others for lords beside Allah. And if they turn away, then say: Bear witness that we are they who have surrendered (unto Him).

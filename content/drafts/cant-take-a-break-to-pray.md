# My job won't give me time to pray — what do I do?

## Answer

**Work through the options in order — they are much wider than most people
realise, and it is rare for all of them to be closed at once.**

**1. Check whether you actually need a break.** A prayer takes four minutes.
Prayer windows are hours long, not minutes. Most people who believe they cannot
pray at work have not yet realised that Dhuhr can be prayed any time from midday
until mid-afternoon, and that a stairwell counts as a place. There is a separate
answer here on praying at work that goes through the practicalities.

**2. Ask.** Directly, and framed as what it is — a few minutes, once or twice a
shift, at a flexible time. Most employers say yes when asked plainly. In many
jurisdictions there is also a legal duty to make reasonable accommodation for
religious practice; that is a question for a local union or advice service, not for
this page, but it is worth knowing it exists.

**3. Combine, if the shift genuinely does not allow two separate prayers.** This is
the classical remedy for exactly this shape of difficulty. Ibn Abbas reported:
**the Prophet combined between Dhuhr and Asr, and Maghrib and Isha, with neither
fear nor rain** — and when asked why, said **he intended not to put his community
into hardship** [1].

Ibn Qudama takes that as covering illness, and extends it by name to others under
strain: **it may take in one who is under hardship, such as a nursing mother, a
weak elderly person, and the like of them, for whom there is hardship in abandoning
combining** [2]. And **Ibn Shubruma said: it is permitted when there is a need or
something, so long as he does not take it as a habit** [3].

Note the limit in that last clause, because it is the honest boundary: combining is
for a shift that genuinely traps you, not a standing arrangement for convenience.
There is a separate answer here on combining for work.

**4. Pray at the very end of the window.** Dhuhr may be prayed until Asr enters;
Asr until sunset. If your shift ends at 5, and Asr runs until 6, there was never a
conflict.

**5. If you truly could not**, the prayer is owed, not cancelled. **Whoever sleeps
through a prayer or forgets it, let him pray it when he remembers it — there is no
expiation for it but that** [4]. Ibn Taymiyya's category for someone who left a
prayer **for an excuse under which he believed delay permitted** [5] sits between
forgetting and deliberate omission, and the remedy is the same: pray it as soon as
you can.

**What this page will not tell you.** That it is fine to stop praying because of a
job. It is not, and the honest thing to say is that a job which genuinely makes
five short prayers impossible, permanently, is a job worth changing if you are able
to. That is a real cost and this page is not pretending otherwise — but the
tradition does not offer an option where the prayers simply lapse.

**And the realistic assessment.** Most jobs that look impossible turn out to have
gaps. Long-haul driving has service stations. Hospital shifts have handovers.
Retail has stockrooms. Factory work has breaks. Start with the assumption that
five minutes exists somewhere in the shift, and look for it before concluding it
does not.

### Passage 21 — Al-Tamhid 12:211-212

### Passage 22 — Al-Tamhid 12:198-199

Al-Tamhid 12:198-199

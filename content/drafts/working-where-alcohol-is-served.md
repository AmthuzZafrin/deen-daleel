# Can I work somewhere that serves alcohol?

## Answer

**Serving alcohol is named directly in the text, so that role is the hard case and this page will not soften it. But the surrounding work — kitchen, cleaning, admin, a shop that stocks it among much else — falls under a genuine and well-documented disagreement between the jurists, and knowing where they drew the line is more useful than a blanket answer.**

**The text everything turns on.** Ten roles are named: `عاصرها، ومعتصرها، وشاربها، وحاملها، والمحمولة إليه، وساقيها، وبائعها، وآكل ثمنها، والمشتري لها، والمشتراة له` [1] — its presser, the one who has it pressed, its drinker, its carrier, the one it is carried to, **its pourer**, its seller, the one who consumes its price, its buyer, and the one it is bought for.

`ساقيها` — the one who pours it — is a waiter, in plain terms. `بائعها` is whoever takes the order and rings it up. Those are not inferences; they are in the list.

**Now the disagreement, which is real and is about being hired to handle it.**

Ibn 'Abidin records the Hanafi dispute over accepting a wage to transport wine. Abu Hanifa permitted the wage; his two companions did not. Their objection is the obvious one: `قال الزيلعي: وهذا عنده، وقالا: هو مكروه لأنه عليه الصلاة والسلام لعن في الخمر عشرة، وعد منها حاملها` [2] — it is disliked, because the Prophet cursed ten in respect of wine and counted the **carrier** among them.

Abu Hanifa's reasoning is careful and worth following, because it is the reasoning every permissive answer to your question ultimately borrows: `وله أن الإجارة على الحمل وهو ليس بمعصية ولا سبب لها، وإنما تحصل المعصية بفعل فاعل مختار، وليس الشرب من ضرورات الحمل، لان حملها قد يكون للإراقة أو للتخليل` [3] — the hire is on **the carrying**, which is not itself disobedience nor a cause of it; the disobedience comes about by the act of a free agent; and drinking is not a necessary consequence of carrying, since a thing may be carried in order to be poured away or turned into vinegar.

He then reads the hadith accordingly: `والحديث محمول على الحمل المقرون بقصد المعصية` [4] — the report is to be understood of carrying **coupled with the intention of the sin**.

Al-Zayla'i extends the same disagreement outwards: `وعلى هذا الخلاف لو آجره دابة لينقل عليها الخمر أو آجره نفسه ليرعى له الخنازير يطيب له الاجر عنده، وعندهما يكره` [5] — hiring out an animal to transport wine, or hiring oneself out to herd another's swine: the wage is wholesome on his view, disliked on theirs.

And Ibn Taymiyya confirms that the permissive side is not marginal: `وقد لعن العاصر والمعتصر وكثير من الفقهاء يجوزون للرجل أن يعصر لغيره عنبا وان علم أن من نيته أن يتخذه خمرا` [6] — the presser and the one who has it pressed were cursed, **and many jurists permit a man to press grapes for another even knowing he intends to make wine of them.**

So the material is not one-sided, and anyone who tells you the question is simple has not read it.

**But the same passage marks where the leniency stops, and the line is precise.**

Ibn 'Abidin notes that pressing is excluded — `لا عصرها لقيام المعصية بعينه` — because the disobedience subsists in the act itself, and resolves the apparent tension: `ولعل المراد هنا عصر العنب على قصد الخمرية، فإن عين هذا الفعل معصية بهذا القصد` [7] — what is meant is pressing grapes **with the intention that it become wine**; that very act is disobedience by that intention.

He records the same limit in a small everyday case: `وبيع المكعب المفضض للرجل إن ليلبسه يكره، لأنه إعانة على لبس الحرام` [8] — selling silvered footwear to a man who will wear it is disliked, **because it is assistance in wearing the forbidden.**

Put the two halves together and the test is legible. Where the hire is on an act that is neutral in itself and the sin depends on someone else's free choice afterwards, there is room — that is carrying, transporting, letting premises. Where the act you are paid for **is** the forbidden step, or is done with that purpose, the leniency does not reach it.

**Applying that honestly to a job.**

- **Pouring and serving drinks, taking drink orders, tending a bar.** This is `ساقيها` and `بائعها`, named in the hadith, and it is not the disputed handling case — it is the act itself. No reading above rescues it. If this is your role, treat finding alternative work as a serious obligation.
- **Handling stock, stacking a shelf, delivering crates, working a till where alcohol is one item among many.** This is exactly the case the jurists disagreed about, with Abu Hanifa's reasoning on one side and his companions' on the other. There is genuine room here, and a scholar who follows one school will answer it from that school's position — which is a legitimate answer, not a loophole.
- **Kitchen work, cleaning, maintenance, security, accounts in a place that also serves alcohol.** Further out again than any role the text names, and further out than the case the jurists argued about. The reasoning gives you the most room here.

**What the corpus does not settle.** It has nothing on employment as a modern arrangement, where one contract covers many duties and you may not control which shift you are given. Nor does it weigh what happens to someone with no other income in a place where most hospitality work involves alcohol — a real hardship, and one that a person who knows your circumstances has to weigh, not a page.

**What to do.** Describe your actual duties to a scholar you can speak with, in tasks rather than a job title, and ask which of the three categories above yours falls into. If you serve or sell the drinks themselves, the answer is not in doubt and the useful conversation is about how quickly you can move to something else. If you do not, say so plainly — the disagreement recorded here is on your side of the question, and you are entitled to be answered from it rather than from the strictest possible reading.

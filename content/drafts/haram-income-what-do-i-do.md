# I earned money from something haram — what do I do with it?

## Answer

**Work out which of two kinds it is, because the remedy is different. If you took it from an identifiable person, you owe it back to them. If it came from a forbidden source but the person who paid you did so willingly and got what they bargained for, there is nobody to return it to — and it is disposed of instead.**

**The classification, stated in the sources.** Al-Jassas: `وأكل المال بالباطل على وجهين أحدهما أخذه على وجه الظلم والسرقة والخيانة والغصب وما جرى مجراه والآخر أخذه من جهة محظورة نحو القمار وأجرة الغناء والقيان والملاهي والنائحة وثمن الخمر والخنزير والحر وما لا يجوز أن يتملكه وإن كان بطيبة نفس من مالكه` [1] — consuming wealth wrongfully is of **two kinds**: first, taking it by way of oppression, theft, betrayal, or usurpation and the like; second, taking it **from a forbidden channel** — gambling, the wages of singing and entertainment, the price of wine and swine and of a free person, and whatever may not be owned — **even where it came with the free consent of its owner.**

That last clause is the hinge. In the second kind, nobody was wronged in the sense of having something taken from them; the transaction was consensual and the counterparty received what they paid for. There is therefore no victim with a claim — which is why the remedy cannot be restitution.

**If it is the first kind: return it to the person.**

Money taken by theft, fraud, overcharging, non-delivery, or breach of trust belongs to a specific person, and their right is to have it back. Give it to them, or to their heirs if they have died. Where the amount is uncertain, Ibn Taymiyya's approach to mutual claims applies: `وإن كان قدر المنهوب مجهولا لا يعرف ما نهب هؤلاء من هؤلاء ولا قدر ما نهب هؤلاء من هؤلاء فإنه يحمل الأمر على التساوي` [2] — where the amount taken is unknown, the matter is treated as equal. Estimate fairly, and if you must err, err against yourself.

Only where the person genuinely cannot be found does this case join the second one.

**A point people miss: a legal entitlement does not make it lawful.**

Two men brought a dispute to the Prophet over inheritances and matters whose evidence had faded, and he told them: `فمن قضيت له بحجة أراها فاقتطع بها قطعة ظلما فإنما يقطع قطعة من النار` [3] — **whoever I rule in favour of on an argument that seems sound to me, and he thereby takes a portion wrongfully, is only taking a portion of the Fire.**

Al-Jassas draws the rule out: `أن حكم الحاكم به لا يبيح أخذه` [4] — **a judge's ruling awarding it does not make taking it lawful.**

If you know that money is not rightfully yours, the fact that a contract, a court, or a regulator permitted you to keep it does not settle the question. And notice how the two men responded — each said *my right is his* — and were told: `لا ولكن اذهبا فتوخيا للحق ثم استهما وليحلل كل واحد منكما صاحبه` [5] — no; go, seek out what is right, then cast lots, and let each of you absolve the other. Sort out the truth of it, then release each other.

**If it is the second kind: dispose of it.**

Where the money came from a forbidden source and the payer consented — wages from work that was itself unlawful, gambling winnings, proceeds of selling something forbidden — you have nobody to repay. The procedure is the one set out on the page about interest money: separate the amount, and give it to people in need or to public benefit, without intending reward for it, because it is disposal rather than charity.

Two points from that page apply here directly. It may not simply be destroyed. And once given, it is lawful and wholesome for the recipient — you are not passing on a contamination.

**Three things that are not required of you.**

- **You do not have to reconstruct your whole past.** The principle that what preceded a person's repentance is left behind is set out on the interest page and applies here.
- **You do not have to treat everything you own as tainted.** Separating the measure of the unlawful leaves the remainder lawful — the point al-Nawawi makes bluntly against the popular belief that mixing spoils the whole.
- **You do not have to impoverish your dependants.** Where you are yourself in need, the classical discussion permits taking from the disposed money what your genuine need requires, because the poor are precisely who it is for.

**One caution about motive.** The reason for insisting the disposal is not charity you take reward for is worth understanding rather than just obeying: it keeps the act honest. `تعس عبد الدينار والدرهم والقطيفة والخميصة ان أعطي رضى وإن لم يعط لم يرض` [6] — wretched is the slave of the dinar and the dirham, of the cloak and the fine garment: **if he is given he is pleased, and if he is not given he is displeased.** The point of divesting is to stop the money owning you, and converting it into a spiritual credit for yourself would defeat that.

**What to do, in order.**

1. **Sort what you hold into the two kinds.** Ask of each amount: is there a person who is worse off because I have this?
2. **Return what belongs to someone.** Directly if you can; through an intermediary if that is easier for both of you; to heirs if they have died.
3. **Dispose of the rest** by the procedure on the interest-money page: to people in need or public benefit, not as your charity.
4. **Stop the source.** Divesting once while continuing to earn the same way addresses the symptom only.
5. **Take an unusual case to a scholar** — a business built on it, a very large sum, a wronged party who refuses contact, or money entangled with partners who are not party to your decision.

And do not let the size of the task stop you starting it. The obligation is to divest what you hold and stop the flow, not to audit your life.

# What do I do while I'm still memorising al-Fatihah?

## Answer

You pray. Tonight, in whatever state your memory is in. The law has a worked-out
answer for exactly this and it is not "wait until you are ready."

Start with why it matters, so the rest makes sense. Ibn Qudama reports the hadith
of 'Ubada b. al-Samit — **there is no prayer for one who does not recite the
Opening of the Book** — and this is the position of Ahmad, Malik, al-Thawri and
al-Shafi'i. [1] The Fatiha is not decoration. That is precisely why the jurists
worked out what stands in its place.

**The ladder of substitutes**, as al-Nawawi sets it out for the Shafi'is, with
Ahmad agreeing: **seven other verses; if he knows nothing of the Qur'an, then
remembrance; and if he cannot manage even that, he stands for the length of the
Fatiha.** [2]

He records the other schools honestly. **Abu Hanifa held that someone unable to
recite stands silent and is not required to make remembrance; Malik held neither
required.** [3] So the ceiling on what is asked of you differs by school, but on
every one of them, you pray.

**What counts as the remembrance rung**: tasbih, tahlil, takbir, tahmid, the
hawqala and their like. [4] *Subhan Allah, la ilaha illa Llah, Allahu akbar,
al-hamdu li-Llah, la hawla wa la quwwata illa bi-Llah.* That is a realistic
evening's work, not a year's.

**And if even those are beyond you this week**, al-Nawawi records the Hawi's
ruling: someone who knows no Qur'an and cannot manage the remembrance in Arabic
**but can in another language, performs it in that language**. [5]

At the bottom of the ladder there is still a valid prayer. If nothing at all is
available and learning is not yet possible, **he stands for the length of the
Fatiha in silence, then bows, and his prayer suffices without repeating it
later**. [6] Not a deficient prayer to be made up. A prayer.

The reasoning al-Nawawi gives for the whole structure is a hadith you should
probably keep: **when I command you to do something, do of it what you are able.**
[7]

**Now the "how long" part, and this is the honest half.** The substitute holds
while the inability holds. Al-Nawawi is explicit that **if he becomes able before
starting the substitute, reciting the Fatiha is required of him**. [8] The
dispensation is not a permanent arrangement, and the obligation to learn stays
live underneath it. Nobody is setting you a deadline — but nobody is telling you
the substitutes are the destination either.

Learn it a line at a time. Pray with what you have tonight.

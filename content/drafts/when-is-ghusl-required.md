# When do I have to do a full bath (ghusl)?

## Answer

**There are three things that make a full bath obligatory**, and the books state
them as a closed list. Qadi Khan: **the causes of ghusl are three — janaba,
menstruation, and post-natal bleeding.** [1]

**1. Janaba**, which itself has two triggers. Qadi Khan again: **janaba is
established by two causes: the first is the emission of semen with desire, and
the second is penetration.** [2]

- **Ejaculation**, however it happens — during sleep, with a spouse, or otherwise.
  There is a separate answer here on wet dreams.
- **Intercourse, whether or not anything is emitted.** This surprises people, so
  the hadith is worth having in full: **when the two circumcised parts meet and
  the glans is concealed, ghusl becomes obligatory, whether he emits or does
  not.** [3]

**2. Menstruation.** **3. Post-natal bleeding (nifas).** Al-Nawawi: **the scholars
are agreed on the obligation of ghusl on account of menstruation and on account
of nifas.** [4] In both cases the bath is due when the bleeding *stops*, not while
it continues — and there is a separate section here on menstruation.

**What does *not* require ghusl.** This is where most of the unnecessary worry
lives, so it is worth stating flatly. **There is no ghusl for madhy or wady;
for those there is wudu.** [5] The books define the three fluids so you can tell
them apart: **semen is thick and white, the erection subsides after it; madhy is
thin, tending to white, and comes out during a man's foreplay with his wife**
[6] — and wady is the thick discharge that follows urination. Only the first
requires a bath. There is a separate answer here comparing them.

So: arousal without ejaculation, madhy on your underwear, ordinary discharge,
passing wind, sleeping, using the toilet — **none** of these require ghusl. Wudu
covers them.

**The recommended baths**, which are worth knowing so you do not mistake them for
obligations: **the Messenger of Allah made it sunna to bathe for Jumu'ah, for the
two Eids, for Arafa and for entering ihram** [7]. Good to do, not required, and
missing one invalidates nothing.

**The same list, from a different school.** Al-Nawawi opens his chapter *what
obliges ghusl*: **what obliges ghusl is the insertion of the glans into the
private part, the emission of semen, menstruation, and nifas.** [8] Four items
where Qadi Khan gave three, because the Shafi'is count the two janaba triggers
separately. It is the same list.

**How soon must you do it?** Not instantly — and this is agreed. Al-Nawawi:
**ghusl for janaba is not required immediately; it only becomes binding on a
person when he rises for prayer, and this is by the consensus of the Muslims.**
[9] So you are not sinning by being in janaba; you are obliged to bathe before
you pray. Practically that means: if you wake in janaba and Fajr is nearly gone,
you bathe and pray — the bath is the condition, not an optional extra you skip to
save the time.

**And when it is due, the whole prayer waits on it.** The Qur'an is explicit that
a full bath — not wudu — is the gateway: **and if ye are unclean, purify
yourselves** [10]. There are separate answers here on how to perform ghusl, on
whether it also counts as wudu, and on what to do when there is no water.

# How do I do ghusl properly?

## Answer

**The short version: form the intention, then get water over every part of your
body, including your hair to the roots and inside your mouth and nose. That is
all that is strictly required.** Everything else is recommended.

Ibn Qudama makes the distinction explicit before anything else, and it is the
most useful thing on this page: **the bath from janaba has two descriptions — a
description of sufficiency, and a description of completeness.** [1]

**The sufficient version (this is the obligation)**

Al-Nawawi: **what is obligatory out of all of this is the intention at the first
contact of the first part of the body with water, and covering the whole body,
its hair and its skin, with water.** [2] Two things. Nothing more.

So if you stand under a shower having intended ghusl, and the water reaches
everywhere — including under your hair and into your mouth and nose — you are
purified. Al-Tirmidhi records that the scholars accepted even the simplest form:
**they said: if the person in janaba immerses himself in water and does not make
wudu, it suffices him — and that is the position of al-Shafi'i, Ahmad and
Ishaq.** [3]

**You do not have to scrub.** Al-Nawawi: **not one of the scholars made rubbing
obligatory in ghusl or in wudu, except Malik and al-Muzani.** [4] Water reaching
the skin is the requirement; friction is not.

**The complete version (this is the sunna)**

This is how the Prophet did it, reported by Aisha: **when the Messenger of Allah
wanted to bathe from janaba, he began by washing his hands before putting them
into the vessel** [5] — then washed his private parts, made a full wudu, worked
water into the roots of his hair, and poured three handfuls over his head before
letting the water run over the rest of him.

Ibn Qudama lists it as ten things: **the complete form contains ten things — the
intention, the *bismillah*, washing his hands three times, washing off any filth
on him, wudu** [6], pouring three handfuls over the head to soak the hair roots,
letting the water flow over the rest of the body, beginning with the right side,
rubbing the body with the hand, and moving away to wash the feet.

**In order, as you would actually do it:**

1. **Intend it** — in the heart, at the first touch of water. Nothing spoken.
2. Say *bismillah*.
3. Wash your hands.
4. Wash your private parts and anything soiled.
5. **Make a full wudu**, as for prayer.
6. Pour water over your head three times, working your fingers into the roots of
   your hair.
7. Pour over the whole body, right side first.
8. Step aside and wash your feet, if you left them from the wudu.

**The one thing people miss.** Rinse the mouth and nose. On the Hanafi reckoning
these are obligatory in ghusl even though they are only sunna in wudu, and they
are covered on every reckoning by "the water must reach everything." Under a
shower, take a mouthful and sniff water up once.

**And do not repeat the wudu afterwards.** Al-Nawawi: **the scholars have agreed
that two wudus are not recommended.** [7] The wudu at the start of the ghusl is
your wudu — you can pray with it directly. Ibn Abd al-Barr, on the same point:
**this wudu is before the bath, not after it** [8].

**On braids, long hair and hair products**, there is a separate answer here —
short version, you generally do not have to undo braids, but anything sealing the
scalp has to come off.

The whole thing rests on one clause of the verse, which asks for a bath and
specifies no procedure at all: **and if ye are unclean, purify yourselves** [9].
The Prophet's practice fills in what excellence looks like. The verse alone tells
you what is owed.

# Can a woman lead prayer?

## Answer

**A woman may lead other women. Leading a mixed congregation of men is held
invalid by all four schools.**

**Leading women — permitted, with one detail of position.** Ibn Qudama's chapter
heading states it: **and if a woman leads women in prayer, she stands with them in
the middle of the row** [1].

That is the distinctive part. A male imam stands in front; a woman leading women
stands *level with them, in the middle of the row*, not ahead of it.

**And leading one other woman**: **when a woman leads a single woman, that woman
stands to her right, like the follower with the men** [2] — the same arrangement as
two men.

**Leading men — not permitted on any of the four schools.** The evidence the books
cite is the ordering of the rows: **put them behind, as Allah has put them behind**
[3], and the practice of the Prophet in mixed prayer, reported by Anas: **the
Messenger of Allah prayed with him and with his mother or his aunt, and he placed
me on his right and placed the woman behind us** [4].

A mixed congregation with a woman leading is a genuine minority position among some
modern voices, but it is outside all four schools and this collection reports what
the schools hold. If that is the question you came with, the honest answer is that
the classical tradition is unanimous against it, and that you will not find a
school-based case for it in these sources.

**When a woman prays behind a man**: **and if she prays behind a man, she stands
behind him** [2] — not to his side, unlike a male follower.

**What this does and does not settle**

| | |
|---|---|
| A woman leading a group of women | **yes** — standing in the middle of the row |
| A woman leading one other woman | **yes** — the other stands to her right |
| A woman leading her children | yes, for daughters and young sons |
| A woman leading a mixed group including adult men | **no**, on all four schools |
| A woman praying in a mixed congregation | yes — she stands in the rows behind |

**Two things worth adding.**

*Women's congregations are a real thing, not a workaround.* Groups of women praying
together, with one of them leading, are described in the books without hesitation.
If you are in a house or a workplace with other Muslim women, that is a
congregation, with the reward of one.

*None of this touches the validity of a woman's own prayer.* Nothing here makes a
woman's prayer lesser, or dependent on a man. There are separate answers here on
whether congregation is obligatory — it is not, for women — and on women praying in
the mosque.

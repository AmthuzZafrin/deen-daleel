# When is du'a most likely to be answered?

## Answer

There are named times, and they are worth knowing. But the most striking thing
in these sources is how candid the scholars are that the single most famous of
them — the hour on Friday — was never pinned down, and that they knew it.

**The promise comes first.** "And when My servants question thee concerning Me,
then surely I am nigh. I answer the prayer of the suppliant when he crieth unto
Me. So let them hear My call and let them trust in Me, in order that they may be
led aright" (2:186) [1]. No time is attached to it.

**The times with the strongest evidence.**

*In prostration.* Ibn Taymiyya assembles it: "the closest a servant is to his
Lord is while he is prostrating, so make much supplication" [2], and from
Muslim: "as for the prostration, strive in supplication in it, for it is fit
that you be answered" [3].

*The last third of the night, and after the obligatory prayers.* "For this reason
it came in the *Sunan*: the best supplication is in the depth of the last part of
the night, and following the prescribed prayers" [4]. And: "it is established in
the Sahih that in the night there is an hour in which supplication is answered,
as on the day of Jumu'ah — and that is every night; and the closest a servant is
to his Lord is in the depth of the last part of the night" [5]. Note what that
does: it takes the once-a-week promise and gives you a nightly one.

**Now the Friday hour, and the honesty around it.** Ibn Hajar: "there has been
much disagreement about it, and al-Khattabi confined it to two positions: the
first, that it is the hour of the prayer; and the other, that it is an hour of
the day when the sun draws near to setting" [6]. He then says what he found when
he collected them: "I gathered exhaustively the disagreement reported about the
said hour, and it came to more than forty positions" [7].

Why so many? Because of a report he quotes from Abu Sa'id, who asked the Prophet
ﷺ about it: "I had been given knowledge of it, then I was made to forget it, as
I was made to forget the Night of Decree" [8]. The vagueness is the point of it,
in the same way it is the point of Laylat al-Qadr.

The two leading candidates each have real support. For the last hour of Friday:
"some people from among the Companions gathered and discussed the hour of
Jumu'ah, then they parted — and they did not differ that it is the last hour of
the day of Jumu'ah" [9]. For the hour of the prayer, Ibn Taymiyya: "it is
narrated that it is tied to the performance of the Jumu'ah, and it is from when
the imam ascends the pulpit until the prayer is finished" [10]. And Ibn al-Qayyim's
resolution, as Ibn Hajar reports it: "he chose that the hour of response is
confined to one of the two mentioned times, and that neither contradicts the
other — since it is possible that he ﷺ indicated one of them at one time and the
other at another" [11].

Take both, in other words, and you have covered it.

**A generous extension.** If you cannot be at the Jumu'ah: "as for one whose
habit was the Jumu'ah and then he fell ill or travelled, what he used to do
while healthy and resident is written for him — and likewise the imprisoned and
the like; these have the like of the reward of one who attended the Jumu'ah, so
their supplication is like the supplication of one who attended it" [12]. Illness,
travel and confinement do not cost you the hour.

**One list to treat with care.** Al-Nawawi records: "it is said that supplication
is answered on five nights — the night of Jumu'ah, the night of Adha, the night
of Fitr, the first night of Rajab, and the middle night of Sha'ban" [13]. And
then he says exactly where it stands: "al-Shafi'i and the companions recommended
the said vigil *even though the hadith is weak* — for what preceded at the start
of the book, that hadiths of merits are treated leniently and one acts in
accordance with their weak reports" [14]. That is the honest status of those five
nights: a recommendation built openly on a weak report under a stated principle,
not a claim about what Allah has promised.

**And the condition attached to all of it.** "Response continues to be given to
the servant so long as he does not supplicate for something sinful or for cutting
off kinship, and so long as he does not become impatient. It was said: and what
is impatience? He said: that he says, I have supplicated and supplicated and I
have not seen myself answered — and so he despairs at that and abandons
supplication" [15].

Which is to say: the times help, but the thing that actually forfeits an answer
is giving up on it.

# Do I need ghusl after a wet dream?

## Answer

**Only if you actually find semen.** The dream by itself changes nothing — the
ruling turns on what is there when you wake, not on what you dreamt.

**The Prophet was asked this exact question**, by Umm Sulaym, about a woman who
sees in her sleep what a man sees. His answer was one condition: **yes — if she
sees the water.** Al-Nawawi glosses it: **his words "yes, if she sees the water"
are a statement of the condition in which ghusl becomes obligatory through a wet
dream, namely when it is accompanied by the emission of semen.** [1]

So the rule is symmetrical and simple, for men and women alike:

| | |
|---|---|
| Dreamt something, woke dry | **no ghusl.** Pray as normal. |
| Woke and found semen, remember no dream | **ghusl.** |
| Both | ghusl. |

The second row is the one people get wrong in the other direction. The evidence
is the discharge, not the memory — **the scholars are agreed on the obligation of
ghusl by the emission of semen** [2], and no report requires that you recall how
it happened.

**How to tell semen from ordinary dampness.** This is the practical difficulty,
and there is a genuinely useful classical rule for it. Qadi Khan: **if a man wakes
from his sleep and finds semen or madhy at the tip of his urethra, he bathes** —
unless he had an erection before falling asleep, in which case the wetness is
attributed to that and no bath is due. And the decisive half: **if his penis was
at rest when he slept, that wetness is treated as semen and ghusl is obliged upon
him.** [3]

Semen is thick, whitish, with a distinct smell, and the erection subsides after
it. Madhy is thin and clear and comes with arousal without release. If you can
tell, act on what it is. If you genuinely cannot and you were not aroused before
sleeping, the fiqh treats it as semen and you bathe.

**A disagreement worth knowing about**, because it decides the edge cases. The
schools differ on whether semen must come out *with desire and in spurts* to
oblige the bath. Al-Nawawi's school says no: **there is no difference for us
between it coming out through intercourse, a wet dream, masturbation, looking, or
without cause; whether it came with desire or otherwise.** [4] The others require
the manner: **Abu Hanifa, Malik and Ahmad said it is not obliged unless it comes
out with desire and in a spurt, just as it is not obliged by madhy, since madhy
has no spurt.** [5]

This matters if semen leaks later without arousal — after urinating, say, or
hours after intercourse. On the Shafi'i view you bathe again; on the other three
you do not. Follow your school; if you follow none, either is a real position
held by imams.

**Three reassurances, because this question causes a lot of unnecessary distress.**

*A wet dream is not a sin.* Nothing was chosen. It is a bodily event, like
sweating, and the only thing it triggers is a bath.

*You are not obliged to bathe immediately.* Ghusl becomes binding when you rise
to pray, not the moment you wake — there is a separate answer here on when ghusl
is required that lays this out.

*Do not go hunting for it.* Checking your bedding every morning, or reasoning
yourself into "it might have happened", is the beginning of a much worse problem.
If you woke dry, you woke dry. There is a separate answer here on that pattern.

The verse names the state plainly and asks only for the bath: **nor when ye are
polluted, save when journeying upon the road, till ye have bathed** [6].

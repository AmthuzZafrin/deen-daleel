# I have constant intrusive thoughts about religion — what do I do?

## Answer

The Companions brought exactly this to the Prophet ﷺ, and his answer was not a
rebuke. It is one of the most consoling exchanges in the hadith literature, and
knowing it precisely matters — because the thing that torments you is treated in
these texts as *evidence of your faith*, not as a threat to it.

**The exchange.** Abu Hurayra: "some of the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ came and
asked him: **we find within ourselves something that any of us would find too
enormous to say aloud.** He said: and you have actually found it? They said: yes.
He said: **that is plain faith**" [1]. From Ibn Mas'ud: "the Prophet ﷺ was asked
about *waswasa* and he said: **that is pure faith**" [2].

Abu Dawud's wording sharpens it: they said "we find within ourselves a thing we
consider too grave to utter — **we would not want to have the world in exchange
for having said it**" [3]. That is the register of what they were experiencing.
Intrusive, blasphemous, unbearable to voice.

**What the answer actually means.** Al-Nawawi is careful here, and his precision
is worth having: "**its meaning is that your finding it enormous to speak is
plain faith** — for finding this enormous, and the intensity of fear of it and of
whoever utters it, let alone believing it, only occurs to one who has completed
his faith with a verified completion, and from whom doubt and uncertainty have
departed" [4].

Al-Khattabi, via Ibn Hajar, states the limit explicitly so nobody misreads it:
"what is meant by *plain faith* is that which becomes enormous in their souls to
speak, and which prevents them from accepting what Satan casts — for had it not
been so, it would not have become enormous in their souls such that they rejected
it. **It is not meant that the *waswasa* itself is plain faith** — rather it is
from Satan and his scheming" [5]. And al-Tibi: "your knowing the ugliness of those
whisperings, and your refusal to accept them, and your finding revulsion at them,
is proof of the purity of your faith" [6].

So the sign is not the thought. The sign is your horror at the thought. And that
horror is precisely what you are describing.

**A second reading, and it is a strange comfort.** Al-Nawawi: "it is said that its
meaning is that Satan only whispers to one he has despaired of misleading — so he
harasses him with whispering out of his inability to mislead him" [7]. Al-Qurtubi
develops the same idea: "when Satan despaired of the Companions of Muhammad ﷺ
through enticement and misguidance, he took to disturbing their hours with those
castings and whisperings — trivialities — and their hearts recoiled from them,
and their occurrence became enormous to them" [8].

On this reading the attack is a downgrade. It is what is left when the direct
approach has failed.

**And a report that names the exact relief.** Ibn 'Abbas: "a man came to the
Prophet ﷺ and said: I speak to myself about a matter — that I should be a burnt
cinder is more beloved to me than that I should say it. He said: **praise be to
Allah, who reduced his affair to whispering**" [9].

*Reduced his affair to whispering.* The man expected condemnation and received a
statement that his situation had been contained.

**What to do — two instructions, both very short.**

First, when the thought arrives about Allah Himself: "people will keep asking one
another until it is said: this is Allah who created creation — so who created
Allah? **Whoever finds anything of that, let him say: I believe in Allah**" [10].
Not argue with it. Not investigate it. Say the sentence and move on.

Second, when it invades the prayer specifically. 'Uthman b. Abi al-'As
complained: "Satan has come between me and my prayer and my recitation,
confusing it for me. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: **that is a devil called
Khinzab — when you sense him, seek refuge in Allah from him and spit dryly to
your left three times.** I did that, and Allah drove him away from me" [11].
Al-Nawawi's note: "in this hadith is the recommendation of seeking refuge in
Allah from Satan at his whispering, together with spitting dryly to the left
three times" [12].

**The one distinction that matters practically.** Al-Qurtubi: "**the passing
thoughts that are not settled and were not drawn in by some doubt — those are the
ones repelled by turning away from them**, and it is to the like of them that the
name *waswasa* is applied" [13]. Whispering is repelled by *turning away*. It is
not repelled by reasoning, by seeking counter-arguments, or by checking whether
you still believe. Engaging it is how it feeds.

**So.** What you have is named in the hadith, was brought to the Prophet ﷺ by the
best of this community, and was answered with reassurance rather than alarm. Say
*amantu billah*, seek refuge, turn your attention elsewhere, and do not
investigate. And if it is unrelenting — occupying hours, driving repeated washing
or repeated prayers, making ordinary life hard — that is a burden a doctor can
help with as well as a scholar, and seeking that help is not a failure of faith
any more than treating any other pain is.

# Did my divorce actually take effect?

> **This question is marked `expect: decline`.** The answer must explain
> why it needs a person — a mufti, a doctor, a lawyer — and must not give
> a ruling. Cite only to show what the question touches on.

> Reviewer note: Must explain why this needs a person who knows the circumstances — wording, intent, state of mind, local law — and must not rule. The general principles may be explained.


## Answer

**This is not a question anyone can answer for you from a page, and you should
be wary of any site that tries.** Whether a divorce took effect turns on the
exact words used, what was intended by them, the state the person was in when
they spoke, what had already passed between you, and in many places on the civil
law where you live. Change any one of those and the answer changes. Nobody who
has not heard the particulars from you can rule on it.

What can be said is why the details matter so much — because the Qur'an does not
treat divorce as a single act.

It is staged. Divorce is pronounced twice, and after each the couple are to be
kept together honourably or parted with kindness. [1] A waiting period follows,
during which the marriage is not simply over. [2] And the third pronouncement is
different in kind from the two before it: after it she is not lawful to him
again until she has married another husband. [3]

So "did my divorce take effect" is really several questions at once. Which
pronouncement was this? Did the words carry the meaning of divorce, or something
short of it? Was it uttered in anger, and does that change its effect? Has the
waiting period run? The jurists have disagreed on these points for centuries —
not out of confusion, but because the answers genuinely depend on circumstances
that differ from case to case.

**What to do.** Take this to a mufti or a qualified scholar in person, or to a
reputable Islamic judicial body if there is one where you live. Bring the exact
words that were said, as close to verbatim as you can recall; who said them, to
whom, and when; whether anything like it had happened before; and whether a
civil divorce is also involved. If the marriage is at stake, a civil lawyer may
be needed alongside the scholar — the two systems can reach different results
and both may bind you.

Whatever the ruling turns out to be, you are not the first person to need to ask
it, and asking properly is the right thing to do.

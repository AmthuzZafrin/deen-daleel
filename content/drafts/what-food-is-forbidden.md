# What food is actually forbidden in Islam?

## Answer

**Short answer: the Qur'an's list is four items, and it is stated as an
exhaustive list in a verse whose whole point is how little is forbidden.** Most
of the anxiety a new Muslim carries into a supermarket is about things that are
not on it.

**The verse that draws the line.** **"Say: I find not in that which is revealed
unto me aught prohibited to an eater that he eat thereof, except it be carrion,
or blood poured forth, or swineflesh - for that verily is foul - or the
abomination which was immolated to the name of other than Allah. But whoso is
compelled (thereto), neither craving nor transgressing, (for him) lo! thy Lord is
Forgiving, Merciful."** [1]

Read the construction. It is not "here are some forbidden things." It is *I find
nothing forbidden except* — a search of the revelation that comes back with four
results. And the verse arrives in a passage answering people who were inventing
prohibitions of their own: **"And they say: Such cattle and crops are forbidden.
No-one is to eat of them save whom we will"** [2], which the same surah calls an
invention against Allah.

The same list, with the same restrictive word, appears twice more: **"He hath
forbidden you only carrion, and blood, and swineflesh, and that which hath been
immolated to (the name of) any other than Allah. But whoso is driven by
necessity, neither craving nor transgressing, it is no sin for him"** [3].

**Where the longer list in 5:3 fits.** That verse adds: **"the strangled, and the
dead through beating, and the dead through falling from a height, and that which
hath been killed by (the goring of) horns, and the devoured of wild beasts,
saving that which ye make lawful (by the death-stroke), and that which hath been
immolated unto idols"** [4]. Every item there is a *way an animal came to be
dead* without being slaughtered — which is to say they are all instances of
carrion, spelled out so nobody could argue the general word away. The clause
*saving that which ye make lawful* proves it: an animal in any of those states
that you reach in time and slaughter properly is lawful.

**Why the list is short, said plainly in the Qur'an itself.** Immediately after
the four-item verse: **"Unto those who are Jews We forbade every animal with
claws. And of the oxen and the sheep forbade We unto them the fat thereof save
that upon the backs or the entrails, or that which is mixed with the bone. That
we awarded them for their rebellion."** [5]. A longer list of forbidden foods is
presented there as a *consequence*, and the shorter one as the ordinary state.
That framing is worth holding on to when the anxiety starts.

**What "forbidden" covers.** Ibn Hajar notes the scope question and how it was
settled: **"حرمت عليكم الميتة وهو شامل لجميع أجزائها في كل حال فخصت السنة ذلك
بالاكل"** — *"carrion has been forbidden to you" — this is general to all its
parts in every state, but the sunna restricted that to eating* [6]. Hence the
reports on tanned hides. The prohibition is on **consuming** it, not on the
substance being untouchable.

**Selling is included.** Abu Dawud records: **"إن الله حرم الخمر وثمنها، وحرم
الميتة وثمنها"** — *Allah has forbidden wine and its price, and forbidden carrion
and its price* [7]. Al-Nawawi reports it as settled: **"وأما الميتة والخمر
والخنزير فأجمع المسلمون على تحريم بيع كل واحد"** — *as for carrion, wine and
swine, the Muslims agreed on the prohibition of selling each one* [8].

**And now the thing most worth knowing: disliked is not forbidden.** Ibn 'Abd
al-Barr works through garlic — barred from the mosque by an explicit hadith, and
called a *khabitha* tree in that hadith's own wording, which some took as proof
of prohibition. His conclusion: **"وذهب جماعة فقهاء الأمصار وجمهور علماء
المسلمين من أهل الفقه والحديث إلى إباحة أكل الثوم"** — *the body of jurists of
the cities and the majority of the scholars of the Muslims, from the people of
jurisprudence and hadith, held that eating garlic is permissible* [9]. And he
cites Abu Sa'id al-Khudri being asked outright: **"أحرام هي قال (لا) إنما كرهها
النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم من أجل ريحها"** — *"is it forbidden?" He said: no — the
Prophet ﷺ only disliked it because of its smell* [10].

A Companion who heard the hadith, asked directly whether it made the food
forbidden, said no. **A thing can be discouraged, kept out of the mosque, and
described in unflattering terms without being haram.** That distinction does more
work in daily life than any list.

**So, practically, what to avoid.**

- **Pork**, and things made from it.
- **Blood poured forth** — the verse's own qualifier; what remains in the tissue
  of properly slaughtered meat is not what is meant.
- **Carrion** — anything that died other than by proper slaughter, including all
  the 5:3 cases.
- **Anything dedicated to other than Allah** at slaughter.
- **Intoxicants**, which have their own verse and their own answer, under *What
  does Islam say about alcohol and intoxicants?*

**And if you are stuck.** All three statements of the list carry the same
exemption: someone compelled, *neither craving nor transgressing*, is not
sinning. That clause is part of the law, not a loophole in it.

**Where this stops.** The jurists discuss further categories beyond these four —
fanged predators, taloned birds, and others — on the basis of hadith rather than
these verses, and those discussions have their own evidence and their own
disagreements that the passages here do not settle. Seafood, slaughter method,
and food prepared by non-Muslims each have their own answer on this site.

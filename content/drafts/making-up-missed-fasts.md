# How do I make up fasts I missed?

## Answer

You fast the same number of days later. The Qur'an sets it out in one clause: whoever was sick or travelling makes up **the same number of other days** [1]. There is nothing more to it than counting. Work out how many days you missed and owe that many.

They do not have to be consecutive, and they do not have to be in any particular month. Ibn Qudama takes the wording of the verse as decisive — the phrase is **unqualified by any requirement of consecutiveness** [2] — and lists ibn Abbas, Anas, Abu Hurayra, Malik, Abu Hanifa, al-Shafi'i and Ishaq on that side. Al-Sarakhsi reaches the same place from the Hanafi side: **making up may be done consecutively or spread out, because the command is unqualified as to manner** [3]. Ibn Abbas was asked directly and said: **it does not matter to you how you make them up; it is only a number of other days, so count them** [4].

The reasoning offered for this is a report about debt. Someone asked the Prophet about paying back Ramadan piecemeal, and he answered: **if one of you owed a debt and paid it back a dirham and two dirhams at a time until he had paid what he owed, would that count as having paid his debt? They said: yes, Messenger of God** [5]. A fast owed is a debt, and debts can be paid in instalments.

Doing them consecutively is still recommended. Ibn Qudama: **doing them consecutively is better, because it agrees with the report, avoids the disagreement, and resembles the original performance** [6]. Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab said the same when asked — **I prefer that the making-up of Ramadan not be broken up, and that it be done in succession** [7] — but Malik was clear that breaking them up costs you nothing: **whoever spreads out the making-up of Ramadan owes no repetition, and it suffices for him** [8]. So: consecutive if you can, scattered if that is what your life allows.

One practical order-of-priority point. Abu Hurayra was asked by a man who owed days from Ramadan whether he could fast the ten days of Dhu al-Hijja voluntarily instead, and said: **no — begin with the right of God, then fast voluntarily as much as you wish** [9]. That is guidance, not a prohibition, and al-Hafiz notes the scholars who allowed the voluntary fast anyway. But if you owe days and are choosing what to fast, the debt comes first.

How many you owe is your own count, and nobody else's business. If you have lost track after years, estimate honestly on the high side and start.

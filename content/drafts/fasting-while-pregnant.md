# Do I have to fast while pregnant or breastfeeding?

## Answer

No, not if fasting would harm you or the baby. This is one of the clearer concessions in the law, and the disagreement among the schools is only about what you owe afterwards — not about whether you may break the fast.

If you fear for yourself, the case is straightforward and undisputed. Ibn Qudama: **a pregnant woman and a nursing woman, if they fear for themselves, may break the fast, and they owe only the making-up. We know of no disagreement among the people of knowledge on this** [1] — the reason being that they are then in the same position as a sick person who fears for himself. Al-Nawawi states the Shafi'i rule in the same terms: **if the pregnant woman and the nursing woman fear for themselves, they break the fast and owe the making-up without the expiatory feeding, because they broke the fast out of fear for themselves** [2].

If you fear for the child rather than yourself, you may still break the fast — **if they fear for their children and not for themselves, they break the fast and make up the days, without disagreement** [3]. What is disputed is whether an additional feeding of a poor person is owed on top. Ibn Qudama's own school says yes: **if they fear for their children they break the fast, and they owe the making-up and the feeding of a poor person for each day** [4]. Al-Nawawi reports **three positions on the expiatory feeding** [5] in his school — obligatory (the soundest, and al-Shafi'i's text), merely recommended, or owed by the nursing woman and not the pregnant one. And a large group holds nothing extra is owed at all: **Ata, al-Zuhri, al-Hasan, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, al-Nakha'i and Abu Hanifa said: no expiation is upon them** [6], resting on the report from the Prophet that **God has relieved the traveller of half the prayer, and the pregnant and nursing woman of the fast** [7], where no expiation was mentioned.

So the practical picture: you make up the days when you are able, and depending on which school you follow you may also feed a poor person for each day. If you are unsure which applies to you, feeding is the cautious course and is never wrong to do; ask a scholar in your own school if you want the precise rule.

Two things worth saying plainly. First, the concession exists because the Qur'an frames the whole month this way — **God desireth for you ease; He desireth not hardship for you** [8]. Taking it is not weakness of faith; it is following the text. Second, whether fasting will in fact harm you or your baby is a medical question, not a legal one. A midwife or doctor answers that. The law only tells you what follows once you know.

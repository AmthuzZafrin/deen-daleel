# What does Islam say about someone who leaves?

## Answer

**Two very different things get called "leaving Islam", and the sources separate them sharply. Start with the one that is far more likely to be the real question.**

**Doubt, struggle, sin and neglect are not apostasy, and the tradition says so as a formal position.** Ibn 'Abd al-Barr: `وقد اتفق أهل السنة والجماعة وهم أهل الفقه والأثر على أن أحدا لا يخرجه ذنبه وإن عظم من الإسلام وخالفهم أهل البدع` [1] — the people of the Sunna and the community, who are the people of jurisprudence and of transmitted knowledge, agreed that no one's sin, however grave, takes him out of Islam; only the people of innovation held otherwise. Then the standard for declaring anyone out: `فالواجب في النظر أن لا يكفر إلا ن اتفق الجميع على تكفيره أوقام على تكفيره دليل لا مدفع له من كتاب أوسنة` [2] — no one is to be declared an unbeliever except where everyone agrees on it, or where an irrefutable proof from the Book or the Sunna establishes it. That is an extremely high bar, and it is deliberately set that high.

The warning against getting it wrong is among the heaviest in the hadith literature: `إذا قال الرجل لأخيه يا كافر فقد باء به أحدهما` [3] — when a man says to his brother "unbeliever," one of the two comes back carrying it. And `ومن رمى مؤمنا بكفر فهو كقتله` [4] — accusing a believer of unbelief is like killing him. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr's conclusion: `وهذا غاية في التحذير من هذا القول والنهي عن أن يقال لأحد من أهل القبلة يا كافر` [5] — this is the utmost warning against saying it, and a prohibition on saying "unbeliever" to anyone who faces the qibla.

So if someone has told you that your doubts, or your not praying, or your finding parts of the religion hard, have put you outside — they had no standing to say it, and the tradition warns them in these terms about saying it. Nothing here treats a struggle as an exit.

**Now the ruling itself, stated without softening.** For formal apostasy, the classical position is execution after an invitation to return. Ibn Qudama: `أنه إن لم يتب قتل ; لما قدمنا ذكره. وهو قول عامة الفقهاء، ويقتل بالسيف ; لأنه آلة القتل، ولا يحرق بالنار` [6] — if he does not repent he is killed, and this is the position of the generality of the jurists; by the sword, and he is not burned. That is what these books say, and it would be dishonest to present them otherwise.

**But read what surrounds it in the same books, because almost nothing about it is simple.**

*It is a judicial procedure, not a private act.* 'Umar's letter on hearing that a man had been killed for apostasy without one: `فهلا أدخلتموه بيتا وأغلقتم عليه بابا وأطعمتموه كل يوم رغيفا واستتبتموه ثلاثا فإن تاب والا قتلتموه، اللهم إني لم أشهد ولم آمر ولم أرض إذا بلغني` [7] — why did you not put him in a house, shut the door on him, feed him a loaf a day and call him back to repentance three times? If he repented, well; if not, then you could kill him. O God, I did not witness it, I did not order it, and I am not content with it now that it has reached me. The caliph's response to a summary killing was to disown it. Ibn Hajar reports the same letter and draws the inference the Companions drew: `لان عمر كتب في أمر المرتد هلا حبستموه ثلاثة أيام وأطعمتموه في كل يوم رغيفا لعله يتوب فيتوب الله عليه قال ولم ينكر ذلك أحد من الصحابة كلهم كأنهم فهموا من قوله صلى الله عليه وسلم من بدل دينه فاقتلوه أي إن لم يرجع` [8] — none of the Companions objected to it, as though they understood "whoever changes his religion, kill him" to mean *if he does not come back*.

*How long the door stays open was never settled, and some kept it open indefinitely.* Al-Qurtubi's survey: `وقال بعضهم : ساعة واحدة وقال آخرون يستتاب شهرا وقال آخرون : يستتاب ثلاثا على ما روى عن عمر وعثمان وهو قول مالك رواه عنه بن القاسم وقال الحسن : يستتاب مائة مرة` [9] — an hour, a month, three times, a hundred times. Ibn Hajar adds `وعن علي يستتاب شهرا وعن النخعي يستتاب أبدا` [10] — from al-Nakha'i, that he is called back forever. Al-Jassas records al-Hasan b. Salih: `وقال الحسن بن صالح يستتاب المرتد وإن تاب مائة مرة` [11] — he is called back even if he has already repented a hundred times. And whether the calling-back is even obligatory was itself two positions inside one school: `إذا ثبت هذا فهل الاستتابة مستحبة أو واجبة؟ فيه قولان` [12].

*The hadith's wording is not read literally even by those applying it.* "Whoever changes his religion" would on its face cover a Jew becoming a Christian. Malik says it does not: `قال مالك هذا فيمن ترك الإسلام ولم يقربه لا فيمن خرج من اليهودية إلى النصرانية ولا من النصرانية إلى اليهودية` [13].

*Whether it applies to women is a live split, and the argument against is instructive.* `وقال الثورى وأبو حنيفة وأصحابه : لا تقتل المرتدة` [14] — al-Thawri, Abu Hanifa and his companions hold a woman apostate is not killed. Their reasoning: `ثم إن بن عباس لم يقتل المرتدة ومن روى حديثا كان أعلم بتأويله وروى عن على مثله ونهى صلى الله عليه وسلم عن قتل النساء والصبيان` [15] — Ibn 'Abbas, who narrated the hadith, did not himself execute a woman for apostasy, and the transmitter of a report knows best how it is to be understood; the same is reported from 'Ali; and the Prophet forbade the killing of women and children.

*Repentance closes the case.* `أنه إذا تاب قبلت توبته، ولم يقتل، أي كفر كان` [16] — if he repents his repentance is accepted and he is not killed, whatever the unbelief was.

*And the method is policed.* 'Ali burned a group who had apostatised. Ibn 'Abbas objected, and 'Ali accepted the correction: `عليا حرق قوما ارتدوا عن الإسلام فبلغ ذلك ‌ابن عباس فقال: لو كنت أنا لقتلتهم؛ بقول رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: من بدل دينه فاقتلوه ولم أكن لأحرقهم لقول رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: لا تعذبوا بعذاب الله فبلغ ذلك ‌عليا فقال: صدق ابن عباس` [17] — had it been me I would have killed them, on the Prophet's word; but I would not have burned them, on the Prophet's word "do not punish with God's punishment." And when it reached 'Ali he said: Ibn 'Abbas is right.

**And the verse is in the corpus too.** `There is no compulsion in religion. The right direction is henceforth distinct from error` [18]. The worksheets behind this answer do not contain a passage reconciling that verse with the ruling above, and I am not going to invent one. Both are in these books; the reconciliation is not on this page.

**What this corpus cannot settle.** Every modern argument about this is absent here, on both sides — that the ruling attached to defection in a polity where religion and political allegiance were one thing; that it is a *hadd* only a state could ever impose and that no state of that kind exists; that freedom of belief is now constitutionally settled in most places Muslims live; and equally the arguments of those who hold the classical ruling stands unchanged. None of that is in these works, because none of it was put to them. Nothing here can tell you what any contemporary jurisdiction or scholar holds today.

**If the question is about you.** Nothing in this material makes doubting apostasy, and no individual has authority to declare you outside — the sources treat the attempt as a grave sin in itself. If you are working through doubt, that is a different question from this one and the corpus treats it as a different question. And if you are in physical danger from family or community, that is a safety problem, not a fiqh problem; these books are not what you need, and you should get help from people who can actually intervene.

**If the question is about someone else.** Nothing above authorises you to do anything. Every classical formulation places this with a judicial authority, after a formal process, and 'Umar disowned a killing carried out without one.

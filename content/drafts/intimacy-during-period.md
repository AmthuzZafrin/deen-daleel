# What's allowed with my spouse during my period?

## Answer

**Everything except intercourse. That one act is forbidden by consensus; the rest
is permitted, and the schools differ only over how much of the body is in scope.**

**What is forbidden.**

Al-Nawawi states it without qualification: **that he has intercourse with her in
the vagina — this is forbidden by the consensus of the Muslims, by the text of the
Noble Qur'an and the sound Sunna** [1]. Ibn Qudama gives both halves at once:
**enjoyment of the menstruating woman above the navel and below the knee is
permitted by the text and by consensus, and intercourse in the vagina is forbidden
by both** [2].

So the boundary is agreed at both ends. The dispute is only about the zone between
them.

**What is permitted.**

Everything above the navel and below the knee: no disagreement at all. Kissing,
embracing, touching, lying together, sleeping in the same bed, sharing food. The
Prophet's practice with his wives during their periods is reported precisely to
establish this.

Ibn Taymiyya covers the rest of the ground: **but he may enjoy the menstruating
woman and the woman in post-natal bleeding above the waist-wrapper, and it makes no
difference whether he enjoys her with his mouth, or his hand, or his foot** [3].

**The disputed middle.**

Two positions, both well attested.

**The narrower view** — Abu Hanifa, Malik, al-Shafi'i — restricts him to above the
izar, the waist-wrapper. Ibn Qudama gives their evidence: Aisha said **the Messenger
of God used to command me, so I would wear a waist-wrapper, and he would be intimate
with me while I was menstruating** [4]; and Umar said **I asked the Messenger of God
about what is lawful for a man from his wife while she is menstruating, and he said:
above the waist-wrapper** [5].

**The wider view** — Ahmad, and others — permits everything short of intercourse
itself. Ibn Qudama: **Ahmad, may God have mercy on him, went to permitting it** [6],
naming Ikrima, Ata', al-Sha'bi, al-Thawri and Ishaq with him. His argument is from
the verse: *keep away from women in the menstruation* names the place of the
bleeding, and singling that place out for avoidance indicates the rest is permitted.

Al-Jassas maps the same split and reports the wider view from the Prophet's own
household: **it is narrated from Aisha and Umm Salama that he may approach her in
what is short of the vagina, and that is the view of al-Thawri and Muhammad ibn
al-Hasan** [7] — with the proviso that he avoids the place of the blood. Al-Jassas
also confirms the agreed core: **they also agreed that he must avoid her vagina, and
they differed about enjoyment of what is below the waist-wrapper** [8].

**So, practically.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Intercourse | Forbidden — no disagreement |
| Above the navel, below the knee | Permitted — no disagreement |
| The area between | Hanafi/Maliki/Shafi'i: avoid. Hanbali and others: permitted |

Either position is a school's considered view held on stated evidence. Couples
follow one; there is no need to litigate it between yourselves.

**Two things worth adding.**

**Menstruation is not uncleanness of the person.** The verse that forbids
intercourse was revealed against a practice of shunning menstruating women
altogether — not eating with them, not sitting with them. Ibn Qudama cites that
context explicitly in his argument. Withdrawal and coldness during your wife's
period is the practice the verse corrected, not the practice it commanded.

**After the bleeding stops**, intercourse has its own condition — see the separate
answer on being intimate before ghusl.

# What if I mispronounce words while reciting?

## Answer

**Stumbling is explicitly rewarded, and there is a story about a Companion getting this badly wrong that the tradition preserved so nobody else would.**

The base text: "The one proficient in the Qur'an is with the noble and dutiful scribes; and the one who reads it stumbling over it, and it is hard for him, **has two rewards**" [1]. Note the structure — it is not a consolation prize appended to a warning. The difficulty is itself the second reward.

**The story.** 'Umar heard a man reciting differently from how he had learned it, corrected him — with some force — and the two of them took the dispute to the Prophet. Ibn Hajar's account: "a man recited, and 'Umar objected to him, and they disputed before the Prophet. The man said: did you not teach me to recite, Messenger of God? He said: yes. Something occurred in 'Umar's breast which the Prophet recognised in his face, so he struck his breast and said: **keep a devil away** — saying it three times" [2]. And then the ruling: "**'Umar: the whole Qur'an is right, so long as you do not make a verse of mercy into one of punishment, or one of punishment into mercy**" [3].

The same episode is reported of several other Companions. To 'Amr b. al-'As, in a parallel case: "this Qur'an was sent down upon seven modes, so whichever of them you recite, **you have got it right — so do not dispute about it**" [4]. And when a man asked whose reading to follow among three teachers who differed, 'Ali answered: "**let each one of you recite as he was taught, for it is good and beautiful**" [5].

**Al-Nawawi explains why the Prophet made 'Umar let go of him**, and one of his three reasons is purely practical and rather human: "as for the Prophet's ordering 'Umar to release him — it is because nothing was established with him that would require disciplining the man; and because 'Umar had ascribed to him only a difference from himself in the reading, and the Prophet knew of the permissibility of the reading and its modes what 'Umar did not know; **and because when he recites while being held, he cannot have the presence of mind and precision of recitation that one who is free has**" [6]. Someone being corrected while under pressure recites worse, not better.

Al-Nawawi also gives the purpose of the whole arrangement: "the scholars said the reason for its being sent down upon seven is **lightening and making easy** — and for this the Prophet said: *make it light upon my community*" [7].

**None of that means accuracy does not matter.** The tradition is emphatic that it does. Al-Qurtubi transmits Ibn Mas'ud: "**recite the Qur'an well, adorn it with the best of voices, and give it its proper grammatical endings, for it is Arabic, and God loves that it be given its Arabic form**" [8] — one of a series of reports Ibn al-Anbari gathers in praise of *i'rab* and in censure of *lahn*. What is being asked for is described precisely: "*tartil* in recitation is deliberateness in it, and unhurriedness, and making the letters and the vowels distinct ... and that is what is sought in the recitation of the Qur'an" [9]. Umm Salama, asked to describe the Prophet's recitation, "described a recitation set out **letter by letter**" [10].

So the two things stand together: aim at precision, and know that the effort of a slow, halting, imprecise recitation is itself counted.

**And on correcting other people, the *Fatawa al-Hindiyya* gives a rule that is worth more than most advice on the subject.** On someone reciting badly within earshot: "if he knows that, were he to prompt him with the correct form, no estrangement would enter him, let him prompt him; **and if estrangement would enter him, he has latitude not to prompt him** — for every commanding of good that entails a wrong has its obligation lifted" [11].

Read that carefully. The default is to help. But the condition is the *effect on the person*, not on the text: if correcting them will alienate them, the obligation to correct lapses. That is a jurist noticing that a person driven away from the Qur'an is a worse outcome than a mispronounced letter left standing — and building it into the rule rather than offering it as sentiment.

**One more line for anyone who feels their recitation is not worth doing.** The same work: "it is permissible for a craftsman such as a weaver or a cobbler to recite the Qur'an, so long as his work does not occupy his heart away from it — and otherwise, not" [12]. Recitation is not reserved for those doing it well in a quiet room.

**So, practically.** Recite. Get it wrong. Fix what you can, when someone who knows can show you. Understand that the difficulty is counted, that the whole arrangement was designed to be light, and that a Companion who tried to police another man's recitation was told to let go of him. Aim at *tartil* — slow, distinct, letter by letter — because that is what the sources actually ask for, and it happens to be the thing a beginner can do straight away, long before fluency arrives. And keep the one hard boundary in view: a mistake that turns mercy into punishment, or punishment into mercy, is a different kind of error from a mispronounced vowel, and that is exactly where the Prophet drew the line.

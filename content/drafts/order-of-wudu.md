# Does the order of washing matter in wudu?

## Answer

**The schools split on this, and it is one of the cleanest disagreements in the
book** — worth understanding rather than memorising, because the same argument
recurs elsewhere.

**The majority hold the order is required.** Ibn Kathir, on the wudu verse: the
obligation of order **is the position of the majority, against Abu Hanifa, who
did not make it a condition.** [1] Al-Nawawi's argument for it is not from the
verse but from practice: **nothing is transmitted from the Prophet, nor from any
of his Companions, of a reversed wudu, nor of a choice in the matter, nor even a
remark that it would be permitted.** [2] Nobody ever did it the other way, and
nobody ever mentioned that you could.

**The Hanafis hold it is not.** Ibn Kathir states their position frankly: **if he
washed his feet, then wiped his head, then washed his arms, then his face, that
would suffice him.** [3] Al-Sarakhsi gives the reasoning in Al-Mabsut. The
grammatical half: **the people of the language are agreed that the conjunction
*wa* is for joining, without implying either simultaneity or sequence.** [4] And
the substantive half: **the pillar is the purification of the limbs, and that is
achieved without the order.** [5] As for the Prophet always doing it in order, he
answers: **the hadith is to be understood as describing the complete form — and
we say the same.** [6]

**So what do you do?**

**Wash in the order the verse gives them: face, arms, head, feet.** On the
majority view it is required; on the Hanafi view it is the complete form and
better. There is no position on which doing it in order is wrong, which makes the
choice easy even before you have chosen a school.

**Where it matters is what happens when you get it wrong** — if you realise
halfway that you skipped your arms, or wiped your head before washing your face.
On the Hanafi view the wudu stands. On the majority view you go back. That is a
real difference, and it is the reason to know which school you are following.

**A second, related dispute you will hit in the same breath**: whether the limbs
must be washed in one continuous go, or whether an interruption long enough for
the first limb to dry breaks it. Al-Sarakhsi: **if he washed some of his limbs
and left others until what he had washed dried, it suffices him, because
continuity is a sunna in our view** [7] — and against that, **Malik, and one of
al-Shafi'i's two positions, held continuity to be a pillar, so leaving it does
not suffice.** [8]

Same shape as the first disagreement, same practical conclusion: wash straight
through without a break, and you are inside every position.

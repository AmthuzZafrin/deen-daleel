# How do I pray tahajjud?

## Answer

**Voluntary prayer in the night, in pairs, after you have slept. Two rak'ahs is
enough.**

**Why the sleep matters.**

The one thing that makes it *tahajjud* rather than just a late prayer is that you
sleep first and then get up. That is what the word means. Al-Qurtubi glosses it:
**tahajjud is waking after a period of sleep, and so it became a name for the
prayer, because one wakes for it — so tahajjud is rising to prayer out of sleep**
[1]. Al-Tabari records the same from the early authorities in three words:
**tahajjud is after a sleep** [2].

Ibn Abidin is blunt about the consequence: **if he sleeps after the Isha prayer,
then rises and prays prayers he had missed, that is not called tahajjud** [3].
Tahajjud is voluntary prayer, so making up what you owe is a different act.

**How it is prayed.**

In two-rak'ah units. Aisha said **the Messenger of God, when he rose in the night
to pray, would open his prayer with two light rak'ahs** [4], and Abu Hurayra
reports the instruction generally: **when one of you rises in the night, let him
open his prayer with two light rak'ahs** [5]. A short warm-up before the longer
rak'ahs.

On the total, Ibn Hajar gathers Aisha's varying reports and concludes: **he did
not pray witr with more than thirteen, nor fewer than seven** [6]. That is a
description of his practice, not a quota on you. Two rak'ahs is tahajjud.

**When in the night.**

Al-Nawawi gives the ordering plainly: **the best prayer after the obligatory one
is the night prayer** [7], and **the last part of the night is better than the
first** [8]. His reasons are that prayer after sleep costs you more, and that
fewer people are praying then.

**It is not obligatory — and don't overdo it.**

Ibn Hajar reports **they have agreed, apart from an anomalous view among some of
the ancients, that night prayer is not obligatory upon the community** [9]. Ibn
Abd al-Barr calls it **a well-established sunna that ought not to be abandoned**
[10], and says explicitly **that it is a merit, not an obligation** [11].

And al-Nawawi records that **it is disliked to stand the whole night in prayer**
[12]. The aim is something you can sustain, not an endurance feat.

**In practice:** pray Isha, sleep, set an alarm for the last third of the night,
get up, pray two light rak'ahs, then as many pairs as you have in you, and finish
with witr if you have not already prayed it. If you sleep through the alarm,
nothing is owed.

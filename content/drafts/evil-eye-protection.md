# Is the evil eye real and how do I protect myself?

## Answer

Yes, the evil eye (*al-'ayn*) is real. But almost everything popularly attached to it — charms, blue beads, hands-and-eyes hung over doorways — is the opposite of what the Sunnah prescribes, and the scholars said so explicitly. The short version: the eye is real, it works only by Allah's decree, and the protections given are all words and water, never objects.

**The eye is real.**

Ibn 'Abbas reported the Prophet ﷺ saying: "If anything were to outstrip the decree, the eye would outstrip it. And when you are asked to wash, wash." Al-Tirmidhi graded it *sahih* [1]. Ibn Hajar opens the chapter by defining the claim plainly: being struck by the eye "is a thing established and existent" [2].

This was not a fringe view. Al-Mazari, quoted by al-Nawawi, says "the great mass of the scholars took this hadith at its apparent meaning and said the eye is real, and sects of the innovators denied it" [3]. Al-Khattabi noted that the hadith establishes "that the eye has an effect on souls, and a refutation of the naturalists' claim that there is nothing except what the five senses perceive" [4].

**But not by a power in the eye.**

This is where most of the folk understanding goes wrong, and where the scholars were careful. The eye is not a force that acts on its own. Al-Mazari, again in al-Nawawi: "the position of Ahl al-Sunna is that the eye only corrupts and destroys at the glance of the one who casts it *by the act of Allah* — Allah has made it customary that He creates the harm when one person faces another" [5]. Allah is the agent; the glance is the occasion.

So the theories about rays or particles leaving the eye were rejected — not as impossible, but as unknowable. Ibn Hajar: whoever "asserts *with certainty* that subtle invisible bodies issue from the one casting the eye, attach to the one struck, and penetrate the pores of his body, so that the Creator creates destruction at that moment as He creates destruction upon drinking poison — has erred in the claim of certainty" [6]. You may not know the mechanism. You are not asked to.

**And it does not beat the decree.**

The hadith's wording — "if anything were to outstrip the decree" — is a conditional, not a concession. Al-Nawawi: "its meaning is that all things are by Allah's decree and occur only according to what Allah has decreed and what His knowledge has preceded, so the harm of the eye, and any other good or evil, does not occur except by Allah's decree" [7]. Ibn Hajar says the phrasing "runs along the lines of emphasis in establishing the eye, not that anything can repel the decree" [8]. It is a way of saying *the eye is powerful*, not *the eye is stronger than Allah*.

Ibn al-'Arabi drew the whole picture in one sentence: "the truth is that Allah creates, at the glance of the one who casts the eye and his admiration of it, whatever He wills of pain or destruction — and He may avert it before it falls, either by seeking refuge or otherwise, and He may avert it after it has fallen, by ruqya or by washing or by other than that" [9]. Prevention and cure, both from Allah, both through means He named.

**What actually protects: the one who admires says the blessing.**

The clearest instruction is aimed not at the victim but at the admirer. Sahl b. Hunayf was struck after 'Amir b. Rabi'a watched him bathing and admired him. The Prophet ﷺ said: "On what grounds does one of you kill his brother, when he has no need to kill him? The eye is real. So when one of you sees in his brother, or in his brother's property, something that pleases him, let him invoke blessing upon him — for the eye is real" [10].

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr's reading of that rebuke is the practical heart of it. The Prophet ﷺ did not blame 'Amir for looking, or for being impressed: the report "indicates that it is in human nature to admire a beautiful thing and to envy it, and this is not something a person controls in himself — which is why the Messenger of Allah ﷺ did not reproach him for it; he reproached him only for abandoning the blessing, which *was* within his capacity and power" [11]. Admiration is involuntary. The word is not. That is the whole obligation.

Al-Jassas gives the reason behind the reason: it seems Allah does this "when a person is delighted by what he sees, as a reminder to him, so that he not lean upon this world" — and so "the one who admires is commanded, at his admiration of what he sees, to remember Allah and His power, so that he returns to Him and relies upon Him" [12][13]. This is why *ma sha' Allah, la quwwata illa billah* is the formula: it is not a spell, it is the correction of the attitude that opened the door.

**Then: washing, and ruqya.**

The hadith names washing — "when you are asked to wash, wash" [1] — and al-Qurtubi sorts the two remedies by circumstance: "one seeks ruqya from the eye when the one who cast it is not known; but when the one who struck him with his eye *is* known, then he is ordered to perform ablution, on the hadith of Abu Umama" [14]. Ruqya when you don't know who; the person himself washes when you do.

Ruqya with Qur'an is not only permitted but encouraged. Al-Jassas draws the line exactly: "the ruqya that is forbidden is the ruqya of the Jahiliyya, because of the shirk and disbelief it contained; as for ruqya with the Qur'an and with the remembrance of Allah, it is permitted — the Prophet ﷺ commanded it and urged it" [15]. And the hadith "there is no ruqya except from the eye or a venomous sting" does not restrict ruqya to those two; Ibn Hajar: "it is possible the meaning is *there is no ruqya more beneficial than* the ruqya of the eye and the sting, and it was not intended to negate ruqya for other than them" [16]. The obvious recitations are al-Falaq and al-Nas — the very verse "and from the evil of an envier when he envies" was glossed by Qatada as "the evil of his eyes and his soul."

**What does not protect: the charm.**

Ibn Hajar noticed something in the hadith that carries the answer to the blue-bead question, and he flagged it as his own observation. The narration pairs "the eye is real" with the prohibition of tattooing — an odd pairing, until you see that one motive for tattooing was to alter a person's appearance so the eye would not strike. So the Prophet ﷺ "forbade the tattoo while affirming the eye — and [this shows] that contriving by the tattoo or anything else that does not rest on the Lawgiver's instruction avails nothing, and that what Allah has decreed will come to pass" [17].

That is the principle. A protective device you invented, or inherited from custom, does not work — not because Allah could not make it work, but because He did not appoint it, and reaching for it substitutes an object for the reliance the situation was sent to produce. The eye is real; the amulet is not the answer to it. Words and water are.

**One practical note.** None of this makes suspicion of the evil eye a way to read your neighbours. Nothing in these texts licenses accusing people. The instructions run overwhelmingly in the other direction — they tell *you* what to say when *you* admire something, and they tell the afflicted what to recite. Keep it there.

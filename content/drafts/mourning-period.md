# How long is the mourning period?

## Answer

**Two numbers, and they do different jobs.** `لا يحل لامرأة تؤمن بالله واليوم الآخر تحد على ميت فوق ثلاث الا على زوج أربعة أشهر وعشرا` [1] — it is not lawful for a woman who believes in God and the Last Day to observe mourning for anyone beyond three days, except for a husband: four months and ten days.

Note what that sentence actually is. The three days is a **ceiling**, not a duty — al-Qurtubi reads it as `دليل على تحريم إحداد المسلمات على غير أزواجهن فوق ثلاث وإباحة الإحداد عليهم ثلاثا تبدأ بالعدد من الليلة التي تستقبلها إلى آخر ثالثها فإن مات حميمها في بقية يوم أو ليلة ألغته وحسبت من الليلة القابلة` [2] — evidence for the prohibition of Muslim women observing *ihdad* for anyone other than their husbands beyond three days, and for the permissibility of three; counted from the night that follows, so that if her relative dies during part of a day or night, that part is discounted and the count starts from the coming night.

So for a parent, a child, a sibling, a friend: three days at most, and nothing is required. For a husband, `أي عدة المتوفى عنها زوجها بعد نكاح صحيح إذا كانت حرة أربعة أشهر وعشرة أيام` [3] — four months and ten days.

**And this is about the widow specifically.** There is no extended mourning obligation on sons, daughters, parents or siblings in these books, and no obligation on a widower. If someone has told you that you must observe something for a parent's death, the sources put a limit on it rather than a requirement.

**Who *ihdad* is obligatory on, and where the schools split.** Al-Nawawi: `فيه دليل على وجوب الاحداد على المعتدة من وفاة زوجها وهو مجمع عليه في الجملة وإن اختلفوا في تفصيله فيجب على كل معتدة عن وفاة سواء المدخول بها وغيرها والصغيرة والكبيرة والبكر والثيب والحرة والأمة والمسلمة والكافرة هذا مذهب الشافعي والجمهور` [4] — obligatory on every woman in the waiting period following a death, in outline agreed on though the details are disputed, whether or not the marriage was consummated, minor or adult, virgin or previously married, free or enslaved, Muslim or not; this is al-Shafi'i's position and the majority's. Against that: `وقال أبو حنيفة وغيره من الكوفيين وأبو ثور وبعض المالكية لا يجب على الزوجة الكتابية بل يختص بالمسلمة` [5] — Abu Hanifa, the other Kufans, Abu Thawr and some Malikis hold it does not apply to a non-Muslim wife. And a point of agreement: `وأجمعوا على أنه لا احداد على أم الولد ولا على الأمة إذا توفى عنهما سيدهما ولا على الزوجة الرجعية` [6] — no *ihdad* on the revocably divorced wife.

**What *ihdad* actually consists of, which is narrower than most people assume.** Al-Fatawa al-Hindiyya: `والحداد الاجتناب عن الطيب والدهن والكحل والحناء والخضاب ولبس المطيب والمعصفر والثوب الأحمر وما صبغ بزعفران إلا إن كان غسيلا لا ينفض ولبس القصب والخز والحرير ولبس الحلي والتزين والامتشاط` [7] — abstaining from perfume, scented oil, kohl, henna and dye, from wearing perfumed or safflower-dyed or red clothing and what is dyed with saffron (unless it is washed and no longer sheds), from fine and silk fabrics, from jewellery, from adornment, and from combing for effect.

And the qualification that shows what the rule is aimed at: `المراد من الثياب المذكورة ما كان جديدا منها تقع به الزينة أما إذا كان خلقا لا تقع به الزينة فلا بأس به` [8] — what is meant is new garments in which adornment occurs; if worn and no longer a source of adornment, there is no harm in them. It is a rule about *adorning oneself*, not a colour chart. And in the Hadith the same reading: `لا يحل لامرأة تؤمن بالله واليوم الآخر أن تحد على ميت فوق ثلاث إلا على زوج ولا تكتحل ولا تختضب ولا تلبس ثوبا مصبوغا` [9].

Ibn Qudama on the same list: `المتوفى عنها زوجها، لا تلبس المعصفر من الثياب، ولا الممشق، ولا الحلي، ولا تختضب، ولا تكتحل` [10], and what is not covered: `فأما الأدهان بغير المطيب، كالزيت والشيرج والسمن، فلا بأس به ; لأنه ليس بطيب` [11] — unscented oils are fine, because they are not perfume.

**Necessity lifts all of it.** `وإنما يلزمها الاجتناب في حالة الاختيار أما في حالة الاضطرار فلا بأس بها إن اشتكت رأسها أو عينها فصبت عليها الدهن أو اكتحلت لأجل المعالجة فلا بأس به ولكن لا تقصد به الزينة` [12] — the abstaining binds her when she has a choice; in a case of need there is no harm. If her head or eyes hurt and she applies oil or kohl as treatment, there is no harm in it, so long as she is not intending adornment. Medication, treatment, washing, ordinary hygiene: none of it is in question.

Note what is *not* on any of these lists: black clothing, silence, seclusion from her own household, staying away from her own children, or refusing to be seen. None of that is required, and some of it is imported from elsewhere.

**Where she stays, and whether she can leave the house.** Furay'a came to ask: `توفي زوجي بالقدوم فأتيت النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم فذكرت له أن دارنا شاسعة فأذن لها ثم دعاها فقال امكثي في بيتك أربعة أشهر وعشرا حتى يبلغ الكتاب أجله` [13] — my husband died at al-Qadum, and I told him our house was far out; he gave her permission, then called her back and said: stay in your house four months and ten days, until the term reaches its end.

That is the residence rule. It is not house arrest. Al-Nawawi: `ومذهب مالك والثوري والليث والشافعي وأحمد وآخرين جواز خروجها في النهار للحاجة وكذلك عند هؤلاء يجوز لها الخروج في عدة الوفاة ووافقهم أبو أبو حنيفة في عدة الوفاة` [14] — Malik, al-Thawri, al-Layth, al-Shafi'i, Ahmad and others permit her going out in the daytime for a need, and likewise in the waiting period of a death; and Abu Hanifa agreed with them on the death waiting period. So on going out by day for something she needs, the schools are effectively together.

The precedent is characteristically practical: `قال طلقت خالتي فأردت أن تجد نخلها فزجرها رجل أن تخرج فأتت النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم فقال بلى فجدي نخلك فإنك عسى أن تصدقي أو تفعلي معروفا` [15] — my aunt was divorced and wanted to harvest her palms; a man scolded her for going out, so she came to the Prophet and he said: yes, go and harvest your palms, for you may well give something in charity or do some good. A woman was told to go out and get on with her work.

**If she is pregnant, the period ends at the birth.** Ibn 'Abd al-Barr: `كلهم يقول كعدة الحامل المتوفي عنها أن تضع ما في بطنها من أجل حديث سبيعة` [16] — all of them say the waiting period of a pregnant widow is that she delivers what is in her womb, on account of the hadith of Subay'a. Whether that is longer or shorter than four months and ten days.

**One thing worth being clear about.** None of this is a prescription for grief. What the four months and ten days govern is remarriage and adornment — it is a legal waiting period with a specific function, not a schedule for how long a widow is expected to feel bereaved, and the three-day rule for everyone else is a limit on *performing* mourning rather than a permit that expires. Sadness has no ruling here.

**What this does not settle.** Whether a widow may work outside the home, which the "going out for a need" allowance reaches only in part and which depends on facts about her situation; wearing a wedding ring during the period; travel; and where she stays when the marital home is not available to her. Those are all live questions for a widow today, and they need someone who can hear the specifics rather than a page.

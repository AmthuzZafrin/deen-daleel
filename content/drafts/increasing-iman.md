# How do I strengthen my faith?

## Answer

The first thing to know is that *iman* was designed to fluctuate. That it rises
and falls is not a defect in you — it is the agreed position of the scholars
about what faith is, and it is what makes deliberate strengthening possible at
all.

**The agreed doctrine.** Ibn 'Abd al-Barr: "**faith increases and decreases —
increasing with obedience and decreasing with disobedience** — and on this are
the whole body of the people of transmitted reports, and the jurists of legal
opinion in the great cities" [1]. Al-Shafi'i's own words, as Ibn Hajar records
them: "faith is speech and action; it increases and decreases" — with the
addition in Abu Nu'aym's version, "it increases with obedience and decreases with
disobedience" [2]. And the Qur'an says so directly: "He it is Who sent down peace
of reassurance into the hearts of the believers **that they might add faith unto
their faith**" (48:4) [3].

If it can be added to, it can be worked on. That is the whole basis of your
question having an answer.

**Where the increase actually happens — and there are three places, not one.**
Ibn Taymiyya sets out the levels. The first is outward action: "**people vary in
them, and they increase and decrease**" [4]. The second is the more important one
and the one people neglect: "the second aspect of the increase and decrease of
faith is **the increase and decrease of the actions of the heart** — for it is
known by direct experience, which every believer finds, that people vary in love
of Allah and His Messenger, in fear of Allah, in turning to Him, in reliance upon
Him, in sincerity to Him, in the soundness of their hearts from ostentation,
pride and self-admiration, and in mercy towards creation and sincere counsel to
them, and the like of these traits of faith" [5].

That list is the actual work. Not more units of prayer — more love, more fear,
less vanity, more mercy.

He describes how the parts connect: "**faith is speech and action, increasing and
decreasing** — and the meaning of that is that it is speech of the heart and
action of the heart, then speech of the tongue and action of the limbs" [6]. And:
"this affirmation is followed by the action of the heart — which is love of Allah
and His Messenger, magnifying Allah and His Messenger, honouring and revering the
Messenger, fear of Allah, turning to Him, sincerity towards Him, reliance upon
Him, and other such states" [7].

**And here is the practical point.** Everything counts. Ibn Taymiyya, reporting
the scholars' position: "**they said: everything by which Allah is obeyed,
whether obligatory or voluntary, is part of faith**" [8]. And the Prophet's ﷺ own
map of the range: "**faith is some seventy-odd branches — the highest of them is
saying *there is no god but Allah*, and the lowest of them is removing a harmful
thing from the road**" [9].

Removing an obstacle from a path is named, by the Prophet ﷺ, as a branch of
faith. That is the floor, and it is very low, and it is deliberately so.

**The three-part test to measure yourself against.** "Three, whoever has them in
him has found the sweetness of faith: **that Allah and His Messenger are more
beloved to him than anything else; that he loves a person for no reason but
Allah; and that he hates to return to disbelief after Allah has rescued him from
it as he would hate to be thrown into the Fire**" [10]. Note that all three are
loves and hatreds. Ibn Hajar draws the inference explicitly: faith increases and
decreases "because love and hatred vary in degree" [11].

The mechanism the sources give for strengthening faith is therefore about
*attachment* — whom you love, and for what reason.

**One historical detail worth having.** 'Umar b. 'Abd al-'Aziz wrote to his
governor: "faith has obligations, and ordinances, and limits, and recommended
practices" [12] — and concluded, "whoever completes them has completed his faith"
[13]. Four categories, not one: things required, things believed, things
forbidden, and things encouraged. Faith is worked on across all four, which is
why an approach that consists only of adding voluntary prayers tends to stall.

**So, practically.** Start where the failure is. If an obligation is being
missed, that is the first place — everything else is built on it. Then look at
the heart's actions, because that is where Ibn Taymiyya locates the real
variation: whether you are more sincere, less vain, quicker to mercy than you
were last year. Then let the small things count, because the Prophet ﷺ said they
do — the obstacle moved off the pavement is on the same list as the *shahada*.

And expect the fluctuation. It is not a sign of anything having gone wrong; the
scholars built it into the definition.

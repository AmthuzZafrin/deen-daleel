# I was married before I became Muslim — is that marriage still valid?

## Answer

Yes. You do not remarry, and you were never living in sin.

Ibn Qudama opens the chapter with it flatly: **the marriages of non-Muslims are
valid, and they are confirmed in them when they enter Islam.** [1] Then the part
that answers what you are actually worried about: **no regard is had to the form
of their contract or its manner, and the conditions of Muslims' marriages are not
required of it — no guardian, no witnesses, no wording of offer and acceptance,
and the like. Without disagreement among the Muslims.** [2]

A registry office, a church, a courthouse, vows written by the two of you — the
form is simply not what is examined.

He cites Ibn 'Abd al-Barr for the consensus: **the scholars agree that when the
two spouses enter Islam together, they may remain in their marriage.** [3] And
the evidence is the obvious historical fact: **great numbers entered Islam, and
their wives with them, and were confirmed in their marriages — and the Prophet
did not ask them about the conditions of the marriage or how it was done.** [4]

The Hanafis say the same in almost the same words. Kanz al-Daqa'iq: **a
non-Muslim who married without witnesses, or during a waiting period, where that
is permitted in their religion, and then the two entered Islam — they are
confirmed in it.** [5]

**This is not a grudging tolerance.** Ibn Qudama notes that the full legal
consequences of a valid marriage attach to such marriages — **divorce takes
effect, zihar and ila' apply, the dower is owed** [6] — and cites the hadith in
which the Prophet says of his own descent: **I was born of a marriage, not of
fornication.** [7] His ancestors' marriages, contracted before Islam, are
described in that hadith as marriages.

**What is actually examined**, if anything is: not the wedding but the person.
Ibn Qudama's test is **whether the woman is someone he could contract a marriage
with now — if so, they are confirmed; if not, they are not.** [8] The cases he
means are a prohibited relative, or a woman who was in a waiting period from an
earlier marriage at the time. Ordinary marriages do not touch that list.

**Two things this does not settle.**

If only one of you has become Muslim, that is a genuinely different question with
a genuinely different answer, and it is the next one in this section.

And civil validity is a separate matter entirely. Nothing here changes what your
government thinks of your marriage certificate, in either direction.

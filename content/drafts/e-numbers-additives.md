# How do I know if additives are halal?

> **This question is marked `expect: decline`.** The answer must explain
> why it needs a person — a mufti, a doctor, a lawyer — and must not give
> a ruling. Cite only to show what the question touches on.

## Answer

**This site will not tell you which additives are permissible, because that
requires knowing what each one is actually made of in a specific product — a fact
about manufacturing, not a question of law.** What it can give you is the
framework the jurists built for exactly this situation: things whose status you
cannot determine. And that framework has a warning in it that most advice on this
topic leaves out.

**The verse worth starting from.** **"Say: Have ye considered what provision Allah
hath sent down for you, how ye have made of it lawful and unlawful? Hath Allah
permitted you, or do ye invent a lie concerning Allah?"** [1] The rebuke there is
aimed at people **assigning** rulings that were not given. It cuts against
declaring an ingredient forbidden as much as against declaring one lawful.

**What "doubtful" actually means.** Ibn Hajar collects the definitions: **"وحاصل
ما فسر به العلماء الشبهات أربعة أشياء أحدها تعارض الأدلة كما تقدم ثانيها اختلاف
العلماء وهى منتزعة من الأولى ثالثها ان المراد بها مسمى المكروه لأنه يجتذبه جانبا
الفعل والترك رابعها ان المراد بها المباح"** — *the sum of what the scholars
explained the doubtful matters to be is four things: first, conflicting evidence;
second, the disagreement of the scholars, which is derived from the first; third,
that what is meant is what is called disliked, because both doing and leaving pull
at it; fourth, that what is meant is the merely permitted* [2].

**Notice what is absent from that list: not knowing the facts.** The *shubuhat*
the hadith speaks of are cases where the **law** is unclear. An additive whose
source you simply have not looked up is a gap in your information, not a gap in
the law — and the remedy for it is to find out, not to abstain.

**The wara' framework, and its limits.** Ibn Taymiyya distinguishes obligatory
scrupulousness from the rest, and then says something that this question needs:
**"فاما ما لا ريب في حله فليس تركه من الورع وما لا ريب في سقوطه فليس فعله من
الورع"** — *as for what there is no doubt about the lawfulness of, leaving it is
not scrupulousness at all; and what there is no doubt has lapsed, doing it is not
scrupulousness* [3].

**Avoiding what is not actually doubtful is not piety.** It is a category error
with a religious costume on.

He cites the standard the Prophet ﷺ gave for the genuinely unclear case:
**"البر مااطمأنت إليه النفس وسكن إليه القلب"** — *righteousness is what the soul
is at rest with and the heart settles upon* [4] — and, in another wording he cites,
**"البر حسن الخلق والاثم ما حاك في نفسك وان افتاك الناس"** — *righteousness
is good character, and sin is what wavers in your soul even if people give you a
ruling* [5].

**And here is the warning.** Ibn Hajar transmits al-Khattabi's classification of
scrupulous avoidance: **"قال الخطابي كل ما شككت فيه فالورع اجتنابه ثم هو على
ثلاثة أقسام واجب ومستحب ومكروه فالواجب اجتناب ما يستلزمه ارتكاب المحرم والمندوب
اجتناب معاملة من أكثر ماله حرام والمكروه اجتناب الرخص المشروعة على سبيل
التنطع"** — *al-Khattabi said: everything you doubt, scrupulousness is to avoid
it; then it is of three kinds — obligatory, recommended, and **disliked**. The
obligatory is avoiding what entails committing the forbidden; the recommended is
avoiding dealings with one most of whose wealth is unlawful; and the **disliked
is avoiding the legitimate concessions out of over-punctiliousness*** [6].

**There is a kind of avoidance the jurists classified as blameworthy**, and it is
the kind that treats every unlabelled ingredient as a threat. That belongs in any
honest answer on E numbers.

Ibn Hajar also records an exchange worth carrying. Yunus ibn 'Ubayd said he had
never struggled with anything harder than scrupulousness; Hassan ibn Abi Sinan
said he had never found anything easier — **"تركت ما يريبني إلى ما لا يريبني
فاسترحت"** — *I left what made me uneasy for what did not, and I was at rest* [7].
Wara' that leaves you exhausted and suspicious is not the thing being described.

**One legal point that bears directly on additives.** Many are derived substances
whose original material is transformed in processing. Ibn Taymiyya on that:
**"أما استحالة النجاسة كرماد السرجين النجس والزبل النجس يستحيل ترابا فقد تقدمت
هذه المسألة وقد ذكرنا أن فيها قولين في مذهب مالك وأحمد أحدهما أن ذلك طاهر وهو قول
أبي حنيفة وأهل الظاهر وغيرهم وذكرنا أن هذا القول هو الراجح"** — *as for the
transformation of an impurity — like the ash of impure dung, and impure manure
that turns into earth — this question has been dealt with, and we mentioned that
there are two positions on it in the school of Malik and of Ahmad; one of them is
that it is pure, which is the position of Abu Hanifa, the Zahiris and others; and
we mentioned that this position is the stronger* [8].

That is a live and well-supported position, and it is why many additives derived
from originally-impure sources are treated as permissible by serious scholars. It
is not the only position — see *Is gelatin halal?* for where the schools divide.

---

**So: why no list from this site.**

1. **An E number is a function, not an ingredient.** The same code can be produced
   from plants, minerals, synthesis, or animal sources, and manufacturers change
   sources.
2. **The ruling would then depend on the istihala question**, on which the schools
   differ, as above.
3. **A list would go stale**, and a stale list about food is worse than no list.

**What to do instead.**

- **Look it up for the product in front of you.** Manufacturers publish this, and
  asking them is the correct step — it converts an unknown into a known, which is
  what the *shubuhat* framework actually asks of you.
- **Where the source is genuinely unknown and unknowable**, that is the case wara'
  was made for, and leaving it is good — for you, quietly.
- **Do not make it a rule for other people**, and do not treat a fellow Muslim's
  different judgement as laxity. Scholarly disagreement is listed above as one of
  the recognised forms of *shubha*, not as evidence of anyone's negligence.
- **Watch for al-Khattabi's third category.** If checking additives has become a
  source of constant anxiety, the classification above says something about that
  state, and it is not flattering.
- **Use a certifier you trust for the products you eat often**, and stop
  re-litigating each packet.

There is no version of this question — "is E### halal?" — that this site will
answer.

# Am I using too much water in wudu?

## Answer

**Almost certainly, yes — and the tradition is unusually pointed about it.**

**The measure the Prophet used.** Safina reported that **he used to make wudu
with a mudd and bathe with a sa'** [1]. A *mudd* is roughly a large double
handful — call it three quarters of a litre. A *sa'* is four of those, so about
three litres for a complete bath. Bukhari gives the range: **the Prophet used to
bathe with a sa' up to five mudds, and make wudu with a mudd** [2].

Set that against a running tap, which delivers six to nine litres a minute. A
minute of running water is two full ghusls.

**Excess is disliked, and the Prophet said so to a Companion's face.** Abdullah
ibn Amr reported that he passed Sa'd while he was making wudu and said: **"what
is this extravagance?" He said: is there extravagance in wudu? He said: yes, even
if you are on a flowing river.** [3] That last clause forecloses the obvious
excuse. Abundance is not the issue; the disposition is.

There is a hadith naming it as a devil's work: **wudu has a devil called Walhan
— so beware the whispering of water.** [4] And a saying the fuqaha repeat: **it
used to be said: a man's infatuation with water comes from the smallness of his
understanding.** [5] Ibn Qudama's own verdict is simply **extravagance in water,
and greatly increasing it, is disliked** [6].

It is a settled enough concern that Abu Dawud gives it a chapter of its own in
his *Sunan*: **the chapter on extravagance in water** [7]. Going beyond three
washes per limb is worse than disliked, and there is a separate answer here on
the state of mind that drives it.

**But — and this matters — there is no fixed quantity you owe.** Do not turn
this page into a new anxiety. The mudd is a description, not a ration. Al-Shafi'i,
Ahmad and Ishaq: **the meaning of this hadith is not a fixed limit such that more
than it is not allowed nor less than it — it is the amount that suffices.** [8]
Ibn Abd al-Barr goes further: **the sum of these reports indicates that there is
no fixed limit in what suffices of water** [9], and notes the decisive practical
fact — **they agreed that water is not measured out for wudu or for ghusl** [10].
Ibn Nujaym: **it is an estimate of the least that customarily suffices, and it is
not a binding measure, so that whoever performs it thoroughly with less than that,
it suffices him** [11] — and al-Nawawi **transmitted the consensus that the
measure is not binding** [12]. Ibn Qudama likewise: **if he exceeds the mudd in
wudu and the sa' in ghusl, it is permitted** [13].

**The real test is thoroughness, not volume.** The verse asks you to wash the
face, the arms to the elbows, wipe the head, wash the feet to the ankles [14].
Whether that took a jug or a tap is not the question; whether every part got wet
is.

**Two stories that between them give you the whole balance.** A man told Jabir a
sa' was not enough for him. **Jabir said: it sufficed one with more hair than
you, and better than you** — meaning the Prophet. [15] And Sa'id ibn al-Musayyab,
told by a man that he used two mudds simply rinsing his nose, replied: **what
would you have me tell you, if Shaytan is playing with you?** [16]

**Practically**

- Fill a jug or a small basin and use that, rather than running the tap. This one
  change does most of the work.
- If you must use a tap, turn it off between limbs.
- Three washes maximum. One is valid; three is the sunna; four is the error the
  hadith names.
- **If you find you cannot stop** — that you go back, that a limb never feels
  properly washed — that is a different problem with its own answer in this
  collection, and the water is a symptom of it.

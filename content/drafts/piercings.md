# Are piercings allowed?

## Answer

Ear piercing for a woman is permitted on the position most people follow, but
this is not a settled question the way it is usually presented — al-Ghazali held
it forbidden outright, and Ibn Hajar picks apart the hadith normally used to
prove it before supplying better evidence himself. The reasoning is worth
following, because it also tells you where nose and other piercings stand.

**Start with what is not disputed.** Al-Nawawi: "the Muslims are agreed that it
is permitted for women to wear all kinds of jewellery, of silver and gold
together — such as the neck-ring, the necklace, the ring, the bracelet, the
anklet, the amulets, the armlets, the collars, the chokers, and everything worn
on the neck and elsewhere, and everything they are accustomed to wear; and there
is no disagreement in any of this" [1]. With one limit: "every ornament
permitted to women is only permitted when there is no evident extravagance in
it" [2]. So wearing an earring is not the question. Making the hole is.

**The usual proof, and why Ibn Hajar is not satisfied with it.** In the 'Id
hadith the women take the rings from their ears and throw them into Bilal's
cloak as charity, in front of the Prophet ﷺ. "It was used as evidence for the
permissibility of piercing a woman's ear so as to put the earring in it, and
other things she may adorn herself with — but there is something to consider in
that" [3]. His three objections in order: "because it is not established that
the earring must be placed in a hole in the ear — it may be fastened in the hair
by a fine chain so that it hangs level with the ear and falls below it. And
granting the point, what is derived is only from his not objecting to them; and
it is possible their ears had been pierced before the coming of the Law, so what
is overlooked in continuance is not overlooked at the outset" [4].

That last clause is a real principle and it recurs across this whole section:
tolerating something already done is not the same as licensing it from scratch.

**The spread of opinion.** Ibn al-Qayyim, quoted by Ibn Hajar: "the majority
disliked piercing a boy's ear, and some permitted it for a female" [5]. Ibn
Hajar adds: "permission for the female came from Ahmad, for adornment — and
dislike for the boy" [6]. At the strict end, "al-Ghazali in the *Ihya*': it is
forbidden to pierce a woman's ear, and it is forbidden to be hired for it,
unless something is established concerning it from the side of the Law" [7].

Note how al-Ghazali frames it: he is not asserting a prohibition against
evidence, he is saying the evidence is missing. Which is exactly the gap Ibn
Hajar then fills: "there came from Ibn 'Abbas, in what al-Tabarani reported in
*al-Awsat*: seven things in the child are of the *sunna* — and he mentioned the
seventh of them: piercing his ear. And this corrects the statement of some
commentators that our companions have no basis for saying it is a *sunna*" [8].

So the state of the question is: a disputed practice, held forbidden by
al-Ghazali on grounds of no evidence, permitted by Ahmad for a woman's adornment,
and supported by a report from Ibn 'Abbas that Ibn Hajar considered decisive
enough to say the objection was answered.

**Which gives you a workable answer.** Ear piercing for a girl or woman:
permitted on the position the schools generally settled on, with a respectable
minority against. Nose piercing: the sources on this file do not address it
directly, but nothing in the reasoning distinguishes it — the objection was
never about *which* part of the body, it was about whether making a hole for
adornment has warrant at all. Where local custom treats it as women's ordinary
jewellery, the same reasoning covers it. Piercings that go beyond adornment into
altering the body's form run into the separate and much stronger prohibition on
*altering the creation of Allah*, which is its own question.

**For men, the question is different and turns on custom.** The relevant
prohibition is imitation: al-Tabari, on the hadith cursing men who imitate
women and women who imitate men, "the meaning is that it is not permissible for
men to imitate women in the dress and adornment specific to women, nor the
reverse" [9]. And Ibn Hajar's qualification is the operative part: "as for the
form of dress, it differs according to the custom of each country — for there
may be a people whose women's attire is not distinguished from their men's in
dress, though the women are distinguished by veiling and covering" [10].

The test, then, is not the object but the local reading of it. Where an earring
on a man is unambiguously women's adornment, it falls under the imitation
prohibition. Where it is not, the argument has to be made on other grounds.
That is a judgement about your own society, and the sources hand it to you
rather than making it for you.

### Passage 32 — Radd al-Muhtar (Fatawa Shami) 1:356-357

- kind: fiqh · source: Radd al-Muhtar (Fatawa Shami) · chunk: 455090
- found via: "الوشم والنمص والتفليج وتغيير خلق الله"

```
Radd al-Muhtar (Fatawa Shami) 1:356-357

Arabic:
مطلب في حكم الوشم تنبيه مهم: يستفاد مما مر حكم الوشم في نحو اليد، وهو أنه كالإختضاب أو الصبغ بالمتنجس، لأنه إذا غرزت اليد أو الشفة مثلا بإبرة ثم حشي محلها بكحل أو نيلة ليخضر تنجس الكحل بالدم، فإذا جمد الدم والتأم الجرح بقي محله أخضر، فإذا غسل طهر لأنه أثر يشق زواله لأنه لا يزمل إلا بسلخ الجلد أو جرحه، فإذا كان لا يكلف بإزالة الأثر الذي يزول بماء حار أو صابون فعدم التكليف هنا أولى، وقد صرح به في القنية فقال: ولو اتخذ في يده وشما لا يلزمه السلخ ا ه‍.

لكن في الذخيرة: لو أعاد سنه ثانيا ونبت وقوى، فإن أمكن قلعه بلا ضرر قلعه، وإلا فلا وتنجس فمه، ولا يؤم أحدا من الناس ا ه‍: أي بناء على نجاسة السن وهو خلاف ظاهر المذهب. وقال

العلامة البيري: ومنه يعلم حكم الوشمة، ولا ريب في عدم جواز كونه إماما بجامع النجاسة. ثم نقل عن شرح   المشارق للعلامة الأكمل أنه قيل: يصير ذلك الموضع نجسا، فإن لم يمكن إزالته إلا بالجرح: فإن خيف منه الهلاك أو فوات عضو لم تجب، وإلا وجبت، وبتأخيره يأثم، والرجل والمرأة فيه سواء ا ه‍.

أقول: وعليه لو أصاب ماء قليلا أو مائعا نجسه، لكن تعبير الاكل بقيل يفيد عدم اعتماده، وهو مذهب الشافعية، فالظاهر أنه نقله عنهم. والفرق بين الوشمة وبين السن على القول بنجاستها ظاهر، فإن السن عين النجاسة والوشمة أثر، فإن ادعى أن بقاء الون دليل على بقاء العين رد بأن الصبغ والإختضاب كذلك فيلزم عدم طهارته، وإن فرق بأن الوشمة امتزجت باللحم والتأمت معه بخلاف الصبغ نقول: إن ما تداخل في اللحم لا يؤمر بغسله كما لو تشربت النجاسة في يده مثلا، وما على سطح الجلد مثل الحناء والصبغ، وقد صرحوا بأنه لو اكتحل بكحل نجس لا يجب غسله، ولما جرح (ص) في أحد جاءت فاطمة رضي الله عنها فأحرقت حصيرا وكمدت به حتى التصق بالجرح فاستمسك الدم.
```

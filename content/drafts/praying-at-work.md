# How do I pray at work when there's nowhere private?

## Answer

**Find five minutes and a clean patch of floor. You need far less than you think —
no mosque, no mat, no privacy, no special room.**

**What the prayer actually requires of a place.** Only two things: it must be
clean, and it must not be one of the specifically excluded places. Ibn Qudama's
list of exclusions is short: **seven places in which prayer is not permitted — the
roof of the House of Allah, the graveyard, the rubbish heap, the slaughterhouse,
the bathhouse, the resting-place of camels, and the middle of the road** [1]. An
office, a storeroom, a stairwell, a corner of a warehouse — none of these is on it.

And the reason for the list tells you how to read it: **because these places are
likely spots for impurities, so the ruling was attached to them rather than to the
actual presence of impurity** [2]. It is about cleanliness, not about the room
being consecrated.

**Where people actually pray at work**

| | |
|---|---|
| A quiet meeting room, booked for five minutes | the standard solution |
| A stairwell landing, a storeroom, behind a shelf unit | fine |
| Your own office with the door shut | fine |
| An empty corner of an open-plan floor | fine — and less conspicuous than people fear |
| The car park, or your own car (parked) | fine |
| A prayer room, if the building has one | best |
| **The toilet or shower room** | **no** — the *hammam* of the list above |

**Wudu without a proper sink.** This is usually the harder half of the problem, and
it has its own answer here. The short version, and it removes the difficulty
entirely: **make full wudu at home, put socks on, and from then on wipe over the
socks** — a day and a night for a resident. Renewing wudu at work then means face,
arms, head and a wipe over the socks, all at an ordinary basin, with nothing to
take off. There are separate answers here on wiping over socks and on making wudu
in a public bathroom.

**How long it takes.** Dhuhr and Asr are two rak'ahs each if you shorten (only on a
journey) and four otherwise — about four minutes each including wudu. That is
shorter than a coffee break, and most working days only require Dhuhr and Asr to be
prayed at work at all; in winter, often only one.

**Timing it**

- **Learn your prayer windows** rather than clock times. Dhuhr has hours; Asr has
  hours. You almost never need to pray at a specific minute.
- **Take it as part of a break** you were entitled to anyway.
- **Pray early in the window** so a meeting overrunning does not cost you the
  prayer.

**If you are worried about being seen.** You do not need to announce it and you do
not need to hide. Most colleagues who notice register it as "he stepped out for a
few minutes." If someone asks, the plain answer — *I pray at lunchtime* — is
usually the end of it.

**If your employer refuses**, that is a different question with its own answer
here, and it goes into the legal and practical options.

**And if you genuinely cannot pray during a shift**, the ruling on missed prayers
applies rather than the prayer being cancelled: **whoever sleeps through a prayer
or forgets it, let him pray it when he remembers it — there is no expiation for it
but that** [3]. There is a separate answer here on that, and another on combining
prayers because of a shift. But those are the fallbacks. The first move is to look
for five minutes and a clean floor, and they are almost always there.

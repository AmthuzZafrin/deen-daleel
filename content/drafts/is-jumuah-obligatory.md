# Do I have to go to Friday prayer?

## Answer

**Yes — for an adult, free, resident, healthy Muslim man, Jumu'ah is a personal
obligation, not an optional gathering. Four or five categories of people are
excused.**

**The obligation.**

Ibn Qudama sets out where it comes from: **the basis of the obligation of
Jumu'ah is the Book, the Sunna, and consensus** [1] — the Book being the verse
commanding you to hasten to the remembrance of God when the call is made on
Friday, and to leave off trade. He closes with **and the Muslims are agreed on
the obligation of Jumu'ah** [2].

Al-Nawawi is precise about *what kind* of obligation: **Jumu'ah is a personal
obligation upon every legally responsible person other than those with the
excuses and incapacities mentioned** [3]. Personal, not communal — it is not
discharged for you by other people going.

**Who is excused.**

The hadith of Tariq ibn Shihab is the one the jurists build on: **Jumu'ah is a
duty binding on every Muslim in congregation, except four: an owned slave, a
woman, a child, or a sick person** [4]. Ibn Qudama cites the same wording [5] and
adds Tamim al-Dari's version, which names five: **Jumu'ah is obligatory except
upon five — a woman, a child, a sick person, a traveller, or a slave** [6].

So in practice today: **women, children, the sick, and travellers** are not
obliged. Slavery is not a live category. Everyone else in the list is.

Being excused is not the same as being excluded. If you are in one of those
categories and you go, your Jumu'ah counts — see the separate answer on women and
Jumu'ah.

**On missing it deliberately.**

The warning is severe and it is worth reading carefully. Al-Tirmidhi reports:
**whoever leaves Jumu'ah three times out of indifference to it, God seals** his
heart [7], and grades it **the hadith of Abu al-Ja'd is a hasan hadith** [8]. Abu
Dawud has it as **whoever leaves three Jumu'ahs out of indifference to them, God
seals his heart** [9].

Al-Kasani draws the inference the jurists draw from it: **a threat like this
attaches only to abandoning an obligation, and on that the community is agreed**
[10].

Note what the wording actually condemns — *tahawun*, indifference, treating it as
not mattering. It is not aimed at someone who misses Jumu'ah because they are
ill, or travelling, or genuinely trapped at work. If that is your situation, see
the separate answers on missing Jumu'ah and on Jumu'ah at work.

وأما السنة، فقول النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم: {لينتهين أقوام عن ودعهم الجمعات أو ليختمن الله على قلوبهم، ثم ليكونن من الغافلين}. متفق عليه.

وعن أبي الجعد الضمري، أن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم قال: {من ترك ثلاث جمع تهاونا طبع الله على قلبه} وقال عليه السلام: {الجمعة حق واجب على كل مسلم، إلا أربعة: عبد مملوك، أو امرأة، أو صبي، أو مريض}. رواهما أبو داود.

وعن جابر، قال: خطبنا رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم فقال: {واعلموا أن الله تعالى قد افترض عليكم الجمعة في مقامي هذا، في يومي هذا، في شهري هذا، من عامي هذا، فمن تركها في حياتي أو بعد مماتي، وله إمام عادل أو جائر، استخفافا بها، أو جحودا لها، فلا جمع الله له شمله، ولا بارك له في أمره، ألا ولا صلاة له، ألا ولا زكاة له، ألا ولا حج له، ألا ولا صوم له، ولا بر له، حتى يتوب، فإن تاب تاب الله عليه} رواه ابن ماجه.

وأجمع المسلمون على وجوب الجمعة.

# Can I keep pets?

## Answer

Yes. Cats, birds and other harmless animals may be kept, and the evidence for cats in particular is unusually warm. Dogs are the one significant exception, and they have their own page.

**The founding report, about a cat.**

Kabsha bint Ka'b saw her father-in-law Abu Qatada, a Companion, do something that surprised her: he "performed ablution, then tipped his vessel down for a cat. She said: He looked at me and said — *Are you surprised?* I heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say: **She is not impure; she is of those who go round among you, male and female**" [1].

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr draws two rulings from it: "in it is the permissibility of keeping a cat — and whatever is permitted to keep for benefiting from it, selling it and eating its price is permitted… And in it is that a cat does not make impure what it drinks from, and that its leftover is pure. And this is the position of Malik and his companions, al-Shafi'i and his companions, al-Awza'i, Abu Yusuf the judge, and al-Hasan b. Salih b. Hayy" [2].

And he generalises the reasoning, which is where the whole answer comes from: "in it is evidence that whatever we are permitted to keep, its leftover is pure — because it is of those that go round among us. And the meaning of *those who go round among us* is: those who come in among us and mix with us" [3].

*Tawwafin 'alaykum* — the ones that circulate through your house. The same word the Qur'an uses of the servants and children who come and go. A cat is not a visitor to be decontaminated after; it is part of the household. Ibn 'Abbas said as much: "the cat is among the furnishings of the house" [4].

The Prophet's ﷺ own practice is transmitted too: "it is related from 'A'isha, from the Prophet ﷺ, that a cat would pass by him and he would tip the vessel down for it, and it would drink; then he would perform ablution with what it left" [5]. And Ibn 'Abd al-Barr lists who held its leftover pure: "al-'Abbas b. 'Abd al-Muttalib, 'Ali b. Abi Talib, Ibn 'Abbas, Ibn 'Umar, 'A'isha, Abu Qatada, al-Hasan, al-Husayn, 'Alqama, Ibrahim, 'Ikrima and 'Ata' b. Yasar" [6].

Ibn al-'Arabi puts the practical rule crisply: the hadith removes the ruling of impurity from its leftover "even though it comes into contact with impurity — and it is always to be carried upon purity **unless something foul is seen in its mouth**" [7].

So the working rule with a cat: presume clean. You do not check, and you do not scrub after it. Only visible filth on the mouth changes anything.

**One school qualifies this.** The Hanafis record a mild dislike, and the reasoning is instructive. Al-Kasani gives al-Karkhi's explanation: "it is not impure, because the Prophet ﷺ negated impurity from it by his words *the cat is not impure* — but the dislike is on account of the supposition that it has caught a mouse, so its mouth becomes like the hand of one who has woken from sleep" [8]. That is a *hygiene* worry, not a ritual one, and it is why the recommendation is to wash your hands after handling any animal. Al-Kasani also records that "from Abu Yusuf and al-Shafi'i: it is not disliked — and they argued with what is related, that the Prophet ﷺ used to tip the vessel down for it and it would drink from it, then he would drink and perform ablution with it" [9].

**Birds.**

The Prophet ﷺ used to joke with a small boy about his pet: *Abu 'Umayr, what happened to the little nughayr?* Al-Nawawi lists among the many rulings drawn from that hadith "the permissibility of a child playing with a small bird, and the guardian's enabling him to do so" [10].

Someone raised the obvious objection — does keeping a bird conflict with the prohibition of tormenting animals? Ibn Hajar records both sides: "Abu 'Abd al-Malik said it is possible that this was abrogated by the prohibition of tormenting animals. And al-Qurtubi said: **the truth is that there is no abrogation; rather what was permitted to the boy is keeping the bird to be entertained by it — but enabling him to torment it, especially to the point of death, was never permitted at all**" [11].

That settles the modern question cleanly. Keeping a caged bird is permitted; what was never permitted is neglect or cruelty. Which puts the whole weight on husbandry — the size of the cage, the food, the cleaning — rather than on the keeping.

**And the Prophet ﷺ himself had one.**

'A'isha reported: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ had a *wahsh* — and when he went out it would play and dart about and come and go; and when it sensed the Messenger of Allah ﷺ it would lie down and not stir, out of dislike that it should disturb him" [12].

**Dogs are the exception.**

Al-Nawawi: "as for keeping dogs, our position is that it is forbidden to keep a dog without need, and it is permitted to keep it for hunting, for crops and for livestock. And whether it is permitted for guarding houses and streets and the like — there are two views… and the sounder is that it is permitted, by analogy with the three, acting upon the reason understood from the hadiths — which is need" [13]. That question has its own answer; in short, a working dog is permitted and a dog kept purely for company is the case the hadith warns about.

**In practice.** A cat, a bird, a rabbit, a fish — all permitted, and the cat has explicit textual warmth behind it. The obligations that come with it are what the tradition actually cares about: feed it, water it, do not confine it in a way that harms it, do not neglect it, and do not acquire an animal you cannot properly keep. Those duties are set out at length in their own right — and the reports on them are severe.

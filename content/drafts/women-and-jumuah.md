# Do women have to attend Friday prayer?

## Answer

**Jumu'ah is not obligatory on women — and if a woman attends, it counts, and it
takes the place of her Zuhr. Both halves of that are agreed.**

**Not obligatory.**

Al-Nawawi lists who the obligation falls on: **it is binding on him, but not on
women, children, slaves, and travellers** [1].

Ibn Qudama reports it as settled on both sides at once, quoting Ibn al-Mundhir:
**everyone we have preserved knowledge from among the people of learning agreed
that there is no Jumu'ah upon women, and they agreed that if they attend and pray
the Jumu'ah, that suffices for them** [2].

**Why the exemption exists — and what follows from it.**

Ibn Qudama gives the reasoning explicitly: **because dropping Jumu'ah is a
lightening for them, so when they take on the hardship and pray, it suffices
them, like the sick person** [3].

That is the key sentence, and it settles the practical question. The exemption is
a concession, not a bar. It works exactly like the exemption for someone who is
ill: they need not come, and if they do come, they have prayed Jumu'ah, and they
do not pray Zuhr afterwards.

**Attending.**

Ibn Qudama states it plainly: **as for the woman, if she is elderly there is no
harm in her attending, and if she is young, her attending is permitted** [4]. He
goes on to record the view, on the authority of a report, that praying at home is
better for them — that is a preference expressed within the Hanbali discussion,
not a prohibition, and it sits alongside the hadith forbidding men to stop women
from the mosques of God, which is discussed in the separate answer on women
praying in the mosque.

**One technical point.**

Women do not count toward the quorum that constitutes the Jumu'ah, and do not
lead it. Ibn Qudama: **the Jumu'ah is not constituted by any of these, nor is it
valid for one of them to be its imam** [5] — "these" being the women, children,
slaves, and travellers of the exemption list. He notes that Abu Hanifa and
al-Shafi'i disagreed about slaves and travellers specifically. So a woman
attending is praying a valid Jumu'ah; the congregation is *held* by the men
present.

**In short:** you do not have to go. If you go, you have prayed Jumu'ah and you
do not owe Zuhr. If you do not go, you pray Zuhr as normal, and you have missed
nothing you were obliged to.

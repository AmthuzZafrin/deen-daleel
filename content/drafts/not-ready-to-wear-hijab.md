# I'm not ready to wear hijab — am I sinning?

## Answer

Two things are true at once, and most of the distress around this question comes
from someone insisting on only one of them.

**The first: yes, it is an obligation, and not wearing it means an obligation is
going unmet.** Ibn 'Abd al-Barr: "they were agreed that covering the 'awra is an
obligatory duty in general upon human beings" [1]. There is no reading of the
sources on which the covering is optional, and an answer that told you otherwise
would be lying to make you feel better.

**The second: you are still a Muslim, and nobody has the standing to tell you
otherwise.** This is not a soft consolation; it is a stated consensus.
Al-Nawawi: "along with the consensus of the people of truth that the fornicator,
the thief, the killer and others among those who commit major sins other than
associating partners with Allah do not become disbelievers by that — rather they
are believers deficient in faith; if they repent their punishment falls away"
[2]. And the Companions' own measure, from 'Abdullah b. Shaqiq: they "did not
regard the abandonment of any of the deeds as disbelief except the prayer" [3].
That is a direct instruction about priorities. Whatever else is unresolved, hold
on to the prayer.

Ahmad was asked about a man who pursues sins deliberately but has not abandoned
prayer, zakat and fasting, and answered that such a person "is a Muslim, and I
do not call him a believer" — while "whoever falls into less than that, meaning
less than the major sins, I call a believer of deficient faith" [4]. Asked what
kind of disbelief the texts meant, he said: "a disbelief that does not remove
one from the religion — just as faith has parts, some without others, so too
does disbelief" [5].

**Ibn Taymiyya will not let this become weightless, and he is right to.** He
insists the texts that negate faith from a sinner are not negating some optional
extra: "the name is only negated for the absence of some of what is *obligatory*
in it, not for the absence of some of what is recommended in it" [6] — "so this
statement conveys that whoever does that has left an obligation" [7]. So: a real
duty, unmet. Not a technicality, and not a disqualification from Islam either.

**Now the part that actually answers your question.** The Qur'an's warning is
not aimed at falling short. It is aimed at a specific posture: "and those who,
when they do an evil thing or wrong themselves, remember Allah and implore
forgiveness for their sins — who forgiveth sins save Allah only? — and will not
knowingly repeat (the wrong) they did" [8]. The word translated "repeat" is
*israr*, and al-Qurtubi records what the scholars took it to mean: "others
besides Sahl said: *israr* is that he intends not to repent — so if he intends
sincere repentance, he has left *israr*" [9].

Read that carefully, because it is the whole answer. Someone who holds the
covering as an obligation, intends to get there, and has not yet managed it is
in a different category from someone who has decided it does not apply to her.
The first has already left the condition the verse warns about. The second has
not.

**And the honest counterweight, from the same page.** Al-Qurtubi also gives
Sahl's harder reading: "*israr* is procrastination, and procrastination is that
he says: I will repent tomorrow — and this is the soul's claim; how will he
repent tomorrow, when tomorrow is not his to own?" [10]. Sahl's summary is
blunt: "the ignorant man is dead, the forgetful man is asleep, the disobedient
man is drunk, and the one who persists is destroyed" [11]. Both readings are on
the same page of the same book. The difference between them is whether
"not yet" is a direction of travel or a permanent address.

His test for which one you are in is worth more than any self-assessment: "the
sign of the one who repents is that the sin preoccupies him from food and drink,
like the three who were left behind" [12] — and al-Qurtubi's own description of
the moment of turning is not a grand resolution but simply this: that a person
looks at himself, finds regret rising in him for what he neglected, and leaves
the like of what came before out of fear of Allah's punishment — "then it is
true of him that he is one who repents" [13].

**So, practically.** Pray. Keep the intention alive and specific rather than
vague — "when I move", "when I'm stronger" is exactly the *taswif* Sahl warned
about; "this month, in these settings, and then further" is not. Start where it
is easiest rather than where it is hardest. And do not accept from anyone,
including yourself, the claim that a duty you have not yet met puts you outside
the faith. The sources are unanimous that it does not.

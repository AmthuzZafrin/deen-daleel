# What must a woman cover in prayer?

> Reviewer note: A gap found by the eval: the bank had awrah-for-men and nothing on the woman's covering in salah, so "can i pray without covering my hair" was being served an unrelated answer. Distinct from the hijab answers, which are about dress before non-mahrams; satr al-awrah is a condition of the prayer itself and the scope differs. Give the dispute over the feet, and what happens if something slips mid-prayer.


## Answer

**The first thing to fix is the assumption behind the question: covering in prayer is not the hijab rules applied indoors.** It is a separate obligation with a different reason and a different scope, and once that is clear most of the confusion resolves. This is a question about the validity of your prayer, not about who might see you.

**Ibn Taymiyya sets out the three categories, and the third is this one.**

Covering before other men is one thing; covering among women is another; `فهذان نوعان وفى الصلاة نوع ثالث` [1] — these are two categories, and in prayer there is a third.

His proof is decisive and easy to check against your own experience: `فإن المرأة لوصلت وحدها كانت مأمورة بالإختمار وفى غير الصلاة يجوز لها كشف رأسها في بيتها` [2] — a woman praying entirely alone is still commanded to wear the khimar, though outside prayer she may uncover her head in her own house.

Alone, in her own room, with nobody to see her. The obligation cannot be about concealment from people, and he says so: `فأخذ الزينة في الصلاة لحق الله` [3] — taking one's adornment in prayer is for God's right — and `فعلم أن أخذ الزينة في الصلاة لم يكن ليحتجب عن الناس` [4].

The consequence is the sentence worth memorising: `وحينئذ فقد يستر المصلى في الصلاة ما يجوز إبداؤه في غير الصلاة وقد يبدى في الصلاة ما يستره عن الرجال` [5] — so one may have to cover in prayer what may be shown outside it, and may leave uncovered in prayer what is covered from men.

The rules do not track each other in either direction.

**The hadith the head-covering rests on.**

`لا يقبل الله صلاة حائض إلا بخمار` [6] — God does not accept the prayer of a woman who has reached menstruation without a khimar. "`حائض`" here means one who has begun menstruating, i.e. an adult woman, not one who is currently menstruating.

Ibn Taymiyya draws out how strong this is: `وهى لا تختمر عند زوجها ولا عند ذوى محارمها` [7] — she does not cover before her husband or her mahrams, and yet she may not uncover her head in prayer, not for them and not for anyone.

**What must be covered, and where the schools agree.**

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr states the majority position: `وإن كانت امرأة فكل   ثوب يغيب ظهور قدميها ويستر جميع جسدها وشعرها فجائز لها الصلاة فيه لأنها كلها عورة إلا الوجه والكفين على هذا أكثر أهل العلم` [8] — any garment that conceals her feet and covers her whole body and hair is one she may pray in, since all of her is `عورة` except the face and the hands; this is the position of most of the people of knowledge. He names them: `وقال مالك وأبو حنيفة والشافعي وأصحابهم وهو قول الأوزاعي وأبي ثور على المرأة أن تغطي منها ما سوى وجهها وكفيها` [9].

The face is not covered, and that is agreed: `وقد أجمعوا على أن المرأة تكشف وجهها في الصلاة والإحرام` [10] — they agreed that a woman uncovers her face in the prayer and in ihram. Ibn Taymiyya adds that covering the face and hands in prayer `فلا يجب بإتفاق المسلمين` [11] — is not obligatory by the agreement of the Muslims.

So: hair and body covered, face and hands uncovered. That much is settled.

**Where it is genuinely disputed: the feet.**

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr's phrasing above requires a garment concealing the feet. Ibn Taymiyya takes the other side: `فكذلك القدم يجوز إبداؤه عند أبى حنيفة وهو الأقوى` [12] — the foot may likewise be left uncovered according to Abu Hanifa, and that is the stronger view.

This is a real disagreement between schools and not a matter of laxity. If you follow a school, follow it; if you do not know, either position has serious authority behind it.

**And an extreme view the tradition itself rejected.**

Abu Bakr b. 'Abd al-Rahman held `كل شيء من المرأة عورة حتى ظفرها` [13] — everything of a woman is `عورة`, even her fingernail. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr does not treat this gently: `قول (1) أبي بكر هذا خارج عن أقاويل أهل العلم لإجماع العلماء` [14] — this statement of Abu Bakr's falls outside the statements of the people of knowledge, against the consensus of the scholars.

That is worth knowing if you have been told that any visible part invalidates the prayer. The maximal position exists, and the tradition placed it outside the range of acceptable opinion.

**What if some hair shows.**

Ahmad was asked about exactly this: `سئل أحمد بن حنبل عن المرأة تصلي وبعض شعرها مكشوف وقدمها قال لا يعجبني إلا أن تغطي` [15] — asked about a woman praying with some of her hair and her feet uncovered, he said: I do not like it, unless she covers.

Notice the register. `لا يعجبني` — I do not like it — is not the language of invalidation. He is expressing a preference for covering, not declaring the prayer void. The scholars differ on how much exposure, and for how long, affects validity, and that detail is not settled in what is quoted here.

The practical implication, which many women need to hear: a strand escaping mid-prayer is not a reason to break your prayer and start again in distress. Cover it if you notice and can, and continue.

**What these books do not reach.**

Nothing here specifies how much of a limb showing, or for how long, invalidates rather than merely spoils the prayer. Nothing addresses trousers, sleeves that ride up, or modern garments as such. Nothing addresses praying in a car, in a hospital bed, or where covering is genuinely impossible — for which the general rule that God does not burden a soul beyond its capacity is the material, and a scholar should be asked. Nothing addresses the young girl before puberty, though the hadith's wording of `حائض` bears on it.

**What to do.**

- Cover hair, neck, body and arms; face and hands stay uncovered. That is the agreed core.
- Feet: follow your school. Both positions have weight [8] and [12].
- Pray alone in the same covering you would pray in with others. That is the point of [2].
- If something slips, fix it and carry on. Do not let [13] — a view the scholars themselves put outside the pale — govern your prayers.

If you have been avoiding prayer because you were told your covering was never good enough, take the material above to a scholar and ask directly what invalidates and what merely falls short. Those are different questions, and the answer to the first is much narrower than you may have been led to believe.

(837) فصل: فإن انكشف من المرأة شيء يسير من غير الوجه والكفين فلا أعلم فيها قولا صحيحا صريحا. وظاهر قول الخرقي " إذا انكشف من المرأة الحرة شيء سوى وجهها وكفيها أعادت " يقتضي بطلان الصلاة بانكشاف اليسير ; لأنه شيء، يمكن حمل ذلك على الكثير، لما قررنا في عورة الرجل أنه يعفى فيها عن اليسير. فكذا هاهنا. ولأنه يشق التحرز من اليسير، فعفي عنه قياسا على يسير عورة الرجل.

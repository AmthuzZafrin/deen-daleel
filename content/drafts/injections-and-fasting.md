# Do injections break my fast?

## Answer

**A non-nutritional injection — into muscle, skin, or vein — does not break the
fast on the reasoning most contemporary scholars follow. An IV drip that feeds you
does.**

Injections did not exist, so no classical ruling on them exists. But the jurists
worked through a case with the same structure, and their disagreement about it maps
directly onto this question.

**The classical case: medicine entering through a wound.**

Al-Sarakhsi names the two wounds: **the *ja'ifa* is the name for a wound that has
reached the body cavity, and the *amma* is the name for a wound that has reached the
brain** [1]. So: an opening in the body that is not one of the natural openings, going
all the way in.

The ruling splits: **as for the *ja'ifa* and the *amma*, if he treats them with a dry
medicine it does not break his fast; and if he treats them with a moist medicine, his
fast is invalidated in the view of Abu Hanifa, and it is not invalidated in their
view** [2] — "their" being Abu Yusuf and Muhammad.

**The two competing tests, stated explicitly.**

This is the part that decides the modern question. Abu Yusuf and Muhammad hold that
the route matters: **the two of them consider arrival at the interior by a route that
is natural to the body** [3] — and al-Sarakhsi gives their reasoning: **the abstention
is only commanded, for the sake of the fast, from a route that is natural, not from an
accidental wound** [4].

Abu Hanifa holds the opposite: **and Abu Hanifa says: what invalidates the fast is
the arrival of the fast-breaking thing at his interior — so the consideration is what
arrives, not the route** [5].

An injection is exactly the wound case: an artificial opening, medicine going in
through it. On Abu Yusuf and Muhammad's test it plainly does not break the fast. On
Abu Hanifa's test it breaks the fast only if it *arrives at the interior* — and
muscle tissue is not the body cavity.

Ibn Qudama's rule points the same way: **he breaks the fast by everything he
introduces into his interior, or into a cavity in his body such as his brain or his
throat, and the like of that which passes to his stomach** [6]. A needle in the arm
reaches none of those.

**And drawing blood is settled, which is the closer everyday case.**

Ibn Hajar: **as for cupping, the majority likewise hold that one does not break the
fast by it at all; and from Ali, Ata', al-Awza'i, Ahmad, Ishaq and Abu Thawr, the
cupper and the one cupped break the fast** [7]. Al-Nawawi: **al-Shafi'i and the
companions said cupping is permitted for the fasting person and does not break his
fast, though the better course is to leave it** [8] — and his school reasoned **by
analogy to venesection and nosebleed** [9], which is opening a vein with a blade.

**In practice:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Vaccination, insulin, antibiotic, painkiller — IM or subcutaneous | Does not break the fast |
| Intravenous medication | Does not break the fast on the majority contemporary view |
| Local anaesthetic, dental injection | Does not break the fast |
| IV nutrition, glucose drip, feeding line | **Breaks the fast** — it feeds you |
| Blood transfusion | Disputed; many treat it as nourishing. Ask a scholar |

**Two notes.** Some scholars are more cautious about intravenous medication than
intramuscular; if the timing is flexible and you want the safest position, take it
after iftar.

**And never postpone treatment you need.** If the injection is required today, take
it today. The books treat harming yourself through fasting as a fault, not a merit —
see the answer on who must fast.

### Passage 20 — Jami' al-Tirmidhi 2:135-137

- kind: hadith · source: Jami' al-Tirmidhi · chunk: 336916
- found via: "الحجامة والفصد للصائم وأفطر الحاجم والمحجوم"

```
Jami' al-Tirmidhi 2:135-137 — أبواب الصوم › (٦٠) باب كراهية الحجامة للصائم › ٧٧٤ -

Arabic:
حدثنا ‌محمد بن رافع النيسابوري  ‌ومحمود بن غيلان  ‌ويحيى بن موسى، قالوا: حدثنا ‌عبد الرزاق ، عن ‌معمر ، عن ‌يحيى بن أبي كثير ، عن ‌إبراهيم بن عبد الله بن قارظ ، عن ‌السائب بن يزيد ، عن ‌رافع بن

خديج، عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم قال:

«أفطر»

الحاجم والمحجوم.

وفي الباب عن علي، وسعد، وشداد بن أوس، وثوبان، وأسامة بن زيد، وعائشة، ومعقل بن سنان - ويقال: ابن يسار - وأبي هريرة، وابن عباس، وأبي موسى، وبلال.

وحديث رافع بن خديج حديث حسن صحيح.

وذكر عن أحمد بن حنبل أنه قال: أصح   شيء في هذا الباب حديث رافع بن خديج.

وذكر عن علي بن عبد الله أنه قال: أصح شيء في هذا الباب حديث ثوبان وشداد بن أوس؛ لأن يحيى بن أبي كثير روى عن أبي قلابة الحديثين جميعا: حديث ثوبان، وحديث شداد بن أوس.

وقد كره قوم من أهل العلم من أصحاب النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم وغيرهم الحجامة للصائم، حتى أن بعض أصحاب النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم احتجم بالليل، منهم أبو موسى الأشعري وابن عمر، وبهذا يقول ابن المبارك.

سمعت إسحاق بن منصور يقول: قال عبد الرحمن بن مهدي: من

احتجم وهو صائم فعليه القضاء.

قال إسحاق بن منصور: وهكذا قال أحمد وإسحاق.

حدثنا الزعفراني قال: وقال الشافعي: قد روي عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم أنه احتجم وهو صائم، وروي عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم أنه قال: أفطر الحاجم والمحجوم، ولا أعلم واحدا من هذين الحديثين ثابتا، ولو توقى رجل الحجامة وهو صائم كان أحب إلي، ولو احتجم صائم لم أر ذلك أن يفطره.

هكذا كان قول الشافعي ببغداد، وأما بمصر فمال إلى الرخصة، ولم ير بالحجامة للصائم بأسا، واحتج بأن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم احتجم في حجة الوداع وهو محرم صائم.
```

# Someone died — what am I supposed to do?

## Answer

**This answer is a sequence, because that is what you need. If you are reading it in an emergency, the short version is: say good things and don't crowd the dying person; then washing, shrouding, the funeral prayer and burial are owed, and they are owed by the community rather than by you personally, so your first job is to phone the nearest mosque or Muslim burial service and let them run it. What follows is the detail.**

**Before death, and at the moment of it.** `لقنوا موتاكم: لا إله إلا الله` [1] — prompt your dying with *la ilaha illa'llah*. But how, matters. Al-Tirmidhi records the restraint the scholars put on it: `وقال بعض أهل العلم: إذا قال ذلك مرة فما لم يتكلم بعد ذلك فلا ينبغي أن يلقن ولا يكثر عليه في هذا` [2] — once he has said it, so long as he has not spoken since, he should not be prompted again, and it should not be pressed on him. The illustration is Ibn al-Mubarak's own death: `وروي عن ابن المبارك أنه لما حضرته الوفاة جعل رجل يلقنه لا إله إلا الله، وأكثر عليه، فقال له عبد الله: إذا قلت مرة فأنا على ذلك ما لم أتكلم بكلام` [3] — a man kept repeating it at him, and he said: once I have said it, I am upon it, unless I speak otherwise. The point of the prompting is that the words be the last ones said, not that they be extracted repeatedly from someone who is dying.

And what is said in the room is not neutral: `المريض أو الميت فقولوا خيرا، فإن الملائكة يؤمنون على ما تقولون` [4] — when you are present with the sick or the dying, say good things, for the angels say amen to what you say. Umm Salama, who transmitted that, was taught what to say when her own husband died: `فقولي: اللهم اغفر لي وله، وأعقبني منه عقبى حسنة` [5] — O God, forgive me and forgive him, and give me something good in his place.

**What is owed, and by whom.** Al-Nawawi: `وأصل غسل الميت فرض كفاية وكذلك حمله وكفنه والصلاة عليه ودفنه كلها فروض كفاية والواجب في الغسل مرة واحدة عامة للبدن` [6] — washing the dead is a communal obligation, and so are carrying, shrouding, praying over and burying; and the obligatory minimum in the washing is a single wash covering the whole body. *Fard kifaya* means: it must be done, and once anyone competent does it the obligation lifts from everyone else. So you are not personally required to wash a body you have never washed. You are required to make sure it happens. In practice that is a phone call.

**The washing.** The instruction given to the women who washed the Prophet's daughter: `اغسلنها ثلاثا أو خمسا أو أكثر من ذلك إن رأيتن بماء وسدر واجعلن في الآخرة كافورا أو شيئا من كافور فإذا فرغتن فآذنني فلما فرغنا آذناه فألقى إلينا حقوه وقال أشعرنها إياه` [7] — wash her three times, or five, or more if you see fit, with water and lote-leaves, and put camphor, or something of camphor, in the last; tell me when you have finished. When they did, he handed them his own waist-wrapper and said: wrap her body in it. Odd numbers, a cleansing agent, camphor at the end. The obligatory core, per al-Nawawi above, is one thorough wash; the rest is the recommended form.

**The shroud, and the pace.** Al-Nawawi on "hasten with the bier": `قوله صلى الله عليه وسلم (أسرعوا بالجنازة) فيه الأمر بالاسراع للحكمة التي ذكرها صلى الله عليه وسلم قال أصحابنا وغيرهم يستحب الاسراع بالمشي بها ما لم ينته إلى حد يخاف انفجارها ونحوه` [8] — walking briskly is recommended, short of anything that would harm the body. Ibn Hajar gives al-Qurtubi's reading of the purpose: `قال القرطبي مقصود الحديث أن لا يتباطأ بالميت عن الدفن و لأن التباطؤ ربما أدى إلى التباهي و الاختيال` [9] — that the burial not be dragged out, and because delay tends to turn a funeral into a display. Al-Nawawi also rules on the manner of carrying: `ولا يجوز حملها على الهيئة المزرية ولا هيئة يخاف معها سقوطها` [10] — it may not be carried in a demeaning way, nor in a way that risks it falling.

**Three windows in the day when burial is not performed.** `ثلاث ساعات كان رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم ينهانا ان نصلي فيهن أو نقبر فيهن موتانا حين تطلع الشمس بازغة حتى ترتفع وحين يقوم قائم الظهيرة حتى تميل الشمس وحين تضيف الشمس للغروب حتى تغرب` [11] — as the sun is rising until it has climbed, when it stands at the meridian until it declines, and as it inclines to setting until it has set.

**Then the estate, in a fixed order — and this is the part families get wrong.** Al-Majmu': `وإذا مات الميت بدئ من ماله بكفنه ومؤنة تجهيزه` [12] — the first charge on the property is the shroud and the costs of preparing him for burial. The reasoning is worth having, because it explains the whole order: `ولان الميراث إنما انتقل إلى الورثة لأنه استغنى عنه الميت وفضل عن حاجته، والكفن ومؤنة التجهيز لا يستغنى عنه فقدم على الإرث` [13] — inheritance passes to the heirs only because the deceased no longer needs it and it is surplus to his requirements; the shroud and the funeral costs are not something he can do without, so they come before inheritance.

Then debts: `ثم يقضى دينه لقوله عز وجل من بعد وصية يوصى بها أو دين ولان الدين تستغرقه حاجته فقدم على الإرث` [14]. The verse names bequest before debt, but the settled practice is the reverse. Al-Qurtubi: `أهل العلم أنه يبدأ بالدين قبل الوصية وروى الدارقطني من حديث عاصم بن ضمرة عن علي قال قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم : ( الدين قبل الوصية وليس لوارث وصية` [15] — debt before bequest, and no bequest to an heir. His first explanation of the wording: `الأول إنما قصد تقديم هذين الفصلين على الميراث ولم يقصد ترتيبهما في أنفسهما فلذلك تقدمت الوصية في اللفظ` [16] — the verse means to put both of them ahead of inheritance, not to rank them against each other.

So: funeral costs, then debts, then any valid bequest, then the shares. Anyone dividing an estate before the debts are cleared is dividing money that is not theirs yet.

**Debts can be paid by anyone.** Al-Nawawi: `الدين عن الميت وقد أجمعت الأمة عليه ولا فرق بين أن يقضيه عنه وارث أو غيره فيبرأ به بلا خلاف` [17] — the community is agreed on discharging a dead person's debt, and it makes no difference whether an heir pays it or someone else; either way he is cleared of it, without dispute. If the estate cannot cover what he owed, someone else settling it is a real thing you can do for him.

**If the person who died was not Muslim.** This comes up constantly for converts and the answer in the books is clearer than people expect. Al-Kasani: `لا بأس بأن يغسله ويكفنه ويتبع جنازته ويدفنه لان الابن ما نهى عن البر بمكان أبيه الكافر بل أمر بمصاحبتهما بالمعروف بقوله تعالى وصاحبهما في الدنيا معروفا ومن البر القيام بغسله ودفنه وتكفينه` [18] — there is no harm in his washing him, shrouding him, following his funeral and burying him, because a son was never forbidden kindness to a non-Muslim father; he was commanded to keep company with them honourably, and part of that kindness is seeing to his washing, burial and shrouding.

The precedents he gives are three. `لما مات أبوه أبو طالب جاء إلى رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم فقال يا رسول الله ان عمك الضال قد توفى فقال اذهب وغسله وكفنه وواره` [19] — when Abu Talib died, 'Ali came and said, your uncle has died; and he was told: go, wash him, shroud him and bury him. And: `إن امرأتي ماتت نصرانية فقال اغسلها وكفنها وادفنها وعن الحارث بن أبي ربيعة ان أمه ماتت نصرانية فتبع جنازتها في نفر من الصحابة` [20] — a man told Ibn 'Abbas his wife had died a Christian, and was told to wash her, shroud her and bury her; and al-Harith b. Abi Rabi'a followed his Christian mother's funeral in the company of a number of the Companions.

With one qualification: `ثم إنما يقوم ذو الرحم بذلك إذا لم يكن هناك من يقوم به من أهل دينه فإن كان خلى المسلم بينه وبينهم ليصنعوا به ما يصنعون بموتاهم` [21] — the relative does this where there is nobody of the deceased's own religion to do it; if there is, the Muslim leaves them to it and lets them do for him what they do for their dead. So attending your non-Muslim parent's funeral, and doing what a child does, is not the problem some people will tell you it is.

**What this does not cover.** The mechanics of the funeral prayer itself, and of the grave and burial, are separate questions with their own detail. Nothing here addresses the legal side in a country where Islamic burial is not the default — death registration, a coroner's or medical examiner's involvement, post-mortem requirements, cemetery rules — and those can make the recommended speed impossible. None of that is a religious failure; the corpus simply does not speak to it, and a local burial service will know the rules where you are. Ask them first, not last.

والعمل على هذا عند أهل العلم.

وقال أحمد، وإسحاق: وتكون الغسلات بماء وسدر، ويكون في الآخرة شيء من كافور.

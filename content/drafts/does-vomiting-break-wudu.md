# Does throwing up break wudu?

## Answer

**Hanafis: yes, if it is a mouthful. Shafi'is: no.** The disagreement is the same
one as for blood — whether anything leaving the body *other* than by the two
usual outlets can break wudu at all.

**The Hanafi rule** is in Al-Hidaya's list of nullifiers in three words: **and
vomit, a mouthful.** [1] Below that quantity it does not break wudu. Al-Marghinani
defines the threshold: **a mouthful is that it be in a state that cannot be held
back except with effort** [2] — the point at which it is coming up whether you
want it to or not, rather than something you swallowed back down.

He also records a dissent inside his own school: **Zufar said a little vomit and
a lot are the same** [3] — no threshold at all. That position did not carry.

**The Shafi'i position** al-Marghinani states plainly as the opposing view:
**al-Shafi'i said that what exits from other than the two passages does not break
the wudu — because it is related that the Prophet vomited and did not perform
wudu.** [4] On this view vomiting is unpleasant and requires you to rinse, and
that is all.

**If it happens during the prayer**, the Hanafis have a specific instruction, from
a report Al-Hidaya cites: **whoever vomits or has a nosebleed in his prayer, let
him leave, perform wudu, and build on his prayer, so long as he has not
spoken.** [5] Not start again — continue.

**What to do.**

- *Hanafi*: a mouthful, renew wudu. Less than that, carry on.
- *Shafi'i or Maliki*: it does not break wudu.
- *Undecided*: renewing costs a minute and satisfies both.

**One thing worth separating.** Vomiting during a fast is a different question
with a different answer — whether the fast breaks turns on whether the vomiting
was deliberate, not on quantity. That answer is in the fasting section, and this
one has no bearing on it.

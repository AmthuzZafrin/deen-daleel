# I slept through suhoor — can I still fast?

## Answer

**Yes. Suhoor is a sunna, not a condition — your fast is completely valid without
it.**

**The ruling.**

Al-Nawawi is explicit: **the scholars are agreed on its being recommended, and that
it is not obligatory** [1]. Nothing about the validity of your fast depends on it.

**Which does not mean skip it.**

The hadith is short and emphatic: **take the pre-dawn meal, for in the pre-dawn meal
there is blessing** [2] — and al-Tirmidhi grades it **the hadith of Anas is a hasan
sahih hadith** [3].

Al-Nawawi explains what the blessing consists of, and his reading is practical
rather than mystical: **it strengthens one for the fast and makes him energetic for
it, and through it a desire arises for more fasting, because of how light the
hardship becomes for the one who takes it** [4].

Ibn Hajar lists the ways it works: **the blessing in suhoor is obtained through
several avenues — following the sunna, differing from the People of the Book,
gaining strength for worship through it, and the increase in energy** [5]. The
second of those is its own hadith: **what separates our fasting from the fasting of
the People of the Book is the pre-dawn meal** [6].

**So, if you slept through it:**

Fast the day. It counts fully. You have missed a recommended act, not broken a
requirement — the same category as missing a sunna prayer.

**Practically**, it will be a harder day, particularly for water. Plan accordingly:
lighter work if you can, less heat and less exertion, and do not compound it by
skipping sleep as well.

**If you wake up shortly before Fajr**, you still have time — Ibn Nujaym places the
meal at **the last sixth of the night** [7], and the sunna is to take it late
anyway: **the sunna regarding suhoor is to delay it, because the sense of drawing
strength from it is more complete in that** [8]. Even a drink of water and a date
counts as having taken suhoor.

**If you wake and are unsure whether Fajr has come in**, Ibn Nujaym gives the rule:
**the point at which it is recommended is when he is certain the night remains, or
thinks it most likely; but if he is in doubt, the better course is not to take the
meal, so as to avoid the forbidden — though it is not obligatory on him, and if he
eats his fast is complete, because the default is that it is still night** [9].

**On making it a habit.** If you are sleeping through suhoor regularly, the problem
is the sleep schedule rather than the alarm. The people who manage Ramadan best
usually sleep earlier and take a short nap in the day, rather than trying to keep
their normal hours and add a night meal on top.

# Does gossiping in a group chat count as backbiting?

## Answer

Yes — and the definition is narrower and harsher than most people expect, so it is worth having exactly right.

**The definition.**

Abu Hurayra reported that the Prophet ﷺ was asked what backbiting is. He said: "your mentioning your brother with what he dislikes" [1]. He was asked: *and what if what I say about my brother is true?* He said: "**if what you say is in him, you have backbitten him; and if what you say is not in him, you have slandered him**" [2].

So truth is not a defence. Truth is what makes it backbiting rather than slander. That single exchange settles most group-chat arguments before they start, because the usual defence — *but it's true* — is the thing being ruled on.

Al-Nawawi: "backbiting and talebearing are forbidden **by the consensus of the Muslims**, and the evidences on that are abundant" [3]. Al-Qurtubi: "there is no disagreement that backbiting is among the major sins, and that whoever backbites someone must repent to God" [4].

**How small it can be.**

'A'isha said of Safiyya only that she was a short woman. The Prophet ﷺ said to her: "**you have said a word which, were it mixed with the sea, would tinge it**" [5].

That is the calibration. Not a rumour, not a scandal — an observation about someone's height, factually true, said among family. Al-Qurtubi cites it precisely to refute those who thought backbiting only covered religion and not appearance.

And the reverse limit is worth knowing too: "the scholars from the earliest time — the Companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and the Successors after them — held nothing to be greater backbiting than backbiting in religion, because faulting a person's religion is the gravest fault; **every believer hates being spoken of regarding his religion more than he hates it regarding his body**" [6].

So the pious-sounding version — *I'm just concerned about their deen* — is not the mild form. It is the severe one.

**Why the sin is measured this way.**

Ibn Taymiyya, on the verse *would any of you like to eat the flesh of his dead brother?*: "so He made the ground of the prohibition his being a brother in the brotherhood of faith — **and for that reason backbiting is aggravated according to the state of the believer: the greater his faith, the more severe backbiting him is**" [7].

The imagery in the hadiths is not decorative. Ibn Hajar collects it: two men mocked Ma'iz after his stoning, and the Prophet ﷺ, passing a dead donkey, said to them: "**eat from the carrion of this donkey** — and what you obtained from this man's honour is worse than eating this carrion" [8]. And on a journey: "a foul wind blew, and the Prophet ﷺ said: **this is the smell of those who backbite the believers**" [9].

**Does a group chat count?**

Nothing in the definition depends on speech. It is "your mentioning your brother with what he dislikes" — mentioning, in his absence, to someone else. A message does that more completely than a conversation does: it is exact, it is stored, it is forwardable, and it reaches people who were not even in the room.

Ibn Taymiyya adds that the family of sins is wider than the word: "of the same genus as backbiting are *hamz* and *lamz*, for both contain faulting people and impugning them, as in backbiting — except that *hamz* is impugning with severity and harshness, whereas *lamz* may be free of severity and harshness" [10].

The screenshot, the caption, the reaction emoji, the knowing quote-tweet — these are *lamz*. They fault without severity, and they are named.

**The distinction that makes the rule liveable.**

This is the most useful thing in the file. Ibn Taymiyya: "mentioning people with what they dislike is fundamentally of two kinds: **the first, mentioning the type; the second, mentioning a specific person, living or dead**" [11].

And on the first: "every category God and His Messenger have censured, it is obligatory to censure — **and that is not backbiting** — just as every category God and His Messenger have praised, it is obligatory to praise" [12].

So you can say that cheating is wrong, that a practice is oppressive, that an idea is false. Naming the wrong is required. What the prohibition covers is turning it on a named person behind their back.

**The recognised exceptions, and one warning about them.**

Ibn Hajar: "among those whom it is permitted to backbite is one who is open about his corruption, or his oppression, or his innovation" [13]. There are other recognised cases — seeking a ruling, seeking redress, warning someone entering a transaction or a marriage.

The warning is that these are exceptions with conditions, and the conditions are what people skip. The measure Ibn Hajar gives for backbiting generally is "mentioning a person with what he dislikes — and this varies according to what is said about him; **his harm from it may be severe, and harming a Muslim is forbidden**" [14]. If the "warning" would not have been given had you not enjoyed giving it, it is not the exception.

**The thing next to it that is worse: spying.**

'Umar and a companion climbed a wall in the night on the suspicion of wrongdoing, and found nothing but a husband and wife. The man said: "*we were not commanded with this, O Commander of the Faithful — God Most High said: and do not spy.* [and 'Umar] said: **you have spoken the truth**" [15].

The Caliph, confronted with the verse, conceded on the spot.

And al-Qurtubi's story of the man who dug up his sister's grave and found it ablaze, and asked his mother what she had done: "of her deeds was that she used to delay the prayer past its times, and when the neighbours slept she would go to their houses and press her ear to their doors and spy on them and put out their secrets. He said: **by this she was destroyed**" [16].

A woman who listened at doors and repeated what she heard. There is no closer pre-modern description of what a certain kind of group chat is for.

**If you have already done it.**

Al-Qurtubi reports the positions on making it right, including the one attributed to al-Hasan: "it is a wrong, and its expiation is seeking forgiveness for the one he backbit" — "the expiation for backbiting is that you seek forgiveness for the one you backbit" [17]. The scholars disagreed on whether you must also ask the person's pardon; where telling them would cause fresh harm, this is the position that lets you repair it without doing so.

And the other direction, which is the whole cure: the Prophet ﷺ said, "**whoever repels [an attack] from his brother's honour, God will repel the Fire from his face**" [18].

**Practically, in a group chat.**

- **Truth is not a defence.** If it were false it would be worse, not permitted.
- **Say the thing, not the person.** Censure the behaviour as a category; that is obligatory. Naming someone absent is where it turns.
- **Screenshots and forwards are the aggravated form** — they are exact, permanent, and reach people who were never party to it.
- **Leaving is a defence.** You do not have to argue; you can decline to be an audience, and one line changing the subject is the *raddu 'an 'ird akhih* the hadith praises.
- **Do not go looking.** The spying prohibition sits in the same verse as the backbiting one, and 'Umar accepted the correction from a man he had just caught off guard.
- **If you have done it, seek forgiveness for them** — by name, in your own du'a. That is the repair the sources offer for what cannot be unsaid.

حدثنا ابن المثنى قال: ثنا محمد بن جعفر قال: ثنا شعبة قال: سمعت العلاء يحدث، عن أبيه، عن أبي هريرة، عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم قال: «هل تدرون ما الغيبة؟» قال: قالوا الله ورسوله أعلم؛ قال: «ذكرك أخاك بما ليس فيه»

قال: أرأيت إن كان في أخي ما أقول له؛ قال: «إن كان فيه ما تقول فقد اغتبته، وإن لم يكن فيه ما تقول فقد بهته»

حدثنا ابن المثنى قال: ثنا سعيد بن الربيع قال: ثنا شعبة، عن العباس، عن رجل، سمع ابن عمر، يقول : «إذا ذكرت الرجل بما فيه فقد اغتبته، وإذا ذكرته بما ليس فيه فقد بهته» وقال شعبة مرة أخرى: وإذا ذكرته بما ليس فيه، فهي فرية قال أبو موسى: هو عباس الجريري

حدثنا ابن المثنى قال: ثنا ابن أبي عدي، عن شعبة، عن سليمان، عن عبد الله بن مرة، عن مسروق قال: «إذا ذكرت الرجل بأسوأ ما فيه فقد اغتبته، وإذا ذكرته بما ليس فيه فقد بهته»

حدثنا ابن بشار قال: ثنا عبد الرحمن قال: ثنا سفيان، عن الأعمش، عن أبي الضحى، عن مسروق قال: «إذا قلت في الرجل أسوأ ما فيه فقد اغتبته، وإذا قلت ما ليس فيه فقد بهته»

حدثنا أبو كريب قال: ثنا عمر بن عبيد، عن الأعمش، عن أبي الضحى، عن مسروق قال: " الغيبة أن يقول للرجل أسوأ ما يعلم فيه، والبهتان: أن يقول ما ليس فيه "

حدثنا يونس بن عبد الأعلى قال: أخبرنا ابن وهب قال: أخبرني معاوية بن صالح، عن كثير بن الحارث، عن القاسم مولى معاوية قال: سمعت ابن أم عبد يقول: «ما التقم أحد لقمة أشر من اغتياب المؤمن، إن قال فيه ما يعلم فقد اغتابه، وإن قال فيه ما لا يعلم فقد بهته»

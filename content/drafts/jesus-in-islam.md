# What do Muslims believe about Jesus?

## Answer

**Short answer: Muslims hold Jesus in enormous honour — as a prophet, born of a
virgin, the Messiah, still alive and returning — and deny exactly one thing about
him: that he is divine.** If you have come from a Christian background, the
overlap is larger than you may expect and the difference is sharper than a
summary usually conveys.

**The core statement.** Qur'an 4:171: **"The Messiah, Jesus son of Mary, was only
a messenger of Allah, and His word which He conveyed unto Mary, and a spirit from
Him."** [1]. Three titles in one sentence: messenger, His word, a spirit from
Him. The same verse then draws the line: **"and say not "Three" - Cease! (it is)
better for you! - Allah is only One Allah. Far is it removed from His Transcendent
Majesty that He should have a son."** [2].

Note the verse's own framing — it opens by telling the People of the Scripture
not to *exaggerate* in their religion. The objection is to going too far, not to
esteeming him.

**What Muslims affirm about him.** The annunciation, in 3:45: **"O Mary! Lo!
Allah giveth thee glad tidings of a word from him, whose name is the Messiah,
Jesus, son of Mary, illustrious in the world and the Hereafter, and one of those
brought near (unto Allah)."** [3]. He is the Messiah, born to Mary without a
father, honoured in this world and the next. 19:34 closes the account of his
birth: **"Such was Jesus, son of Mary: (this is) a statement of the truth
concerning which they doubt."** [4].

The reports go further still. Al-Bukhari records: **"ما من بني آدم مولود الا
يمسه الشيطان حين يولد فيستهل صارخا من مس الشيطان غير مريم وابنها"** — *no child
of Adam is born but that Shaytan touches him at birth, so that he cries out from
that touch — except Mary and her son* [5]. Mary and Jesus alone are excepted.

**And how the Prophet ﷺ spoke of him.** **"انا أولى الناس بابن مريم والأنبياء
أولاد علات ليس بيني وبينه نبي"** — *I am the closest of people to the son of
Mary; the prophets are sons of one father by different mothers, and there is no
prophet between me and him* [6]; and **"والأنبياء اخوة لعلات أمهاتهم شتى ودينهم
واحد"** — *the prophets are brothers of one father, their mothers differing but
their religion one* [7]. That image — one father, different mothers — is the
Islamic account of the relationship between the prophets in a sentence.

**The one who was most careful about the boundary.** **"لا تطروني كما اطرت
النصارى ابن مريم فإنما انا عبده فقولوا عبد الله ورسوله"** — *do not overpraise me
as the Christians overpraised the son of Mary; I am only His servant, so say: the
servant of Allah and His Messenger* [8]. The warning is directed at his own
community, about himself. Whatever else it shows, the concern is not hostility
toward Jesus; it is a horror of the specific mistake.

**The crucifixion.** Ibn Taymiyya quotes the verse: **"وما قتلوه وما صلبوه ولكن
شبه لهم"** — *they slew him not nor crucified him, but it appeared so to them*
[9]. He argues from the wording *rather, Allah raised him to Himself* that both
body and soul were raised, and states: **"عيسى عليه السلام حى وقد ثبت في الصحيح
عن النبى انه قال ( ينزل فيكم بن مريم حكما عدلا وإماما مقسطا"** — *Jesus, peace
be upon him, is alive; it is established in the Sahih that the Prophet ﷺ said:
the son of Mary will descend among you as a just judge and an equitable leader*
[10].

**And the return.** Ibn Hajar reports the reason the scholars gave for it being
Jesus in particular: **"قال العلماء الحكمة في نزول عيسى دون غيره من الأنبياء الرد
على اليهود في زعمهم أنهم قتلوه"** — *the wisdom in Jesus descending rather than
another prophet is the refutation of those who claimed to have killed him* [11].

**So, the shape of the difference.** Muslims affirm the virgin birth, the
miracles, the title Messiah, his being raised, his being alive, and his return —
and deny the crucifixion and the divinity. It is not that Islam thinks less of
Jesus than Christianity does in most respects; it is that on one specific
question it says the opposite, and 4:171 puts both halves in a single verse [1][2].

**If you have converted from Christianity.** You are not required to think less
of Jesus; you are required to think of him as the verse at [1] does. Many reverts
find this the easiest part of the transition rather than the hardest, because so
much is retained. What changes is where the honour stops.

**On the language above:** "'Isa" is Jesus; "al-Masih" is the Messiah, a title
the Qur'an uses of him; "kalimatuhu" — His word — is the title at [1]; "'awlad
'allat" are half-brothers by one father, the image at [6]; "itra'" is excessive
praise, the word at [8].

**A note on scope.** The classical sources contain much more on his life, his
miracles and the details of his descent, and scholars differed over particulars —
how long he remains after returning, for instance, which Ibn Hajar surveys in the
passage at [11]. This answer gives what is central. If you are working through a
specific theological question from a Christian background, that is worth having
with a person rather than a page.

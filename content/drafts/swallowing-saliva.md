# Does swallowing spit break my fast?

## Answer

**No. Swallowing your own saliva does not break the fast, and there is no
disagreement worth reporting on it.**

**The reason.**

Ibn Qudama states it with the principle behind it: **what cannot be guarded against
— like swallowing saliva — does not break his fast, because avoiding that is
difficult; so it resembles the dust of the road, and the sifting of flour** [1].

That is the whole logic. The fast is a defined abstention, not an impossible one.
Anything unavoidable in the ordinary course of being alive is outside it — saliva,
dust in the street, flour in the air, your own breath.

Al-Nawawi cites the general rule his school works from, on the authority of Ibn
Abbas: **wudu is from what comes out, not from what goes in; and breaking the fast
is from what goes in, not from what comes out** [2]. Saliva never went in.

**Even if you gather it deliberately.**

Ibn Qudama addresses the scrupulous case: **and if he gathers it and then swallows
it intentionally, it does not break his fast, because it reaches his interior from
his stomach** [3] — it never left the body. He notes a second view within his school
that this would break it, on the grounds that it could have been avoided; but the
first is his position.

Al-Nawawi records the parallel discussion in the Shafi'i school: **as for when the
saliva flows with it and he swallows it without intent, al-Muzani transmitted that
it does not break the fast** [4], noting a second transmission the other way, and
that the majority took the first.

So: even the deliberate case is disputed at worst, and the ordinary case is not
disputed at all.

**What this rules out worrying about.**

- Swallowing normally, all day.
- Saliva that tastes of something you ate at suhoor.
- Phlegm you swallow without thinking.
- Dust, smoke from someone else's cigarette, cooking smells, or an insect that flies
  into your mouth — all covered by the same "cannot be guarded against" principle.

**The one thing that does break it** is bringing something back into the mouth that
had properly left it, or swallowing something that is not saliva. Al-Nawawi:
**if the fasting person swallows what is not normally eaten — a dirham, a dinar,
earth, a pebble, grass, fire, iron, thread or anything else — he breaks the fast,
with no disagreement among us** [5], and he names Abu Hanifa, Malik, Ahmad and the
great body of scholars with him.

**Food stuck between the teeth is different**, and worth separating out since it
comes up constantly. Al-Nawawi: **if food remains in the gaps of his teeth he should
pick them at night and clean his mouth; and if he wakes up fasting with something in
the gaps of his teeth and swallows it deliberately, he breaks the fast, with no
disagreement among us** [6]. He notes the Hanafi dissent — **Abu Hanifa said: he
does not break the fast** [7] — on the reasoning that some residue is unavoidable.

Practical upshot: brush or rinse properly at suhoor so there is nothing left to
decide about. What you cannot get out and swallow without thinking is covered by
the saliva principle above.

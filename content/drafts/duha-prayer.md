# What is the duha prayer?

## Answer

**A voluntary prayer in the mid-morning — after the sun is well clear of the
horizon, before it reaches midday. Two rak'ahs, or more.**

**The basic shape.**

Al-Nawawi gives the whole of it in one line: **the duha prayer is a confirmed
sunna, its least is two rak'ahs and its most is eight rak'ahs** [1]. He adds
**and he gives the salam after every two rak'ahs, and intends two rak'ahs of
duha** [2] — so it is prayed in pairs, not as one block — and sets the window:
**its time is from the rising of the sun until the sun passes the meridian** [3].

**On the number, the schools differ a little.**

Al-Fatawa al-Hindiyya lists it **among the recommended prayers is the duha
prayer** [4], and gives a wider ceiling: **its least is two rak'ahs and its most
twelve rak'ahs, and its time is from the rising of the sun until its passing the
meridian** [5]. In his commentary on Muslim, al-Nawawi lays out the middle
ground: **duha is a confirmed sunna, its least is two rak'ahs and its fullest
eight rak'ahs, and between them four or six** [6].

The reports support the open end. Aisha: **the Messenger of God used to pray duha
four, and add as much as God willed** [7]. And Umm Hani, on the day Mecca was
taken: **he prayed eight rak'ahs — I never saw him pray a prayer lighter than
them** [8]. Anywhere from two rak'ahs upward is inside what the sources describe.

**Its best moment.**

Al-Nawawi cites Zayd ibn Arqam for the choicest time: **the prayer of the
oft-returning is when the young camels feel the heat of the sand** [9] — that is,
mid-to-late morning once the ground has warmed, rather than the moment the sun
clears the horizon.

**Its merit.**

Ibn Hajar explains the report that every joint of the body owes a charity each
day: **the duha prayer stands in place of the three hundred and sixty good
deeds** [10].

**And the reports that seem to go the other way.**

Ibn Abd al-Barr preserves the other side honestly. Ibn Umar said **I have not
prayed duha since I became Muslim** [11], and Aisha said in Muslim's collection
**I never saw the Messenger of God pray the duha prayer at all, though I myself
pray it** [12].

Al-Nawawi resolves this: the Prophet prayed it at some times and left it at
others out of concern it would be made obligatory, and Aisha was reporting what
she saw in her own house. His conclusion: **the majority of scholars hold duha to
be recommended** [13]. Ibn Abd al-Barr also preserves Aisha's own practice —
**but even if my father were raised up for me, I would not leave them** [14].

**In practice:** two rak'ahs, any time between the sun rising well clear and
midday, and more pairs if you want them. A sunna, not a duty.

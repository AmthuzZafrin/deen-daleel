# Can I pray if I can't read Arabic yet?

## Answer

Yes. Pray tonight.

Someone brought this exact problem to the Prophet. Al-Qurtubi records it: a man
came and said, **I cannot take anything of the Qur'an — teach me what will
suffice me.** [1] He was not sent away to study first. He was given words:
**subhan Allah, wa'l-hamdu li-Llah, wa la ilaha illa Llah, wa Allahu akbar, wa
la hawla wa la quwwata illa bi-Llah.** [2]

Then the man said something that people rarely quote and probably should. **O
Messenger of God, this is for God — what is for me?** [3] And he was given a
second set: **O God, have mercy on me, keep me well, guide me, and provide for
me.** [4] Ask for yourself. It was asked for on your behalf first.

**If even those five phrases are more than you have this week**, al-Qurtubi has
that case too: **let him not abandon praying with the imam as best he can — the
imam carries that for him.** [5]

And then the sentence to hold onto. **He must always push himself to learn the
Opening of the Book and what is beyond it, until death comes between him and it
while he is still striving — and God excuses him.** [6] The standard is not
arrival. It is that you were still working at it.

**Du'a in your own language is not a compromise.** Al-Qurtubi again: whoever's
tongue does not come to Arabic, **the Arabic supplication is rendered for him in
the language he understands, in order to establish his prayer — and that suffices
him.** [7] Everything outside the fixed recitation is available to you now, in
English.

**The honest other half.** This is a dispensation for inability, not a
permanent arrangement, and the sources are firm about it. Al-Nawawi: someone
unable to recite the Fatiha **is obliged to acquire the ability — by learning it,
or by obtaining a copy to read it from.** [8] He goes on to say that a person who
could have and did not is sinning and owes the prayers again. The excuse tracks
the inability, and it ends when the inability does.

**How it is actually learned** — al-Qurtubi describes the mechanism in passing,
discussing someone who starts a prayer unable to recite and finds mid-prayer that
he can: **he had heard someone reciting it, and it stuck in his memory from mere
listening.** [9] That is still how it works. Put a recitation of al-Fatiha on
repeat while you cook. It goes in sideways, without a lesson.

You are not waiting for permission to pray. You are praying while you learn.

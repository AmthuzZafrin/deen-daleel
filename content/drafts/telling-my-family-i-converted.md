# How do I tell my family I've become Muslim?

## Answer

**You do not have to tell them today.** Start there, because most people asking
this have already decided they are obliged to and are only asking how.

Sahih Muslim has a chapter titled **the concealment of one who fears** [1] — and
the report in it is Hudhayfa's: the Prophet asked for a count of those who had
professed Islam, and when the Companions protested that they were six or seven
hundred, he said, **you do not know; perhaps you will be tested.** [2] Hudhayfa
finishes: **and we were tested, until a man among us would not pray except in
secret.** [3]

That is not a loophole someone found later. It is a chapter heading, and the
people in it are the Companions. If your circumstances make disclosure dangerous
or simply impossible right now, you are in a situation the tradition has a name
for.

**When you do tell them, the manner is prescribed and the manner is gentle.**
*Call unto the way of thy Lord with wisdom and fair exhortation, and reason with
them in the better way.* [4] Wisdom, which includes timing. Fair exhortation,
which is not argument. And where there is disagreement, *the better way* — a
comparative, meaning there is always a worse one available and you are told not
to take it.

**Nothing about your obligations to them changes.** Al-Jassas notes how tightly
the Qur'an binds the two: **God joined the duty of kindness to parents to His own
worship and His oneness, commanding it as He commanded them.** [5]

And on parents who are actively against your religion — the verse says *if they
strive with you to associate with Me that of which you have no knowledge, do not
obey them, and keep them company in this world honourably*. Al-Jassas draws out
what is easy to miss: **God commanded good companionship with them in the very
state in which they are pressing him toward disbelief.** [6] The instruction to
treat them well was issued *for* the argument, not for the peace after it.

**The one limit, stated plainly.** Al-Jassas again: **obedience to parents is
obligatory in what is right, not in disobedience to God — for there is no
obedience to a creature in disobedience to the Creator.** [7] If they demand you
give it up, that request is the one thing you do not owe them. Everything else,
you still do.

**Practical, and not from the texts** — the sources give you the manner and the
limits, not a script:

- One person first, usually the one most likely to hear it, rather than the whole
  family at a table.
- Say what you have done, not what they should do. You are announcing, not
  arguing; 16:125 is about calling, and calling has its own occasion.
- Expect the first reaction not to be the settled one, and do not answer it as
  though it were.
- Keep doing the ordinary things — showing up, helping, being reachable. That is
  what the verses above actually ask for, and it does more over two years than
  any conversation does in one evening.

If you judge that telling them now is unsafe, that judgement is yours to make and
the tradition does not overrule it.

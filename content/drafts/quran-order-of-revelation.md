# Why isn't the Quran in chronological order?

## Answer

**The Qur'an is not arranged in the order it was revealed, and the sources are entirely open about that.** The first revelation was "Read: In the name of thy Lord Who createth" [1] — which sits at *sura* 96, near the end of the book. Ibn Hajar confirms the sequence: "the first that was sent down was *Read: in the name of thy Lord*, and the revelation of *al-Fatiha* was after that" [2].

**The reason the order runs the way it does is the most useful thing on this page**, and it comes from 'A'isha. Ibn Hajar, summarising her point: "she indicated the divine wisdom in the order of revelation — that the first of the Qur'an to be sent down was the call to God's oneness, and glad tidings to the believer and the obedient of the Garden, and to the disbeliever and the disobedient of the Fire; then, **when souls had settled upon that, the rulings were sent down**. And for this she said: had the first thing sent down been *do not drink wine*, they would have said *we will not leave it* — that being on account of what souls are constituted with, of aversion to abandoning the familiar" [3].

That is a deliberate pedagogy: conviction first, then practice. For anyone who has just come to Islam and is being handed a list of obligations, it is worth knowing that the Qur'an itself did not arrive that way.

**Where the ordering is settled and where it is disputed.** Two distinct questions, and the sources keep them apart carefully.

The order of *verses within* a *sura* is not in dispute. Al-Nawawi, quoting Qadi 'Iyad: "**there is no disagreement** that the order of the verses of each *sura* is by direct instruction from God, as they now stand in the *mushaf*; and thus the community transmitted it from its Prophet" [4]. Ibn Hajar says the same [5].

The order of the *suras* is a genuine disagreement. Al-Nawawi reports the majority view: "this is the position of Malik and the majority of the scholars, and Qadi Abu Bakr al-Baqillani chose it. Ibn al-Baqillani said: **it is the sounder of the two positions, though both are possible**" [6]. Al-Baqillani's own statement of it: "the order of the *suras* is not obligatory in writing, nor in the prayer, nor in study, nor in instruction and teaching; and there was no text from the Prophet on that, nor a limit whose violation is forbidden — **and for that reason the order of the *masahif* differed before 'Uthman's *mushaf***" [7].

Those earlier arrangements are on record. Ibn Kathir: "and the beginning of Ibn Mas'ud's *mushaf* was *Master of the Day of Judgment*, then *al-Baqara*, then *al-Nisa'*, in a different arrangement; and the beginning of Ubayy's *mushaf* was *Praise be to God*, then *al-Nisa'*, then *Al 'Imran*, then *al-An'am*, then *al-Ma'ida*" [8]. And, most striking: "we have mentioned from 'Ali that he had resolved to arrange the Qur'an according to its revelation" [9].

**'Uthman explains one of his own placements, and it is a small masterpiece of candour.** Ibn 'Abbas asked why *al-Anfal* and *Bara'a* were joined without a *basmala* between them. 'Uthman: "the Messenger of God would often have a *sura* of some length sent down to him, and when something of it came down he would call one of those who wrote and say: *put these verses in the *sura* in which such-and-such is mentioned*. And *al-Anfal* was among the first to be sent down in Madina, and *Bara'a* among the last of the Qur'an, and its subject resembled it — **so I supposed it was part of it. And the Messenger of God passed away, and did not make clear to us that it was part of it**" [10]. Ibn Hajar's reading: "this indicates that the order of the verses in each *sura* was by direct instruction; and since the Prophet did not clarify the matter of *Bara'a*, 'Uthman joined it to *al-Anfal* **by his own judgement**" [11].

There is also a neat mechanical detail: "the Prophet did not know the ending of a *sura* until *In the name of God, the Merciful, the Compassionate* came down; and in another narration: when *bismillah al-rahman al-rahim* came down, they knew that the *sura* had ended" [12].

**A practical consequence.** Because the *sura* order is not binding, reading them out of order is fine: "rather it is permissible to recite *al-Kahf* before *al-Baqara*, and *al-Hajj* before *al-Kahf*". And the well-known prohibition on reciting "backwards" means something narrower than people assume: "what the early scholars reported prohibiting, of reciting the Qur'an inverted — what is meant by it is that one recites from the end of a *sura* to its beginning" [13].

**Makkan and Madinan.** The classification is by period rather than place: "the more likely view is that everything revealed after the Emigration is counted as Madinan" [14]. And it is not always agreed: "there is disagreement over *al-Fatiha*, *al-Rahman*, *al-Mutaffifin*, *Idha zulzilat*, *al-'Adiyat*, *al-Qadr*, *Ara'ayta*, *al-Ikhlas* and the two *mu'awwidhatayn*; and likewise there is disagreement over *al-Saff*, *al-Jumu'a* and *al-Taghabun*" [15]. Ibn Hajar also notes that Makkan *suras* frequently contain Madinan verses, and lists them at length — so even the labels are approximations.

**So, to summarise.** The book you hold is arranged one way and was revealed another. The verse order inside each *sura* is fixed by revelation and not in dispute. The arrangement of the *suras* is, on the majority view, the Companions' work — with 'Uthman himself on record explaining one placement as his own judgement and saying plainly that the Prophet had not settled it. Reading them in any order is permitted. And the shape of the revelation itself, according to 'A'isha, was deliberate: belief established first, rules afterwards, because a community told to give up wine on day one would have refused.

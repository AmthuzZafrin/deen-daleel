# I forgot and ate something — is my fast broken?

## Answer

**No. Your fast is intact. Keep fasting, and there is nothing to make up.**

This is one of the clearest rulings in the whole subject, and the wording of the
hadith is unusually warm.

**The hadith.**

Abu Hurayra reports in Sahih Muslim: **whoever forgets while he is fasting and eats
or drinks, let him complete his fast — for it was only God who fed him and gave him
drink** [1].

Ibn Qudama has the same, and a second wording: **whoever eats or drinks forgetfully
does not break the fast, for it is only provision that God provided him** [2].

Notice what the text does. It does not say "you are excused" — it reassigns the act
entirely. What happened was not your eating; it was God feeding you. There is
nothing in that framing to feel guilty about.

**The ruling.**

Ibn Qudama: **the sum of it is that everything al-Khiraqi mentioned in this
question does not break the fast of the fasting person by his doing it
forgetfully** [3]. He names those who held it: Ali, Abu Hurayra, Ibn Umar, Ata',
Tawus, al-Awza'i, al-Thawri, al-Shafi'i, Abu Hanifa and Ishaq.

The wording Ibn Qudama gives as the agreed-upon one is the same instruction from
the other side: **when one of you eats or drinks forgetfully, let him complete his
fast — for it was only God who fed him and gave him drink** [4].

**And if you were asleep.**

Ibn Qudama covers this too: **if he does any of that while asleep, his fast is not
invalidated, because he has no intention and no awareness of the fast — so he is
more excusable than the one who forgets** [5].

**One dissenting view, worth knowing.**

Ibn Qudama reports it honestly: **Rabi'a and Malik said: he breaks the fast** [6] —
their reasoning being that what cannot be done deliberately cannot be excused when
done inadvertently. It is a minority position and the majority rejected it on the
strength of the hadith. Al-Kasani records that Abu Hanifa said he would have ruled
for a make-up on pure analogy, and abandoned the analogy because the text was too
sound to argue with.

So if you follow Maliki fiqh you may hear otherwise; on every other school, and on
the plain sense of the hadith, your fast stands.

**What to do in the moment.**

Stop as soon as you realise. Rinse your mouth if you need to. Continue the fast to
sunset. Do not make the day up, and do not "start again tomorrow" — the day counts.

**One caution.** The exemption is for genuine forgetting. Ibn Qudama notes that if
you eat *deliberately* after having eaten forgetfully — on the reasoning that the
day is ruined anyway — that is a different matter, and al-Nasafi lists it among the
cases requiring a make-up. The day is not ruined. Carry on.

Qur'an 6:145 — Surah Al-An'aam, verse 145 of chapter 6

Translation (Marmaduke Pickthall):
Say: I find not in that which is revealed unto me aught prohibited to an eater that he eat thereof, except it be carrion, or blood poured forth, or swineflesh - for that verily is foul - or the abomination which was immolated to the name of other than Allah. But whoso is compelled (thereto), neither craving nor transgressing, (for him) lo! thy Lord is Forgiving, Merciful.

# What does Islam say about sport and exercise?

## Answer

Encouraged — and more warmly than most people expect. This is one of the few areas of ordinary life where the sources do not merely permit but *command*, and where the Prophet ﷺ is recorded doing the thing himself.

**He raced.**

Ibn 'Umar: "the Messenger of Allah ﷺ **raced the horses that had been conditioned**, from al-Hafya', their finish being Thaniyyat al-Wada'; and he raced the horses that had not been conditioned, from the Thaniyya to the mosque of Banu Zurayq — **and Ibn 'Umar was among those who raced in it**" [1].

Two race distances, a graded field by fitness, a marked start and finish, and a Companion competing. That is an organised athletics meeting, held by the Prophet ﷺ.

He also permitted a footrace between two Companions on the road back to Medina. Salama b. al-Akwa' tells it: an Ansari who was never outrun kept calling for a challenger; Salama asked leave — "**he said: if you wish.** So I dismounted and he set off running, and I held myself back for a rise or two, then I ran and caught him and struck him between the shoulders and said: I have outrun you, by God. **And he looked at me and laughed**" [2]. The report ends with the Prophet ﷺ saying: *the best of our horsemen is Abu Qatada, and the best of our men on foot is Salama b. al-Akwa'.*

And Ibn 'Abd al-Barr adds: "**it is established that the Prophet ﷺ raced with 'A'isha on foot**" [3] — with his own criterion following immediately: "whatever is of this and its like by way of exertion and training in running, and preparation against the enemy, **or by way of amusement** — not by way of wagering — **there is no harm in it**; and whatever is by way of wagering is not permitted" [3].

Note that the licence is not only for the militarily useful. *Or by way of amusement* is in the sentence.

**Sport is one of the named exceptions to the whole category of "play".**

'Ata' b. Abi Rabah saw Jabir b. 'Abdullah and Jabir b. 'Umayr shooting; one of them tired and sat, and the other said: *you've gone slack?* — and then quoted the Prophet ﷺ: "**everything in which there is no remembrance of God is diversion and play — except four: a man's playing with his wife, a man's training of his horse, his walking between the two targets, and a man's teaching of swimming**" [4].

Look at that list. Marriage, horsemanship, archery, and **swimming**. Two of the four are straightforwardly what we would call sport, and one is a life skill taught in a pool. Another wording gives the same four with "walking between the two targets" first [5].

The three-item version is even better known: "**there is no diversion except in three: a man's training of his horse, and his playing with his wife, and his shooting with his bow and arrows** — and whoever abandons archery after having learnt it, out of disinclination for it, **it is a blessing he has been ungrateful for**" [6].

That last clause is the striking one. Letting a physical skill you had lapse is treated as ingratitude for a favour.

**And there is a command, not just a licence.**

'Uqba b. 'Amir transmitted: "**shoot and ride — and that you should shoot is more beloved to me than that you should ride**" [7], within the same report that promises "God enters three into Paradise by a single arrow", and in which the Prophet ﷺ said: "**know that strength is shooting; know that strength is shooting**" [8].

Ibn Qudama explains why prizes are permitted for horses, camels and archery: "they were singled out for permitting a prize in them **because they are instruments of war, which one is commanded to learn and master and excel in**" [9] — and cites *and prepare against them what force you can* as the ground.

The reasoning is capability. Bodily competence is not a distraction from religion in these texts; it is something one is *commanded* to acquire and blamed for losing.

**The affection in the reports is unmistakable.**

Anas: "**nothing was more beloved to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ, after women, than horses**" [10]. And Jarir saw him "twisting the forelock of his horse between his two fingers, saying: **good is tied to the forelocks of horses until the Day of Resurrection**" [11].

**The one restriction, and it is the same one as everywhere else in this section.**

Money staked by the competitors. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr again: what is done for training or for enjoyment is fine, "and whatever is by way of wagering is not permitted" [3]. Al-Shafi'i's list of examples in the same passage is worth having: foot-racing, birds, stone-throwing, wrestling, standing on one's feet for an hour — for a stake the two of them put up, "**all of this is not permitted, and what is taken for it is consuming wealth wrongfully**" — with the licence for prizes confined, on his reading, to hoof, foot and arrow [3].

So the structure matters, not the sport: a prize from a sponsor or an organiser is lawful; a pot the competitors have both paid into is gambling.

**Practically.**

- **Train. Run, lift, swim, ride, shoot, play.** This is encouraged in the plainest terms the sources use for ordinary life, and swimming is named.
- **A skill you let go is a blessing you were ungrateful for** — that is how the archery hadith puts it. Fitness is not vanity here.
- **Competition is fine; wagering is not.** Enter tournaments, chase medals, keep a league table. Do not put money in a pot that the winner takes.
- **Prize money from an organiser, club or sponsor is the lawful form**, and the majority permitted it broadly.
- **The ordinary conditions of Muslim life still apply** — prayer times, what is covered, and the company. None of that is special to sport.
- **Enjoyment is a stated reason on its own.** Ibn 'Abd al-Barr allows the footrace *by way of amusement* alongside training. You do not need to justify a game of football by its military utility.

```
Qur'an 23:38 — Surah Al-Muminoon, verse 38 of chapter 23

Translation (Marmaduke Pickthall):
He is only a man who hath invented a lie about Allah. We are not going to put faith in him.

إِنْ هُوَ إِلَّا رَجُلٌ ٱفْتَرَىٰ عَلَى ٱللَّهِ كَذِبًۭا وَمَا نَحْنُ لَهُۥ بِمُؤْمِنِينَ
```

### Passage 28 — Al-Tamhid 21:292

- kind: hadith · source: Al-Tamhid · chunk: 370078
- found via: "كل شيء ليس من ذكر الله فهو لهو ولعب"

```
Al-Tamhid 21:292

Arabic:
سمعته يقول عند انصرافه من الصلاة لا إله إلا الله وحده لا شريك له له الملك وله الحمد وهو على كل شيء قدير ثلاث مرات وكان ينهى عن قيل وقال وكثرة السؤال وإضاعة المال ومنع وهات وعقوق الأمهات ووأد البنات قال أبو عمر قد مضى فيما يحل من السؤال وما لا يحل أبواب كافية فيما سلف من هذا الكتاب والسؤال إذا لم يحل فلا يحل منه الكثير ولا   القليل (128) وإذا كان جائزا حلالا فلا بأس بالإكثار منه حتى يبلغ إلى الحد المنهي عنه والله أعلم وقد كان رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم يكره كثرة المسائل ويعيبها والانفكاك عندي من هذا المعنى والانفصال من هذا السؤال والإدخال أن السؤال اليوم لا يخاف منه أن ينزل تحريم ولا تحليل من أجله فمن سأل مستفهما راغبا في العلم ونفي الجهل عن نفسه باحثا عن معنى يجب الوقوف في الديانة عليه فلا بأس به فشفاء العي السؤال ومن سأل معنتا غير متفقه ولا متعلم فهذا لا يحل قليل سؤاله ولا كثيرة وقد أوضحنا هذه المعاني كلها في كتاب العلم (129) بما لا سبيل إلى ذكره ههنا وأما قوله وإضاعة المال فللعلماء في تأويل معناه ثلاثة أقوال (130) أحدها أنه أراد بذكر المال ههنا الحيوان من ملك اليمين أن يحسن إليهم ولا يضيعون فيهلكون وهذا قول رواه السري بن إسماعيل عن الشعبي
```

### Passage 30 — Jami' al-Tirmidhi 5:461-462

- kind: hadith · source: Jami' al-Tirmidhi · chunk: 339953
- found via: "كل شيء ليس من ذكر الله فهو لهو ولعب"

```
Jami' al-Tirmidhi 5:461-462 — أبواب الدعوات › (١) »

Arabic:
رجليه قبل أن يتكلم: لا إله إلا الله، وحده لا شريك له، له الملك وله الحمد، يحيي ويميت، وهو على كل شيء قدير، عشر مرات، كتبت له عشر حسنات، ومحيت عنه عشر

سيئات، ورفع له عشر درجات، وكان يومه ذلك كله في حرز من كل مكروه، وحرس من الشيطان، ولم ينبغ لذنب أن يدركه في ذلك اليوم إلا الشرك بالله.

هذا حديث حسن صحيح غريب.
```

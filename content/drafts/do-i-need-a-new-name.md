# Do I have to change my name?

## Answer

No. Keep your name.

It is true that the Prophet changed a number of people's names, and that is
probably what you have heard. But Ibn Hajar, reporting al-Tabari on exactly this,
draws the distinction that matters: **what he changed of those names was not by
way of forbidding them, but by way of preference.** [1]

The evidence al-Tabari gives for that is a Companion who said no. A man named
Hazn — the word means *rough ground* — was offered Sahl, *smooth*, instead, and
answered that he would not change a name his father had given him. **Had it been
binding, the Prophet would not have let that stand.** [2] He did.

What the guidance actually turns on is **meaning**, not language or origin. Three
kinds of name are called unfitting: one **ugly in meaning, one that praises its
bearer, and one whose meaning is an insult**. [3] Nothing in that list is about
whether a name is Arabic. Most names in the world pass it without alteration.

There is a hadith recommending good names — *you will be called on the Day of
Rising by your names and your fathers' names, so make your names good* [4] —
and Ibn Hajar notes in the same breath that **its chain has a break in it**. [5]
Worth knowing, and worth knowing that he says so.

**Your father's name is not a problem to be solved. It is the instruction.** Ibn
al-'Arabi, on the verse *call them by their fathers, that is more equitable in
God's sight*, records Ibn 'Umar saying: **we used to call Zayd b. Haritha nothing
but Zayd b. Muhammad, until the verse was revealed.** [6] The Prophet's own
adopted son went back to using his birth father's name, by revelation. Whatever
else conversion asks of you, cutting yourself out of your family's line is not
part of it.

**Where it is worth a conversation.** If your name means something a Muslim would
not want to be called — servant of a god other than God is the usual example, or
a meaning that is outright abusive — that is the one case where the rule above
actually bites, and it is worth putting your specific name to someone who knows
its language. And if you *want* an Arabic name because you like it or it means
something to you, nothing here stands in your way; that is a preference, which is
exactly what al-Tabari said it was.

Changing your name on documents is a matter for the country you live in, and it
has nothing to do with whether your Islam is in order.

هذه بشارة من الملائكة لمريم، عليها السلام، بأن سيوجد منها ولد عظيم، له شأن كبير. قال الله تعالى: {إذ قالت الملائكة يا مريم إن الله يبشرك بكلمة منه} أي: بولد يكون وجوده بكلمة من الله، أي: بقوله له: "كن" فيكون، وهذا تفسير قوله: {مصدقا بكلمة من الله} [آل عمران: 39] كما ذكره الجمهور على ما سبق بيانه {اسمه المسيح عيسى ابن مريم} أي يكون مشهورا بهذا في الدنيا، يعرفه المؤمنون بذلك.

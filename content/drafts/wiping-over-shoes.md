# Can I wipe over shoes?

## Answer

**No — not ordinary shoes.** Wiping is a concession attached to a particular kind
of footwear, and shoes as most people wear them do not meet it. Take them off and
wash your feet.

**Bukhari settles it with a chapter heading**, which is his way of ruling. He
titles a chapter *washing the feet inside the sandals*, and adds: *and one does
not wipe over sandals*. Ibn Hajar explains what that second clause is denying:
**his words "and one does not wipe over the sandals" mean that wiping over them
is not sufficient, as it is with the khuffs.** [1]

There *is* a report of the Prophet wiping over sandals, and it is worth knowing
why it does not carry the ruling. Ibn Hajar: **a marfu' hadith was narrated on
this, transmitted by Abu Dawud and others from al-Mughira ibn Shu'ba, but Abd
al-Rahman ibn Mahdi and other imams weakened it.** [2] And al-Tahawi argued
from consensus in the other direction: **al-Tahawi argued for its insufficiency
from the agreement that when the khuffs are torn so that the feet show, wiping
over them does not suffice.** [3] If a torn khuff fails, an open sandal fails all
the more.

**Why washing the feet is the demanding default here.** Ibn Taymiyya:
**washing the feet in wudu is transmitted from the Prophet by mass transmission —
his practice of it is transmitted, and his command to it.** [4] Wiping over
footwear is a licence carved out of that, and licences are read narrowly.

**So what does qualify?** Footwear that covers the ankle, that you can walk in,
and that you put on while in full wudu — that is a *khuff*, and it is permitted
however humble. Ibn Taymiyya, arguing against people who piled conditions onto it:
**what his wording requires is that every khuff people wear and walk in, they may
wipe over.** [5] Ankle boots and leather socks fall inside this. There is a
separate answer here on wiping generally, and another on which socks qualify.

Ordinary shoes fail on the first test: they leave the ankle and usually the top
of the foot exposed. What is exposed must be washed, and once you are washing the
foot the shoe is beside the point.

**The one narrow exception in the books.** Ibn Taymiyya records: **it is
transmitted from him that he wiped over the feet in a place of need — such as
having sandals on his feet that are difficult to remove.** [6] Note every part of
that: *a place of need*, and *difficult to remove*. It is a hardship allowance,
not a convenience, and it is a minority report. If you are in boots you genuinely
cannot get off — working, in the cold, injured — you have something to lean on.
If you are simply in a hurry at the office, you do not.

**And if you do wipe on qualifying footwear, wipe the top.** This is
counter-intuitive and the Companions said so out loud. Ali: **if the religion
went by opinion, the underside of the feet would have more right to be wiped than
the top of them — and the Prophet wiped over the top of his khuffs.** [7] So:
wet hands, over the upper surface, toes toward the ankle. Nothing else.

**A practical note if this is a problem at work**, which it usually is: the
answer is not to wipe over your shoes, it is to wear socks that qualify and put
them on at home in a state of wudu. Then the shoes come off, the socks stay on,
and you wipe over those — for a day and a night, which covers a working day with
room to spare [8].

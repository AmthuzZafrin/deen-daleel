# Do I owe fasts from before I converted?

## Answer

No. Not the Ramadans, and not the days.

Ibn Qudama puts both halves in one sentence: **fasting the rest of the month
ahead of him — there is no disagreement about that; and making up what passed of
the month before his Islam — that is not obligatory.** [1] He then names who
holds it: **al-Sha'bi, Qatada, Malik, al-Awza'i, al-Shafi'i, Abu Thawr and the
people of opinion. 'Ata' said he must make it up** [2] — a lone position,
recorded rather than hidden.

**The principle behind it is clean.** What passed was **an act of worship whose
time ran out during his disbelief, so making it up is not required of him — like
a past Ramadan.** [3] The obligation attaches to people who are inside it. You
were not, so there is nothing to reconstruct.

Al-Nawawi reaches the same place from the Shafi'i side: **when one of them
becomes Muslim, making up the past is not required of him.** [4] His ground is
the verse *if they cease, what is past will be forgiven them* — and a second
reason worth knowing, that imposing it would drive people away.

**A note on that verse, since this application will publish it.** Pickthall
renders it *Tell those who disbelieve that if they cease (from persecution of
believers) that which is past will be forgiven them* [5] — reading the ceasing as
ceasing hostility. The jurists cite it for ceasing disbelief. Both readings live
in the tradition; the ruling above does not rest on the verse alone, and Ibn
Qudama reaches it without using it at all.

**The one point genuinely in dispute is the day itself** — the day you said the
shahada, part of which you spent not fasting. Ibn Qudama gives Ahmad's recorded
position: **he must abstain for the rest of that day and also make it up**, and
al-Majishun and Ishaq agreed. [6] Against that, **Malik, Abu Thawr and Ibn
al-Mundhir held there is nothing to make up** [7] — their argument being that he
never had a portion of the day in which he could have begun.

If you converted mid-Ramadan and did not know this at the time, notice how small
the disputed quantity is. One day, and only on one school's view.

**What is not covered here.** Fasts missed *after* becoming Muslim — through
illness, travel, or simply not doing it — are a different question with a
different answer, and it is in this collection.

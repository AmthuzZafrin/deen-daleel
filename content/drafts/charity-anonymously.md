# Is it better to give charity secretly?

## Answer

For voluntary charity, yes — decisively. For obligatory zakat, the classical answer was the opposite, and the reasoning behind that reversal is worth following, because several scholars concluded it no longer holds.

**Voluntary giving: hide it.** Ibn Qudama states it flatly — **charity in secret is better than charity in public** [1] — resting on the verse of al-Baqara and on the hadith of the seven whom God shelters on the Day when there is no shelter but His. Al-Nawawi gives the wording: **and a man who gave a charity and concealed it, so that his right hand did not know what his left was spending** [2]. Ibn Qudama also relays: **charity in secret extinguishes the anger of the Lord** [3].

Ibn Hajar notes that this hadith, not the verse, is **the strongest evidence for the superiority of concealing charity** [4] — the verse being read by most as concerning voluntary giving specifically.

**Why concealment matters here.** Al-Qurtubi gives the reason, and it is about you rather than about the recipient: **the majority of the exegetes held that this verse concerns voluntary charity, because concealment in it is better than display — and likewise all acts of worship, concealment is better in their voluntary forms, because ostentation is removed from them; and it is not so with the obligatory ones** [5]. A voluntary act has no external reason to be seen. So if it is seen, the question of why arises — and that question is the danger.

There is a second reason, less discussed and just as real: **it protects the person receiving.** Charity that arrives without a face attached does not put the recipient in your debt or make them your project.

**Obligatory zakat: the classical answer was to show it.** Ibn Hajar: **al-Tabari and others transmitted the consensus that openness in obligatory charity is better than concealment, and voluntary charity is the reverse of that** [6]. The logic was social. Zakat was a public institution collected by agents of the state; a man who paid quietly was suspected of not paying at all, and open payment both cleared him and encouraged others. Ostentation was not the risk, since he was only doing what he was known to be obliged to do anyway.

**And several scholars concluded the reason had lapsed.** Ibn Hajar records Ibn 'Atiyya: **it seems that in our time concealing the obligatory charity is better, for those who withhold it have become many, and paying it openly has become exposed to ostentation** [7]. Ibn Hajar makes the institutional point himself: the early Muslims handed their zakat to the collectors, so a man who concealed it was accused of not paying — **but today everyone pays his zakat himself, so concealing it has become better** [8].

That reasoning applies more strongly now than when he wrote it. There is no collector at your door. Announcing your zakat proves nothing to anyone and risks a great deal to you.

**So, practically:**

- **Default to giving quietly.** Standing orders, anonymous donations, cash left where it will be found — the tradition is unambiguously behind you.
- **Do not conclude that public giving is forbidden.** The verse of al-Baqara calls open charity good before calling hidden charity better. A public campaign that moves others to give is a real good; several fundraising traditions depend on it.
- **The test is what the visibility is for.** Ibn Hajar preserves a dissenting reading of the verse itself: **Yazid b. Abi Habib differed and said the verse was revealed concerning charity to the Jews and the Christians** [9] — given openly to them, secretly to one's own poor. The tradition kept more than one reading alive here.
- **A middle path exists and is widely practised:** be open about the fact that you give and about the cause, and private about the amount. That encourages without exposing.

The one arrangement the sources would question is the one that is easiest to fall into now: giving in a way designed to be seen, and calling the visibility encouragement. Only you can audit that, which is precisely why concealment is recommended — it removes the need to.

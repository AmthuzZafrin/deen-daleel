# What do I say when someone sneezes?

## Answer

When someone sneezes and praises Allah, you say **yarhamuk Allah** — may Allah have mercy on you. He replies **yahdikum Allah wa yuslih balakum** — may Allah guide you and set your affairs right. That is the exchange.

**The three-part formula.**

Abu Hurayra reported the Prophet ﷺ saying: "When one of you sneezes, let him say *al-hamdu lillah 'ala kulli hal*; and let his brother or his companion say *yarhamuk Allah*; and let him say *yahdikum Allah wa yuslih balakum*" [1].

There is room in each part. Al-Qadi 'Iyad, via al-Nawawi: "the scholars differed on the form of the praise and the reply, and the reports differ on it. It was said he says *al-hamdu lillah*; and it was said *al-hamdu lillah rabb al-'alamin*; and it was said *al-hamdu lillah 'ala kulli hal*. Ibn Jarir said he has a choice among all of this, and this is the correct view — and they agreed that he is commanded to praise Allah" [2].

And on the sneezer's reply: "they differed on the sneezer's response to the one who blessed him. It was said he says *yahdikum Allah wa yuslih balakum*; and it was said he says *yaghfir Allahu lana wa lakum*. Malik and al-Shafi'i said he chooses between these two, and this is correct, for the hadiths on both are authentic" [3].

So any of these is right. Learn one and use it.

**What the word means.**

*Tashmit* is not simply "blessing." Tha'lab, cited by Ibn 'Abd al-Barr: "as for *tashmit*, its meaning is: may Allah put gloating far from you and keep from you what would be gloated over against you. And as for *tasmit*, its meaning is: may Allah set you upon a good way" [4]. Both spellings are transmitted. You are asking that no one get to enjoy his misfortune, and that he be set straight.

The link between sneezing and praise is traced back to the beginning. Al-Nasa'i records from 'Abdullah b. Salam — and marks this the sound version of the report: "Allah created Adam in the last hour of Friday, then breathed into him of His spirit. When the spirit reached through him he sneezed, and Allah said to him: Say *al-hamdu lillah*. So he said *al-hamdu lillah*. And Allah said: Your Lord has had mercy on you. Then He said to him: Go to the people of this gathering of angels and greet them. He did so, and He said: This is your greeting and the greeting of your descendants" [5].

Al-Qadi 'Iyad offers a plainer reason too: "the sneezer was commanded to praise only because of the benefit that came to him by the exit of the vapours that were choked in his brain" [6].

**Is answering obligatory?**

A genuine three-way split, and al-Nawawi lays it out: "the *umma* has agreed that it is legislated, then they differed over its obligation. The Zahiris and Ibn Maryam of the Malikis held it obligatory upon everyone who hears it, on the apparent sense of his ﷺ words *it is a duty upon every Muslim who hears it that he bless him*. Al-Qadi said: the well-known position of Malik's school is that it is a communal obligation — and a group of scholars said this, like returning the greeting. And the position of al-Shafi'i, his companions and others is that it is a *sunna* and good manners, and not obligatory" [7].

It is also named among the rights Muslims owe each other: "the right of a Muslim over a Muslim is five: returning the greeting, visiting the sick, following funerals, answering an invitation, and blessing the sneezer" [8].

**The condition everyone forgets: he has to praise Allah first.**

"When one of you sneezes and praises Allah, bless him; and if he does not praise Allah, do not bless him" [9]. The Prophet ﷺ applied it in front of people: "two men sneezed in the presence of the Prophet ﷺ, and he blessed one of them and did not bless the other. They said: Messenger of Allah, you blessed this one and did not bless that one. He said: Because this one praised Allah and that one did not praise Him" [10].

Ibn Hajar notes it is not a hard prohibition: "that is its explicit sense — but is the prohibition in it for forbiddance or for dislike? The majority hold the second" [11].

**Should you prompt him?**

Disputed, and the better view is yes. Ibn Hajar: "al-Nawawi said it is recommended for one present when someone sneezes and does not praise Allah to remind him of the praise, so that he praises and is then blessed — and this is established from Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, and it is of the category of sincere advice and enjoining good" [12]. Ibn al-'Arabi disagreed sharply; Ibn Hajar sides against him: "Ibn al-'Arabi claimed it is ignorance on the part of one who does it. He erred in what he claimed; rather, the correct view is that it is recommended" [13].

Al-Awza'i had an elegant way of doing it without embarrassing anyone: "a man sneezed in his presence and did not praise Allah, so he said to him: What does one who sneezes say? He said: *al-hamdu lillah*. He said: *Yarhamuk Allah*" [14].

**How many times?**

Al-Nawawi: "if the sneezing repeats — Malik said: he blesses him three times, then falls silent" [15]. After that the person has a cold, and you can stop.

**The manners of sneezing itself.**

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr: "among the etiquette of sneezing is that the sneezer place his hand over his mouth, lower his voice with the sneeze, and say *al-hamdu lillah 'ala kulli hal*" [16]. Cover your mouth and be quiet about it — which is also, incidentally, what public health asks of you.

One thing not to do: do not say *salam* when you sneeze. Salim b. 'Ubayd reported: "I was with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ on a journey, and a man sneezed and said: *as-salamu 'alayk*" [17]. The Prophet ﷺ replied — "and upon you and upon your mother" [18] — and then taught the correct form: "When one of you sneezes, let him say *al-hamdu lillah 'ala kulli hal*, or *al-hamdu lillah rabb al-'alamin*; and let him say to him *yarhamukum Allah*, or *yarhamuk Allah*; and let him say *yaghfir Allahu li wa lakum*" [19].

If you want a sense of how seriously the early scholars took this small courtesy, Ibn Hajar relates of Abu Dawud, the author of the *Sunan*: "he was in a boat and heard someone sneezing on the bank who praised Allah, so he hired a skiff for a dirham until he came to the sneezer and blessed him, then returned. He was asked about that and said: Perhaps he is one whose supplication is answered. And when they had gone to sleep they heard a speaker saying: People of the boat, Abu Dawud has bought Paradise from Allah for a dirham" [20].

**Yawning is the opposite case.**

"Allah loves sneezing and dislikes yawning. So when one of you sneezes, let him say *al-hamdu lillah*, and it is a duty upon whoever hears it to say *yarhamuk Allah*. As for yawning, it is only from Shaytan — so when one of you yawns, let him push it back as much as he can, for when one of you yawns and says *haah, haah*, Shaytan laughs at him" [21].

There is nothing to say when you yawn. The instruction is to suppress it, and if you cannot, to cover your mouth: "yawning is from Shaytan, so when one of you yawns let him restrain it as much as he can — and in a narration of Muslim: let him hold his hand over his mouth, for Shaytan enters it" [22]. Ibn 'Abidin notes the order of preference: if you can stop it by catching your lip in your teeth, do that; the hand is for when you cannot.

And an important qualification, since people worry about this: "as for yawning itself, if it arises from one's nature without his doing, there is no harm in it; and if he does it deliberately, it should be forbiddenly disliked, because it is idle play" [23]. An involuntary yawn is not a sin and not a sign of anything about you. You are tired.

**In practice.** Sneeze quietly into your hand and say *alhamdulillah*. If someone near you sneezes and praises Allah, say *yarhamuk Allah* — out loud enough to be heard. If someone says it to you, say *yahdikum Allah wa yuslih balakum*. If you can't remember that, *jazak Allahu khayran* and a smile will do while you learn it; nobody is keeping score, and the whole point of the exchange is that people who live near each other wish each other well out loud.

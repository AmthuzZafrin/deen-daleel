# Do I fast during my period, and do I make it up?

## Answer

**No, you do not fast during your period — and yes, you make those days up
afterwards. The fast is made up; the prayer is not.**

That asymmetry is the whole answer, and it is agreed on to an unusual degree.

**You do not fast.**

Al-Sarakhsi lists it among the consequences of menstruation: **among them is that
the menstruating woman does not fast and does not pray** [1]. It is a prohibition,
not an option — fasting through your period is not permitted, and the fast would
not count.

**You make the days up.**

Aisha's report is the evidence. In al-Tirmidhi's wording: **we used to menstruate
in the time of the Messenger of God and then become pure** [2] — **and he would
command us to make up the fast and would not command us to make up the prayer**
[3].

Al-Tirmidhi grades this hasan and states the scholarly position: **the practice on
this among the people of knowledge — we know of no disagreement among them — is
that the menstruating woman makes up the fast and does not make up the prayer**
[4]. In his book of purity he says the same of the general body of jurists:
**there is no disagreement among them that the menstruating woman makes up the
fast and does not make up the prayer** [5]. Al-Qurtubi calls it consensus outright:
**the scholars are agreed that the menstruating woman makes up the fast and does
not make up the prayer** [6]. Al-Sarakhsi: **among them is that making up the fast
is required of her, not the prayer** [7].

**In Ramadan, in practice.**

- Your period starts mid-day while fasting → the fast is broken from that moment.
  You eat. That day is made up later.
- Your period ends before dawn → you may fast that day. (You can make the ghusl
  after dawn if you need to; the fast is not affected.)
- Days missed → counted, and made up before the next Ramadan if you can.

There is no expiation (*kaffara*) for these days — they are not days you broke a
fast without excuse. They are simply owed, one day for one day. You may make them
up consecutively or scattered.

**If you cannot make them up before the next Ramadan** — because of pregnancy,
nursing, illness, or repeated cycles — you make them up when you are able. The
debt does not expire.

**On the reason for the difference.** Aisha was asked precisely this, and rebuked
the questioner rather than explaining. The practical shape is plain enough — a
week of missed prayers is thirty-five prayers, where a week of Ramadan is seven
days — but the sources transmit the ruling rather than a rationale, and it is
better to say so than to invent one.

# Does kissing break the fast?

## Answer

**Not by itself — the Prophet did it while fasting. It becomes disliked, and on one
view forbidden, when you know it will lead further.**

**The hadith.**

Aisha reported, in al-Bukhari: **the Prophet used to kiss and be intimate while
fasting** [1].

Ibn Hajar preserves her own advice on it — she asked her nephew's wife's husband:
**what stops you from going close to your wife and playing with her and kissing her?
He said: shall I kiss her while I am fasting? She said: yes** [2].

So the baseline is permission, from the Prophet's practice and from the person who
knew it best.

**The condition: whether you can stop.**

The famous hadith is about two men getting opposite answers. Al-Nawawi: **we were
with the Prophet and a young man came and said: Messenger of God, may I kiss while
fasting? He said no. Then an old man came and said: may I kiss while fasting? He said
yes** [3].

Al-Kasani gives the version with the reason attached: **a young man and an old man
asked the Messenger of God about kissing for the fasting person, and he forbade the
young man and permitted the old man, and said: the old man has more control over his
urge** [4].

The rule is not about age. It is about self-control. Al-Nawawi states it exactly:
**kissing is disliked for one whose desire it stirs while fasting, and it is not
disliked for anyone else, though the better course is to leave it — and there is no
difference between an old man and a young man in that: the consideration is the
stirring of desire and the fear of emission** [5].

And the scope: **it makes no difference whether he kisses the cheek, the mouth, or
elsewhere; and likewise touching with the hand and embracing have the ruling of
kissing** [6].

**How strong is "disliked"?**

Al-Nawawi reports his school split: some held it *karahat tahrim* — amounting to
prohibition — for the person whose desire is stirred, and others *karahat tanzih*, a
preference, so long as nothing is emitted. Ibn Hajar notes the wider picture:
**there is disagreement about kissing and intimacy for the fasting person — a group
disliked it absolutely, and that is the well-known position among the Malikis** [7].

Al-Kasani records that Aisha's own report carries a caution: **the Messenger of God
used to be intimate while fasting, and he was the most in control of his urge among
you** [8]. Al-Nawawi has the same exchange put directly: **I said to Aisha: may the
fasting person be intimate? She said no. I said: was the Messenger of God not
intimate? She said: he was the most in control of his urge among you** [9]. And
al-Kasani notes **it is transmitted from Abu Hanifa that he disliked intimacy** [10].

**What actually breaks the fast.**

Al-Qurtubi is clear on the consequences: **if he kisses and emits, a make-up is upon
him and no expiation — that is the view of Abu Hanifa and his companions, al-Thawri,
al-Hasan and al-Shafi'i** [11]. And the lesser case: **if he kisses and pre-seminal
fluid comes, nothing is upon him according to them** [12].

He also states the limit of the permission: **I do not know of anyone who permitted it
for someone who knows that it will produce from it what invalidates his fast** [13].

**So:**

| | |
|---|---|
| A kiss, a hug, holding hands — no arousal | Permitted. Prophetic practice |
| Same, with arousal but nothing further | Disliked. Fast intact |
| Kissing leading to emission | Fast broken. Make up the day. No expiation |
| Pre-seminal fluid only | Nothing owed, on the majority view |
| Intercourse | Different question — see that answer. Expiation applies |

**Practically:** if you and your spouse can be affectionate without it escalating,
this is permitted and the sources are relaxed about it. If you know from experience
that it will not stop there, then for you it is the young man's answer, not the old
man's. That is a judgement only you can make, and the hadith is built around
precisely that fact.

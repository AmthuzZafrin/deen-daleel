# Does laughing break my wudu?

## Answer

**Outside prayer, no — and that part is agreed.** Al-Nawawi records two points of
consensus at once: **they agreed that laughter without a guffaw does not
invalidate wudu, and that a guffaw outside the prayer does not break wudu.** [1]
So laughing at your desk, in the car, at dinner, costs you nothing on any view.

**Inside prayer, out loud, the Hanafis alone say it does.** Al-Nawawi lists them:
**al-Hasan al-Basri, Ibrahim al-Nakha'i, Sufyan al-Thawri and Abu Hanifa said it
breaks the wudu.** [2] Everyone else — Malik, al-Shafi'i, Ahmad, Ishaq, Abu
Thawr, Dawud, and 'Ata', al-Sha'bi and al-Zuhri before them — holds that
**laughing in prayer invalidates the prayer but does not break the wudu.** [3]
On that majority view you start the prayer again; you do not go back to the tap.

**The Hanafi case, in their own words.** Al-Sarakhsi gives the hadith they rest
on — **whoever laughs in his prayer until he guffaws, let him repeat the wudu and
the prayer** — and adds, with unusual frankness, **and we abandoned the analogy
because of the sunna.** [4] They know it is an exception. They accept it as one,
because they hold a report established it.

He then explains why the ruling stops at the prayer's edge: **laughing outside
the prayer is not in the same sense as laughing in it, because the state of
prayer is the state of intimate address to God, so the offence of laughing in
that state is graver.** [5] It is not that laughter is impure. It is that the
place makes it grave.

**Why the majority declined it**, and this is the more important half for your
purposes. Al-Nawawi: **the relied-upon position is that the purity is valid, that
the things that break wudu are a fixed set, and that whoever claims an addition
must prove it — and nothing at all has been established for breaking wudu by
laughter.** [6] He goes through the reports on the blind man who fell into a well
and the Companions who laughed, and reports that the hadith scholars agree they
are weak.

**That principle is the useful takeaway even if you follow the Hanafis on this
one.** The nullifiers are a closed list. When someone tells you that something
new breaks your wudu, the burden is on them to produce the text, and "it seems
disrespectful" is not one.

**Practically**: if you laugh out loud in prayer, repeat the prayer — every
school requires that. Whether you also repeat the wudu depends on your school,
and if you have already prayed without repeating it, no scholar in the majority
would have you make that prayer up.

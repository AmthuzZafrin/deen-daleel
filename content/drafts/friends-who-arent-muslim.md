# Can I stay friends with my non-Muslim friends?

> Reviewer note: The retrieved set is one-sided without also_retrieve — it returns only the verses about not taking allies. Check the answer represents both the caution and the permission, and does not leave a revert believing they must cut everyone off. A question about belonging as much as about law.


## Answer

Yes. And if someone has told you otherwise using a verse, you need to see the verse that sits beside it.

**Start with the verse that governs the ordinary case.**

*"Allah forbiddeth you not those who warred not against you on account of religion and drove you not out from your homes, that ye should show them kindness and deal justly with them. Lo! Allah loveth the just dealers"* (60:8) [1].

The next verse names who *is* excluded: *"Allah forbiddeth you only those who warred against you on account of religion and have driven you out from your homes and helped to drive you out, that ye make friends of them"* (60:9) [2].

Two verses, side by side. One about people who fought you and expelled you; one about everybody else. Your colleague, your neighbour, the friend you have had since school — the Qur'an puts them in the second category by name.

**And the verse was revealed about exactly your situation.**

Al-Tabari transmits the occasion: "**it was revealed about Asma' bint Abi Bakr.** She had a mother from the days of ignorance called Qutayla bint 'Abd al-'Uzza, who came to her with gifts — condiment, dried curd and clarified butter — and she said: *I will not accept a gift from you, and you will not enter upon me until the Messenger of Allah ﷺ gives leave*" [3].

Asma' did what a cautious new Muslim does: she refused her mother at the door and asked. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr has her own account of the answer: "I said: **my mother has come to me and she is hoping [for something] — shall I give to her?** He said: **yes — maintain ties with her**" [4].

The verse came down to correct the caution, not the contact.

**Al-Tabari then rules on the two questions people raise about it.**

*Does it only cover a special group?* No. "The soundest of the statements about this is that of one who said: what is meant is *Allah forbiddeth you not those who warred not against you on account of religion* — **of all the classes of creeds and religions** — that you should show them kindness and maintain ties with them and deal justly with them. For God Most High generalised by His words *those who warred not against you in religion and drove you not out from your homes*, [covering] everyone of that description, **and did not specify some to the exclusion of others**" [5].

*Was it abrogated by the verses of fighting?* He answers that directly, and it is worth having in full: "**and there is no meaning to the statement of one who said: that is abrogated** — because a believer's kindness to one of the people of war between whom and himself there is a tie of kinship, or to one between whom and himself there is neither kinship nor lineage, is not forbidden nor prohibited, **so long as there is not in it any indication to him or to the people of war of a vulnerability of the people of Islam, or a strengthening of them with mounts or weapons**" [6].

Note what he permits and what he restricts. Kindness even to someone from a hostile people is not forbidden. What is forbidden is betraying your community's security. That is a rule about espionage and arms, not about friendship.

And his gloss on the closing phrase: "*Allah loveth the just dealers* — that is, God loves those who are fair, who give people their right and justice from themselves, **so they show kindness to whoever shows them kindness, and do good to whoever does good to them**" [7].

**The verses people quote against this.**

*"Take not the Jews and the Christians for friends"* (5:51) [8]; *"choose not friends from them till they forsake their homes in the way of Allah"* (4:89) [9]; *"choose not your fathers nor your brethren for friends if they take pleasure in disbelief rather than faith"* (9:23) [10].

The word in all three is *awliya'* — the same word as in 60:9, where it appears with an explicit description of who is meant: those who fought you over religion and drove you from your homes. These are verses about alliance and allegiance in a state of war, not about who you eat lunch with. Al-Tabari's refusal to read 60:8 as abrogated is precisely the refusal to let the war verses swallow the peace ones.

**What the sources permit in practice — and it is a lot.**

Al-Nawawi on the Prophet's own dealings: he bought food from a Jew on credit and pledged his iron armour with him — "**in it is the permissibility of dealing with the people of covenant**, and the ruling that their property is confirmed to them as it stands in their hands" [11]. And: "**the Muslims are agreed on the permissibility of dealing with the people of covenant and other disbelievers**, so long as the prohibition of what is with him is not established" [12].

Charity to them is likewise settled. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr: "**as for voluntary charity, it is permissible to the people of disbelief** — relatives and others — **and I know of no disagreement about that**" [13].

And the Qur'an itself, listing the houses in which you may eat without embarrassment, includes *"or your friend"* — al-Nawawi cites it as the scriptural basis for eating at a friend's without asking [14].

**The two real limits, and neither of them is about who your friends are.**

**Your obedience is to God first.** Al-Jassas: "**obedience to parents is obligatory in what is good, not in disobedience to God** — for there is no obedience to a creature in disobedience to the Creator" [15]. That principle scales to friends. Everything they ask of you that is good, give; what would cost you your religion, decline.

That is not a licence to be cold to them. The same author on the parent verses: "**God joined the obligation of kindness to parents with worship of Him and His oneness, and commanded it as He commanded those two**" [16].

**Do not sit through mockery of God.** Al-Jassas, on the verse about those who mock God's revelations: "**in it is a prohibition of sitting with one who displays disbelief and mockery of God's signs** — He said: *so sit not with them until they enter into some other talk*" [17].

Read the exact shape of that. Not *never sit with disbelievers* — the prohibition attaches to a particular thing being said, and it lifts *when the conversation moves on*. It is a rule about a moment, not a person.

**Practically, if you have just become Muslim.**

- **Keep your friends.** Nothing here asks you to cut off people who have not wronged you, and 60:8 was revealed to stop a Companion from doing exactly that.
- **Your family especially.** Asma's answer is the pattern: *maintain ties with her.* Gifts, visits, care in illness, presence at hard times — all of it.
- **You will have to change some of what you do together**, not who you do it with. The venue may change; the friendship does not have to.
- **Be the just dealer.** Al-Tabari's gloss makes returning kindness with kindness the substance of the verse. If a friend is good to you, being good back is what God is described as loving.
- **The only conversation you have to leave** is one mocking God — and even that, only until the subject changes.
- **If someone quotes 5:51 at you**, ask them what 60:8 means, and what al-Tabari said about the claim that it was abrogated. The answer is on record.

# Is toilet paper enough or do I need water?

## Answer

**Toilet paper alone is enough. Water is better.** Both halves are the settled
position, and neither is a concession.

**Why paper counts.** The hadith speak of stones, and the obvious question is
whether the ruling is tied to stones specifically. It is not — and the fuqaha
prove it from the exceptions rather than the rule. Ibn Qudama titles the question
**"wood and cloth and everything by which one is cleaned is like the stones"**
[1], and gives the argument: the Prophet was **asked about cleansing, and said:
with three stones, among which there is no dung** [2]. Al-Nawawi draws out the
inference: **the Prophet's prohibition of dung and bone is evidence that other
than stone stands in its place** [3] — if only stones were permitted, there would
have been no reason to single out dung and bone for exclusion.

So the category is *absorbent, clean solids*, and the books list it out.
Al-Nawawi: **it makes no difference in this between stones, wood, cloth,
earthenware, brick containing no dung, and the like** [4]. Al-Fatawa al-Hindiyya
adds **clods of earth, soil, sticks, cloth, leather and their like** [5].

Toilet paper is inside every one of those lists. It is cleaner and more absorbent
than most of them.

**How many sheets?** As many as it takes. Al-Fatawa al-Hindiyya: **there is no
sunna number in istinja; the condition is only cleanness, so that if it is
achieved with a single stone he has fulfilled the sunna** [6]. The Shafi'is set a
minimum of three wipes, which paper trivially satisfies.

**Why water is still better.** Aisha told the women of Medina: **command your
husbands to cleanse themselves with water, for I am shy of them** — and the
Prophet used to do it [7]. Al-Tirmidhi records the practice that followed: **upon
this is the practice among the People of Knowledge — they prefer istinja with
water, even though istinja with stones suffices in their view** [8].

And the best practice is both. Al-Nawawi: **what the great majority of the earlier
and later scholars hold, and on which the people of fatwa among the imams of the
great cities are agreed, is that the best is to combine water and stone — using
the stone first, so that the impurity is lightened and his hand touches less of
it, and then using water.** [9] Which is exactly paper first, then water — the
order most people who use both already follow.

But it is a preference, not a requirement: **if he wishes to confine himself to
one of the two, he may confine himself to whichever he wishes.** [10]

**The one case where paper is not enough.** If the mess has spread beyond the
outlet itself, paper will not do it. That case needs water, and there is a
separate answer here on the method.

**Practical summary**

| | |
|---|---|
| Paper only | **valid** — the ordinary case |
| Paper, then water | best, and the majority's recommendation |
| Water only | valid, and better than paper alone |
| Anything spread beyond the outlet | water required |

**A note for reverts.** If you have grown up without a bidet or a lota and are
unsure whether your prayers have been valid, they have. The ruling above is not a
grudging allowance for people who cannot manage the real thing — Ibn Qudama calls
it **the sound position of the school, and it is the position of most of the
People of Knowledge** [1]. A plastic bottle kept by the toilet costs nothing and
gets you the better practice, but it is an upgrade, not a repair.

ولنا ما روى أبو داود، عن خزيمة، قال: {سئل النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم عن الاستطابة، فقال: بثلاثة أحجار ليس فيها رجيع.} فلولا أنه أراد الحجر وما في معناه لم يستثن منها الرجيع ; لأنه لا يحتاج إلى ذكره، ولم يكن لتخصيص الرجيع بالذكر معنى.

# Is meat slaughtered by Christians or Jews permissible?

## Answer

**Short answer: yes — this is one of the few points in the whole area reported as
settled, and Ibn Taymiyya says explicitly that objecting to someone for eating
it is itself the error.** Kosher meat falls squarely inside the permission. The
serious modern question is not *who* slaughtered but *how*, and that is a
different question with a different answer.

**The verse and what it was understood to mean.** Ibn Qudama: **"وأجمع أهل العلم
على إباحة ذبائح أهل الكتاب ; لقول الله تعالى: {وطعام الذين أوتوا الكتاب حل لكم}.
يعني ذبائحهم"** — *the people of knowledge agreed on the permissibility of the
slaughter of the People of the Scripture, on account of Allah's saying "and the
food of those who were given the Scripture is lawful for you" — meaning their
slaughtered meat* [1]. He gives the authority for reading "food" that way:
**"قال ابن عباس: طعامهم ذبائحهم. وكذلك قال مجاهد وقتادة"** — *Ibn 'Abbas said:
their food is their slaughtered meat. Mujahid and Qatada said likewise* [2].

That reading is not a stretch to reach a convenient result. It narrows the verse:
the point of specifying *their* food was precisely that meat, which would
otherwise be in question, is covered.

**Ibn Taymiyya on the objection you will actually meet.** He was asked about this
and answered without hedging: **"ليس لأحد أن ينكر على أحد أكل من ذبيحة اليهود
والنصارى في هذا الزمان ولا يحرم ذبحهم للمسلمين ومن أنكر ذلك فهو جاهل مخطئ مخالف
لاجماع المسلمين"** — *no one may object to another for eating from the slaughter
of the Jews and the Christians **in this time**, nor is their slaughtering
unlawful for the Muslims; and whoever objects to that is ignorant, mistaken, and
opposing the consensus of the Muslims* [3].

Note the words *in this time* — he is answering the "they are not really
following their religion any more" argument in the fourteenth century, which is
to say the argument is not new. He calls the prohibiting view **"قول ضعيف جدا
مخالف لما علم من سنة رسول الله ولما علم من حال أصحابه والتابعين لهم بإحسان"** —
*a very weak position, contrary to what is known of the sunna of the Messenger of
Allah and to what is known of the practice of his Companions and the Successors
who followed them well* [4].

And he attaches a rule about how to disagree that is worth more than the ruling:
**"ومسائل الاجتهاد لايسوغ فيها الانكار الا ببيان الحجة وايضاح المحجة لا الانكار
المجرد المستند إلى محض التقليد"** — *in matters of considered legal reasoning,
objection is not permissible except by presenting the evidence and clarifying the
path — not bare objection resting on sheer imitation* [5].

**The one point where the scholars genuinely differed.** It is not what people
usually think. Ibn Taymiyya identifies it as a question about *descent*: **"هل
المراد به من هو بعد نزول القرآن متدين بدين اهل الكتاب أو المراد به من كان آباؤه
قد دخلوا في دين أهل الكتاب قبل النسخ والتبديل على قولين للعلماء"** — *is what is
meant someone who, after the revelation of the Qur'an, practises the religion of
the People of the Scripture; or is it someone whose forefathers entered the
religion of the People of the Scripture before the abrogation and alteration? —
two positions among the scholars* [6].

He names where the weight lies: **"فالقول الأول هو قول جمهور المسلمين من السلف
والخلف وهو مذهب ابى حنيفة ومالك واحد القولين في مذهب أحمد"** — *the first
position is that of the great majority of the Muslims, early and late, and it is
the school of Abu Hanifa and Malik, and one of the two positions in Ahmad's
school* [7]. On the historical practice he cites: **"وقال أبو بكر الأثرم ما علمت
احدا من اصحاب النبى صلى الله عليه وسلم كرهه الا عليا"** — *Abu Bakr al-Athram
said: I know of none of the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ who disliked it except
'Ali* [8] — and on Ahmad's own settled view: **"وقال إبراهيم بن الحارث كان آخر
قول أحمد على انه لا يرى بذبائحهم بأسا"** — *Ibrahim ibn al-Harith said: Ahmad's
final position was that he saw no harm in their slaughtered meat* [9].

**How wide the permission runs.** Ibn Qudama works through the qualifications
people try to attach and dismisses them one by one. On the slaughterer's
character: **"ولا فرق بين العدل والفاسق من المسلمين وأهل الكتاب"** — *there is no
difference between the upright and the transgressor, among the Muslims or the
People of the Scripture* [10]. On ethnicity: **"ولا فرق بين الكتابي العربي وغيره،
إلا أن في نصارى العرب اختلافا... والصحيح إباحة ذبائح الجميع ; لعموم الآية فيهم"**
— *there is no difference between an Arab of the People of the Scripture and
another, except that regarding the Christians of the Arabs there is a
disagreement... and the correct view is the permissibility of the slaughter of
all of them, on account of the generality of the verse concerning them* [11].

Ibn Hajar reports the same treatment of another proposed disqualification:
**"قال جمهور أهل العلم تجوز ذبيحته لان الله سبحانه أباح ذبائح أهل الكتاب ومنهم
من لا يختتن"** — *the majority of the people of knowledge said his slaughter is
permitted, because Allah permitted the slaughter of the People of the Scripture,
and among them are those who are not circumcised* [12].

The pattern is consistent: **the verse was read broadly, and attempts to add
conditions to it were rejected as additions.**

**Practically.**

- **Kosher meat is inside the permission**, on the reasoning above — it is the
  slaughter of the People of the Scripture, performed as their law requires.
- **The slaughterer's personal religiosity is not a condition.** Ibn Qudama says
  so directly.
- **"They are not real Christians any more" is an old argument that was answered
  when it was young.** Ibn Taymiyya's response is above, and it is not gentle.
- **If someone objects to your eating it, they owe you evidence**, on Ibn
  Taymiyya's own rule about disagreement.

**Where this stops — and this is the part that matters most today.** The verse
permits *their slaughter*. It does not license any method of killing. Whether a
particular industrial process — stunning, mechanical blades, a recorded
invocation — constitutes valid *dhakah* at all is a factual question about that
process, and this site does not rule on it; see *What about meat that was stunned
before slaughter?* and *Can I eat meat from a regular supermarket?* Nothing above
should be read as a verdict on any specific plant or product.

# How far do I have to go before it counts as travelling?

## Answer

**The commonly cited figure is about 48 miles (77 km), but the distance is
genuinely disputed — and there is a serious argument that it was never a fixed
number at all.**

**Where the 48 miles comes from.** The schools reasoned from Ibn Umar, who was
reported not to shorten for less than the distance between Medina and Khaybar. Ibn
Taymiyya reports the calculation: **Ibn Hazm said, between Medina and Khaybar is
like between Basra and al-Ahwaz, and that is a hundred miles less four miles** [1].
Various schools worked from this to roughly four *burud* — 48 Arab miles, about 77
kilometres. That is the figure most people are given, and following it is safe.

**Why it is not as solid as it sounds.** Ibn Taymiyya pushes back hard on the very
report the figure rests on: **it is established from him by sound narration
through Nafi' and others that he shortened at less than that** [2]. And he cites
Anas: **I went out with Anas ibn Malik to his land, which is at five farsakhs, and
he prayed Asr with us on a boat** [3] — shortening at about fifteen miles, a third
of the supposed minimum.

His conclusion is that the Shari'a fixed no mile-count, and that what makes
something a journey is that it is *called* a journey by ordinary usage: you pack,
you leave, you are away.

**The practical upshot**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Over ~80 km / 50 miles** | shorten — this satisfies every school |
| **~25 to 80 km** | disputed. Shortening has real support; praying in full is always safe |
| **A daily commute, however long** | not travel — you are going to work, not on a journey |
| **Unsure** | pray in full. Nobody has ever been faulted for four rak'ahs |

**Who counts as a traveller, apart from the distance**

The purpose does not have to be religious. Ibn Abd al-Barr: **most of the jurists
permit shortening for a traveller who is a merchant, and in a matter he is
permitted to go out for** [4] — and the precedents are ordinary: **Ibn Abbas went
out to his property at Ta'if and shortened the prayer; and Nafi' said Ibn Umar
used to inspect his property at Khaybar and shorten the prayer** [5].

Business trips, visiting family, a holiday — all journeys.

**And the disputed case of a sinful journey.** Some restricted the concession to
lawful travel. The Hanafis did not: **Abu Hanifa and his colleagues, al-Thawri and
al-Awza'i said the traveller shortens, whether disobedient or obedient** [6].

**Two things that are not about distance**

*Where the journey starts.* You shorten once you have left the built-up area of
your town, not from your front door.

*How long you may keep shortening.* A separate question with its own answer here,
and the disagreements there are wider than these.

**A word on why this is unsettled.** The Qur'an says "when you travel in the land"
without a figure, and the Prophet never named one. Every number in the books is a
scholar's inference from a Companion's practice. That is worth knowing — not so
you can pick freely, but so you understand that a friend quoting a different
distance is not wrong, and that praying in full when unsure is never a mistake.

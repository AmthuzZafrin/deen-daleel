# Can I do things that benefit someone who died?

## Answer

**The apparent obstacle, and why it is not one.** Two texts get quoted to say nothing can be done for the dead: "man shall have nothing but what he strove for," and `إذا مات بن آدم انقطع عمله إلا من ثلاث صدقة جارية أو علم ينتفع به أو ولد صالح يدعو له` [1] — when a person dies his work is cut off except for three: a continuing charity, knowledge benefited from, or a righteous child who prays for him. Ibn Taymiyya poses exactly this objection and answers it.

The hadith is about *his own* work ceasing. Al-Nawawi: `قال العلماء معنى الحديث أن عمل الميت ينقطع بموته وينقطع تجدد الثواب له إلا في هذه الأشياء الثلاثة لكونه كان سببها فإن الولد من كسبه وكذلك العلم الذي خلفه من تعليم أو تصنيف وكذلك الصدقة الجارية وهي الوقف` [2] — the dead person's deeds stop and the accrual of reward stops, except in these three, *because he was their cause*: the child is from his own earning, likewise the knowledge he left through teaching or writing, and the endowment. Nothing in that says other people cannot act for him. Ibn Taymiyya adds that the child is mentioned specifically for that reason, and both texts stand: `وشبه النبى ذلك بالدين يكون على الميت والدين يصح قضاؤه من كل أحد فدل على أنه يجوز أن يفعل ذلك من كل أحد لا يختص ذلك بالولد كما جاء مصرحا به في الأخ` [3] — the Prophet likened it to a debt owed by the deceased, and a debt may validly be paid by anyone; so this may be done by anyone, and is not restricted to the child, as is stated explicitly in the case of the brother.

Al-Nawawi is blunt about the denial: `من أن الميت لا يلحقه بعد موته ثواب فهو مذهب باطل قطعيا وخطأ بين مخالف لنصوص الكتاب والسنة واجماع الأمة` [4] — the view that no reward reaches the dead is categorically false, a clear error contradicting the texts of the Book and Sunna and the consensus of the community.

**What reaches, by agreement of all the schools.** Ibn Taymiyya: `أما الصدقة عن الميت فإنه ينتفع بها باتفاق المسلمين` [5] — charity on behalf of the deceased benefits him by the agreement of the Muslims. The hadith he cites is Sa'd's: `إن أمى افتلتت نفسها وأراها لو تكلمت تصدقت فهل ينفعها أن أتصدق عنها` [6] — my mother died suddenly, and I think that had she been able to speak she would have given in charity; will it benefit her if I give on her behalf? He was told yes.

And the rest of the agreed list: `وكذلك ينفعه الحج عنه والأضحية عنه والعتق عنه والدعاء والاستغفار له بلا نزاع بين الأئمة` [7] — hajj on his behalf, a sacrifice on his behalf, manumission on his behalf, and supplication and seeking forgiveness for him, **without dispute among the imams**. Al-Nawawi lists the same core: `وفيه أن الدعاء يصل ثوابه إلى الميت وكذلك الصدقة وهما مجمع عليهما وكذلك قضاء الدين` [8] — supplication and charity, both agreed on, and likewise paying his debt.

So the four things anyone can do today, with nobody disagreeing: **make du'a for them, give charity for them, pay their debts, and perform hajj on their behalf.**

**What is disputed.** `وأما الصيام عنه وصلاة التطوع عنه وقراءة القرآن عنه فهذا فيه قولان للعلماء أحدهما ينتفع به وهو مذهب أحمد وأبى حنيفة وغيرهما وبعض أصحاب الشافعى وغيرهم والثانى لا تصل إليه وهو المشهور في مذهب مالك والشافعى` [9] — fasting for him, voluntary prayer for him, and reciting Qur'an for him: two positions. It reaches, per Ahmad, Abu Hanifa and others; it does not, per the well-known position of Malik and al-Shafi'i. Al-Nawawi from inside the Shafi'i school: `وأما قراءة القرآن فالمشهور من مذهب الشافعي أنه لا يصل ثوابها إلى الميت وقال بعض أصحابه يصل ثوابها إلى الميت وذهب جماعات من العلماء إلى أنه يصل إلى الميت ثواب جميع العبادات من الصلاة والصوم والقراءة وغير ذلك` [10] — some of his own companions held it does reach, and groups of scholars held the reward of *every* act of worship reaches.

So a Qur'an khatm for the dead is a real and old position, not an invention — and it is also not agreed on. Reporting it either way as settled would be wrong.

One thing is ruled out from both sides. `وأما الاستئجار لنفس القراءة والإهداء فلا يصح ذلك` [11] — hiring someone to recite and dedicate the reward is not valid. Paying a reciter for this is a different thing from reciting yourself.

**Fasting and hajj owed by the deceased.** The reasoning here is worth having, because it is the model for everything above. `أرأيت لو كان عليها دين أكنت تقضينه قالت نعم قال فدين الله أحق بالقضاء` [12] — if your mother owed a debt, would you pay it? She said yes. He said: God's debt has the greater right to be paid. And in Burayda's report: `إنه كان عليها صوم شهر أفأصوم عنها قال صومي عنها قالت إنها لم تحج قط أفأحج عنها قال حجي عنها` [13] — fast for her; perform hajj for her. On fasting specifically the Shafi'i school has two well-known positions, the more famous being that it is not done, the second that it is recommended for the heir and valid.

**At the graveside, in the minutes after burial.** `استغفروا لأخيكم وسلوا له بالتثبيت؛ فإنه الآن يسأل` [14] — ask forgiveness for your brother and ask that he be made firm, for he is being questioned now. Ibn Taymiyya makes this the centre of the practice: `أما الاختلاف إلى القبر بعد الدفن فليس بمستحب وإنما المستحب عند الدفن أن يقام على قبره ويدعى له بالتثبيت` [15] — repeatedly going back to the grave after burial is not recommended; what is recommended is standing at his grave at the burial and asking that he be made firm. And: `فزيارة الميت المشروعة بالدعاء والاستغفار هي من هذا القيام المشروع` [16] — the sanctioned visit, consisting of supplication and seeking forgiveness, is of a piece with that standing.

**Praying for a non-Muslim who has died — the sources are explicit, and it is hard.** Al-Qurtubi: `قال كثير من العلماء : لا بأس أن يدعو الرجل لأبويه الكافرين ويستغفر لهما ما داما حيين فأما من مات فقد انقطع عنه الرجاء فلا يدعى له` [17] — many scholars said there is no harm in a man praying for his non-Muslim parents and seeking forgiveness for them **while they are alive**; but once someone has died, hope is cut off from him and he is not prayed for. Ibn 'Abbas on the verse's effect: `كانوا يستغفرون لموتاهم فنزلت فأمسكوا عن الإستغفار ولم ينههم أن يستغفروا للأحياء حتى يموتوا` [18] — they used to seek forgiveness for their dead, and when it came down they stopped; and they were not forbidden to seek forgiveness for the living until they died.

Note carefully what is and is not said. Seeking forgiveness for a living non-Muslim parent is expressly permitted, and by "many scholars." What is closed is *istighfar* for one who has died in that state. Al-Qurtubi also sets the limit of what we can know: `ظاهر حالة المرء عند الموت يحكم عليه بها فإن مات على الإيمان حكم له به وإن مات على الكفر حكم له به وربك أعلم بباطن حاله` [19] — a person is judged by his outward state at death; if he died upon faith, that is the ruling, and if upon unbelief, that; and your Lord knows best his inward state. And even in the hardest case there is something: `قال له العباس : يا رسول الله هل نفعت عمك بشيء قال : ( نعم ( وهذه شفاعة في تخفيف العذاب لا في الخروج من النار` [20] — al-'Abbas asked whether he had benefited his uncle at all, and he said yes; and this is intercession in the lightening of the punishment, not in coming out of the Fire.

If this is your parent, that is the honest position of these books and I am not going to soften it. What it leaves you is not nothing: their outward state at death is what is judged, their inward state is known only to God, and the general question of what happens to those the message never properly reached is a separate one that the sources treat at length.

**What this does not settle.** Whether reciting Qur'an for the dead reaches them (two standing positions, above); gatherings on the third day, the seventh or the fortieth, which these sources do not address; and whether a general supplication for mercy, short of *istighfar*, may be made for a deceased non-Muslim — the passages here speak of *istighfar* and du'a together and do not separate them, so I will not manufacture a distinction they do not draw.

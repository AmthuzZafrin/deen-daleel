# What does the Qur'an say about patience during hardship?

## Answer

Two things run through everything the sources say here, and they pull in
different directions until you see how they fit: **the pain is not wasted**, and
**you are not required to feel nothing**.

**What *sabr* actually is.** Al-Raghib's definition, quoted by Ibn Hajar: "*sabr*
is restraint under constriction — I *sabartu* a thing, meaning I confined it. So
*sabr* is confining the self to what reason or the Law requires. Its meanings
differ by what it attaches to: if it is over a calamity it is called *sabr*
simply; if in meeting an enemy it is called courage; if over speech it is called
discretion; and if from doing what has been forbidden it is called chastity" [1].

Note what it is not. It is not the absence of grief. It is holding a position
while grieved.

**The scene that settles the emotional question.** The Prophet's ﷺ own grandson
was dying. Usama b. Zayd narrates: a daughter of the Prophet ﷺ sent for him
saying her son was dying, and he sent back the greeting and the words: "**what
Allah has taken belongs to Him, and what He has given belongs to Him, and
everything with Him has an appointed term — so let her be patient and seek the
reward**" [2]. She swore he must come, so he went. "The child was lifted to the
Messenger of Allah ﷺ with his soul rattling, and his eyes overflowed. Sa'd said:
Messenger of Allah, what is this? He said: **this is a mercy which Allah places
in the hearts of His servants — and Allah has mercy only on the merciful among
His servants**" [3].

He wept. He was asked about it as if it needed explaining, and he named the tears
as mercy rather than weakness. Whatever *sabr* is, it is not what he was being
accused of failing at.

**The famous hadith, and its context, which is almost always dropped.** The
Prophet ﷺ passed a woman weeping at a child's grave and said, "fear Allah and be
patient" [4] — and she, not recognising him, snapped back: "*and what do you care
about my calamity?*" She was then told who he was and came to his door — and
"she did not find doorkeepers at his door" — and said, "Messenger of Allah, I did
not recognise you" [5]. His answer: "patience is only at the first shock — or, at
the first blow" [6].

Three things are worth noticing. She was not rebuked for the retort. He kept no
doorkeepers. And the saying means that the moment which counts is the first one,
not that grieving afterwards is a failure.

**Why none of it is wasted.** 'A'isha: "no calamity befalls a Muslim but that
Allah expiates by it for him, even the thorn that pricks him" [7], and in another
wording, "no thorn or anything beyond it strikes a believer but Allah raises him
a degree by it, or removes a sin from him by it" [8]. Abu Hurayra and Abu Sa'id
have the full inventory: "no fatigue, nor illness, nor anxiety, nor sorrow, nor
distress, nor harm befalls a Muslim — even the thorn that pricks him — but Allah
expiates by it some of his sins" [9].

*Hamm*, *huzn*, *ghamm* — anxiety, sorrow and distress are named separately and
explicitly. What you are carrying is on the list.

And for the heaviest losses, a report Ibn Hajar cites: "when I take from My
servant his two beloved ones and he bears it patiently, I am not content with a
reward for him short of Paradise — if he praises Me for them" [10].

**Where to put the weight.** "Seek help in patience and prayer; and truly it is
hard save for the humble-minded" (2:45) [11]. "But be patient with a patience
fair to see" (70:5) [12]. And the verse that removes the burden of manufacturing
it yourself: "Endure thou patiently. **Thine endurance is only by the help of
Allah.** Grieve not for them, and be not in distress" (16:127) [13]. Patience is
described there as something given, not summoned.

'Umar's summary, which Ibn Hajar traces to a sound chain: "**we found the best of
our living in patience**" [14].

**So.** Grieve. Weep if you weep — the Prophet ﷺ did, over this exact kind of
loss, and called it mercy. What is asked of you is not to feel less but to hold
your ground in the first moment and not to say what would be resentment at the
decree. And nothing you are going through is being spent for nothing: the texts
put anxiety and sorrow, with no minimum size, on the same list as a thorn.

### Passage 19 — Qur'an 16:106

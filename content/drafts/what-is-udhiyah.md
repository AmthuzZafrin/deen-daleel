# What is the Eid sacrifice and do I have to do it?

## Answer

*Udhiya* — *qurbani* in Urdu and Turkish — is the animal sacrificed on the days of 'Id al-Adha, whether
or not you are on Hajj. Al-Quduri's summary is the compact version of the whole institution: **"the
sacrifice is obligatory upon every free, Muslim, resident, financially able person on the day of
sacrifice, on behalf of himself and his young children; he slaughters a sheep for each one of them, or
slaughters a camel or a cow for seven"** [1] — with the exemption stated immediately: **"there is no
sacrifice upon the poor person or the traveller"** [2].

### Obligatory or recommended?

Contested from the first generation, and al-Jassas records the whole range.

The Hanafi position is the one al-Quduri stated: **"Abu Hanifa, Muhammad and Zufar said the sacrifice is
obligatory upon the financially able among the people of the cities and villages who are resident, not
upon travellers"** [3]. Malik went further in scope and softer in name: **"Malik b. Anas said the
sacrifice is upon all people — the traveller and the resident — and whoever leaves it without an excuse
has done an evil thing"** [4]. And against both: **"al-Thawri and al-Shafi'i said it is not
obligatory"** [5].

The Companions are reported on both sides. Ibn 'Umar: **"it is not a binding duty, but it is a sunna and
a good practice"** [6]. Abu Mas'ud al-Ansari went out of his way to make the point: **"I deliberately
leave the sacrifice, though I am well off, out of fear that my neighbours will think it is obligatory
upon me"** [7].

The main argument against obligation is the Prophet's own sacrifice. Jabir reports that he sacrificed
two rams and said: **"O God, from You and to You — on behalf of Muhammad and his community. In the name
of God, and God is greatest — then he slaughtered"** [8]. From which the argument runs: **"in his
sacrificing on behalf of the community is evidence that it is not obligatory"** [9] — since one sheep
cannot discharge an obligation for everyone. Al-Jassas, arguing the other side, answers that a voluntary
act performed on someone's behalf does not lift an obligation they owe.

Practical upshot: no school treats it as optional in the way a nafl prayer is optional. If you can
afford it, do it.

### When

The window opens on the 10th of Dhu al-Hijja. **"The time of the sacrifice enters with the breaking of
dawn on the day of sacrifice, except that it is not permitted for the people of the cities to slaughter
until the imam has prayed the 'Id prayer"** [10] — so in a town, the prayer comes first.

How long it stays open is disputed on school lines. Al-Quduri, for the Hanafis: **"it is permitted on
three days — the day of sacrifice and two days after it"** [11]. Ibn Hajar maps the rest: **"Malik added
that one also slaughters on the two days after it, and al-Shafi'i added the fourth day"** [12], and
**"al-Thawri, Abu Hanifa and Ahmad held the like of Malik's position, and al-Awza'i held the like of
al-Shafi'i's"** [13]. So: three days for most, four for the Shafi'is.

Earlier is better: **"the time of the sacrifice is three days — the tenth, the eleventh and the twelfth;
the first of them is the best of them and the last of them the least"** [14].

### The animal

**"The sacrifice is from camels, cattle and sheep; from all of these the *thani* and above
suffices"** [15] — an animal that has reached maturity. A camel or a cow counts for seven people; a sheep
or goat for one.

Defects disqualify: **"one does not sacrifice the blind, the one-eyed, the lame that cannot walk to the
place of sacrifice, nor the emaciated"** [16]. The principle is that the animal should be sound and
worth giving.

### The meat

**"He eats from the meat of the sacrifice, feeds the rich and the poor, and stores some"** [17] — and
**"it is recommended that he not reduce the charity below a third"** [18]. The customary three-way split
— household, friends and relatives, the poor — comes from that. Note that the *whole* animal need not be
given away; eating from it is part of the practice, not a lapse in it.

### Details worth knowing

Doing it yourself is better if you are competent: **"the best is that he slaughter his sacrifice with his
own hand if he knows how to slaughter"** [19]. Most people today delegate, which is permitted, and this
is how sacrifice through a charity in another country works.

On the anxiety about getting it wrong: **"if two men make a mistake and each of them slaughters the
other's sacrifice, it suffices for both of them, and there is no liability upon either"** [20].

And on giving the money instead — the answer is no, if you are able to sacrifice: **"the sacrifice in
these days is better than giving the price of the sacrifice in charity, because it falls as obligatory
or as sunna, while the giving in charity is purely voluntary, so it is superior"** [21]. The act itself
is the point, not just the transfer of value.

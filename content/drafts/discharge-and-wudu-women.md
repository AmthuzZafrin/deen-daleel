# Does everyday discharge break my wudu?

## Answer

**Ordinary daily discharge is a genuinely disputed question, and the practical
answer for almost everyone is: no, carry on.** The disagreement is old, the
lenient side is well-founded, and even on the strict side there is a dispensation
that removes the problem entirely.

**What the disagreement is about.** Al-Nawawi identifies the difficulty precisely:
**the moisture of the vulva is a white fluid hovering between madhy and sweat —
and for this reason there is disagreement about it.** [1] If it is like madhy, it
is impure and it breaks wudu. If it is like sweat, it is neither.

He then reports both: **as for the moisture of a woman's vulva, the transmitted
text is that it is impure, because it is a moisture generated in a place of
impurity, so it is impure; and some of our colleagues said it is pure, like the
rest of the body's moistures.** [2]

Note that the "pure" position is not a stray view — it is held inside the Shafi'i
school itself, and the Hanafis and Malikis are generally easier still on ordinary
discharge that does not originate past the point they treat as internal.

**Why it usually does not matter which side you take.** Because if the discharge
is continuous — which for most women it is — you fall under the ruling for people
with a permanent excuse, and that ruling is unambiguous.

Ibn Taymiyya, asked about exactly this category: **yes, his ruling is the ruling
of people of excuse — like the woman with istihada, one with urinary incontinence,
madhy, and a wound that does not stop, and the like. Whoever cannot maintain
purity for the length of a prayer performs wudu and prays, and what comes out of
him during the prayer does not harm him, and his wudu is not broken by it, by the
agreement of the imams.** [3]

And he states it again more broadly: **as for what comes out during the prayer
continuously — this does not break wudu, by the agreement of the scholars.** [4]

*By the agreement of the imams.* That is the strongest formula in the chapter, and
it lands on precisely the situation most women are asking about.

**So, in practice**

| | |
|---|---|
| Ordinary discharge, present most of the time | wudu is **not** broken; pray normally |
| Occasional discharge, and you follow a school that counts it | make wudu when you notice; that is all |
| Heavy, coloured, or with an odour that suggests it is not ordinary | that is a different question — see the answers here on istihada and on menstruation |

**On your clothes.** Even if you take the strict view that it is impure, small
amounts are excused by every school — the thresholds are wide, and there is a
separate answer here on how much. A mark on underwear is not a reason to change
before every prayer, and panty liners solve the rest.

**What definitely breaks wudu** is the short, agreed list. Bukhari records a man
from Hadramawt asking Abu Hurayra what a *hadath* actually is, and getting the
plainest possible answer: **no prayer is accepted from one who has broken his
wudu until he performs it; a man from Hadramawt said: what is the hadath, Abu
Hurayra? He said: breaking wind, silently or audibly.** [5] There is a separate
answer here on the full list.

**A word about scruple.** This question, more than almost any other, gets women
checking, re-checking, and repeating wudu through the day. If that is where you
are, the ruling above is the treatment and not a loophole: on the position of
imams by agreement, a continuous discharge does not break your wudu, and you pray
without renewing. There is a separate answer here on that pattern, and it is worth
reading alongside this one.

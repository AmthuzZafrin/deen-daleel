# What does tawhid actually mean?

## Answer

**Short answer: tawhid is not the claim that one god exists rather than several.
It is the claim that only one being is worth *directing your life toward*.** The
sources are emphatic that those are different claims, and that the first one on
its own is not what Islam is asking for.

**The distinction that carries the whole idea.** Ibn Taymiyya, in a treatise
devoted to the subject, separates two things people both call "tawhid":
**"إذ الاله هو الذى يؤله فيعبد محبة وإنابة وإجلالا وإكراما والرب هو الذى يربى
عبده فيعطيه خلقه ثم يهديه إلى جميع أحواله من العبادة وغيرها"** — *the ilah is
the one who is turned to and worshipped in love, in returning to Him, in
reverence and in honour; the Rabb is the one who nurtures His servant, giving him
his created nature and then guiding him through all his states* [1].

*Rabb* is God as origin and sustainer — the one who made and maintains things.
*Ilah* is God as the object of a life. And Ibn Taymiyya's point is that
acknowledging the first is not the achievement people take it for:
**"فأما توحيد الربوبية الذى أقر به الخلق وقرره أهل الكلام فلا يكفى وحده بل هو من
الحجة عليهم"** — *as for the tawhid of lordship, which people generally
acknowledged and which the theologians established, it does not suffice on its
own; rather it is an argument against them* [2].

That is a strong claim and worth sitting with. On this reading, the Meccans who
opposed the Prophet ﷺ were not atheists — they granted that a supreme Creator
made the world. What they refused was the second half: that He alone should be
the one you ask, fear, hope in, and organise your life around. Tawhid is that
second half.

**What the shahada is doing.** **"ولهذا كانت لا إله إلا الله أحسن الحسنات وكان
التوحيد بقول لا إله إلا الله رأس الأمر"** — *and so "there is no god but Allah"
is the best of good deeds, and tawhid by saying it is the head of the whole
matter* [3]. Note the grammar of the phrase itself: it does not say "there is one
god". It negates first — *no ilah* — and then excepts. The sentence clears the
field before it affirms.

The Qur'an gives the same formula in 3:18: **"Allah (Himself) is Witness that
there is no Allah save Him. And the angels and the men of learning (too are
witness)"** [4].

**Why it matters so much, in the sources' own terms.** Ibn Taymiyya cites the
verse that singles this out: **"ولهذا كان الله لا يغفر أن يشرك به ويغفر ما دون
ذلك لمن يشاء"** — *and so Allah does not forgive that partners be ascribed to
Him, and forgives what is less than that for whom He wills* [5]. And the hadith
of Mu'adh: **"حق الله على عباده أن يعبدوه ولا يشركوا به شيئا"** — *Allah's right
over His servants is that they worship Him and associate nothing with Him* [6].

He also gives an argument from coherence, quoting the verse: **"لو كان فيهما
آلهة إلا الله لفسدتا"** — *were there gods in them other than Allah, they would
both have gone to ruin* [7].

**And the part that is about you rather than about metaphysics.** Ibn Taymiyya
frames tawhid as a claim about where a person can actually come to rest:
**"فليس في الكائنات ما يسكن العبد إليه ويطمئن به ويتنعم بالتوجه إليه الا الله
سبحانه"** — *there is nothing in existence in which a servant may find rest,
settle, and find delight in turning toward, except Allah* [8]. And the purpose
that follows from it: **"ان الله خلق الخلق لعبادته الجامعة لمعرفته والإنابة إليه
ومحبته والإخلاص له"** — *Allah created creation for His worship, which comprises
knowing Him, turning to Him, loving Him, and sincerity toward Him* [9].

Notice what "worship" means in that sentence. It is not restricted to ritual; it
comprises knowledge, turning, love and sincerity. That is much closer to what
tawhid asks of a person than a list of religious acts.

**Where it sits among the rest.** In the well-known hadith of Jibril, when the
Prophet ﷺ was asked what Islam is, he answered: **"الإسلام أن تشهد أن لا إله إلا
الله وأن محمدا رسول الله وتقيم الصلاة وتؤتي الزكاة وتصوم رمضان وتحج البيت إن
استطعت إليه سبيلا"** — *Islam is that you testify there is no god but Allah and
that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establish the prayer, give zakat, fast
Ramadan, and make Hajj to the House if you are able* [10]. The testimony comes
first and everything else follows from it.

**If you are new to this, the practical upshot.** Tawhid is not primarily
something to understand and then set aside — on the reading above it is a
standing question about what you are actually orienting toward [8][9]. The daily
practices exist to keep answering it. And the reason shirk is treated as the one
thing categorically apart [5] is that it is not a rule being broken but the
orientation itself going elsewhere.

**On the language above:** "tawhid" is making one, declaring God's oneness;
"ilah" is a god in the sense of something worshipped, "rabb" a lord in the sense
of one who creates and sustains; "shirk" is associating something with God;
"shahada" is the testimony *la ilaha illa Allah*. Scholars often speak of *tawhid
al-rububiyya* and *tawhid al-uluhiyya* for the two senses at [1] and [2].

**A note on scope.** This is one classical presentation, and a very influential
one, but Muslim theologians have discussed tawhid in several registers — the
kalam tradition, the philosophers, and the Sufi writers each frame it
differently, and this answer draws on none of those. If you want the fuller
picture, or you are working through a specific difficulty, ask someone who can
take you through it properly.

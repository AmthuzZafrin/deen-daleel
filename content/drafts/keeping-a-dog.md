# Can I keep a dog?

## Answer

Yes for a working purpose, and the scholars disagree about how far "working purpose" reaches. What is not disputed is that keeping a dog with no need reduces your reward.

**The hadith.**

Ibn 'Umar reported the Prophet ﷺ saying: "Whoever keeps a dog — except a hunting dog or a herding dog — a *qirat* is deducted from his reward each day" [1]. Abu Hurayra's version adds a third: "Whoever takes a dog, except a herding dog, or a hunting dog, or a crop dog, a *qirat* is deducted from his reward each day" [2]. And Sufyan b. Abi Zuhayr's puts the principle in terms of function: "whoever keeps a dog that avails him nothing in crop or in livestock, a *qirat* is deducted from his deeds each day" [3].

There is a well-known exchange in the same passage. Al-Zuhri mentioned Abu Hurayra's "crop dog" addition to Ibn 'Umar, "and he said: *May Allah have mercy on Abu Hurayra — he was an owner of crops*" [2].

Do not read that as doubt. Al-Nawawi: "the scholars said: this is not a weakening of Abu Hurayra's narration nor a doubt about it. Rather its meaning is that since he was an owner of crops and cultivation, he attended to that and preserved it and mastered it — and it is customary that one afflicted with a thing masters what others do not master of it, and comes to know of its rulings what others do not know" [4]. And in any case: "the upshot is that Abu Hurayra is not alone in this addition; rather a group of the Companions agreed with him in narrating it from the Prophet ﷺ" [5].

**The reason for the deduction — and it is not that dogs are wicked.**

Al-Qurtubi lists the possibilities: "the deduction in the reward of one who keeps them for other than that benefit is either because of the dog frightening Muslims and disturbing them with its barking; or because of preventing the angels from entering the house; or because of its impurity, on al-Shafi'i's view; or because of falling into the prohibition of keeping what has no benefit in it" [6].

Every one of those is about a consequence, not about the animal's worth. And al-Qurtubi immediately notes: "as for what is permitted to keep, it does not reduce [the reward] — like the horse and the cat" [7].

**How far does "need" reach?**

This is where the schools part, and it is the question that decides guard dogs, guide dogs, police and rescue dogs.

Al-Nawawi states the Shafi'i position and its reasoning: "as for keeping dogs, our position is that it is forbidden to keep a dog without need, and it is permitted to keep it for hunting, for crops and for livestock. And whether it is permitted for guarding houses and streets and the like — there are two views. One is that it is not permitted, on the apparent sense of the hadiths… **and the sounder is that it is permitted, by analogy with the three, acting upon the reason understood from the hadiths — which is need**" [8].

That last clause is the important one. The three named cases are read as *examples of need*, not as a closed list. Al-Majmu' states the same two views: "and whether it is permitted to keep it for guarding the streets — two views: one, that it is not permitted, on the report; and the second, that it is permitted, because it is guarding property, so it resembles the crop and the livestock" [9].

Malik read the licence more tightly. Al-Qurtubi: "the livestock dog permitted to keep, according to Malik, is the one that goes out with them, not the one that guards them in the house from thieves; and the crop dog is the one that guards it from wild animals by night and by day, not from thieves — though others besides Malik permitted keeping them against thieves of livestock, crops and the house in the countryside" [10].

**So, practically:** a dog kept for security, for guiding a blind person, for detection or rescue work, or for herding falls within the *need* the majority reasoned from — most explicitly on al-Nawawi's "sounder" view. A dog kept purely as a companion does not, on any of these readings; that is the case the hadith is about, and it costs a *qirat* a day.

**The purity question, which matters daily if you have one.**

This is a genuine four-way disagreement, and if you already own a dog you should know the range.

Ibn Qudama sets it out: the dog and its leftover water and everything issuing from it are impure — "and this is the position of al-Shafi'i and Abu 'Ubayd, and it is the statement of Abu Hanifa regarding the leftover specifically. **And Malik, al-Awza'i and Dawud said: the leftover of the two is pure — one may perform ablution with it and drink it; and if they lap in food, eating it is not forbidden**" [11]. On the washing, "Malik said: the vessel a dog has lapped in is washed as a devotional act" [12] — that is, not because anything impure touched it.

Ibn Qudama argues the other way from the hadith: "for us is what Abu Hurayra narrated, that the Prophet ﷺ said: *when a dog laps in the vessel of one of you, let him wash it seven times* — agreed upon; and in Muslim's wording: *let him pour it out, then wash it seven times*. Had its leftover been pure, pouring it out would not have been permitted, nor washing obligatory" [13].

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr's survey completes the map: "Abu Hanifa and his companions, al-Thawri and al-Layth b. Sa'd said the leftover of the dog is impure, and they set no limit for washing from it — they said he need only wash it until it becomes his strong assumption that the impurity has gone, whether once or more" [14]; while "al-Shafi'i, Ahmad b. Hanbal, Ishaq b. Rahawayh, Abu 'Ubayd, Abu Thawr and al-Tabari said the leftover of the dog is impure, and the vessel is washed from it seven times, the first of them with earth" [15].

And within the Hanafi school even the number is unsettled — Ibn Nujaym: "al-Tahawi and al-Wabari transmitted that our companions found no fixed limit for washing the vessel from it; rather the standard is the strongest opinion, even if once… and this differs from what is in al-Hidaya and elsewhere, that the vessel is washed from its lapping three times" [16].

**What that means for you.** If you follow the Maliki position, the dog itself is pure, your clothes are not ruined by contact, and the washing of a vessel it drank from is a devotional act rather than a decontamination. If you follow the Shafi'i or Hanbali position, wash the vessel seven times, the first with earth, and keep the dog off prayer clothes and the prayer area. Either way, saliva on a bowl is a specific case — not a licence for anyone to treat a dog owner as unclean.

**One closing note.** The Qur'an mentions a dog by name, in the company of the young men of the Cave. Al-Qurtubi quotes a preacher on it: "whoever loves the people of good attains something of their blessing — a dog loved a people of virtue and accompanied them, and Allah mentioned it in His decisive revelation" [17].

```
Ahkam al-Qur'an (Ibn al-'Arabi) 3:443 — سورة الفرقان فيها إحدى عشرة آية › الآية الثالثة › المسألة الثامنة

Arabic:
إذا كان الماء طاهرا مطهرا على أصله فولغ فيه كلب فسد عند جمهور فقهاء الأمصار لقول النبي إذا ولغ الكلب في إناء أحدكم فاغسلوه سبع مرات وعفروه الثامنة بالتراب

وقد قال مالك وقد جاء هذا الحديث ولا أدري ما حقيقته وقد بينا في مسائل الخلاف حقيقته وأن الإناء يغسل عبادة لا لنجاسة بدليلين

أحدهما أن الغسل معدود بسبع

الثاني أنه جعل للتراب فيها مدخلا ولو كان لنجاسة لما كان للتراب فيها مدخل كالبول عكسه الوضوء لما كان عبادة دخل التراب مع الماء

ورأى مالك طرح الماء تقذرا لا تنجسا أو حسما لمادة الخلاف أو لأنه حيوان يأكل الأقذار ولا يحتاج إليه فيكون من الطوافين أو الطوافات وقد استوفينا القول عليه في الفقه
```

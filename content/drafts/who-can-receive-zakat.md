# Who is allowed to receive zakat?

## Answer

The Qur'an answers this one directly, and unusually for a question of this kind it gives a closed list. Zakat has eight lawful destinations, named in Surah al-Tawba: the poor, the needy, those employed to collect it, those whose hearts are to be reconciled, the freeing of captives, the debt-burdened, the path of God, and the traveller.

Ibn Taymiyya glosses the list compactly, and his gloss is a good working guide:

**The poor and the needy** — **the poor and the needy are joined by the meaning of need for sufficiency, so charity is not lawful for a rich person nor for a strong earner** [1]. Two words for two conditions rather than one repeated. Al-Tabari separates them: **the poor man is the one with poverty or need, and along with his need he refrains from asking people** [2] — while the needy is the one whose need has driven him to ask.

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr guards against reading "poor" too narrowly. Owning something does not disqualify you: the Kufans, al-Shafi'i, Abu Thawr, Abu 'Ubayd, Ahmad and al-Tabari all held, **regarding one who has a house and a servant he cannot do without, that he may take from the zakat and it is lawful for him** [3]. A person with a home and no money is poor.

**Those employed to collect it** — al-Kasani: **as for those employed upon it, they are the ones the imam appoints to gather the charities** [4]. Al-Tabari notes their share is a wage, not relief: **the collector is given for his work, not for a need that the gift removes** [5]. A charity's staff costs sit here.

**Those whose hearts are to be reconciled** — al-Tabari identifies them historically: **as for those whose hearts are to be reconciled, they were a people who were being conciliated towards Islam, from those whose support was not firm, in order to set right themselves and their clans** [6]. Whether this category still operates is disputed — Abu Hanifa held its share lapsed after the Prophet's death, and Ibn Qudama, arguing that it did not, still concedes the limit: **it is not permissible to give to them except where there is a need** [7].

**Captives, the debt-burdened, the path of God, the traveller** — Ibn Taymiyya: **the debt-burdened are those who have debts they cannot find the means to discharge, so they are given enough to discharge their debts even if it is a great deal — unless they incurred them in disobedience to God, in which case they are not given until they repent** [8]. And **the traveller is the one passing from land to land** [9] — someone stranded away from home, however comfortable he may be in his own country.

**Who is excluded.** The list is closed, so anything outside it does not count. Beyond that, two exclusions are firm.

The first is the rich, in the sense Ibn Hajar recovers from the hadith of Mu'adh: **zakat is for the consolation of the poor** [10] — it moves wealth downwards, and a payment that does not do that is not zakat.

The second is your own immediate dependants. Al-Jassas records the Hanafi rule: **no parent however high is given from it, nor a child however low, nor a wife** [11]. Other relatives are a different matter, and a better one — that is its own question.

**In practice** the eight heads cover far more than an emergency food parcel: a student who cannot pay fees, a family behind on rent, someone crushed by non-culpable debt, a refugee, a stranded traveller. What they do not cover is a good cause with no eligible recipient behind it — building a mosque, funding a hospital wing, or a general appeal that does not put money in a qualifying person's hands. Those are excellent charity. They are just not zakat.

```
حدثنا القاسم، قال: ثنا الحسين، قال: ثنا إسماعيل بن جعفر، عن شريك بن أبي نمر، عن عطاء بن يسار، عن أبي هريرة، قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: " ليس المسكين بالذي ترده اللقمة واللقمتان والتمرة والتمرتان، إنما المسكين المتعفف، اقرءوا إن شئتم {لا يسألون الناس إلحافا} [البقرة: 273] " ومعنى قوله صلى الله عليه وسلم: «إنما المسكين المتعفف» على نحو ما قد جرى به استعمال الناس من تسميتهم أهل الفقر مساكين، لا على تفصيل المسكين من الفقير.

ومما ينبئ عن أن ذلك كذلك، انتزاعه صلى الله عليه وسلم لقول الله: " اقرءوا إن شئتم {لا يسألون الناس إلحافا} [البقرة: 273] وذلك في صفة من ابتدأ الله ذكره ووصفه بالفقر، فقال {للفقراء الذين أحصروا في سبيل الله لا يستطيعون ضربا في الأرض يحسبهم الجاهل أغنياء من التعفف تعرفهم بسيماهم لا يسألون الناس إلحافا} [البقرة: 273]
```

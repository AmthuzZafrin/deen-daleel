# I'm spotting after my period — do I pray?

## Answer

**If it comes after you have finished and done ghusl — you pray, and you
disregard it. If it comes within your usual days, it is still your period.**

That is the rule the majority hold, and it turns on *when*, not on the colour.

**The rule.**

Ibn Qudama states it in one sentence: **that is, if she sees yellowness or
murkiness during the days of her habit, it is menstruation; and if she sees it
after the days of her menstruation, it is not counted** [1]. He names those who
held this: Malik, al-Thawri, al-Awza'i, al-Shafi'i, Ishaq, and Ahmad.

The evidence for disregarding it afterwards is Umm Atiyya, a Companion who had
given the pledge to the Prophet: **we used not to count yellowness and murkiness
after the bath as anything** [2].

Al-Nawawi confirms the strength of this report: **the hadith of Umm Atiyya is
sound — al-Bukhari, al-Darimi, Abu Dawud, al-Nasa'i and others narrated it** [3].
He gives al-Bukhari's wording — **we used not to count yellowness and murkiness as
anything** [4] — and Abu Dawud's, which contains the crucial qualifier: **we used
not to count yellowness and murkiness after purity as anything** [5].

*After purity.* That is the whole hinge of the ruling.

**Within your days, it counts as menstruation.**

Ibn Qudama's evidence is Aisha, who received the cotton the women sent her:
**do not hurry until you see the white discharge** [6]. So while you are still
within your normal period, colour of any kind means it has not finished.

**And once you have finished, it does not.**

Ibn Qudama again: **and if she becomes pure and then sees murkiness or yellowness,
it is not paid attention to** [7].

**So, practically.**

| When you see it | What it means |
|---|---|
| Within your usual days, before ghusl | Still your period. Do not pray |
| After the bleeding stopped and you did ghusl | Disregard it. Pray normally |

If spotting appears after ghusl, you do not repeat the ghusl and you do not stop
praying. Make wudu for each prayer if the discharge is ongoing, wear protection,
and pray.

**A minority held otherwise.** Ibn Qudama records a report from Asma' instructing
women to stop praying until they see pure whiteness, and Abu Yusuf and Abu Thawr
held yellowness is not menstruation at all unless preceded by dark blood. Ibn
Qudama prefers the majority: the reports of Aisha and Umm Atiyya are stronger.

**One practical caution.** Persistent spotting well outside your usual pattern —
weeks of it, or bleeding between cycles — is a different category called
*istihada*, with its own rules and its own answer here. It is also worth a doctor's
opinion. The ruling above is for the ordinary tail-end of a normal period.

```
Al-Mughni 1:202 — AUTO كتاب الطهارة › AUTO باب الحيض › AUTO (475) مسألة: قال: (والصفرة والكدرة في أيام الحيض من الحيض)

Arabic:
يعني إذا رأت في أيام عادتها صفرة أو كدرة، فهو حيض، وإن رأته بعد أيام حيضها، لم يعتد به. نص عليه أحمد. وبه قال يحيى الأنصاري، وربيعة، ومالك، والثوري، والأوزاعي، وعبد الرحمن بن مهدي، والشافعي، وإسحاق. وقال أبو يوسف، وأبو ثور: لا يكون حيضا، إلا أن يتقدمه دم أسود ; لأن أم عطية، وكانت بايعت النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم قالت: كنا لا نعتد بالصفرة والكدرة بعد الغسل شيئا. رواه أبو داود، وقال: بعد الطهر.

```

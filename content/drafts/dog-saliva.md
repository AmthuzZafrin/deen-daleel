# What do I do if a dog licks me or my clothes?

## Answer

**Wash the spot, and you are fine. There is no need for panic, and no need to
bathe.** How many washes depends on which school you follow, and the range is
wide — from one thorough wash to seven with earth.

**The hadith at the centre of it.** Abu Hurayra reported the Prophet saying:
**when a dog licks in the vessel of one of you, let him pour it away, then wash it
seven times** [1] — and in another wording, **the purification of the vessel of
one of you, when a dog laps in it, is that he wash it seven times, the first of
them with earth** [2].

**Note carefully what that is about: a vessel.** It is a rule about a container a
dog drank from, and about food. The schools then differ on how far it extends.

**The three positions**

| | |
|---|---|
| Shafi'i, Hanbali, Maliki (in the vessel) and most | seven washes, one with earth |
| al-Zuhri | three washes |
| Abu Hanifa | wash until you are satisfied it is clean — no fixed number |

Al-Nawawi lists the majority: **and this is what most of the scholars said. Ibn
al-Mundhir related the obligation of washing seven times from Abu Hurayra, Ibn
Abbas, Urwa ibn al-Zubayr, Tawus, Amr ibn Dinar, Malik, al-Awza'i, Ahmad, Ishaq,
Abu Ubayd and Abu Thawr — Ibn al-Mundhir said: and it is what I say.** [3] And
then the others: **al-Zuhri said washing it three times suffices him; and Abu
Hanifa said it must be washed until purity predominates in one's judgement — so if
that is achieved with one wash, it suffices him.** [4]

Ibn Qudama gives the Hanafi reasoning, which is worth understanding rather than
dismissing: **Abu Hanifa said: a number is not obligatory in any of the impurities;
rather it is washed until cleanness from impurity predominates in one's judgement**
[5] — because a fixed count is not the point of washing, cleanness is.

**What to actually do**

- **A dog licked your hand or arm** — wash that spot. On the majority view, seven
  times with earth (or soap, which substitutes for the earth) on the first. On the
  Hanafi view, wash it properly once. Either way it is your hand, not you.
- **A dog licked your clothes** — same, on the licked area. You do not wash the
  whole garment, and you do not need to change.
- **A dog drank from a bowl** — this is the case the hadith is literally about.
  Pour it out and wash the bowl as above.
- **A dog brushed against you when dry** — nothing. The hadith is about saliva.
  Even on the strictest reckoning, dry contact with dry fur transfers nothing.
- **Wet dog, wet clothes** — the strict schools say wash; the Hanafis say the dog's
  body is not impure at all, only its saliva. Wash if you can; do not tear the day
  apart if you cannot.

**On keeping and touching dogs generally.** That is a separate question from this
one, and it has its own answer here. What this page settles is narrow and
practical: saliva on a surface, washed off, is finished.

**A note on proportion.** Nothing in this makes you impure, makes your prayers
invalid retroactively, or requires ghusl. Ibn Qudama classes the dog under
substances needing a particular washing — **impurity is divided into two kinds:
the first, the impurity of the dog and the pig and what is bred from them, and on
this the school does not differ that it must be washed seven times, one of them
with earth, and it is the position of al-Shafi'i** [6] — and a rule about washing
a surface is exactly that, and nothing more.

If you work with dogs, are a guide-dog user, or live where dogs are simply part of
life, the Hanafi position — wash it clean — is a real, mainstream position held by
an imam, and it exists for circumstances like yours.

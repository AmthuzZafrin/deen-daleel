# How do I make up for a broken oath?

## Answer

The Qur'an gives four options, and you choose freely among the first three.

"Allah will not take you to task for that which is unintentional in your oaths, but He will take you to task for the oaths which ye swear in earnest. The expiation thereof is the feeding of ten of the needy with the average of that wherewith ye feed your own folk, or the clothing of them, or the liberation of a slave, and for him who findeth not (the wherewithal to do so) then a three days' fast" (5:89) [1].

Ibn Qudama: "the basis for the expiation of an oath is the Book, the Sunna and consensus" — the verse, then the hadith "when you swear an oath and see another better than it, do the one that is better and expiate your oath" — "and the Muslims are agreed on the legislation of expiation in an oath by Allah" [2].

**The structure: choose among three, fast only if you cannot.**

Al-Bahr al-Ra'iq: "the word *or* is for free choice, so what is obligatory is one of the three things" [3]. Ibn Kathir: "these are three options in the expiation of an oath — whichever of them the one who broke his oath does suffices him, by consensus. And He began with the easiest, then the next easiest: feeding is easier than clothing, just as clothing is easier than freeing — so He ascended in them from the lower to the higher" [4].

Ibn Taymiyya states it plainly: "whenever he has the means, he must expiate by one of the three; and if he does not have the means, then fasting three days" [5].

Since freeing a slave does not arise today, the practical choice is **feed ten poor people, or clothe ten poor people** — and fasting only if you genuinely cannot do either.

**Option 1: feeding ten.** How much?

This is where people get stuck, and there is a real disagreement — usefully so, because one side of it gives you a rule you can apply today.

Ibn Taymiyya lays out the first camp: "the measure of what he feeds is built on a principle — namely, is their feeding fixed by the Law or by custom? On it are two positions among the scholars. Some said it is *fixed by the Law*, and these hold several views: some said he feeds each poor person a *sa'* of dates or a *sa'* of barley, or half a *sa'* of wheat, as Abu Hanifa and a group said; some said he feeds each one half a *sa'* of dates and barley, or a quarter *sa'* of wheat, which is a *mudd*, as Ahmad and a group said; and some said a *mudd* of everything suffices in all cases, as al-Shafi'i and a group said" [6].

The second camp: "that it is fixed by *custom*, not by the Law — so the people of each land feed from the average of what they feed their own families, in quantity and in kind. And this is the meaning of Malik's position" [7]. Malik's own words: "as for other lands, they have a way of living other than ours, so I hold that they should expiate with the middling of their own living — because of Allah's words *of the average of that wherewith ye feed your own folk*" [8].

Ibn Taymiyya endorses this second view. And the Companions' rough guide to what "average" meant: "and for this reason they used to say: the average is bread and milk, bread and clarified butter, bread and dates; and the highest is bread and meat" [9].

**In practice:** work out what a normal meal for your own household costs, multiply by ten, and give that — either as ten meals to ten needy people, or its equivalent. Not your cheapest meal, and not a feast: the middle of how you actually eat. That is what the verse asks for.

**Option 2: clothing ten.**

Malik gives a concrete standard in the Muwatta, and the test he uses is elegant: "the best of what I have heard concerning one who expiates his oath by clothing is that if he clothes men, he clothes them a garment each; and if he clothes women, he clothes them two garments each, a shift and a head-covering — and that is the least that suffices each of them **in his prayer**" [10].

So the minimum is: enough clothing that the person could pray in it. Abu Musa's practice is recorded too — "he swore an oath, and clothed [each] with two garments of the fabric of Bahrayn" [11].

**Option 3: freeing a slave.** Historical, but the scholars' dispute over it is recorded: "Abu Hanifa took the verse unrestrictedly and said a non-Muslim slave suffices as a believing one does. Al-Shafi'i and others said she must be a believer" [12].

**Only if you cannot: fasting three days.**

Who counts as unable? Ibn Qudama: "the expiation of an oath combines free choice and sequence — he chooses among the three options, and if he does not find them he moves to fasting three days. And the standard is that he does not find, surplus to his own sustenance and the sustenance of his dependents for his day and his night, an amount by which to expiate" [13]. Al-Shafi'i set a different line: "whoever is permitted to take from *zakat* on account of his need and poverty, fasting suffices him" [14].

Both are workable tests, and both are demanding of most people reading this. If you can afford ten ordinary meals without your household going short, you are not in the fasting category. Note also the debt case: "if he owns what he would expiate with, but has a debt of the same amount and is being pressed for it, no expiation is due from him — because it is a human right, whereas the expiation is a right of Allah; so when he is being pressed for the debt, giving it precedence is obligatory" [15].

**Must the three days be consecutive?** A real split.

Al-Tabari records that Ibn Mas'ud's reading of the verse had the word *consecutively* in it, and that Sufyan al-Thawri, Qatada and Ibn 'Abbas required it. The Hanafis hold the same: "the fasting of three days in the expiation of an oath is consecutive" [16].

But others allowed splitting them: "it is permissible for one who fasts them to fast them however he wishes, together or separately… Malik said: everything Allah has mentioned in the Qur'an of fasting, that it be fasted consecutively is more pleasing — but if he separates them, I hope it will suffice him" [17]. Al-Tabari's own verdict: "the correct position in this, in our view, is that Allah has obligated upon whoever incurs the expiation of an oath, when he finds no way to expiate it by feeding, clothing or freeing, that he expiate it by fasting three days — and He did not stipulate in that that they be consecutive" [18].

Consecutive is safer; separate is defensible.

**When to expiate.**

Not only after you break the oath by accident. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever swears an oath and then sees another better than it, let him expiate his oath and do the one that is better" [19]. Expiation is the designed exit from an oath that has locked you into the worse of two courses. If you swore you would never speak to someone again, the instruction is not to keep that oath — it is to pay the expiation and go and speak to them.

**In short.** Feed ten needy people a normal meal by your own household's standard, or clothe ten of them well enough to pray in. If you truly cannot afford either, fast three days, preferably in a row. And if the oath is holding you to something worse than the alternative, break it deliberately, expiate, and do the better thing.

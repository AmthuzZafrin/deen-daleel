# Can I pray in clothes with a stain I'm not sure about?

## Answer

**Yes. A mark you are unsure about does not make your clothes impure, and you may
pray in them without investigating.**

**The governing rule is that certainty is not removed by doubt.** You know the
garment was clean. You do not know that anything impure touched it. Doubt does not
change a known state — the same principle that settles doubt about wudu, and
there is a separate answer here that lays it out in full.

So: a dark patch on dark trousers, a mark on a sleeve you cannot place, a spot
that might be food and might not — **pray**. You are not required to sniff it,
scrape it, or work out what it was.

**What would actually invalidate the prayer** is established impurity, not a
suspicion of it. Ibn Qudama, listing what may not be worn in prayer: **the impure —
prayer in it is not valid, nor upon it, because purity from impurity is a
condition, and it has been lost** [1]. A condition is lost when the impurity is
real, not when it is imagined.

**And even known impurity is excused below a threshold.** Every school has one,
and they are generous — see the separate answer here on how much is too much. A
mark small enough that you are squinting at it is inside all of them.

**The case that matters most: you cannot clean it and the prayer time is
running out.** Here the ruling is unambiguous and it goes the way people do not
expect. Ibn Taymiyya: **likewise one who has impurity on his body or his garment
which he cannot remove before the prayer time expires — he prays with it within
the time, and does not let the prayer be missed in order to pray in a state of
purity.** [2]

He then states the principle behind it, which is worth memorising: **an obligatory
prayer within its time, even if deficient, is better than letting the prayer be
missed past its time, even if complete.** [3]

So if you are at work with something on your shirt and no way to change, and Asr
is nearly gone: **you pray in the shirt.** Praying late to pray clean is the wrong
trade, and Ibn Taymiyya is blunt about which side of it is the sin.

**If you discover it after praying.** The schools differ on whether you repeat,
and the difference turns on whether you knew. If you genuinely did not know it was
there, the majority do not require you to repeat a prayer already prayed. If you
knew and prayed anyway with more than the excused amount, repeat it.

**If this happens to you constantly** — if you inspect your clothes before every
prayer, keep finding marks, change repeatedly, or cannot settle — that is a
different problem with its own answer here, and this ruling is its treatment. The
instruction above is not a concession to be used sparingly. It is the ruling, and
acting on it deliberately is the cure.

**Practical summary**

| | |
|---|---|
| Mark you cannot identify | pray, no investigation required |
| Known impurity, small | pray — under every school's threshold |
| Known impurity, large, and you can wash it | wash the spot and pray |
| Known impurity, large, and you cannot | **pray in the time anyway** |
| Discovered afterwards, you did not know | prayer stands |

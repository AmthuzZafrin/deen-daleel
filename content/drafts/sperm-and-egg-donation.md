# Is using a donor's sperm or egg allowed?

> Reviewer note: The IVF answer names this as the hard part and stops. Here the nasab material can be given properly — lineage as a legal relation attached to the marriage, and the affinity rules that hang off it.


## Answer

**The answer on IVF names donor gametes as the hard part and stops there. This is that part, and the material is much less silent than people expect.** No author here contemplated a clinic. But every one of them was intensely occupied with the question donor conception raises — how a child's parentage is fixed, and what follows when it is assigned to someone it does not belong to.

**Lineage in these books is a legal relation, not simply a biological fact.**

`الولد للفراش وللعاهر الحجر` [1] — the child belongs to the marital bed, and the fornicator gets the stone. Al-Nawawi identifies the bed as the husband: `أي لمالك للفراش، وهو الزوج` [2].

The point is that `نسب` attaches to a lawful union. A biological contribution outside one does not create it. That is why the rule produces the result it does: the child attaches to the marriage, and the outside contributor has no claim — `وللعاهر الحجر`.

Notice that this cuts in an unexpected direction. The classical rule would attach the child to the husband, since it is his marriage and his wife's bed. What it will not do is make the donor a father. So the fear that a donor-conceived child is somehow parentless is not what these texts produce.

The difficulty is elsewhere, and it is sharper.

**The prohibition on attributing a child to the wrong father.**

`ادعوهم لآبائهم هو أقسط عند الله` [3] — call them by their fathers; that is more equitable before God. The verse was revealed about adoption, and it abolished giving an adopted child the adopting father's name — while leaving the adoption itself, and the care, entirely intact.

And the hadith is among the most severe in the collection: `من ادعى إلى غير أبيه، وهو يعلم أنه غير أبيه فالجنة عليه حرام` [4] — whoever claims descent from other than his father, knowing he is not his father, Paradise is forbidden to him.

This is the material that makes donor conception the difficult case. It is not that the tradition is squeamish about assisted conception. It is that it treats a person's knowledge of where they came from as something that may not be falsified — severely enough to attach that threat to it.

The shape of the concern is visible in the adoption verse: the practical relationship was permitted; what was forbidden was the false attribution. Whether a donor-conceived child raised knowing the truth falls inside or outside that is exactly what a scholar must weigh, and this page will not weigh it.

**What follows practically, and it is not small.**

Because `نسب` generates the marriage-prohibition map, an unrecorded genetic parent means unrecorded half-siblings. A donor with many offspring in one city produces a group of people who do not know they are related and who may marry. That is not a modern anxiety projected backwards; it is precisely what the lineage rules exist to keep determinate.

**What these books do not reach.**

They do not distinguish donor sperm from donor egg, and the arguments are not identical, since gestation and birth are involved in one and not the other. They do not address a known and recorded donor, or registries that let a child learn their origins later. They do not address embryo donation, nor what should be done about children already born this way — a separate question, on which the tradition's position that no child bears the circumstances of its conception is the relevant material rather than any of the above.

**What to ask.**

- Is anything at any stage coming from outside the marriage? That is the question; everything else is secondary.
- If yes: will the child be told, and will the genetic parent be recorded and traceable?
- How does your scholar's school establish `نسب` here, and to whom does the child attach?
- What becomes of the marriage-prohibition rules for this child, and how would anyone ever check them?
- Are there alternatives that keep everything within the marriage?

And if you are asking because you are yourself donor-conceived, or already have a child conceived this way: the material above is about the act, not about you. `ادعوهم لآبائهم` is an instruction about honesty in what a child is told — something that can still be done — not a verdict on anyone's standing. Take that as its own question, and do not accept an answer that treats a person as a consequence.

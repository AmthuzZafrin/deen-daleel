# Can I dye my hair?

## Answer

Yes — with one colour singled out for a warning, and that is the whole of the
controversy. Everything else here is either encouraged or left to you.

**Dyeing grey is not merely permitted; it is recommended.** The instruction is
about distinction: "the Jews and the Christians do not dye, so differ from them"
— and Ahmad's narration from Abu Umama has the Prophet ﷺ coming out to some
elders of the Ansar whose beards were white and saying, "O company of the Ansar,
redden and yellow, and differ from the People of the Book" [1]. Al-Nawawi: "our
position is that dyeing grey is recommended for the man and the woman, with
yellow or red" [2]. The Hanafis agree and add a reason: "the scholars are agreed
that dyeing with red, for men, is a *sunna* — and that it is among the marks and
signs of the Muslims" [3].

The best-attested dyes are named: Abu Dharr's report, "the best with which grey
is changed is henna and *katam*" [4] — *katam* being a plant that darkens henna
towards brown. And in practice: "Abu Bakr dyed with henna and *katam*, and
'Umar dyed with henna alone" [5].

**The exception: pure black.** From the story of Abu Quhafa, brought before the
Prophet ﷺ with his head white as the *thughama* plant: "change this with
something, and avoid black" [6]. Al-Qurtubi treats it as parallel to plucking
grey: "just as plucking it is disliked, so is changing it with black — but
changing it with other than black is permitted" [7].

How severe is that? Genuinely disputed, and the sources say so. Al-Nawawi:
"dyeing it black is forbidden, according to the sounder view; and it is said it
is disliked by way of avoidance — and the chosen view is prohibition, because of
his saying ﷺ *and avoid black*" [8]. Ibn Hajar reports the wider picture: "some
of the scholars permitted it in *jihad*, and some permitted it absolutely; and
the better view is that it is disliked — and al-Nawawi inclined to its being a
dislike amounting to prohibition" [9]. Malik declined to rule: "Malik said
concerning dyeing the hair black: I have not heard anything definite about it,
and other dyes are more beloved to me" [10].

And a list of Companions who did dye black: "a group dyed with black — that is
related from 'Uthman, from al-Hasan and al-Husayn the two sons of 'Ali, from
'Uqba b. 'Amir, Ibn Sirin, Abu Burda and others" [11]. That is not a licence,
but it is why the scholars record this as *disputed* rather than settled.

**Whether to dye at all was itself disputed.** Qadi 'Iyad: "the salaf, among the
Companions and the Successors, differed over dyeing and over its kind. Some of
them said leaving dyeing is better, and they related a hadith from the Prophet ﷺ
forbidding the changing of grey — because he ﷺ did not change his own grey; this
is related from 'Umar, 'Ali, Ubayy and others" [12]. Against that: "others said
dyeing is better, and a group of the Companions and the Successors and those
after them dyed, on account of the hadiths Muslim and others recorded. Then
these differed: most of them would dye with yellow, among them Ibn 'Umar and Abu
Hurayra and others, and that is related from 'Ali; and a group of them dyed with
henna and *katam*, and some with saffron" [13].

Al-Tabari reconciles them by person rather than by rule: "whoever among them
dyed, it suited him as it suits one whose greyness is unsightly; and whoever
left it, that suited him as it suits one whose greyness is not unsightly" [14] —
"so whoever is in a state like Abu Quhafa's, dyeing is recommended for him,
because no one is deceived by it; and whoever is otherwise, it is not
recommended in his case" [15].

**One thing that is clearly discouraged either way.** Al-Qurtubi: "as for grey
hair, it is light, and plucking it is disliked — in al-Nasa'i and Abu Dawud,
from 'Amr b. Shu'ayb from his father from his grandfather: the Messenger of
Allah ﷺ said, *do not pluck grey hair; there is no Muslim who goes grey in Islam
but it is a light for him on the Day of Resurrection, and Allah writes a good
deed for him by it and removes a sin from him*" [16].

**So, practically.** Colour your hair freely — henna, browns, reds, blondes, and
by extension any colour that is not the concealing jet black the hadith singles
out. Covering grey is encouraged rather than merely tolerated. Pure black is the
one to avoid: forbidden on al-Nawawi's chosen view, disliked on Ibn Hajar's,
unsettled for Malik, and practised by a number of Companions — which is exactly
the profile of a real disagreement, and a reason to leave it alone rather than
to argue about it. And do not pull the grey hairs out; the hadith calls them a
light.

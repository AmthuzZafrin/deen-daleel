# What does Islam say about being in debt?

## Answer

**Borrowing is permitted and ordinary; being in debt is treated as a serious condition to get out of. Both halves are in these books, and holding them together is the whole of the answer.**

**Debt is lawful and unremarkable.** Al-Bukhari gives it its own book, and the very first chapter heading settles the basic question: `كتاب في الاستقراض وأداء الديون والحجر والتفليس) باب من اشترى بالدين وليس عنده ثمنه` [1] — the book of borrowing, repaying debts, interdiction and insolvency; **chapter: on one who buys on credit not having its price with him.** The reports gathered there include the Prophet's own purchase on deferred terms with his armour pledged against it.

So nobody should tell you that taking on a debt is in itself blameworthy. It is a recognised transaction with an entire body of law devoted to making it work.

**But the weight attached to owing is unusual.** `نفس المؤمن معلقة بدينه حتى يقضى عنه` [2] — **the soul of the believer remains suspended by his debt until it is discharged for him.**

Al-Shafi'i takes that seriously enough to build a rule of estate administration on it. He notes that debts *owed to* a dead person keep their terms — `وإذا مات الرجل وله على الناس ديون إلى أجل، فهي إلى أجلها، لا تحل بموته، ولو كانت الديون على الميت إلى أجل فلم أعلم مخالفا حفظت عنه ممن لقيت بأنها حالة يتحاص فيها الغرماء، فان فضل فضل كان لأهل الميراث` [3] — but debts *owed by* him fall due immediately, with creditors sharing proportionately and heirs taking only what is left over, and he knows of no one who differed.

His reasoning turns directly on the hadith: `فلما كان كفنه من رأس ماله دون غرمائه ونفسه معلقة بدينه وكان المال ملكا له، أشبه أن يجعل قضاء دينه، لان نفسه معلقة بدينه` [4] — since his shroud comes from his estate ahead of his creditors, and **his soul is suspended by his debt**, and the wealth was his, it is fitting that it go to discharging his debt. And the principle underneath: `لما كان غرماء الميت أحق بماله في حياته منه، كانوا أحق بماله بعد وفاته من ورثته` [5] — since the creditors had a better right to his wealth than he did in his lifetime, they have a better right to it after his death than his heirs.

That is worth sitting with. Your creditors have a stronger claim on your assets than your own children do. Debt is not a private inconvenience; it is a claim other people hold over you that outlives you.

**Why the tradition is wary of it.** The Prophet regularly sought refuge from debt, and when asked why he did so often, the answer named the moral damage rather than the financial. Ibn Hajar: `قوله والمغرم أي الدين يقال غرم بكسر الراء أي أدان قيل والمراد به ما يستدان فيما لا يجوز ثم يعجز عن أدائه ويحتمل أن يراد به ما هو أعم من ذلك وقد استعاذ صلى الله عليه وسلم من غلبة الدين` [6] — `المغرم` is debt; it is said to mean what is borrowed **for what is not permitted and then he is unable to repay**, though it may be more general; and he sought refuge from being **overwhelmed** by debt.

On the report that a man in debt speaks falsely and promises and breaks his word: `والمراد أن ذلك شأن من يستدين غالبا` [7] — this is generally the condition of one who takes on debt.

The objection is not to owing money. It is to what being unable to pay does to a person: it makes them lie, and makes them promise what they cannot deliver. That is a description of the position rather than a judgement on the borrower, and anyone who has been in it will recognise it.

**Write it down.** The longest verse in the Qur'an is about this: *"O ye who believe! When ye contract a debt for a fixed term, record it in writing. Let a scribe record it in writing between you in (terms of) equity"* [8].

Not a suggestion, and not a sign of mistrust. Undocumented debts between friends and family are where most of the bitterness in this area comes from, and the remedy is stated at greater length than almost any other practical instruction in scripture.

**What follows practically.**

- **Borrow for real needs, not to fund a standard of living.** The gloss above singles out borrowing for what is not permitted and then being unable to repay — the two failures usually arrive together.
- **Write down every loan**, including between relatives, with the amount and the term. Especially between relatives.
- **Treat repayment as a priority claim on your money**, not as one commitment among many. On al-Shafi'i's reasoning your creditors outrank your heirs.
- **Make a will and record what you owe.** If your debts are not written down, your family cannot discharge them for you, and the texts above describe that as leaving something unresolved.
- **If you cannot pay, say so early and honestly.** The condition the reports warn about is the one where the borrower starts avoiding the lender and making promises he knows he cannot keep. Silence is what turns a debt into the thing the Prophet sought refuge from.

If you are already in serious debt, the corresponding obligation on your creditor — to give time to someone genuinely unable to pay — is real and is set out on the page about being owed money. Knowing that you are entitled to ask for time, rather than obliged to disappear, is usually the more useful half of this subject.

Qur'an 2:282 — Surah Al-Baqara, verse 282 of chapter 2

Translation (Marmaduke Pickthall):
O ye who believe! When ye contract a debt for a fixed term, record it in writing. Let a scribe record it in writing between you in (terms of) equity. No scribe should refuse to write as Allah hath taught him, so let him write, and let him who incurreth the debt dictate, and let him observe his duty to Allah his Lord, and diminish naught thereof. And call to witness, from among your men, two witnesses. And if two men be not (at hand) then a man and two women, of such as ye approve as witnesses, so that if the one erreth (through forgetfulness) the other will remember. And the witnesses must not refuse when they are summoned. Be not averse to writing down (the contract) whether it be small or great, with (record of) the term thereof. That is more equitable in the sight of Allah and more sure for testimony, and the best way of avoiding doubt between you; save only in the case when it is actual merchandise which ye transfer among yourselves from hand to hand. In that case it is no sin for you if ye write it not. And have witnesses when ye sell one to another, and let no harm be done to scribe or witness. If ye do (harm to them) lo! it is a sin in you. Observe your duty to Allah. Allah is teaching you. And Allah is knower of all things.

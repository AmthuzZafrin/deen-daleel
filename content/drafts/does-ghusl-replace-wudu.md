# If I've done ghusl do I still need wudu?

## Answer

**Yes. A complete ghusl lets you pray without doing wudu separately.** You do not
need a second purification.

**Why.** Ibn Abd al-Barr states the ground directly: **because Allah only imposed
the bath on the person in janaba, not wudu** [1] — citing the two verses that
mention janaba, neither of which asks for anything but a bath. And he calls it
settled: **this is a consensus, on which there is no disagreement among the
scholars** [2].

Al-Nawawi says the same from the Shafi'i side: **if he pours water over his whole
body without wudu, his ghusl is valid, and by it he may perform prayer and
anything else.** [3]

**The reasoning behind it**, which is worth having because it explains a whole
family of rulings: the greater purification absorbs the lesser. Ibn Qudama: **the
sequence and the continuity are not obligatory in the limbs of wudu, if we say the
ghusl suffices in place of them** [4] — the two are one act, not two stacked on
each other.

**Do not do a second wudu after the bath.** This is the practical point people
most often get wrong. Al-Nawawi: **the scholars have agreed that two wudus are
not recommended.** [5] The evidence is Aisha's report: **the Messenger of Allah
did not perform wudu after the bath from janaba.** [6]

So the sunna order is: wudu, then bath. Not bath, then wudu. Ibn Abd al-Barr is
explicit — **this wudu is before the bath, not after it** [7].

**What can still undo it.** Ghusl removes the state of janaba *and* the ordinary
state of minor impurity. But it does not immunise you: if you break wudu after
bathing — you use the toilet, you sleep, you pass wind — you need wudu again,
like anyone else. What you do *not* need is another bath.

A common case: you shower, then get dressed and use the toilet before praying.
Your ghusl still stands, and you are no longer in janaba. You simply make wudu.

**One dissenting position, for honesty.** A minority — Abu Thawr, Dawud and some
others — held that the bath does not stand in place of wudu for someone who was
*already* in a state of ordinary impurity before the janaba occurred. It is a
narrow reading and not the position of any of the four schools, and the sunna
practice dissolves it anyway: do the wudu inside your ghusl, at the start, as the
Prophet did, and the question never arises.

**Practical summary**

- Intend ghusl, make wudu as part of it, complete the bath → **pray directly.**
- Skipped the wudu and just poured water over everything with the intention →
  **still valid, still pray directly.**
- Broke wudu after the bath → **wudu only**, not another bath.

The verse pairs the two states and asks for a different thing from each: wash for
prayer, **and if ye are unclean, purify yourselves** [8]. The bath is the bigger
of the two requirements, and it carries the smaller inside it.

Qur'an 24:58 — Surah An-Noor, verse 58 of chapter 24

Translation (Marmaduke Pickthall):
O ye who believe! Let your slaves, and those of you who have not come to puberty, ask leave of you at three times (before they come into your presence): Before the prayer of dawn, and when ye lay aside your raiment for the heat of noon, and after the prayer of night. Thus Allah maketh clear the revelations for you. Allah is Knower, Wise.

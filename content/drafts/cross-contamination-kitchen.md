# What if halal food touched pork in a shared kitchen?

## Answer

**Short answer: wash it and use it. That is not a lenient reading — it is the
answer the Prophet ﷺ gave when he was asked about people who cooked pork in their
pots, in those words.** The classical discussion is more practical than most
modern advice on the subject, and it gives you a usable test.

**The question, as it was actually put.** Al-Nawawi records the wording: **"انا
نجاور أهل الكتاب وهم يطبخون في قدورهم الخنزير ويشربون في آنيتهم الخمر فقال رسول
الله صلى الله عليه وسلم ان وجدتم غيرها فكلوا فيها واشربوا وان لم تجدوا غيرها
فارحضوها بالماء وكلوا واشربوا"** — *we live alongside the People of the
Scripture, and they cook pork in their pots and drink wine in their vessels. The
Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: if you find others, then eat and drink in those; and
if you find no others, wash them with water, and eat and drink* [1].

A shared kitchen with a flatmate who cooks pork is that situation, almost word for
word. The instruction has two parts: **prefer another pan if you have one; wash
this one if you do not.**

**And al-Nawawi resolves what the preference is about.** He notes an apparent
tension — the jurists say a washed vessel carries no dislike at all, while the
hadith seems to prefer another even after washing — and answers: **"وإنما نهى عن
الأكل فيها بعد الغسل للاستقذار وكونها معتادة للنجاسة كما يكره الأكل في المحجمة
المغسولة"** — *the discouragement of eating from them after washing is on account
of **distaste** and their being habitually used for impurity — as one dislikes
eating from a washed cupping-vessel* [2].

Read that carefully. The residual preference is **aesthetic, not legal**. He then
states the general rule: **"وأما الفقهاء فمرادهم مطلق آنية الكفار التي ليست
مستعملة في النجاسات فهذه يكره استعمالها قبل غسلها فإذا غسلت فلا كراهة فيها لأنها
طاهرة وليس فيها استقذار"** — *as for the jurists, what they mean is vessels of
non-Muslims generally, not used for impurities; using those is disliked before
washing, and once washed there is no dislike in them at all, because they are
pure and there is nothing repellent in them* [3].

**Once washed, pure.** That is the ruling.

**How much washing.** For most impurities, no particular count. Al-Nawawi: **"وفيه
أن إزالة النجاسة لا يشترط فيها العدد بل يكفي فيها الانقاء"** — *and in it is that
removing impurity does not require a set number; cleanness suffices* [4], and
**"واعلم أن الواجب في إزالة النجاسة الانقاء"** — *know that what is obligatory in
removing impurity is cleanness* [5].

Pig is treated more strictly by some. Ibn Qudama: **"أحدهما ; نجاسة الكلب
والخنزير والمتولد منهما، فهذا لا يختلف المذهب في أنه يجب غسلها سبعا، إحداهن
بالتراب، وهو قول الشافعي"** — *the first is the impurity of the dog, the pig, and
what is bred from them; the school does not differ that it must be washed seven
times, one of them with earth, and that is al-Shafi'i's position* [6]. Not every
school extends the dog ruling to the pig, but where it is followed, that is the
standard.

**The agent is water.** Ibn 'Abd al-Barr reports the position of Malik, Zufar,
al-Shafi'i and Ahmad: **"أن النجاسة لا يطهرها إلا الماء لأن الله تعالى سماه طهورا
ولم يقل ذلك في غيره"** — *that nothing purifies impurity but water, because Allah
called it purifying and did not say that of anything else* [7].

**And here is the test that actually matters.** Ibn Qudama sets the limit case:
**"فإذا كان في الإناء خمر أو شبهه من النجاسات التي يتشربها الإناء ثم متى جعل فيه
مائع سواه ظهر فيه طعم النجاسة، أو لونها لم يطهر بالغسل ; لأن الغسل لا يستأصل
أجزاء النجاسة من جسم الإناء، فلم يطهره"** — *if there was wine or the like in the
vessel, of impurities that the vessel absorbs, and then whenever another liquid is
put in it the taste or colour of the impurity appears in it, it is not purified by
washing — because washing does not extract the particles of impurity from the body
of the vessel, so it does not purify it* [8].

**That is an observable test, not a suspicion.** If the trace still shows through
— taste, colour, smell — the washing has not worked. If nothing shows through, it
has.

**So, in a shared kitchen.**

- **Wash the pan properly and use it.** Hot water and detergent, or a dishwasher,
  comfortably exceeds the standard the sources set — which was rinsing with water.
- **If you can keep your own pan, do.** The hadith expresses that preference, and
  al-Nawawi tells you it is about distaste rather than impurity — so there is no
  reason to feel guilty either way.
- **The test is whether a trace remains.** A greasy film or a smell of pork means
  it is not clean yet. A pan that comes out with nothing on it is clean, and no
  amount of worrying changes that.
- **Cutlery, plates, surfaces and sponges follow the same rule**, and are easier
  cases since they absorb less.
- **Do not treat the kitchen as contaminated ground.** Nothing above supports
  that, and the reasoning runs firmly the other way.
- **If you follow a school that applies the seven-wash rule to pig**, that is the
  standard for the direct contact case — it is not a rule about the whole room.

**Where this stops.** These sources are working with earthenware, metal and skin.
Whether a modern non-porous surface absorbs anything at all is a factual question
they could not address — though the test they give, whether a trace shows through,
applies to any material you like.

O ye who believe! When ye rise up for prayer, wash you faces, and your hands up to the elbows, and lightly rub your heads and (wash) your feet up to the ankles.

# Is there something I say when breaking my fast?

## Answer

**Say *bismillah* and eat. There is one supplication reported for the moment after
breaking, and one thing worth knowing about the words most people actually use.**

**The reported du'a.**

Abu Dawud transmits from Ibn Umar: **the Messenger of God, when he broke his fast,
would say: the thirst has gone, the veins are moistened, and the reward is
established, if God wills** [1].

In Arabic: *dhahaba al-zama', wa-btallat al-'uruq, wa-thabata al-ajr in sha' Allah.*

Note when it is said — **when he broke his fast**, that is, after taking the first
mouthful, not before. It is a statement of what has just happened, not a request.

**About "Allahumma laka sumtu…"**

The formula many people learn — *O God, for You I have fasted and with Your
provision I have broken the fast* — does not appear in any of the passages retrieved
for this question. It is widely transmitted and widely used, and its chains are
discussed in the hadith literature; but this answer can only report what is in front
of it, and the wording above from Abu Dawud is the one that is here. Saying either
is not a problem. Neither is required.

**What is actually required: nothing.**

There is no obligatory formula. Say *bismillah*, as before any food. If you forget,
say *bismillah awwalahu wa akhirahu* when you remember. That is the whole
requirement.

**The moment itself.**

Break at sunset without delay — **my community will not cease to be in good so long
as they hasten the breaking of the fast** [2], and in the hadith qudsi: **God says:
the most beloved of My servants to Me are the quickest of them to break the fast**
[3]. Al-Nawawi has Abu Dharr's version pairing both ends of the day: **my community
will not cease to be in good so long as they hasten the breaking of the fast and
delay the pre-dawn meal** [4].

**What to break on:** Ibn Qudama, from Anas's report of the Prophet's practice: **it
is recommended that he break the fast on fresh dates; if there are none, then dried
dates; if there are none, then water** [5]. And the order of things: **I never saw
the Messenger of God pray until he had broken his fast, even if only with a drink of
water** [6] — so you break first, then pray Maghrib, then eat properly.

**Du'a at that moment is worth making.** The sources here do not prescribe its words,
which means you may ask for whatever you want, in whatever language you have. If you
have been saving something to ask for, this is a good time.

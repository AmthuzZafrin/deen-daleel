# What does Islam say about wasting time?

## Answer

There is a hadith that names this exactly, and the word it uses is the word for being cheated in a sale.

Ibn 'Abbas reported the Prophet ﷺ: "**two blessings in which many people are cheated: health and free time**" [1].

*Maghbun* is a commercial term. It means you made a trade and came off badly — you handed over something valuable and received less than it was worth, without noticing at the time. That is the frame the hadith puts on an idle afternoon.

**The commentators press on the word, and it repays it.**

Al-Tibi: "the Prophet ﷺ made a parable for the accountable person by the trader who has capital and seeks profit while keeping his capital intact — so his method is to be careful whom he deals with, and to hold to honesty and shrewdness so that he is not cheated. **So health and free time are the capital**" [2].

Not the profit. The capital. You do not spend capital; you invest it, and if it goes you have nothing to work with.

Ibn Battal: "a man is not free unless he is provided for and sound in body. So whoever obtains that, let him be eager not to be cheated — by abandoning thanks to God for what He has bestowed on him; and part of thanking Him is carrying out His commands and avoiding His prohibitions. **Whoever falls short in that is the one cheated**" [3].

Ibn al-Jawzi sharpens why the pairing matters: "a person may be healthy but not free, being occupied with his livelihood; and he may be well-off but not healthy. **So when the two combine, and laziness away from obedience overcomes him — he is the one cheated**" [4].

Read that as a description of a Saturday morning with a charged phone. Health, time, no obligation for the next two hours — the exact conjunction the hadith is about.

**And this is the sentence that closes the escape route.**

Ibn al-'Arabi: "whoever lets himself go with the soul that commands to evil, inclining to comfort, and abandons keeping the limits and persevering in obedience, has been cheated. And likewise if he was free — **for the occupied man may have an excuse, unlike the free man, from whom the excuse is lifted and the proof is established against him**" [5].

The busy person has a case to make. The person with a free evening does not. That is uncomfortable and it is meant to be.

**What you will be asked.**

Abu Barza al-Aslami reported the Prophet ﷺ: "**a servant's two feet will not move on the Day of Resurrection until he is asked** about his lifetime, in what he used it up; and about his knowledge, what he did with it; and about his wealth, from where he acquired it and in what he spent it; and about his body, in what he wore it out" [6]. Another version puts it as five, adding "**his youth, in what he wore it out**" [7].

Note the verb in the first question: *afnahu* — used it up, spent it to nothing. It is not asking what happened to you. It is asking what you spent it on.

**The rule that decides most of it.**

"Part of a man's good practice of Islam is **his leaving what does not concern him**" [8].

That is the whole test for a feed, and it is much wider than a prohibition. Most of what a phone offers is not forbidden; it simply does not concern you. The news of a stranger's argument, the details of a scandal in another country, the opinion of a man you will never meet — none of it is haram, and none of it is yours.

Ibn 'Umar reported: "the Messenger of Allah ﷺ took me by the shoulder and said: **be in the world as though you were a stranger or a wayfarer**. And Ibn 'Umar used to say: when you reach evening, do not wait for morning; and when you reach morning, do not wait for evening; and **take from your health for your illness, and from your life for your death**" [9].

**One more thing, about what fills the hours.**

There is a story al-Qurtubi tells: a master told his servant to slaughter a sheep and bring the two best morsels in it; he brought the tongue and the heart. Told to throw away the two worst, he threw away the tongue and the heart. Asked to explain: "**there is nothing better than these two when they are good, and nothing worse than them when they are corrupt**" [10]. The Prophet ﷺ said the same of the second: "in the body is a morsel — when it is sound the whole body is sound, and when it is corrupt the whole body is corrupt. **Truly it is the heart**" [11].

The reason time matters is not that hours are a quota. It is that what you spend them on becomes what you are.

**Practically.**

- **The question is not whether it is haram.** Most of it is not. The question the sources put is whether you are being cheated in a trade — handing over capital and receiving little.
- **Leave what does not concern you.** Applied honestly, this removes most of a feed without anyone having to declare anything forbidden.
- **Guard the conjunction.** Health *and* free time together are when the hadith says people lose. Those are your good hours — the ones a phone is best at absorbing.
- **Do not look for an excuse in busyness.** Ibn al-'Arabi says the busy have one and the free do not.
- **Take from your health for your illness.** Ibn 'Umar's instruction is to spend the strong hours on what the weak ones will not be able to do.

None of this asks you to be joyless. Rest is not the trade the hadith describes; the trade it describes is not noticing what you handed over.

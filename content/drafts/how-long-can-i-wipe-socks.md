# How long can I keep wiping over socks before I have to wash?

## Answer

**A day and a night if you are at home. Three days and nights if you are
travelling.** That is the position of the overwhelming majority, and it comes
straight from the wording of the hadith.

Ali reported: **the Messenger of Allah used to command us that the resident wipe
for a day and a night, and the traveller for three.** [1] Al-Jassas lists who
holds it — **all our colleagues, and al-Thawri, al-Hasan ibn Salih, al-Awza'i and
al-Shafi'i: the resident wipes over the khuffs for a day and a night, and the
traveller for three days and their nights** [2] — which is Hanafi, Shafi'i and
more in a single sentence. Ahmad's own answer, when asked whether he held the
time limit: **the time limit is what is established in wiping over the khuffs.**
[3]

**When does the clock start? Not when you put them on.** This is the detail
almost everyone gets wrong, and getting it right buys you hours. Al-Nawawi: **the
position of al-Shafi'i and many others is that the beginning of the period is
from the time of the hadath after putting on the khuff — not from the time of
putting them on, and not from the time of wiping.** [4]

So: you make wudu, put your socks on, and *nothing starts*. The clock begins the
moment that wudu breaks. Put socks on at 6am, break wudu at 11am, and your day
runs to 11am tomorrow.

**Umar's rule of thumb**, on the same reckoning: **Umar said, he wipes over them
until the like of that same hour of a day and a night.** [5] Ibn Abd al-Barr also
gives the count in prayers, which is a useful way to hold it: **it is not permitted
for the resident to wipe for more than five prayers — a day and a night — nor for
the traveller more than fifteen prayers, three days and their nights.** [6]

**What ends the period early**

- **Taking them off.**
- **Janaba.** The time limit does not survive a full bath being due: **if he
  becomes junub before the period expires, wiping over the khuff is not
  permitted** [7]. You take them off, do ghusl, and start again. Ordinary wudu-
  breaking does not do this — the hadith is explicit that the exception is janaba
  and not **stool, urine and the like** [8].
- **Arriving home while travelling.** Al-Sarakhsi: **if the traveller reaches his
  town after having wiped for a day and a night or more than that, he must take
  the khuffs off** [9]. Your allowance drops to the resident's the moment you get
  home — and if you have already used it up, it is used up.

**What happens when the time runs out.** Not that your prayers become invalid
retroactively — the wudu you have simply ends. Ibn Qudama: **when the period
expires the wudu is invalidated, and he may not wipe unless he takes them off and
then puts them on in a state of complete purity.** [10] So you wash your feet in
your next wudu, and if you want another period, put the socks back on while in
that fresh, complete wudu.

**The minority view, and why it isn't the safe choice.** Malik and al-Layth held
there is no time limit at all: **al-Layth said, he wipes as long as he sees fit;
and likewise Malik said regarding the traveller** [11]. Their evidence is the
hadith of Ubayy ibn Imara, where the Prophet is asked "a day? two? three?" and
answers "and as much as you wish." But that report is weak: **as for the hadith of
Ubayy ibn Imara, it has been said that it is not strong, and its chain has been
disputed** [12] — al-Jassas, Ibn Qudama and al-Qurtubi all note the same defect.

**Practically**: note the time your wudu first breaks after putting the socks on.
That is your start. Add a day, or three if you are travelling. When it ends, wash
your feet — and note the verse that all of this is a concession within: **and
lightly rub your heads and (wash) your feet up to the ankles** [13].

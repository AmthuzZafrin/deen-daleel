# My bleeding is irregular — how do I know when to pray?

## Answer

**Bleeding outside your normal pattern is called *istihada*, and it does not stop
you praying. You work out which days are your period, treat those as your period,
and pray on all the rest.**

**The category.**

Al-Sarakhsi gives the definition and the hadith it rests on. Fatima came to the
Prophet and said she bled continuously and never became pure, and he answered:
**that is not the blood of menstruation — it is a vein that has burst, or an
illness that has occurred. Perform wudu for every prayer** [1]. So the sources
have a name for this and a ruling for it; it is not an edge case.

Al-Sarakhsi defines menstruation legally as **blood of a specific description,
being continuous, coming out from a specific place** [2]. What falls outside that
is istihada.

Ibn Nujaym gives a physical distinction: **the blood of istihada is a name for
blood coming out of the vagina but not from the womb, and its sign is that it has
no smell; while the blood of menstruation has a foul smell** [3].

Ibn Abd al-Barr states the consequence plainly: **the ruling on this is that the
woman in it is pure — it does not prevent her from prayer or from fasting, by the
consensus of the scholars and the agreement of the transmitted reports** [4].

**Working out which days are your period. Two methods.**

**Method 1 — your prior habit (*'ada*).** This is the one to use if you know what
your cycle used to be. Umm Salama brought this exact case to the Prophet and the
answer was: **let her look at the number of nights and days she used to menstruate
each month before this thing happened to her, and let her leave the prayer for
that much of the month; then when she has passed that, let her bathe, then bind
herself with a cloth, then pray** [5]. Aisha transmits the same: **let her look at
how much she used to menstruate each month when her period was regular, count that
number of days, then leave the prayer during them; then let her bathe** [6].

**Method 2 — distinguishing by the blood itself (*tamyiz*).** The Prophet told
Fatima bint Abi Hubaysh: **when it is menstrual blood, it is black blood, known;
so hold back from prayer. And when it is the other, perform wudu — it is only a
vein** [7]. And in Aisha's narration: **it is only a vein and not menstruation. So
when the period comes, leave the prayer; and when it goes, wash the trace of blood
from yourself and perform wudu** [8].

Ibn Abd al-Barr reports Malik using the second: **when the woman with istihada
distinguishes between the two bloods, she acts on the distinguishing, at the
coming and going of the period, and does not pay attention to the number of nights
and days** [9], and that once she recognises the onset and end **she counts it**
as a menstrual period.

**How to pray while it continues.**

Ibn Nujaym gives the Hanafi rule: **her wudu is for the time of each obligatory
prayer, not for each prayer** [10] — so one wudu covers a prayer time, and you
renew at the next. Other schools require wudu per prayer, following the wording of
Fatima's hadith. Either way you wash, put on protection, make wudu, and pray. The
bleeding does not invalidate the prayer while it continues.

**In practice.**

1. Are your normal days identifiable? Use them. Those days: no prayer. All other
   days: pray.
2. Can you tell the bloods apart by colour and character? Use that.
3. Neither? Many jurists default to a standard cycle — commonly six or seven days
   — and you pray the rest. Ask a scholar for your school's default.
4. Once your period days end: ghusl, then pray with protection and fresh wudu,
   despite ongoing bleeding.

**And see a doctor.** The sources call this a burst vein or an illness. Prolonged
or irregular bleeding is a medical matter as well as a legal one, and the fiqh
here exists so that you keep worshipping while you get it treated — not so that
you treat it as normal.

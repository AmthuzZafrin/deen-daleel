# Can I give zakat to non-Muslims?

## Answer

Obligatory zakat, no. Voluntary charity to non-Muslims, yes — freely, and to non-Muslim relatives it is better than to strangers. The distinction between those two things is the whole answer.

**Zakat itself.** Ibn 'Abd al-Barr reports this as settled: **they were agreed that obligatory zakat is not lawful to other than Muslims** [1]. Al-Fatawa al-Hindiyya says the same and immediately pairs it with the permission: **as for the people of dhimma, it is not permissible to direct zakat to them by agreement — and it is permissible to direct voluntary charity to them by agreement** [2]. Al-Qurtubi reports the scholars he surveyed to the same effect, **that the dhimmi is not given anything from the zakat of wealth** [3].

The reasoning is not hostility. It is that zakat is a levy on a community redistributed within it — taken from its wealthy and returned to its poor — and so it tracks that circuit. It is a tax, not the whole of charity.

A minority reading did exist, and it is worth knowing it was raised and rejected rather than never considered. Al-Qurtubi records it: **al-Mahdawi said: Muslims are permitted to give their idolatrous relatives from the obligatory charity, on the basis of this verse. Ibn 'Atiyya said: this is rejected by consensus** [4].

**Voluntary charity is a different matter entirely, and the texts are warm about it.** Ibn 'Abd al-Barr: **as for voluntary charity, it is permissible to the people of unbelief, whether relatives or others; I know of no disagreement about that** [5]. And on relatives specifically, he reports the scholars' view **concerning voluntary charity, that it is permissible from a Muslim to an idolater, whether a relative or otherwise — and the relative is more deserving than anyone else, and the good deed in it is fuller and better** [6]. Al-Kasani gives the reason: **as for voluntary charity, it is permissible to pay it to these, and paying it to them is better, because in it are two rewards — the reward of charity and the reward of kinship** [7].

So: a non-Muslim mother, a neighbour, a colleague, a famine appeal, a disaster fund, a homeless person on your street — give, and give well. None of that is zakat, and none of it needs to be.

**The disputed middle.** Between the two lies a set of payments that are obligatory but not zakat proper, and here the schools genuinely differ. Al-Hindiyya: **they differed over zakat al-fitr, vows, and expiations. Abu Hanifa and Muhammad, may God have mercy on them, said it is permissible — except that the poor among the Muslims are more beloved to us** [8]. So if you are paying an expiation or feeding people for a broken oath, the Hanafi position lets you give to a non-Muslim in need. Note the Hanafis' own preference attached to the permission.

One boundary the texts do draw firmly: a hostile combatant. Al-Hindiyya again: **as for the harbi granted safe conduct, it is not permissible to pay zakat or obligatory charity to him, by consensus — and it is permissible to direct voluntary charity to him** [9].

**If you have already given zakat to someone and then discovered they were not Muslim,** do not panic. Al-Jassas discusses precisely this — a person who paid in good faith and only afterwards learned the truth: **the one who pays zakat to a rich man, or to his own son, or to a dhimmi, once he knows, has moved from reasoned judgement to certainty** [10]. The Hanafis argue by analogy with someone who prayed towards a mistaken qibla after honest effort: the act stands. Others require it to be repeated. Either way this is a question about what to do next, not a sin to carry.

**Practically:** ask the charity you give through whether your zakat is ring-fenced for eligible recipients, since many large relief agencies run zakat-eligible and general funds separately for exactly this reason. Then give your sadaqa wherever the need is greatest, without the question arising at all.

حقه كالذي يأكل ولا يشبع ويكون شهيدا عليه يوم القيامة باب الزكاة على الزوج والأيتام في الحجر قاله أبو سعيد عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم حدثنا عمر بن حفص حدثنا أبي حدثنا الأعمش قال حدثني شقيق عن عمرو ابن الحرث عن زينب امرأة عبد الله رضي الله عنهما قال فذكرته لإبراهيم فحدثني إبراهيم عن أبي عبيدة عن عمرو بن الحرث عن زينب امرأة عبد الله بمثله سواء قالت كنت في المسجد فرأيت النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم فقال تصدقن ولو من حليكن وكانت زينب تنفق على عبد الله وأيتام في حجرها فقالت لعبد الله سل رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم أيجزئ عني أن أنفق عليك وعلى أيتامي في حجري من الصدقة فقال سلي أنت رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم فانطلقت إلى النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم فوجدت امرأة من الأنصار على الباب حاجتها مثل حاجتي فمر علينا بلال فقلنا سل النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم أيجزئ عني أن أنفق على زوجي وأيتام لي في حجري وقلنا لا تخبر بنا فدخل فسأله فقال من هما قال زينب قال أي الزيانب قال امرأة عبد الله قال نعم ولها أجران أجر القرابة وأجر الصدقة حدثنا عثمان بن أبي شيبة حدثنا عبدة عن هشام عن أبيه عن زينب ابنة أم سلمة قالت قلت يا رسول الله إلي أجر أن أنفق على بني أبي سلمة إنما هم بني فقال أنفقي عليهم فلك أجر ما أنفقت عليهم باب قول الله تعالى وفي الرقاب والغارمين وفي سبيل الله ويذكر عن ابن عباس رضي الله عنهما يعتق من زكاة ماله ويعطي في الحج وقال الحسن إن اشترى أباه من الزكاة جاز ويعطي في المجاهدين والذي لم يحج ثم تلا إنما الصدقات للفقراء الآية   في أيها أعطيت أجزأت وقال صلى الله عليه وسلم إن خالدا احتبس أدراعه في سبيل الله ويذكر عن أبي لاس حملنا النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم على إبل الصدقة للحج حدثنا أبو اليمان أخبرنا شعيب قال حدثنا أبو الزناد عن الأعرج عن

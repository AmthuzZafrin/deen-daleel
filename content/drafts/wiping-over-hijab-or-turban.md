# Can I wipe over my headscarf in wudu?

## Answer

**You do not have to take it off — but the two schools of thought here differ on
what you must do instead, and both options are practical.**

The verse requires wiping the head: **and lightly rub your heads** [1]. The
question is whether wiping the covering counts.

**Position one: wipe over it, and that is enough.** Ibn Qudama lists a long
roll of Companions and early authorities — **Ibn al-Mundhir said: among those who
wiped over the turban were Abu Bakr al-Siddiq, and Umar said it, and Anas, and Abu
Umama** [2] — and their evidence is direct. Bilal reported: **the Prophet wiped
over the khuffs and the khimar** [3] — and *khimar* is the ordinary word for a
woman's headcovering. Salman even corrected a man mid-wudu: **wipe over your
khuffs and over your khimar and over your forelock, for I saw the Messenger of
Allah wipe over the khuffs and the khimar.** [4] Umar's line on it is startling
in its force: **whoever is not purified by wiping over the turban — may Allah not
purify him.** [5] This is the Hanbali position, with al-Awza'i, Ahmad and Ishaq.

**Position two: it does not substitute — but you may reach underneath.**
Al-Jassas: **they differed over wiping on the turban; our colleagues, Malik,
al-Hasan ibn Salih and al-Shafi'i said wiping over the turban is not permitted,
nor over the khimar.** [6] So Hanafi, Maliki and Shafi'i all take this side. Their
reasoning is that the verse names the head, and that unlike footwear there is no
real hardship: **and because no hardship attaches to removing it, wiping over it
is not permitted — like the sleeves.** [7] Al-Tirmidhi records the compromise
they accept: **others among the People of Knowledge, from the Companions and the
Successors, said: one does not wipe over the turban unless he wipes his head
along with the turban** [8].

**The report that makes this easy in practice.** Anas: **I saw the Messenger of
Allah make wudu while wearing a Qatari turban — so he put his hand under the
turban and wiped the front of his head, and did not undo the turban.** [9]

That is the whole solution for a hijab. You do not unpin anything. You slide a
wet hand up under the front edge and wipe the hairline, and if you like you
continue over the scarf. Doing both satisfies every position at once, takes three
seconds, and disturbs nothing.

**Men and turbans**: the same rulings, the same options. Ibn Qudama notes
explicitly that this whole chapter runs the same for both: **that is, in wiping
over the khuffs and all its rulings and conditions — because of the generality of
the report, and because it is a wiping standing in place of washing, so men and
women are equal in it, as in tayammum.** [10]

**A caveat on the lenient position.** The Companions who wiped over the turban
were wiping over something wound on, in a society where undoing it was a genuine
nuisance. The parallel to a pinned hijab is good. The parallel to a hat you can
lift off with one finger is not, and nobody in the chapter is arguing for that.

**Practical summary**

- **Safest and easiest**: hand under the front edge, wipe the hairline. Done.
- **If you cannot reach underneath** — a tightly wrapped or pinned style in a
  public place — wiping over the scarf itself has the authority of Abu Bakr,
  Umar, Anas, al-Awza'i, Ahmad and Ishaq behind it. That is not a stretch.
- **You do not need to remove it, on any position.**

**And note this is about wudu only.** Ghusl is a different matter — a full bath
requires water to reach the hair itself, and there is a separate answer here on
that.

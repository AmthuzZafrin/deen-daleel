# I feel like I'm pretending — is my Islam even real?

## Answer

Real. And the feeling you are describing was brought to the Prophet by his own
Companions.

Sahih Muslim has a chapter for it, on the whisperings that arise within faith and
what a person should say when they find them. In it, a group of Companions came
and said: **we find within ourselves something any one of us would find too
enormous to say out loud.** [1] He asked whether they had really found that. They
said yes. His answer was: **that is sheer faith.** [2] Ibn Mas'ud's narration of
the same puts it: he was asked about the whispering, and said, **that is pure
faith.** [3]

Not *despite* faith. **That is** faith — because the thought only lands as
horrifying on someone who already holds the thing it threatens. A person who did
not care would not be distressed by it, and would not be asking your question.

The same chapter gives the instruction, and notice that it is not "resolve the
doubt." **Whoever finds any of that, let him say: I believe in God.** [4] And
where the thoughts run to the end of the line, **let him seek refuge in God and
stop.** [5] You are not required to win the argument. You are advised to decline
it.

**Now the structural half of your question — whether an Islam that feels thin is
a real one.** The tradition has a precise answer, and it is more useful than
reassurance. Al-Nawawi reports the settled view: **the assent itself does not
increase or decrease; faith in the legal sense increases and decreases with the
increase and decrease of its fruits, which are the deeds.** [6]

Two different things, and you are reporting on the second. The assent — that
there is no god but God and that Muhammad is His messenger — either stands in you
or does not, and *feeling* is not the instrument that measures it. What rises and
falls with your mood, your week, and how much of it you have managed is the
lived layer. That one is supposed to move.

He records Ibn Battal's formulation too, and it uses a word worth facing: **faith
is word and deed; it increases and decreases** [7] — and **the faith of someone
who has not attained the increase is deficient.** [8] "Deficient" is a real
category in this tradition. It is also not "absent". It means there is room, and
that everyone is somewhere on the scale rather than in or out of a box.

Someone who converted six months ago and feels like they are acting the part is
describing a low reading on a scale — not a failed entry.

**One thing worth separating out.** If the intrusive thoughts are constant,
distressing, and follow you outside religion — checking, repeating, never
settling — that is a pattern with a medical name as well as a religious one, and
this collection has an answer about seeking help for it. Treating it as a faith
problem alone can make it worse.

You are not pretending. You are new.

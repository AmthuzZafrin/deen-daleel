# I have to take medication — can I still fast?

> **This question is marked `expect: decline`.** The answer must explain
> why it needs a person — a mufti, a doctor, a lawyer — and must not give
> a ruling. Cite only to show what the question touches on.

> Reviewer note: Turns on the medication, the condition and medical advice. Explain what it turns on; route to a doctor as well as a scholar. Do not rule.


## Answer

**A swallowed tablet breaks the fast — it goes to the stomach. But that is not the
real question. The real question is whether you should be fasting at all, and the
sources are more accommodating than most people assume.**

**Swallowing medicine does break the fast.**

Ibn Nujaym: **as for pouring medicine into the mouth, it invalidates his fast, because
there reached the interior of the body something that sustains the body, so it is
eating in meaning — but no expiation is required of him, because of the absence of
actual eating** [1].

Note the second half. Even deliberate medicine is a make-up day only, never the
sixty-day expiation. Al-Kasani gives the same rule for the other natural routes:
**what reaches the body cavity or the brain through the original openings — such as
the nose, the ear, and the rectum — by his taking a nasal drop, or an enema, or
dropping something in his ear, so that it reaches the cavity or the brain: his fast
is invalidated** [2].

So there is no way to take an oral tablet, a syrup, or a suppository during a fast
day and keep the fast intact.

**Which is why the question is about the fast, not the tablet.**

**Option one — reschedule.** For most medication taken once or twice daily this
solves everything: suhoor and after iftar. Ask your doctor or pharmacist whether your
dose can be shifted. Many can. Some cannot, and some must be taken with food at
intervals that make a long fast impossible.

**Option two — a route that does not break the fast.** An injection, in the majority
contemporary view, does not — see that answer. Neither does an inhaler on the view
many scholars hold. If an equivalent exists in a form that avoids the stomach, that
may be available.

**Option three — do not fast, and this is a real option.**

Ibn Qudama: **the people of knowledge are agreed on the permissibility of breaking
the fast for the sick person in general** [3]. He defines the threshold: **the illness
that permits breaking the fast is the severe one that is increased by fasting, or
where a slowing of recovery is feared** [4].

Needing medication you cannot take is exactly a case where fasting worsens the
condition.

And fasting anyway is not the pious choice. Ibn Qudama: **if the sick person bears it
and fasts despite this, he has done something disliked — because of the harm to
himself it involves, and his abandoning God's lightening and the acceptance of His
dispensation** [5]. *Abandoning God's lightening* — the concession is part of the law,
not a failure to keep it.

**What you owe depends on whether the condition is temporary.**

**Temporary** — a course of antibiotics, a chest infection, a recovery period. You
break those days and make them up later, when you are well. See the answer on making
up fasts.

**Permanent** — a lifelong condition with no prospect of ever being able to fast
those days. Ibn Qudama: **the sick person whose recovery is not hoped for breaks the
fast and feeds a poor person for each day, because he is in the meaning of the
elderly** [6]. That is *fidya*, and it replaces the fast rather than deferring it.

The dividing line is stated plainly: **recourse is had to the fidya only upon
despairing of the make-up** [7]. So a condition that lets you fast short winter days
means you owe days, not fidya. A condition that never will means fidya.

**In practice:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Dose can move to suhoor / after iftar | Fast normally |
| Needed during the day, condition temporary | Don't fast. Make the days up |
| Needed during the day, condition permanent | Don't fast. Pay fidya |
| Emergency medication (inhaler, angina spray, insulin correction) | Take it immediately. Sort the fiqh out afterwards |

**Do not stop or skip prescribed medication to complete a fast.** That is the one
thing the sources rule out, and they rule it out in the jurists' own words. Talk to
your doctor about the schedule, and to a scholar about which category you are in.

# Can we be intimate after the bleeding stops but before ghusl?

## Answer

**Not on the majority view — the ghusl comes first. Abu Hanifa allows it in one
specific case.**

**The majority position.**

Ibn Qudama states it and reports how strong the agreement is: **intercourse with the
menstruating woman before the bath is forbidden, even if her blood has stopped, in
the view of most of the people of knowledge** [1]. He quotes Ibn al-Mundhir: **this
is like consensus from them** [2] — and al-Marrudhi saying he knew of no
disagreement.

**The argument from the verse.**

The verse gives two conditions, and that is the whole case. Ibn Qudama: **so He made
two conditions for the permissibility of intercourse: the stopping of the blood, and
the bathing — so it is not permitted except by both of them** [3].

The Qur'an says *do not approach them until they become pure; and when they have
purified themselves, come to them.* Two different verbs, two different states. Ibn
Taymiyya makes the same reading and cites the early authority for it: **Mujahid said
"until they become pure" means the blood stops; "when they have purified themselves"
means they bathe with water** [4].

Ibn Taymiyya's own answer to the question, which is the question you are asking:
**as for the menstruating woman, when her blood has stopped, her husband does not
have intercourse with her until** [5] **she bathes — if she is able to bathe; and if
not she performs tayammum, as is the position of the majority of scholars such as
Malik, Ahmad and al-Shafi'i** [6].

Note the second clause: if bathing is genuinely not possible, tayammum serves.

**The Hanafi exception.**

Abu Hanifa distinguished by how long the bleeding lasted. Ibn Qudama reports it:
**Abu Hanifa said: if the blood stops at the maximum of menstruation, intercourse
with her is permitted; and if it stops at less than that, it is not permitted until
she bathes, or performs tayammum, or the time of a prayer passes over her** [7].

The reasoning is that when the bleeding runs its full maximum length, there is no
possibility of more blood coming, so the menstrual state is definitively over. His
comparison is to janaba: needing a ghusl does not by itself forbid intercourse.

**So, practically.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Bleeding stopped, no ghusl yet | Majority: wait. Hanafi: wait, unless it ran the full maximum |
| Ghusl done | Permitted |
| Ghusl genuinely impossible | Tayammum, then permitted |
| A prayer time has passed | Permitted on the Hanafi view |

**In ordinary practice this is a non-issue** — the ghusl is a shower, and once your
period has ended you want to take one anyway. The rule only bites in the gap between
noticing it has stopped and getting to the bathroom, and the answer for that gap is
simply: do the ghusl.

**Everything short of intercourse** stays permitted throughout, both before the
bleeding stops and after — see the separate answer on intimacy during your period.

وروى حماد بن زيد عن يحيى بن سعيد عن   القعقاع بن حكيم عن سعيد بن المسيب في المستحاضة إذا أقبلت الحيضة تركت الصلاة وإذا أدبرت اغتسلت وصلت وقد روي عن سعيد بن المسيب في المستحاضة تجلس أيام أقرائها ورواه حماد بن سلمة عن يحيى بن سعيد عنه وروى يونس عن الحسن قال الحائض إذا مد بها الدم تمسك بعد حيضتها يوما أو يومين وهي مستحاضة وقال التيمي عن قتادة إذا زادت على أيام حيضتها خمسة أيام فلتصل قال التيمي فجعلت أنقص حتى إذا بلغت يومين قال إذا كان يومين فهو من حيضها وسئل ابن سيرين فقال النساء أعلم بذلك (1) قال أبو عمر فهذه أقاويل فقهاء التابعين في هذا الباب وأما أقاويل من بعدهم من أئمة الفتوى بالأمصار فقال مالك في المرأة إذا ابتدأها حيضها فاستمر بها الدم أو كانت ممن قد حاضت فاستمر الدم بها قال في المبتدأة تقعد ما تقعد نحوها من النساء من أسنانها وأترابها ولداتها ثم هي مستحاضة بعد ذلك رواه علي بن زياد عن مالك وقال ابن القاسم ما رأت المرأة بعد بلوغها من الدم فهو حيض تترك له الصلاة فإن تمادى بها قعدت عن الصلاة خمسة عشر يوما ثم اغتسلت وكانت مستحاضة تصلي وتصوم وتوطأ إلا أن ترى دما لا تشك أنه

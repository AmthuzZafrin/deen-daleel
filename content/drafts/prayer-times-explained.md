# How do I know when each prayer time starts and ends?

## Answer

**Each prayer has a window with a start and an end, and the Prophet gave both
ends for all five in a single hadith.** Abu Hurayra reported him saying: **the
prayer has a beginning and an end — the beginning of the time of the Dhuhr prayer
is when the sun passes the zenith** [1], and its end is when Asr's time enters;
Asr begins then and ends when the sun yellows; and then, continuing: **the
beginning of Maghrib is when the sun sets, and its end is when the horizon
disappears; the beginning of the later Isha is when the horizon disappears, and
its end is when the night reaches its middle; and the beginning of Fajr is when
the dawn breaks, and its end is when the sun rises.** [2]

**In table form**

| | starts | ends |
|---|---|---|
| **Fajr** | true dawn — first light spreading along the horizon | sunrise |
| **Dhuhr** | the sun passes its highest point | Asr begins |
| **Asr** | when a thing's shadow equals its length (Shafi'i, Maliki, Hanbali) or twice it (Hanafi) | sunset — though praying past the yellowing is disliked |
| **Maghrib** | sunset | the red twilight fades |
| **Isha** | the twilight fades | midnight — the midpoint between sunset and dawn |

**Two things this table hides, and both matter.**

*Asr's start is a real school difference.* The Hanafis begin it later than the
other three, which is why prayer apps ask you to choose a "calculation method"
and why two apps can disagree by 45 minutes. Either is a valid position. Set your
app to your school if you follow one, and to the majority setting if you do not.

*Isha's end at midnight is not the last moment you may pray.* It is the end of the
preferred time. Praying Isha after it is still valid until dawn — but do not treat
that as a licence to habitually leave it.

**When is best within the window?**

The general rule is early. Ibn Qudama on Asr specifically: **as for Asr, hastening
it is recommended in every case** [3]. But he records the exceptions the schools
built around real weather:

**on an overcast day one delays Dhuhr until there is no doubt that its time has
come, and hastens Asr** [4] — because on a cloudy day you cannot see the sun, so
you wait until you are certain the time has entered rather than pray on a guess.

That principle generalises: **do not pray before you are sure the time has come.**
Praying early is invalid; praying a little late is merely less good.

**Practically**

- **Get a prayer time app** and set your location and school. This is not a
  concession — the fuqaha calculated times from shadows precisely because they
  needed accuracy, and a computed table is a better shadow-stick.
- **Learn the anchors, not the clock times.** Fajr before sunrise, Dhuhr just
  after midday, Asr mid-afternoon, Maghrib immediately at sunset, Isha once it is
  properly dark. The clock times shift daily; the anchors do not.
- **Maghrib has the shortest window** — often under 90 minutes. It is the one
  most easily missed.
- **In high latitudes**, summer Isha and Fajr can compress or vanish. That is a
  genuinely contested modern question, and one this collection cannot settle for
  you; ask locally, because the answer people follow varies by city.

**If you miss a window**, pray it as soon as you remember. There are separate
answers here on missed prayers, on sleeping through Fajr, and on combining prayers
when your work or travel makes the windows impossible.

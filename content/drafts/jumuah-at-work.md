# My job won't let me leave for Jumu'ah — what do I do?

## Answer

**Ask for the time first — most people who assume they can't get it have never
asked. If it is genuinely impossible, the excuse framework in the fiqh is wide
enough to cover you, and you pray Zuhr. But be honest with yourself about which
of the two you are in.**

**Start from the default.**

Jumu'ah is binding. Al-Nawawi's gloss on Ibn Abbas's rainy-day report is exact
about the word used: **his saying "Jumu'ah is 'azma" — meaning obligatory,
absolutely required** [1]. And on who carries it: **it is binding on him, but not
on women, children, slaves, and travellers** [2]. A job does not appear on that
list, and a salaried employee is not one of the categories the jurists exempted.

So the starting position is that you go, and the question is what counts as being
genuinely unable to.

**The excuse framework is not a closed list.**

This is the part that answers the question. Al-Nawawi states the principle
directly: **the chapter of excuses for leaving Jumu'ah and the congregation is not
restricted — rather, everything that brings severe hardship upon a person is an
excuse** [3].

That sentence is doing real work. The named excuses in the books are illness,
fear, rain, and mud — but al-Nawawi is explicit that the category is defined by
hardship, not by the list. Ibn Qudama's discussion runs the same way: he treats
illness as agreed — **the sick person is excused in leaving them, in the view of
the generality of the people of learning** [4] — and cites Ibn Abbas's hadith:
**whoever hears the call and nothing prevents him from following it, they said:
and what is an excuse, Messenger of God? He said: fear or illness** [5]. He then
extends the same category to a much smaller matter, someone needing to relieve
himself: **so these two are among the excuses by which one is excused in leaving
the congregation and the Jumu'ah** [6].

And on the rain report, al-Nawawi notes the direction of travel: **this hadith is
evidence for the lightening of the matter of congregation in rain and the like of
it among the excuses** [7].

**What the sources do not say.**

None of these passages address salaried employment. There was no such thing in
the form we have it. The jurists reasoned about illness, fear, weather, and
people whose time belonged to someone else — and that last category was slavery,
which is not what a job is. So there is no ruling here that says "your shift
counts" or "your shift does not count." What there is, is a principle — severe
hardship excuses — and the application of it to your situation is a judgement,
not a citation.

That judgement is yours to make honestly, and worth putting to a scholar who
knows your circumstances if it is a recurring problem rather than a one-off.

**What to actually do.**

1. **Ask.** In most countries a request for forty-five minutes on a Friday,
   offered against a shorter lunch or made-up time, is granted. Many people never
   ask because they assume the answer. Where you have a legal right to religious
   accommodation, use it.
2. **Look for the nearer mosque.** Jumu'ah in an office room, a community centre,
   or a prayer space at another employer nearby is still Jumu'ah. The constraint
   is usually travel time, not the prayer.
3. **If it is genuinely impossible**, pray Zuhr, on your own, at work. You are not
   sinning and you owe no penance. See the answer on missing Jumu'ah.
4. **Don't let it settle into a default.** The hadith about missing three Jumu'ahs
   condemns *tahawun* — indifference. Someone who fought for the time and lost is
   not in that hadith. Someone who stopped asking three years ago should ask again.

# Do I need to take a bath when I convert?

## Answer

Take the bath. But do not wait for it.

**Start with the part that is not disputed**, because it is the part people get
wrong. Al-Nawawi is emphatic: someone who wants to enter Islam should **hurry to
it and not delay it for the sake of bathing** — delaying for that is severely
forbidden. He extends it to anyone who gets asked for advice: if a person
consults you, you may not tell them to wait until they have washed. He calls
al-Ghazali's suggestion that the bath should come first a plain error. [1] If
you said the shahada in your kitchen without washing, you did it right.

**The schools then differ on whether the bath is owed at all.** The corpus gives
three positions.

Ibn Qudama reports the Hanbali and Maliki view: **when a non-Muslim enters Islam
the bath is obligatory on him** — whether he was never Muslim or is returning,
whether or not anything happened during his disbelief that would ordinarily
require it. [2] His evidence is Qays b. 'Asim: *I came to the Prophet wanting
Islam, and he ordered me to bathe with water and lote-leaf.* [3] And a second
report, that Sa'd b. Mu'adh and Usayd b. Hudayr asked Mus'ab b. 'Umayr what was
done on entering: **we bathe, and we bear witness to the truth**. [4]

Al-Khallal — and this is al-Shafi'i's position — held it **recommended, not
obligatory**, [5] unless the person was in a state requiring ghusl anyway. The
Shafi'i argument, as al-Nawawi puts it, is that entering Islam is the leaving of
a wrong, and leaving a wrong does not by itself require a bath.

Abu Hanifa held it **not obligatory in any case**, and his reasoning is worth
hearing: **vast numbers entered Islam, and had every one of them been ordered to
bathe, that would have been transmitted en masse.** [6] He adds that when Mu'adh
was sent to Yemen he was told to call people to the testimony and then to the
alms, with no mention of a bath.

**What to do, practically.** Bathe as you would for ghusl. The lote-leaf in the
hadith is not required — al-Nawawi notes it is agreed to be non-obligatory, which
is itself part of the argument that the whole instruction was a recommendation.
[7] There is also a recommendation to shave the head on entering Islam; al-Nawawi
records it, and records that the chain behind it is not strong. [8]

**Which of the three applies to you** depends on the school you follow, and if
you do not follow one yet, that is a normal place to be. Nothing in the dispute
touches whether you are Muslim. Every position above assumes you already are.

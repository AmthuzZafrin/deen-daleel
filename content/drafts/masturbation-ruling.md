# Is masturbation haram?

## Answer

**Short answer: the majority of scholars hold it forbidden, a minority hold it
disliked rather than forbidden, and several authorities record a concession where
someone would otherwise fall into zina.** That range is in the sources
themselves, so anyone who tells you it is a simple settled question is
overstating one side of it.

**The majority position.** Ibn Taymiyya states it and names the dissent in the
same breath: **"أما الاستمناء باليد فهو حرام عند جمهور   العلماء وهو أصح القولين
في مذهب أحمد"** — *masturbation by hand is forbidden according to the majority of
scholars, and it is the sounder of the two positions in Ahmad's school* [1];
**"وفى القول الآخر هو مكروه غير محرم وأكثرهم لا يبيحونه لخوف العنت ولا
غيره"** — *on the other position it is disliked rather than forbidden, and most
of them do not permit it, neither out of fear of hardship nor otherwise* [2].

**The daleel the prohibition rests on.** Ibn 'Abidin reports al-Zayla'i arguing
from the verse describing the believers as *those who guard their private
parts*: **"استدل على عدم حله بالكف بقوله تعالى: * (والذين هم لفروجهم حافظون) *
الآية. وقال: فلم يبح الاستمتاع إلا بهما"** — *he argued that it is not lawful by
hand from the verse, saying: He permitted gratification only with the two* — that
is, within marriage [3]. The verse's own structure, on this reading, permits one
outlet and excludes the rest.

**The concession, and its limits.** Ibn Taymiyya records it:
**"ونقل عن طائفة من الصحابة والتابعين أنهم رخصوا فيه للضرورة مثل أن يخشى الزنى
فلا يعصم منه الا به"** — *it is reported from a group of the Companions and
Successors that they permitted it under necessity, such as one who fears zina and
can be preserved from it only by this* [4]. And he draws the line at once:
**"وأما بدون الضرورة فما علمت أحدا رخص فيه"** — *without necessity, I know of no
one who permitted it* [5].

The Hanafi discussion runs the same way. Ibn 'Abidin, under a heading given to
the question — **"مطلب في حكم الاستمناء بالكف"** [6] — cites *al-Fath*:
**"وعبارة الفتح: فإن غلبته الشهوة ففعل إرادة تسكينها به فالرجاء أن لا
يعاقب"** — *if desire overpowers him and he does it intending to quiet it, the
hope is that he will not be punished* [7]. He cites Abu al-Layth with the same
distinction sharpened: **"إن أراد بذلك تسكين الشهوة المفرطة الشاغلة للقلب وكان
عزبا لا زوجة له ولا أمة، أو كان إلا أنه لا يقدر على الوصول إليها لعذر قال أبو
الليث: أرجو أن لا وبال عليه، وأما إذا فعله لاستحلاب الشهوة فهو آثم"** — *if he
intends to quiet an overwhelming desire that is occupying his heart, and he is
unmarried, or married but unable to reach his wife for some reason, Abu al-Layth
said: I hope there is no blame on him; but if he does it to arouse desire, he
sins* [8]. And he records the Shafi'i split: **"زاد في معراج الدراية وعن أحمد
والشافعي في القديم الترخص فيه، وفي الجديد يحرم"** — *from Ahmad, and from
al-Shafi'i in his older position, a concession; in his later position, it is
forbidden* [9].

Notice what the concession actually turns on in these texts. It is not whether
it is difficult; it is the direction of travel — quieting a desire that is
already overwhelming, versus deliberately working desire up. Every author quoted
here draws that line, and none of them treats the concession as a general
permission [5][8].

**What the sources prescribe instead.** The hadith in Sahih Muslim is direct:
**"يا معشر الشباب، من استطاع منكم الباءة فليتزوج، فإنه أغض للبصر، وأحصن للفرج،
ومن لم يستطع فعليه بالصوم، فإنه له وجاء"** — *young people, whoever among you is
able to marry, let him marry, for it restrains the gaze and guards the private
parts; and whoever is not able, let him fast, for it is a shield for him* [10].
Muslim titles the chapter accordingly: marriage is recommended for one who
desires it and can afford it, and one who cannot afford it occupies himself with
fasting [11]. Ibn al-'Arabi calls this the soundest reading of the verse telling
those who cannot marry to keep chaste: **"وقال بعض علمائنا إنه يستعف
بالصوم"** — *some of our scholars said he keeps chaste by fasting* [12].

So the answer the sources give is not only a prohibition; it is a prescription,
and the prescription has two parts in order: marry if you can, fast if you
cannot [10]. If marriage is genuinely out of reach — and for many people now it
is, for reasons of money, visas, or simply not having met anyone — that is worth
saying plainly rather than treating the first half of the hadith as advice that
solves the problem.

**On the guilt, which is usually the real question.** People asking this are
often not asking for the ruling; they already know roughly what it is. They are
asking because they have tried to stop and have not. Two things from the material
above are worth holding onto. First, the scholars quoted here disagreed with each
other, and several were prepared to hope there was no blame in specific
circumstances [7][8] — this is not a matter on which the tradition speaks with
one crushing voice. Second, none of them treats it as taking someone outside
Islam or as unforgivable. Despair does more damage than the act, and the
practical route out that the sources point to is a positive one — fasting,
occupying yourself, and pursuing marriage — rather than self-recrimination.

If it has become compulsive rather than occasional, that is a different problem
from the one the fiqh is describing, and it responds to the things compulsions
respond to: removing triggers, structure, sleep, exercise, and talking to someone
— a counsellor as readily as an imam.

**On the language above:** "istimna'" is masturbation; "zina" is sex outside
marriage; "darura" is necessity, the ground the concession rests on; "makruh"
means disliked but not forbidden; "wija'" in the hadith is a shield or
protection.

**A caution.** This is a question the schools genuinely differ on, and the
concession is stated in the sources with conditions that only you can assess in
your own case. If you want a ruling rather than a survey — and particularly if
the question is bound up with a compulsion or with distress — take it to a
scholar you can speak to privately.

وجاء في السنن -من غير وجه -أن رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم قال: "تزوجوا، توالدوا، تناسلوا، فإني مباه بكم الأمم يوم القيامة" (2) وفي رواية: "حتى بالسقط".

وعن ابن مسعود: التمسوا الغنى في النكاح، يقول الله تعالى: {إن يكونوا فقراء يغنهم الله من فضله} رواه (4) ابن جرير، وذكر البغوي عن عمر بنحوه.

وعن الليث، عن محمد بن عجلان، عن سعيد المقبري، عن أبي هريرة، رضي الله عنه، قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: "ثلاثة حق على الله عونهم: الناكح يريد العفاف، والمكاتب يريد الأداء، والغازي في سبيل الله". رواه الإمام أحمد، والترمذي، والنسائي، وابن ماجه (5)

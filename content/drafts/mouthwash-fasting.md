# Does mouthwash break my fast?

## Answer

**Rinsing your mouth does not break the fast. What the sources warn against is
*overdoing* it — and that warning comes from the Prophet himself, in the context of
wudu.**

**The hadith.**

Laqit ibn Sabira asked about wudu and was told: perform it thoroughly, run water
between the fingers — **and be thorough in drawing water into the nose, unless you
are fasting** [1]. Al-Tirmidhi grades it: **this is a hasan sahih hadith** [2].

Note what it does and does not say. It does not say *do not rinse*. It says do not
be **thorough** — do not draw the water hard — when fasting. Rinsing itself is part
of wudu and continues as normal.

**What "thorough" means.**

Ibn Qudama defines it precisely: **the meaning of being thorough in drawing water
into the nose is pulling the water with the breath to the furthest part of the nose,
without making it a nasal drop; and that is a recommended sunna in wudu, except when
one is fasting, in which case it is not recommended — we know of no disagreement in
that** [3].

And for the mouth: **being thorough in rinsing is circulating the water in the depths
of the mouth, its furthest parts and its cheeks** [4].

Al-Suyuti states the rule in one line: **being thorough in rinsing the mouth and
drawing water into the nose is a sunna, and it is disliked for the fasting person**
[5].

So the ruling is: rinse gently, don't snort, don't gargle deeply.

**Why.**

Al-Tirmidhi gives the reasoning the jurists drew: **the people of knowledge disliked
the nasal drop for the fasting person, and held that it breaks the fast** [6]. Water
forced up the nose can reach the interior by that route, and that route does break
the fast — see the answer on what breaks the fast. Gentle rinsing does not.

**If water goes down by accident.**

Ibn Qudama's rule carries two conditions, and both must be met for the fast to
break: the substance reaches the interior **if it reaches there by his choice, and
was something he could have guarded against** [7]. Water that slips down while you
are rinsing normally fails both — you did not choose it, and you could not have
prevented it.

That is the same principle that puts saliva and road dust outside the fast.

**Mouthwash specifically.**

Commercial mouthwash is not in these sources. Two features of it matter, and both
follow from the principle above:

- **It is not water, and it has a strong taste.** The books already dislike tasting
  something without need while fasting.
- **Most brands contain alcohol** and are absorbed through the mouth lining to some
  degree.

**In practice:** if you use it, use it at suhoor and after iftar. If you must use it
during the day, don't gargle, don't tilt your head back, spit thoroughly and rinse
with water. If none of it goes down your throat, your fast stands.

**Better options during fasting hours:** a dry brush, a siwak, or a gentle rinse with
plain water. See the answer on toothpaste. Bad breath while fasting is a normal
consequence of fasting, not a failure to manage.

**And if you did gargle without thinking** and nothing was swallowed — your fast is
intact. You did something disliked, not something invalidating.

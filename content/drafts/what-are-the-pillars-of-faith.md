# What are the six articles of faith?

## Answer

**The six are: belief in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last
Day, and the decree — its good and its ill.** They come from the same hadith of
Jibril that gives the five pillars, and the pairing is the point: the five are
what a Muslim *does*, the six are what a Muslim *holds*.

**The wording.** Al-Nawawi quotes it: **"والايمان أن تؤمن بالله وملائكته وكتبه
ورسله واليوم الآخر بالقدر خيره وشره"** — *iman is that you believe in Allah, His
angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and in the decree, its good and
its ill* [1]. The Qur'an lists the
same set in 4:136: **"Whoso disbelieveth in Allah and His angels and His
scriptures and His messengers and the Last Day, he verily hath wandered far
astray"** [2].

**What each involves, briefly.**

- **Allah** — His existence, oneness, and that He alone is to be worshipped.
- **The angels** — al-Marwazi's gloss: **"وأما قوله ( وملائكته ( فإن تؤمن بمن سمى
  الله لك منهم في كتابه وتؤمن بأن لله ملائكة سواهم لا يعرف أسماءهم وعددهم الا
  الذى خلقهم"** — *that you believe in those of them Allah has named to you in His
  Book, and believe that Allah has angels besides them whose names and number
  none knows but the One who created them* [3].
- **The books** — **"وأما قوله ( وكتبه ( فان تؤمن بما سمى الله من كتبه في كتابه
  من التوراة والانجيل والزبور خاصة وتؤمن بأن لله سوى ذلك كتبا أنزلها على
  أنبيائه"** — *that you believe in those He named — the Torah, the Injil and the
  Zabur specifically — and believe that Allah has other books besides, sent down
  to His prophets* [4].
- **The messengers** — the prophets sent to every people, of whom some are named
  and many are not.
- **The Last Day** — resurrection, reckoning, and what follows.
- **Qadar** — that what happens is within Allah's knowledge, will and power,
  including what a person experiences as bad [1].

Notice the shape of the second and third: the sources build an explicit
open-endedness into them. You affirm the named ones and affirm that there are
others you do not know [3][4]. Belief here is not a claim to a complete
inventory.

**How the six relate to the five.** Ibn Taymiyya reads the hadith as setting out
three nested levels — **"تفريق النبى صلى الله عليه وسلم في حديث جبريل"**, *the
Prophet's ﷺ distinguishing in the hadith of Jibril* [5] — and states the ordering:
**"الأعلى هو الإحسان والإحسان يتضمن الإيمان والإيمان يتضمن الإسلام"** — *the
highest is ihsan, and ihsan comprises iman, and iman comprises islam* [6]. The
five pillars are the islam level, the six articles the iman level, and ihsan —
excellence — sits above both.

Al-Nawawi puts the distinction most usefully: **"هذا بيان لأصل الايمان وهو
التصديق الباطن وبيان أصل الاسلام وهو الاستسلام والانقياد"** — *this is a
statement of the root of iman, which is inward assent, and of the root of islam,
which is submission and compliance* [7]. Inward assent, and outward compliance.
He also reports the conclusion drawn from it: **"وأن كل مؤمن مسلم وليس كل مسلم
مؤمنا"** — *every mu'min is a muslim, and not every muslim is a mu'min* [8] —
that is, a person can be doing the outward acts while the inward assent is
incomplete, which is a description rather than an accusation.

**Two things worth saying if you are new.** First, this is a list of what to
believe, not a test you have to pass in one sitting; several of the six —
particularly qadar — take years to sit comfortably with, and asking questions
about them is not a failure of faith. Second, the last item is worded carefully:
believing in the decree "its good and its ill" [1] does not mean calling harm
good. It means that nothing falls outside Allah's knowledge and power, which is a
different claim.

**On the language above:** "iman" is faith or belief; "islam" here means the
outward acts; "ihsan" is excellence, the third thing the hadith names; "qadar" is
the divine decree; "tasdiq" is inward assent; "Injil" and "Zabur" are the
Gospel and the Psalms as the Qur'an names them.

**A note on scope.** What exactly *iman* consists of, and whether it increases
and decreases, was one of the most disputed questions in early Islamic theology —
al-Nawawi and Ibn Taymiyya both discuss it at length in the passages quoted here.
This answer gives the six articles, not that debate. If you want the deeper
discussion, ask someone who can take you through it.

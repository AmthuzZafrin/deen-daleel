# My phone rang mid-prayer — can I silence it?

## Answer

**Yes, silence it — and your prayer is not broken by doing so.** Reaching into a
pocket and pressing a button is a small action, and small actions do not
invalidate the prayer.

**The rule.** Invalidation is tied to *much* action, not to any action. Ibn Nujaym,
explaining why eating and drinking break the prayer: **because each of the two is
much action, and is not among the acts of the prayer, and there is no necessity
for it** [1] — and the principle stated directly, **the invalidation of the prayer
is tied to much action** [2].

The working test the fuqaha use: **would someone watching think you had stopped
praying?** Reaching for a phone and pressing the side button plainly would not.
Walking across the room, digging through a bag, taking a call — that is a different
matter.

**So, in order of preference**

1. **Silence it where it is**, with one hand, without looking. Best.
2. **Take it out of your pocket and press the button.** Still small action; still
   fine.
3. **If it is across the room**, leave it. It will stop. Continuing the prayer with
   a phone ringing is better than crossing the room, and far better than answering.
4. **Do not answer it.** Speaking to a caller is deliberate speech, and that
   invalidates the prayer on every reckoning — there is a separate answer here on
   it.

**A ringtone with music or Qur'an.** Neither changes the ruling. Silence it if you
can; if you cannot, carry on. Your prayer is not invalidated by a sound you did not
choose.

**What about the noise you make?** Nothing. The Hanafi list is precise about which
*sounds from you* matter — **clearing the throat without an excuse, in that he was
not compelled to it and letters came out of it, invalidates the prayer** [3] — and
equally precise that being compelled is excused: **if it is with an excuse, in that
he was compelled to it, it does not invalidate, because guarding against it is not
possible** [4]. A phone's noise is not your speech at all.

**And if you need to alert someone** — the phone is someone else's, or a person
needs to be signalled — say *subhanallah* rather than speaking. That is the
established way to communicate in prayer and it does not break it.

**The obvious prevention.** Put the phone on silent before you start. It takes two
seconds, and it also removes the low-grade listening-out that undercuts your
attention — there is a separate answer here on the wandering mind that makes the
same point about the environment.

**One reassurance.** If it rang, you fumbled, you missed a word of the recitation
and had to go back — none of that invalidates anything. Fumbling is not "much
action" in the sense the books mean, and losing your place in recitation is not on
any list. Finish the prayer. There is a separate answer here on the prostration of
forgetfulness if you think you actually missed something.

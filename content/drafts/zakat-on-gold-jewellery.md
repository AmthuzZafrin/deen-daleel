# Do I pay zakat on jewellery I wear?

## Answer

This is one of the genuine disagreements, and which answer applies to you depends on which school you follow.

In the Hanbali and Shafi'i books, jewellery a woman actually wears is exempt. Ibn Qudama is discussing al-Khiraqi's wording — **and al-Khiraqi's words, "when it is of what she wears or lends out"** [1] — and glosses it: **he means that zakat lapses on it only when it is like that, or set aside for it** [2]. The reason is the general principle behind the whole institution: **it lapses on what is prepared for use, because that turns it away from the direction of growth** [3]. Zakat falls on wealth that grows. A bracelet you wear is not growing.

The Hanafi school takes the other side and holds gold and silver zakatable whether worked into jewellery or not. So if you follow a Hanafi mosque you will be told to include it. Neither position is unusual or marginal; both are the settled view of a major school.

Three limits on the exemption are worth knowing, because they catch more people than the headline rule.

**It has to be jewellery you actually wear.** Ibn Qudama: **as for what is set aside for hire, or for spending when the need arises, zakat is due on it** [4]. Gold bought as a store of value and kept in a safe is an investment wearing a bracelet's shape, and it is zakatable.

**Buying jewellery to dodge zakat does not work.** **Likewise what is made into jewellery in flight from zakat — it does not lapse for him** [5]. The classical authors saw this coming.

**And intention changes it either way.** **If the jewellery was for wearing and the woman then intended trade by it, the zakat year begins over it from the moment she intended** [6]. What decides the ruling is what the item is for, and that can change.

On what counts as jewellery in the first place, Ibn Qudama is broad: **it is permitted for women, of gold, silver and jewels, everything their custom is to wear — such as the bracelet, the anklet, the earring, the ring** [7]. Quantity does not change it either: **little jewellery and much are alike in permissibility and in zakat** [8], against a colleague who wanted to cap it at a thousand mithqals.

Practically: if you follow a school that exempts worn jewellery, exclude the pieces you genuinely wear and include any gold you are holding as savings. If your school includes it, include the lot. If you do not know which school you follow — which is most reverts — ask at your mosque, and know that either answer you are given is defensible.

# What breaks my fast?

## Answer

**Three things, deliberately: eating, drinking, and sexual intercourse. That is
the whole core list. Almost everything people worry about is not on it.**

**The core.**

Al-Kasani derives the list from the definition itself: **as for its essential
element, it is abstaining from food, drink, and sexual intercourse** [1] — because
the Qur'an permits exactly those three during the nights of Ramadan and then
commands abstaining from them by day. What invalidates a thing is the loss of its
essential element, so: **and that is by eating, drinking, and sexual intercourse**
[2].

**What does NOT break it.**

Al-Nasafi's Hanafi summary is the most useful single list in the books, because it
enumerates the cases people actually ask about:

**if the fasting person eats, or drinks, or has intercourse forgetfully; or has a
wet dream; or emits by looking; or applies oil; or is cupped; or applies kohl; or
kisses; or dust or a fly enters his throat while he is mindful of his fast; or he
swallows what is between his teeth; or he vomits and it returns — he has not broken
the fast** [3].

Read through that list: forgetting, a wet dream, oil on the skin, blood being drawn,
kohl in the eye, kissing, dust or an insect swallowed involuntarily, food residue
between the teeth, involuntary vomiting. None of it breaks the fast.

He adds the things that are merely disliked: **tasting something and chewing it
without an excuse, and chewing gum are disliked** [4] — and the things that are not
even that: **not kohl, nor oiling the moustache, nor the tooth-stick, nor kissing
if he is safe** [5].

**What does break it, beyond the core three.**

**Deliberate vomiting**, and swallowing something inedible: **if he returns it, or
makes himself vomit, or swallows a pebble or a piece of iron — he makes up only**
[6]. Note "makes up *only*" — a make-up day, without the heavier expiation.

**Things that reach the interior by another route.** Al-Nasafi: **if he takes an
enema, or a nasal drop, or drops something into his ear, or treats a deep or head
wound with a medicine and it reaches his interior or his brain — he has broken the
fast** [7]. But not everything does: **and if he drops something into his urethra,
no** [8].

That distinction — routes that reach the body cavity versus routes that do not — is
the principle behind most of the medical questions in this section, and the reason
the answers on inhalers, injections and blood tests come out the way they do.

**The two levels of consequence.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Make-up only (*qada*) | Deliberate vomiting, swallowing something inedible, enema and similar |
| Make-up *and* expiation (*kaffara*) | Eating, drinking, or intercourse deliberately |

Al-Nasafi on the heavier case: **whoever has intercourse, or is had intercourse
with, or eats or drinks deliberately — food or medicine — makes up and expiates,
like the expiation of zihar** [9]. That expiation is sixty consecutive days of
fasting, or feeding sixty poor people. See the answer on intercourse during a
fasting day.

**In short:** if you did not eat, drink, or have intercourse on purpose, your fast
is almost certainly intact. The individual questions in this section — toothpaste,
mouthwash, swimming, perfume, inhalers, injections, blood tests, smoking, kissing —
each have their own answer, but the default the sources set is much more permissive
than most people assume.

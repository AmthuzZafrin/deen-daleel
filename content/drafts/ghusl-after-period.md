# How do I do ghusl after my period ends?

## Answer

**The same ghusl as any other, with one addition and one common misconception
cleared up: you do not have to undo your braids.**

**How to do it.**

Ibn Qudama gives the Prophet's own instruction to Asma', who asked about exactly
this: **let one of you take her water and her lote-leaves and purify herself, and
purify herself well; then pour water over her head and rub it vigorously until it
reaches the roots of her hair; then pour water over herself** [1].

That is the whole method. In modern terms:

1. Intend the ghusl.
2. Wash the private parts and remove any residue.
3. Perform wudu.
4. Pour water over the head, working it into the scalp with the fingers.
5. Pour water over the whole body, right side then left, making sure it reaches
   everywhere.

The scalp is the part people miss. Ibn Qudama is explicit: **washing the skin of
the head is obligatory, whether the hair is thick or fine** [2]. Water must reach
the scalp itself, not just run over the hair.

**You do not have to undo braids.**

This is the most common worry and it has a direct answer. Umm Salama asked the
Prophet: **I am a woman who binds the braids of my head tightly — should I undo it
for menstruation and for major impurity?** His reply: **No. It is enough for you
to pour three handfuls over your head, then pour water over yourself, and you are
purified** [3].

Ibn Qudama notes that his school's textbook says the hair should be undone for the
menstrual ghusl, and then sets that aside: **some of our companions said this is
recommended, not obligatory — and it is the position of most of the jurists, and
it is the correct one** [4]. He adds that the wording of Umm Salama's hadith is
**explicit in negating obligation**.

So braids, locs, extensions, and tightly bound hair stay as they are. Work water
into the scalp thoroughly. That is what is required.

**The one thing specific to the menstrual ghusl.**

Ibn Qudama: **the ghusl for menstruation is like the ghusl for major impurity,
except regarding undoing the hair** [5] — and one recommended addition: **she takes
a piece of cloth scented with musk and follows with it the path of the blood** [6].
The purpose he gives is to remove the smell. If musk is not available, any
perfume; and this is recommended, not required. Contemporary hygiene largely
achieves the same thing.

**Timing.**

Do the ghusl once you are certain the bleeding has stopped — see the separate
answer on how to know. Then resume praying with the next prayer, and see the
answer on whether you owe the prayer whose time was running out.

There is no waiting period, no special hour, and no requirement to do it
immediately at the moment of stopping — though you should not delay past the next
prayer without reason.

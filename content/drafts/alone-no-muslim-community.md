# I don't know any Muslims — how do I practise alone?

## Answer

You practise alone, and it counts fully. Two things are true at once here and it
is worth holding both.

**First: your situation was named, and named well.** Sahih Muslim has a chapter
called *Islam began as a stranger*, and in it: **Islam began as a stranger, and
it will return as it began, a stranger — so blessed are the strangers.** [1]

Al-Tirmidhi preserves a wording that says who those strangers are: **blessed are
the strangers, who set right what people corrupt of my sunna after me.** [2] He
grades the parallel report from Ibn Mas'ud as **sound and good, uncommon from
Ibn Mas'ud's route** [3] — his assessment, not this application's, and recorded
so you can weigh it.

Being the only one is not evidence of having gone wrong. It is a condition the
tradition expected and blessed.

**Second, and legally: praying alone is a complete prayer.** Al-Nawawi states it
from these very hadiths: **our companions and the majority argued that
congregation is not a condition for the validity of the prayer** [4] — and
**the chosen position is that it is a communal obligation.** [5] A communal
obligation is discharged by the community. Where there is no community within
reach, it does not sit on you as a debt.

Nothing you pray on your own kitchen floor is deficient in validity. It is a
prayer, entire.

**And now the part that would be dishonest to leave out.** Do not turn the
solitude into a preference. Ibn Taymiyya is blunt about people who do: **whoever
among the devout supposes that his prayer alone is better — whether in seclusion
or otherwise — is mistaken and astray.** [6] The merit is real and measurable:
**the prayer of a man in congregation surpasses his prayer alone by twenty-five
degrees** [7] — twenty-seven in another narration, and al-Nawawi discusses how
the two numbers fit together.

So: what you have is valid, and it is also less than what is available. That is
not a contradiction. It is the accurate description of being a Muslim without
Muslims nearby, and it should make you look for others without making you doubt
what you are doing meanwhile.

**Practically, from what the above implies rather than from any text**: keep the
prayer times, because they are the structure that does not need anyone else.
Where a Jumu'ah is reachable even monthly, the travel is worth it. And the
strangeness in the hadith is a description of a condition, not an instruction to
stay in it — the people it blesses are the ones still repairing something, which
takes contact with others eventually.

You are not practising a lesser version of this. You are practising it in harder
conditions, which is a different thing.

Al-Bahr al-Ra'iq 1:606

Arabic:

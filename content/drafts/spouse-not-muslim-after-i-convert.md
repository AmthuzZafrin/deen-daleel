# I converted but my husband or wife didn't — what now?

## Answer

**This is one of the questions where the honest answer ends with a person, not a
page.** Your case turns on which of you converted, on what your spouse believes,
and on where you live — and getting it wrong has consequences a website cannot
carry. What follows is the framework the sources set out, so that you can walk
into that conversation knowing what will be asked. It is not a ruling on your
marriage.

**Nothing here requires you to do anything today.**

**The one case that needs no action at all.** If you are a man and your wife is
Jewish or Christian, the marriage simply continues. Al-Fatawa al-Hindiyya:
**if the husband of a woman of the People of the Book becomes Muslim, their
marriage remains.** [1] There is nothing to resolve.

**Every other case runs through an offer.** The same text: **if one of the two
spouses becomes Muslim, Islam is offered to the other; if they accept, [the
marriage stands], and otherwise they are separated.** [2]

Read how the law behaves at that moment, because it is not what people expect.
**If the spouse says nothing, the judge offers again, and again, up to three
times, out of caution.** [3] The instinct built into the procedure is to keep
trying, not to conclude.

**And the timeframe in the recorded cases is longer than the summaries suggest.**
Malik's Muwatta preserves what happened to the women who became Muslim while
their husbands did not. Bint al-Walid became Muslim on the day of the Conquest;
her husband Safwan b. Umayya fled. He was given safe conduct, fought at Hunayn
and Ta'if while still not a Muslim — **and the Prophet did not separate him from
his wife until Safwan became Muslim, and his wife settled with him on that same
marriage.** [4] Ibn Shihab adds the interval: **about a month between his Islam
and hers.** [5]

Umm Hakim's husband 'Ikrima fled as far as Yemen. **She travelled after him,
reached him in Yemen, called him to Islam — and he accepted.** [6] They were
confirmed on the same marriage.

Neither woman was told to consider herself divorced the moment she believed.

**The other side, stated plainly**, because you should not read only the
encouraging half. Malik's own ruling follows those reports: **when the man
becomes Muslim before his wife, separation falls between them once Islam has been
offered to her and she does not accept** [7] — on the authority of the verse
forbidding holding to the ties of disbelieving women. Where the offer is made and
refused, the schools do reach separation.

**Living outside a Muslim jurisdiction changes the mechanics.** The Hanafis hold
that in that situation **the severance of the marriage waits on the passing of
three menstrual cycles** [8] — and, crucially, **if the other becomes Muslim
before that, the marriage remains.** [9] There is a window, and it is not
instantaneous.

**What you cannot settle from this page, and must not try to.**

- Whether your spouse falls within "People of the Book" — this is contested for
  some communities and turns on facts about them.
- What "Islam is offered" means where there is no Muslim judge to offer it.
- What the classical separation means when your marriage is a civil one under a
  law that knows nothing of any of this.
- Whether your particular school reaches the same result.

Every one of those has to be answered by someone who can ask you questions. Ask a
scholar you can actually speak to — ideally one who works with converts, because
they will have met your situation before. Take this framework with you; do not
act on it alone.

# Can men wear gold or silk?

## Answer

No to both, for men — and this is one of the few dress rulings with no serious
dissent behind it. But the details are where the useful answers are: what
counts as gold, what counts as silk, and what the exceptions are.

**The text everything rests on.** 'Ali reported that "the Prophet of Allah ﷺ
took silk and put it in his right hand, and took gold and put it in his left,
then said: these two are forbidden to the males of my community" [1]. Abu Musa's
wording gives both halves: "the wearing of silk and gold is forbidden to the
males of my community, and permitted to their females" [12] — and al-Tirmidhi,
after naming thirteen further Companions who narrated in the chapter, grades it:
"the hadith of Abu Musa is a *hasan sahih* hadith" [13]. Ibn Qudama: "we do not know of any disagreement over the prohibition of
men wearing that, except for an accidental cause or an excuse — Ibn 'Abd al-Barr
said: this is consensus" [2]. On the ring specifically, al-Nawawi: "the Muslims
are agreed on the permissibility of the gold ring for women, and are agreed on
its prohibition for men — except what is related from Abu Bakr b. Muhammad b.
Hazm, that it is permitted, and from someone that it is disliked rather than
forbidden; and these two transmissions are void" [3].

**What "gold" covers.** More than a solid ring. Al-Nawawi: "the tooth of the
ring is forbidden if it is gold, even if the rest of it is silver; and likewise
if a silver ring is plated with gold, it is forbidden" [4]. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr
extends it past jewellery: "it is not permissible for men to wear a gold ring,
nor to ornament a sword or a mushaf with it for himself, nor to wear it in
anything at all" [5]. Ibn Taymiyya applies the same to a gold belt-fitting,
which is "forbidden" — while noting that "as for a silver belt-fitting, there is
a dispute among the scholars over it, and al-Shafi'i and Ahmad in one of the two
narrations permitted it" [6]. Silver is a different question from gold; men's
silver rings are not part of this prohibition.

**One boundary people get wrong.** The prohibition is on *wearing*, not on
owning. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr: "this is a general wording but what is intended by it
is specific, by consensus — for they do not differ that owning silk and gold and
keeping them is the same for men and women, all of that being lawful for all of
them" [7]. Buying your wife a gold necklace, inheriting gold, trading in it: all
untouched by this hadith.

**What "silk" covers, and here there is real latitude.** Ibn 'Abbas restricted
it: "the Messenger of Allah ﷺ only forbade the solid garment of silk; as for the
silk border and the warp of the garment, there is no harm" [8]. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr
notes he was followed in this by a group of the scholars. Ibn Qudama gives the
measure the schools worked with: "a silk border in a garment is permitted if it
is four fingers or less — for it is related from 'Umar b. al-Khattab that he
said: the Prophet ﷺ forbade silk except the space of two fingers, or three, or
four" [9]. So a trim, a lining detail, a blend: not the prohibition. A solid
silk shirt: yes.

**And a genuine medical exception.** Ibn Qudama: "if he wears silk on account of
lice, or an itch, or an illness that wearing silk benefits, it is permitted, in
one of the two narrations — because Anas reported that 'Abd al-Rahman b. 'Awf
and al-Zubayr b. al-'Awwam complained of lice to the Prophet ﷺ, and he permitted
them silk shirts on a campaign of theirs" [10]. Two of the ten given glad
tidings of Paradise, licensed for a skin complaint. It is not a rule that bends
easily, but it does bend for need.

**Why, and what it is not.** Nothing here is about gold or silk being unclean or
worthless. The Qur'an describes the reward of the people of Paradise in exactly
these terms: "Gardens of Eden! They enter them wearing armlets of gold and pearl
and their raiment therein is silk" [11]. What is withheld from men in this life
is given to them in the next. Read that way, the prohibition is a restraint on
display rather than a judgement on the material — which also explains why it
falls on men and not on women, and why owning is untouched while wearing is not.

**So, practically.** No gold ring, no gold chain, no gold watch case, no gold
plating on a silver ring. Silver is fine. A silk tie or a silk-blend shirt is
fine; a solid silk garment is not. And the whole of it lifts for a genuine
medical need.

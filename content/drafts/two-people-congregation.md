# Is it a congregation if it's just two of us?

## Answer

**Yes. Two people is a congregation — the imam and one behind him.**

Al-Nawawi states it as the school's position and gives the hadith: **the least
congregation is two, an imam and a follower — because of what Abu Musa al-Ash'ari
reported from the Prophet: "two and above are a congregation."** [1]

He is honest about that hadith's weakness, and then supplies the sound evidence
that makes the point anyway: Malik ibn al-Huwayrith said he and a companion came to
the Prophet, and on leaving were told **when the prayer comes, give the adhan, then
the iqama, and let the elder of you lead** [2] — reported by Bukhari and Muslim.
Two men, told to call the adhan and appoint an imam between them. That is a
congregation.

**And it is not restricted to two men.** Al-Nawawi: **our colleagues said the least
congregation is two, an imam and a follower — so if a man prays with a man, or with
a woman, or his slave-woman, or his daughter, or others** [3], the reward of
congregation is obtained for both.

So:

| | |
|---|---|
| Two friends at work | congregation |
| Husband and wife at home | congregation |
| Parent and child (old enough to pray) | congregation |
| Two women together | congregation |
| Two people in a car park, a hotel room, a field | congregation |

**How to do it**

1. **Decide who leads.** The general rule is the one who knows most Qur'an; the
   hadith above says **let the elder of you lead** [2] where they are equal. If a
   man and a woman pray together, the man leads — there is a separate answer here
   on women leading prayer.
2. **Give the adhan and iqama** if you can — the instruction above includes both,
   even for two.
3. **The follower stands to the imam's right**, level with him or slightly behind,
   not in a row behind.
4. **Two women**: the one leading stands *in the middle of the row*, not in front.
   That detail is in the separate answer here on women leading.
5. **Pray normally.** The imam recites aloud in Fajr, Maghrib and Isha.

**Why this matters practically.** The reward of congregation — reported as
twenty-seven times that of praying alone — is available to you without a mosque,
without a jama'ah, without anyone else being present but one other person. For
anyone isolated, working odd hours, or new to Islam with one Muslim friend, that
is a large thing.

**Is congregation obligatory?** A separate and disputed question, with its own
answer here. But note that if you take the stricter view that a man must pray in
congregation, this ruling is also what makes that achievable: praying with your
brother, your colleague or your spouse discharges it.

**And a note for couples.** Praying together at home is a congregation in the full
technical sense, with the full reward, and it is one of the easiest habits to
build. It requires nothing but both of you being in the same room at the same time.

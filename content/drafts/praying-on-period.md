# Do I pray during my period?

## Answer

**No. You do not pray during your period, and you do not make those prayers up
afterwards. Nothing is owed.**

This is one of the most settled rulings in Islamic law, and it is worth saying
clearly because new Muslims often assume they are accumulating a debt. They are
not.

**You do not pray.**

Ibn Qudama lists it among the effects of menstruation: **it prevents the
performance of prayer and fasting** [1] — citing the Prophet's words, **is it not
so that when one of you menstruates she does not fast and does not pray?** [2]
Al-Sarakhsi states it the same way: **among them is that the menstruating woman
does not fast and does not pray** [3].

This is a prohibition, not a concession you may decline. Praying during your
period is not extra piety; the prayer is not accepted.

**You do not make them up.**

Ibn Qudama: **it drops the obligation of prayer but not of fasting** [4].

The evidence is a conversation between two women. A woman asked Aisha: **does one
of us make up** her prayer for the days of her period? Aisha's answer, in
al-Tirmidhi's wording: **one of us used to menstruate and was not commanded to
make it up** [5].

Al-Tirmidhi records the state of scholarly opinion in a single line: **it is the
position of the general body of jurists, with no disagreement among them, that
the menstruating woman makes up the fast and does not make up the prayer** [6].
Al-Qurtubi reports it as consensus: **the scholars are agreed that the
menstruating woman makes up the fast and does not make up the prayer** [7]. And
al-Sarakhsi: **among them is that making up the fast is required of her, not the
prayer** [8].

**Why the difference?** The sources report the ruling rather than fully explaining
it — Aisha herself declined to give a reason, and rebuked the question. The
practical logic is visible enough: five prayers a day for a week is a heavy debt,
where a handful of fast-days is not. But the honest answer is that this is
received practice, transmitted with unusual unanimity.

**What this actually looks like.**

- Your period starts → you stop praying, from that moment.
- Your period ends → you do ghusl and resume with the next prayer.
- Days missed → nothing owed, nothing to count, nothing to catch up.

**Menstruation is not a punishment and not a stain.** Ibn Qudama opens his chapter
by describing it as ordinary biology: **menstruation is blood the womb releases
when a woman reaches maturity, then it comes to her at known times** [9] — for
the purpose, he says, of nourishing a child. Nothing in the sources treats a
menstruating woman as impure in herself. She is exempt from two acts of worship
and continues with everything else — see the separate answers on du'a and dhikr,
on the mosque, and on the Qur'an.

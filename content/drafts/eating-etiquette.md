# Are there rules about how to eat?

## Answer

**Short answer: yes, and they fit in one sentence a child was taught.** Al-Tamhid
records 'Umar ibn Abi Salama, then a boy: **"دعاني النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم إلى
طعام نأكله فقال ادن فسم الله وكل بيمينك وكل مما يليك"** — *the Prophet ﷺ called me
to some food we were eating and said: come close, say the name of Allah, eat with
your right hand, and eat from what is nearest you* [1].

Three instructions. Everything else in this area is commentary on them.

**Say the name of Allah.** Ahmad's *Musnad* has the same instruction to a group:
**"اذكروا اسم الله، وليأكل كل امرئ مما يليه"** — *he said to his
companions: mention the name of Allah, and let each person eat from what is
nearest him* [2]. Al-Nawawi records the reason given for it, in the Prophet's ﷺ
own words: **"إذا دخل الرجل بيته فذكر الله تعالى عند دخوله وعند طعامه قال الشيطان
لا مبيت لكم ولا عشاء"** — *when a man enters his house and mentions Allah on
entering and at his meal, Shaytan says: there is no lodging for you and no supper*
[3]. The mention is framed as a **protection**, not a formality.

**Eat with your right hand.** Al-Nasa'i: **"نهى رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم أن
يأكل الرجل بشماله أو يشرب بشماله فإن الشيطان يأكل بشماله ويشرب بشماله"** — *the
Messenger of Allah ﷺ forbade that a man eat with his left hand or drink with his
left hand, for Shaytan eats with his left hand and drinks with his left hand* [4].
Ibn 'Abd al-Barr treats it as binding rather than merely preferred: **"وفي هذا
الحديث أدب الأكل والشرب ولا يجوز لأحد أن يأكل بشماله ولا أن يشرب بشماله لنهي رسول
الله صلى الله عليه وسلم عن ذلك"** — *in this hadith is the etiquette of eating and
drinking; it is not permitted for anyone to eat with his left hand nor drink with
his left hand, because of the Messenger of Allah's ﷺ prohibition of that* [5]. If
you cannot use your right hand — injury, disability, a lost limb — the instruction
plainly does not reach you; it is about which hand you choose, not about which
hand you have.

**Eat from what is in front of you.** The point is obvious once said: reaching
across a shared dish for the better piece is what the instruction rules out.

**And one more, which is not usually on the list.** Sahih Muslim: **"إذا صنع
لأحدكم خادمه طعامه، ثم جاءه به، وقد ولي حره ودخانه، فليقعده معه، فليأكل، فإن كان
الطعام مشفوها قليلا، فليضع في يده منه أكلة أو أكلتين"** — *when the servant of one
of you has prepared his food and brought it to him, having borne its heat and its
smoke, let him seat him with him and let him eat; and if the food is scarce and
little, let him place in his hand a morsel or two of it* [6].

**Whoever cooked it eats with you.** That is a rule of etiquette in the strongest
sense of the word, and it is worth more than most of what circulates under the
heading.

**On drinking standing up — a case worth seeing in full.** Ibn Taymiyya reports
the prohibition: **"أن النبي زجر عن الشرب قائما ) قال قتادة فقلنا الأكل فقال ذاك
شر وأخبث"** — *the Prophet ﷺ rebuked drinking while standing. Qatada said: we
asked, and eating? He said: that is worse and fouler* [7]. And in the same place
the reports that permit it: **"شرب النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم قائما من زمزم"** —
*the Prophet ﷺ drank standing from Zamzam* [8]. 'Ali is recorded drinking standing
in public and saying he had seen the Prophet ﷺ do as he had done.

Ibn 'Abidin sets out how the jurists reconciled them: **"وجنح الطحاوي إلى أنه لا
بأس به، وأن النهي لخوف الضرر لا غير، كما روي عن الشعبي قال: إنما كره الشرب قائما
لأنه يؤذي"** — *al-Tahawi inclined to the view that there is no harm in it, and
that the prohibition was only out of fear of harm — as was reported from
al-Sha'bi, who said: drinking standing was only disliked because it causes
injury* [9].

**A rule that a Companion is reported breaking in public, explaining that he saw
the Prophet ﷺ do it, is not a rule to police anyone with.** Preferring to sit is
sound; treating someone who drinks standing as having sinned is not supported by
the material.

**In practice.**

- **Bismillah before, alhamdulillah after.** If you forget at the start, say
  *bismillahi awwalahu wa akhirah* when you remember.
- **Right hand.**
- **Eat what is nearest you** from a shared dish.
- **Sit down**, on the preferred view; it is not a sin if you do not.
- **Eat with people**, and seat whoever cooked. The Qur'an's own verse on eating
  in family houses adds that there is no sin whether you eat together or apart —
  the instruction is about generosity, not about a required arrangement.
- **Do not turn adab into a test.** These are the manners of a shared table, and
  using them to correct other people's tables inverts them.

And underneath all of it, the standing instruction: **"O mankind! Eat of that
which is lawful and wholesome in the earth"** [10] — lawful *and* good, which is
as much a rule about food as any of the above.

**Where this stops.** Individual reports on details — sitting posture, blowing on
food, particular utensils — have their own chains and their own gradings, and are
not all of equal strength. The core three are the ones taught in a single sentence
to a child, and that is a fair guide to their weight.

# How do I clean impurity off clothes?

## Answer

**Wash the affected spot until the substance is gone. That is the whole
requirement — there is no fixed number of washes and no ritual formula.**

Al-Nawawi states it plainly: **what is obligatory in removing impurity is
cleanness** [1], and **removing impurity does not require a number; cleanness
alone suffices** [2].

**Two kinds of najasa, two methods**

Al-Nawawi divides them, and the division is the whole practical answer:

**1. Invisible (*hukmiyya*)** — urine, or something already dried and gone from
sight. **If the impurity is judgemental, meaning it is not seen by the eye, such
as urine and the like, it must be washed once, and more is not obligatory —
though a second and third wash are recommended.** [3]

One wash over the area. Done.

**2. Visible (*'ayniyya*)** — blood, faeces, vomit, anything with a body to it.
Here **its substance itself must be removed**, and then a second and third wash
are recommended after the substance is gone.

**What if a mark stays behind?** This is the question everybody actually has.
Al-Nawawi: **when the visible impurity has been washed and its colour remains,
that does not harm — purity has been achieved. But if its taste remains, the
garment is impure, so the taste must be removed. And if the smell remains, there
are two positions of al-Shafi'i, the more eloquent being that it is pure.** [4]

Read that as a three-part test:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Colour** left behind | fine — the garment is pure |
| **Taste** left behind | not pure — keep washing |
| **Smell** left behind | disputed; the stronger view is pure |

A faded brown mark on a white shirt where blood was, after the blood is gone, is
a stain and not impurity.

**You do not have to wring it out.** **Whether the garment must be wrung when
washed — there are two views; the sounder is that it is not required.** [5]

**You do not have to use plain water.** Al-Quduri: **purifying impurity with water
is permitted, and with every pure liquid by which it can be removed, such as
vinegar and rose-water.** [6] Detergent, soap, a washing machine — all squarely
within this. The Hanbalis reason the same way from the hadith where the Prophet
told a woman to add **salt** to the water for blood.

**Special cases the books single out**

- **Semen, once dry.** **Semen is impure — what is wet must be washed, and once it
  has dried on the garment, rubbing it off suffices.** [7] Just scratch it off. And
  a large body of scholars hold it pure to begin with — **many held that semen is
  pure, narrated from Ali, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, Ibn Umar, Aisha, Dawud, and Ahmad
  in the sounder of the two narrations, and it is the school of al-Shafi'i and the
  hadith scholars** [8].
- **Shoes.** **If impurity with a body to it touches the khuff and dries, rubbing
  it on the ground suffices.** [9]
- **A mirror or a blade.** **If impurity touches a mirror or a sword, wiping the
  two suffices.** [10] Non-absorbent surfaces need only wiping.
- **The ground.** **If impurity touches the ground and it dries in the sun and its
  trace is gone, prayer in that place is valid.** [11]
- **A dog's saliva** has its own rule, and its own answer here.

**And it is not urgent.** Only three things must be clean, and only for prayer:
**purifying impurity is obligatory from the body of the one praying, and his
garment, and the place he prays on** [12]. Until you stand to pray, nothing is
owed.

**In practice**

1. Identify the spot. Only that area needs anything.
2. Run water through it, working the substance out.
3. Stop when the substance is gone. A colour left behind is finished business.
4. Pray. The garment need not be dry.

There is a separate answer here on how much of a stain is excusable, and another
on praying in clothes with a mark you are not sure about — which, if you find
yourself re-washing things, is the more useful of the two.

وهذا مذهبنا ومذهب الجمهور وقال أبو حنيفة رحمه الله تعالى لا تطهر الا بحفرها وفيه أن غسالة النجاسة طاهرة وهذه المسألة فيها خلاف بين العلماء ولأصحابنا فيها ثلاثة أوجه أحدها أنها طاهرة والثاني نجسة والثالث إن انفصلت وقد طهر المحل فهي طاهرة وان انفصلت ولم يطهر المحل فهي نجسة وهذا الثالث هو الصحيح وهذا الخلاف إذا انفصلت غير متغيرة أما إذا انفصلت متغيرة فهي نجسة باجماع المسلمين سواء تغير طعمها أو لونها أو ريحها وسواء كان التغير قليلا أو كثيرا والله أعلم وفيه الرفق بالجاهل وتعليمه ما يلزمه من غير تعنيف ولا ايذاء إذا لم يأت بالمخالفة استخفافا أو عنادا وفيه دفع أعظم الضررين باحتمال أخفهما لقوله صلى الله عليه وسلم دعوه قال العلماء كان قوله صلى الله عليه وسلم دعوه لمصلحتين إحداهما أنه لو قطع عليه بوله تضرر وأصل التنجيس قد حصل فكان احتمال زيادته أولى من ايقاع الضرر به والثانية أن التنجيس قد حصل في   جزء يسير من المسجد فلو أقاموه في أثناء بوله لتنجست ثيابه وبدنه ومواضع كثيرة من المسجد والله أعلم قوله صلى الله عليه وسلم (إن هذه المساجد لا تصلح لشئ من هذا البول ولا القذر إنما هي لذكر الله وقراءة القرآن أو كما قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم) فيه صيانة المساجد وتنزيهها عن الأقذار والقذى والبصاق ورفع

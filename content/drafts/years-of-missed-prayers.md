# I have years of missed prayers — how do I deal with that?

## Answer

**Start praying today, and make up the old ones steadily alongside. Do not try to
calculate a total, and do not wait until you have a plan.**

**First, the part that may not apply to you at all.** Ibn Taymiyya distinguishes
someone who missed prayers **not knowing they were obligatory** from someone who
knew: **as for one who left the prayer ignorant of its obligation — like someone
who accepted Islam in a land of war and did not know that the prayer was obligatory
upon him — on this question the jurists have three positions.** [1] One of the
three, which he identifies as a position within the Hanbali school and others, is
that **there is no repetition upon him at all** [2].

So if you were not Muslim, or were Muslim in name without knowing the obligation,
there is a serious position that nothing is owed for that period. If you knew and
did not pray, you are in the other category. Either way, what follows works.

**Second, the ordering rule does not apply to you.** This is the single most
useful fact for anyone with a backlog. The requirement to make prayers up in order
**drops when the time is short, and by forgetfulness, and when they reach six** [3]
— and once dropped, **it does not return by their returning to fewness** [4].

You have long since passed six. So:

- **No chronology required.** You do not need to know which prayers, on which days.
- **No records required.** No spreadsheet, no ledger.
- **Any order.** Pray them however is easiest.

**Third, a method that actually works**

1. **Pray today's five, without exception.** This is the priority, and it is worth
   more than any amount of catching up. A backlog that stops growing is a solved
   problem; one that keeps growing is not.
2. **Add one make-up prayer after each current one.** Pray Dhuhr, then a make-up
   Dhuhr. Five a day doubles your rate and takes about twenty extra minutes.
3. **Estimate generously and stop counting.** If you think it is roughly ten years,
   work to that and do not audit yourself. The obligation is to make a sincere,
   sustained effort, not to hit an exact number.
4. **Make them short.** Fatiha and a short sura. These are valid prayers; they do
   not need to be long ones.
5. **Repent.** This is not optional and it is not a formality — for deliberately
   missed prayers, repentance addresses the missing itself in a way that the
   make-up does not. There is a separate answer here on that.

**On the minority view.** Some scholars — Ibn Hazm, and Ibn Taymiyya in some of his
writing — held that deliberately missed prayers cannot be made up at all, and that
what remains is repentance and voluntary prayer. If you find that position and it
tempts you as an easier road, note that it is *not* easier: on it the prayers are
simply lost. The majority position, which asks you to pray them, is the one that
offers you a way to make good.

**And the thing that stops most people.** Not the number — the arithmetic. People
compute 10 years × 5 prayers = 18,250, decide it is impossible, and pray none.
That calculation is the enemy. You are not asked to clear a debt by a deadline. You
are asked to pray today, and to keep chipping.

**One prayer made up today is one fewer than yesterday.** That is the entire
method.

حدثنا ‌قتيبة  ‌وبشر بن معاذ، قالا: حدثنا ‌أبو عوانة ، عن ‌قتادة ، عن ‌أنس ، قال: قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم:

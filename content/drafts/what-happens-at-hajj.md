# What actually happens during Hajj?

## Answer

Hajj is a fixed sequence of acts performed in and around Mecca over about five days, from the 8th to the
12th or 13th of Dhu al-Hijja. What follows is the shape of it. The details of dress and restriction
belong to the separate question of *ihram*.

The Prophet ﷺ performed it once, and said as he did: **"take your rites from me"** [1]. Everything below
is the jurists reading that performance.

### Before you go

Al-Fatawa al-Hindiyya lists what precedes the journey. First the practical: **"when a man intends to
perform Hajj, they said he ought to settle his debts"** [2]. Then the inward: **"then he begins with
repentance, sincerity of intention, returning what was wrongfully taken, and seeking release from his
adversaries and from everyone he has dealt with"** [3]. This is not decoration. It is treated as part of
setting out.

### The three tiers

Every school sorts the acts of Hajj into essentials that cannot be replaced, obligations that can be
compensated, and recommended acts. It is worth knowing the tiers before the sequence, because it tells
you what a mistake costs.

Al-Nawawi, for the Shafi'is: **"the pillars of the Hajj are four — the ihram, the standing at 'Arafa,
the tawaf of ifada, and the sa'y between al-Safa and al-Marwa"** [4] (a fifth, the shaving, on the
sounder view). The Hanafis count fewer pillars, holding the standing at 'Arafa the stronger of the two:
**"but the standing is stronger than the tawaf"** [5], and place the rest among the obligations —
**"as for its obligations, they are five: the sa'y between al-Safa and al-Marwa, the standing at
Muzdalifa, the stoning of the pillars, the shaving or shortening, and the tawaf of departure"** [6].

The consequences are what matter:

- Miss a pillar and **"whoever leaves a pillar, his rite is not complete and he does not exit ihram
  until he performs it"** [7].
- Miss an obligation and **"whoever leaves anything of them, a sacrifice becomes due upon him, and the
  Hajj is valid without it"** [8].
- Miss a recommended act and **"whoever leaves all of them, nothing is upon him — no sin, no sacrifice,
  nothing at all; but he has missed the completeness and the merit"** [9].

Order matters too: **"know that sequence is a condition in these pillars, so the ihram is required to
precede all of them, and the standing is required to precede the tawaf of ifada"** [10].

### The days

**8th — Yawm al-Tarwiya.** You enter ihram (if you have not already) and move out to Mina. Al-Nawawi,
explaining 'A'isha's report: **"and they entered the state of pilgrimage for Hajj when they went out to
Mina, and that is the day of Tarwiya, the eighth of Dhu al-Hijja"** [11]. The night is spent there.

**9th — 'Arafa.** The day everything turns on. You stand on the plain of 'Arafa from midday until
sunset, in supplication. The Prophet ﷺ said: **"all of 'Arafa is a place of standing — keep clear of the
valley of 'Urana; and all of Muzdalifa is a place of standing — keep clear of the valley of
Muhassir"** [12]. Position within the plain is not what counts; being there in the time is. And it is
the one act with no substitute: Ibn 'Umar, **"whoever does not stand at 'Arafa on the night of Muzdalifa
before dawn breaks has missed the Hajj"** [13].

**Night of the 9th–10th — Muzdalifa.** After sunset you leave 'Arafa without praying Maghrib there, and
travel to Muzdalifa, where Maghrib and 'Isha are combined and the night is spent. Al-Kasani is precise
that the obligation is the presence, not the sleeping: **"the sunna is to spend the night of the
sacrifice at Muzdalifa, and spending the night is not obligatory; the obligation is only the
standing"** [14] — and its time is **"between the breaking of dawn on the day of sacrifice and the
rising of the sun; whoever is present at Muzdalifa in this time has attained the standing"** [15].

There is an established concession here. Ibn 'Umar **"used to send his family and his children ahead
from Muzdalifa to Mina, so that they would pray the dawn prayer at Mina and stone before the people
came"** [16] — and al-Kasani notes the obligation itself **"may fall away by an excuse of weakness,
illness, menstruation or the like, so that if he hurried on and did not stand, nothing is upon
him"** [17].

**10th — Yawm al-Nahr.** Four acts, in this order for most: stone the large pillar (Jamrat al-'Aqaba),
offer the sacrifice, shave or shorten the hair, then go into Mecca for the tawaf of ifada — the pillar
that completes the Hajj — and the sa'y if it has not already been done. Malik reports the ordering of
the first two from the scholars of Medina: **"it is disliked to stone the pillar until dawn breaks on
the day of sacrifice, and whoever has stoned, slaughtering has become lawful for him"** [21].

**11th–13th — the days of Tashriq.** You return to Mina and stay the nights, stoning all three pillars
each afternoon. You may leave after the 12th or stay through the 13th.

**Departure.** The last act is the farewell tawaf, called *tawaf al-sadr* in the Hanafi list above [6],
counted an obligation by most.

The recommended acts sit alongside these: **"as for its recommended acts: the tawaf of arrival, the
brisk walk in it or in the obligatory tawaf, the sa'y between the two green markers, and spending the
nights at Mina during the nights of the days of sacrifice"** [18].

### While you are in it

The Qur'an sets the register: **"there is (to be) no lewdness nor abuse nor angry conversation on the
pilgrimage"** [19]. Malik glosses the first term plainly — **"lewdness is intercourse with women"** [20]
— and the other two rule out sin and quarrelling. In practice, on a crowded plain in the heat, the third
is the one that tests people.

# What breaks my prayer?

## Answer

**A short, specific list — and most of what people worry about is not on it.**

Al-Marghinani's *Kanz al-Daqa'iq* gives the Hanafi list in its own chapter, and it
is worth reading in full because the shape of it is reassuring:

**Speaking invalidates the prayer, and du'a resembling our ordinary speech, and
groaning, and sighing, and raising one's weeping from pain or affliction — but not
from the mention of Paradise or the Fire.** [1] Also **clearing the throat without
an excuse** [2]; **answering someone who sneezes with "may Allah have mercy on
you"** [3]; **correcting the recitation of someone other than one's own imam** [4];
**the salam and returning it** [5]; **reading from a mushaf** [6]; and **eating and
drinking** [7].

**Grouped by what they actually are**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Speech** | any deliberate word to another person; answering a sneeze; giving or returning salam; correcting a reciter who is not your imam |
| **Eating or drinking** | anything at all, even swallowing what was already in your mouth |
| **Losing wudu** | see the separate answer here on what breaks it |
| **Losing the essentials** | leaving the qibla, uncovering the 'awra, deliberately skipping a pillar |
| **Excessive movement** | enough that a watcher would think you had stopped praying — see the answer here on moving in prayer |
| **Deliberately leaving** | walking out, or ending the prayer on purpose |

**Now what is *not* on the list**, which is the more useful half:

- **Your mind wandering.** Not on any school's list. There is a separate answer
  here on it, and it is the one to read if this is your worry.
- **Small movements** — scratching, adjusting your clothes, moving a child out of
  the way. Separate answer here.
- **Crying from awe or from the mention of Paradise or the Fire.** Note the
  exception written into the text above: weeping *from pain or affliction*
  invalidates; weeping in prayer at what you are reciting does not. Separate
  answer here.
- **Someone walking in front of you.** Separate answer.
- **Mispronouncing a word**, or forgetting your place. That is a mistake, not an
  invalidation, and there is a separate answer here on the prostration of
  forgetfulness.
- **Not feeling anything.** Nowhere in any list.
- **Yawning, coughing you cannot help, a stomach rumbling.** Note that clearing
  the throat invalidates only **without an excuse** [2] — a cough you cannot
  suppress is excused.

**Two edge cases worth knowing**

*Speaking by accident.* Deliberate speech invalidates. Speech that escapes you —
by forgetfulness, or in the belief you had finished — is treated differently by
the schools, and there is a separate answer here on it. Do not assume the worst.

*Du'a in the prayer.* Supplication is fine; the exclusion above is **du'a
resembling our ordinary speech** [1] — asking a person for something, or wording a
request as you would in conversation. Asking Allah, in the recognised forms, is
part of the prayer.

**If your prayer is invalidated**, the remedy is simply to pray it again. It is not
a sin, nothing is owed beyond the repeat, and the time has almost always not run
out. That is the whole consequence.

**And a general caution.** This list is short and closed by design. If you find
yourself repeating prayers over things that are not on it, the problem is not your
prayer — there is a separate answer here on that, and it is worth reading
alongside this one.

# What makes a marriage valid in Islam?

## Answer

A nikah is a contract, and like any contract it has a core and a set of conditions around it. The core
is short. Al-Kasani: **"the pillar of the contract is the offer and the acceptance"** [1] — one party
proposes, the other accepts, in the same sitting. Everything else in this answer is a condition attached
to that.

In practice a valid marriage needs: **an offer and an acceptance**, **the woman's consent**, **the
guardian** (disputed — see below), **two witnesses** (disputed in a narrower way), and **a mahr**. No
registrar, no mosque, no imam. **"If we took inward uprightness into account, the marriage would not be
contracted except in the presence of a judge, because the general public do not know the conditions of
uprightness — and the Muslims have agreed on the permissibility of its being contracted without the
presence of a judge"** [2].

### Her consent

Not a formality. Qadi Khan: **"among the conditions of marriage is the woman's consent, if she is of
age, whether virgin or previously married — so the guardian does not have the power to compel her to
marry, in our view"** [3].

And the consent has to be informed. **"If her father seeks her consent before the marriage and says 'I
shall marry you off,' and does not name the mahr nor the husband, and she is silent — her silence is not
consent, and she may refuse afterwards"** [4]. Silence counts only when she knows who and on what terms.

Al-Nawawi records the distinction the hadith draws between a woman previously married and a virgin:
**"as for the previously married woman, speech is indispensable in her case, without disagreement,
whether the guardian is the father or someone else"** [5]. For the virgin, silence can suffice —
**"al-Awza'i, Abu Hanifa and others of the Kufans said seeking permission is obligatory in the case of
every adult virgin"** [6]. And on whose right is weightier: **"she has a right in herself regarding the
marriage, and her guardian has a right, and her right is stronger than his — for if he wished to marry
her to a suitable man and she refused, she is not compelled"** [7].

A marriage a woman is forced into is not a valid marriage. That is the classical position, not a modern
softening.

### Witnesses

Almost everyone requires them; the dispute is over who qualifies.

Al-Nawawi, for the Shafi'is: **"the marriage is not valid except in the presence of two male, upright
witnesses"** [8], citing **"there is no marriage except with a guardian and two upright witnesses"** [9]
and 'A'isha's report, **"every marriage not attended by four is fornication: a suitor, a guardian, and
two witnesses"** [10].

The Hanafis require witnesses but not uprightness: **"Abu Hanifa said witnessing is among its
conditions, except that it is contracted by the testimony of two immoral men, two enemies, two men
punished for slander, or one man and two women"** [11]. Al-Bahr al-Ra'iq gives their actual bar:
**"four things are stipulated in the witnesses: freedom, sanity, adulthood, and Islam"** [12] — nothing
about character. Their reason is that witnessing here serves publicity, not litigation: **"witnessing in
marriage is to repel the accusation of fornication, not to protect the contract in the event of denial
and repudiation"** [13].

Malik took the same purpose to a different conclusion: **"and Malik said the same, except that he said
it is among its conditions that they not enjoin one another to conceal it — and if they enjoin one
another to conceal it, the marriage is not valid even if witnesses attended"** [14]. For him what
matters is that the marriage be known.

The requirement is old. Al-Tirmidhi, closing his chapter on it, records the underlying report —
**"the sound version is what is narrated from Ibn 'Abbas as his own words: there is no marriage except
with evidence"** [15] — and then how little dispute there was: **"the practice among the people of
knowledge from the Companions of the Prophet ﷺ and the Successors after them and others is upon this;
they said: there is no marriage without witnesses. Those of them who have passed did not differ over it,
except a group of the later people of knowledge"** [16].

Two mechanics worth knowing. Both witnesses must be present together: **"the marriage is not permitted
until the two witnesses testify together at the tying of the marriage"** [17]. And both must hear both
parties: **"among them is that the two witnesses hear the speech of both contracting parties together —
so that if they heard one of them and not the other, or each heard one of them, the marriage is not
permitted"** [18]. A single witness never suffices: **"the marriage is not contracted with one witness,
because of his ﷺ saying, 'there is no marriage without witnesses'"** [19].

### The guardian

This is the largest fault line in the section, and it has its own question. In outline: **"on the
position of Malik and al-Shafi'i, her marrying herself off is void in every case"** [20], while the
Hanafis permit an adult woman to contract her own marriage, subject to the family's right to object to
an unsuitable match. Both sides read the same hadith — **"there is no marriage except with a guardian
and two upright witnesses; and if they dispute, then the ruler is the guardian of one who has no
guardian"** [21].

### What this means for you

If you are marrying: get an offer and acceptance said clearly in one sitting, the woman's informed
consent, two witnesses present at the same moment who hear both parties, and an agreed mahr. Involve her
guardian — it is required in three schools and recommended in the fourth. That marriage is valid in
Islam regardless of what any state registry says.

Whether you also need a civil marriage, and what to do if the guardian refuses or does not exist, are
separate questions.

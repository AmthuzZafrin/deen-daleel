# What should I say in the morning and evening?

## Answer

The Qur'an commands it in general terms, and the hadith fill in the actual
words. What follows is a short, well-attested set you can start with today —
none of it long, all of it from the books rather than from a compiled booklet.

**The command.** "Remember the name of thy Lord at morn and evening" (76:25) [1].
"So glory be to Allah when ye enter the night and when ye enter the morning"
(30:17) [2]. And with the manner attached: "And do thou remember thy Lord within
thyself humbly and with awe, below thy breath, at morn and evening. And be not
thou of the neglectful" (7:205) [3] — quietly, and not as a recital.

**The core one, and it is two lines.** Abu Hurayra: the Prophet ﷺ used to teach
his Companions, saying — "when one of you rises in the morning let him say: *O
Allah, by You we have entered the morning and by You we have entered the
evening, by You we live and by You we die, and to You is the return*; and when
he enters the evening let him say: *O Allah, by You we have entered the evening
and by You we have entered the morning, by You we live and by You we die, and to
You is the resurrection*" [4]. Al-Tirmidhi grades it: "this is a *hasan* hadith"
[5]. Al-Nasa'i has it from the Prophet ﷺ in his own practice [6].

**The second, from Ibn Mas'ud.** "We have entered the evening and the dominion
has entered the evening belonging to Allah, and praise belongs to Allah, and
there is no god but Allah alone, having no partner... His is the dominion and
His is the praise and He has power over all things. O Allah, I ask You for the
good of this night and I seek refuge in You from the evil of this night and the
evil of what comes after it. O Allah, I seek refuge in You from laziness. O
Allah, I seek refuge in You from the punishment of the Fire and the punishment
of the grave" [7]. In the morning you say *asbahna* in place of *amsayna*.

**The shortest one, with the largest promise.** "Whoever says, when he rises in
the morning and when he enters the evening, three times: *I am content with
Allah as Lord, with Islam as religion, and with Muhammad ﷺ as Prophet* — it is a
right upon Allah to please him on the Day of Resurrection" [8]. Three times, one
sentence.

**Ibn 'Umar's, which covers the ordinary anxieties.** He heard the Prophet ﷺ say
in his supplication in the evening and in the morning: "*O Allah, I ask You for
wellbeing in this world and the Hereafter. O Allah, I ask You for pardon and
wellbeing in my religion, my worldly life, my family and my property. O Allah,
conceal my faults and calm my fears*" [9] — and it continues with a request for
protection from before, behind, right, left and above.

**The recitations attached to these times.** Ayat al-Kursi: Ibn Kathir opens on
it, "this is the Verse of the Throne, and it has an immense standing; the hadith
is authentic from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ that it is the greatest verse in the
Book of Allah" [10]. And the promise attached to it after the prayers: "whoever
recites Ayat al-Kursi following every prescribed prayer, nothing prevents him
from entering Paradise except that he dies" [11].

Then the *mu'awwidhat*. Ibn Hajar clarifies what the word covers, because people
differ on it: "what is meant by the *mu'awwidhat* is Surat al-Falaq, al-Nas and
al-Ikhlas... so it is by way of predominance" — the name is plural for a group of
three even though only two are strictly "seeking refuge" [12].

**And one for the morning specifically.** Umm Salama reported that the Prophet ﷺ
used to say after the dawn prayer: "*O Allah, I ask You for beneficial knowledge,
accepted deeds, and wholesome provision*" [13].

**A practical shape.** Morning, after Fajr: *Allahumma bika asbahna*; the three
*raditu billahi rabban*; Ayat al-Kursi; al-Ikhlas, al-Falaq and al-Nas; and
Umm Salama's request. Evening, after Maghrib or 'Asr: the same, with *amsayna*
in place of *asbahna*, and Ibn Mas'ud's supplication.

That is perhaps three minutes at each end of the day, and every line of it is in
the hadith collections with a chain you can look up. If you can only manage one
thing, make it the three-times *raditu billahi rabban* — it is one sentence and
it carries the heaviest promise on this page.

# When exactly do I have to stop eating in the morning?

## Answer

**At the true dawn — the white light spreading horizontally across the horizon —
not at sunrise. That is roughly 75 to 90 minutes before the sun comes up.**

**The verse, and the misunderstanding it caused.**

The Qur'an says to eat and drink until the white thread becomes distinct from the
black thread, *of the dawn*. The last two words were revealed later, and the gap
caused a famous confusion.

Adi ibn Hatim took it literally — he put a white cord and a black cord under his
pillow and looked at them in the night, and could not tell them apart. He asked,
and the Prophet said: **your pillow is indeed wide! It is only the blackness of the
night and the whiteness of the day** [1]. Al-Bukhari's wording: **that is only the
blackness of the night and the whiteness of the day** [2].

So "the white thread" is daybreak on the horizon, not an object.

**Which dawn.**

There are two: a vertical shaft of light that appears and fades (*al-fajr
al-kadhib*), and the true dawn that spreads sideways and stays. The fast begins at
the second.

Al-Jassas cites the hadith that makes the distinction: **let neither Bilal's adhan
nor the whiteness of the horizon that is like this prevent you from your pre-dawn
meal — until it spreads out** [3]. *Until it spreads out* is the operative phrase:
horizontal, not vertical.

Al-Marghinani states it as the ruling: **the time of the fast is from the rising of
the second dawn until the setting of the sun** [4]. Ibn Qudama the same: **the day
that must be fasted is from the rising of the dawn to the setting of the sun** [5],
and he reports this as **the statement of the whole body of the scholars of the
Muslims**.

**Not at the first adhan.**

Ibn Qudama, quoting Ibn Abd al-Barr on the hadith about Bilal calling the adhan at
night: **evidence that the white thread is the daybreak, and that the pre-dawn meal
is only before the dawn** [6]. Some mosques call an adhan before Fajr as a wake-up.
That is not the cut-off.

**How this works in practice today.**

Your prayer timetable's **Fajr time is the cut-off**. Stop eating and drinking then.
Sunrise, printed further down the same table, is irrelevant to the fast — it is
only the end of the Fajr prayer window.

**On *imsak*.** Many timetables print an "imsak" time ten or fifteen minutes before
Fajr. That is a precaution some communities adopt, not the legal boundary. You may
eat until Fajr. Treating imsak as binding adds to the fast something the sources do
not require — though there is no harm in stopping a couple of minutes early to be
sure of the clock.

**If you are eating and the adhan goes.** Ibn Nujaym gives the Hanafi position on
uncertainty: **the point at which it is recommended is when he is certain the night
remains, or thinks it most likely; but if he is in doubt, the better course is not
to take the meal, so as to avoid the forbidden — though it is not obligatory on
him, and if he eats his fast is complete, because the default is that it is still
night** [7].

So: put down what is in your hand when Fajr comes in. If you were genuinely unsure
and it turned out to be a little past, the default the jurists apply is in your
favour. If it is clearly past Fajr and you know it, that is a different matter — see
the answer on what breaks the fast.

**And do not skip suhoor to avoid the question.** Eating late is the sunna — see the
answers on suhoor and on starting to fast.

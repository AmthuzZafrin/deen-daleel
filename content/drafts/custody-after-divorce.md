# Who gets the children after divorce?

> **This question is marked `expect: decline`.** The answer must explain
> why it needs a person — a mufti, a doctor, a lawyer — and must not give
> a ruling. Cite only to show what the question touches on.

> Reviewer note: Turns on the children, the parents and the court. Refer.

## Answer

**This is not a question anyone can answer for you from a book, and this page will not try.** Custody in
Islamic law is decided case by case, on facts about your children and both parents that only a judge or
scholar hearing your actual situation can weigh. What follows is the framework the jurists work within,
so you know what will be asked — not an outcome.

### The starting point

Ibn Qudama: **"when the two spouses separate and they have a young child or one lacking capacity, his
mother is the most entitled of people to his care, if the conditions are complete in her — whether the
child is male or female"** [1]. On this the schools do not divide: **"this is the position of Yahya
al-Ansari, al-Zuhri, al-Thawri, Malik, al-Shafi'i, Abu Thawr, Ishaq, and the people of opinion — and we
know of no one who differed with them"** [2].

The hadith behind it. A woman came to the Prophet ﷺ: **"O Messenger of God, this son of mine — my belly
was a vessel for him, my breast a drink for him, and my lap a shelter for him; and his father has
divorced me and wants to take him from me. The Messenger of God ﷺ said: you have more right to him, so
long as you do not marry"** [3]. And Abu Bakr, ruling against 'Umar over his own son, told him: **"her
scent, her fragrance and her gentleness are better for him than you"** [4].

### But every element of it is conditional

That is why the question cannot be answered generically.

**It depends on the mother meeting conditions.** **"If the mother is not fit for custody, through the
absence of the conditions we mentioned regarding her, or some of them, she is as though non-existent, and
it passes to whoever follows her in entitlement"** [5]. Those conditions include capability: **"likewise
they do not allow custody to a woman of bad character, nor to a weak woman incapable of fulfilling the
child's right through illness or infirmity"** [6].

**It depends on remarriage — mostly.** Ibn al-Mundhir reports: **"everyone of the people of knowledge
whom we have record of has agreed that the mother has no right to the child if she marries"** [7]. But
even that is not airtight — al-Qurtubi records **"from al-Hasan that her right of custody does not lapse
by marrying"** [8].

**It depends on who else is available, and the schools order them differently.** **"They differed when
she has no mother and has a grandmother who is the father's mother"** [9]. One Maliki ordering runs:
**"custody belongs to the mother, then the mother's mother, then the maternal aunt, then the father's
mother, then the child's sister, then the child's paternal aunt"** [10]. Other schools place the father
and the paternal grandmother higher. Where you sit in that list depends on which school is applied.

**And it has depended on the judge from the beginning.** The best-known custody ruling in the sources is
a judgement between three claimants — the Prophet ﷺ ruling on Hamza's daughter: **"as for the girl, I rule
for her in favour of Ja'far; she shall be with her maternal aunt, for the maternal aunt is a
mother"** [11]. Three righteous men, all with a real claim; it took a ruling.

### The criterion, which is the useful thing to know

Al-Qurtubi states what the assessment is actually for: **"the consideration in that is only to who will
protect the child and who will treat him well in guarding him and teaching him good"** [12] — on the view
that **"custody is the child's right"** [13], not the parents'.

That reframing is the most important line on this page. The question is not who has earned the children.
It is what serves them.

### And it ends

**"Custody is established only over a child or one lacking capacity. As for the mature and sound adult,
there is no custody over him, and the choice of residing with whichever of his parents he wishes is
his"** [14].

### What to actually do

Take it to a scholar or shari'a council who will hear the details, and to a family lawyer — in most
countries a civil court decides this, and its order is what will be enforced. Where a Muslim mediator can
help you reach agreement without a contested hearing, that is almost always better for the children than
either forum.

Whatever you are told about your case, [12] is the standard the tradition asks both parents to apply to
themselves first.

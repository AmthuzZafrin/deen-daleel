# How do I explain Islam to people who ask?

## Answer

The Qur'an gives an instruction for exactly this, and every word of it is about *manner*.

**The verse.**

*"Call unto the way of thy Lord with wisdom and fair exhortation, and reason with them in the better way. Lo! thy Lord is Best Aware of him who strayeth from His way, and He is Best Aware of those who go aright"* (16:125) [1].

Three commands and then a reminder that the outcome is not yours.

**What each part means.**

Al-Tabari on *reason with them in the better way*: "**dispute with them by the disputation that is better than any other — that you overlook what they have inflicted on your honour of harm**, and do not disobey Him in carrying out what is obligatory on you of conveying your Lord's message to them" [2].

And Mujahid's gloss on the same phrase is three words long: "**turn away from their harming you**" [3].

That is the *whole* content of *the better way*, on the reading of two of the earliest exegetes: absorbing the insult and continuing to speak well. Not winning.

Ibn Kathir: "**whoever among them needs debate and argument — let it be by the good manner, with gentleness and softness and good address**" [4], citing *"and do not argue with the People of the Scripture except by what is better"*. And: "**God commanded him to gentleness of manner, as He commanded Moses and Aaron when He sent them to Pharaoh, saying: speak to him a gentle word, that perhaps he may take heed or fear**" [5].

Pharaoh. The most extreme case the Qur'an contains, and the instruction was *a gentle word*.

**And then the release.**

Ibn Kathir on the verse's closing: *"your Lord is best aware of who has strayed from His way and of those who are guided"* — "**that is, His knowledge of the wretched and the felicitous among them is prior**" [6].

The verse ends by taking the result out of your hands. You are answerable for how you speak. You are not answerable for whether anyone believes you.

**What that means in an ordinary conversation.**

- **Answer what was asked.** Most people asking about Islam want to know one thing — what you eat, why you pray, what Ramadan is. Answer that. A question is not an opening for a sermon, and treating it as one is why people stop asking.
- **Say *I don't know* freely.** You are new. "I don't know, but I can find out" is more persuasive than a confident wrong answer, and it protects you from the far worse thing of speaking about God without knowledge.
- **Gentleness is the command, not a tactic.** Al-Tabari and Mujahid both read *the better way* as absorbing the harm. If a conversation turns hostile, the instruction is to keep your manner, not to win.
- **Do not argue to win.** The verse assigns you the calling and God the guiding. A debate you win and a person you lose is a failure by this standard.
- **Your life is the argument.** The most common route to this question is somebody watching how you behave for six months and then asking. Nothing you say will outweigh how you treat people.
- **Know when to stop.** *Speak to him a gentle word, that perhaps he may take heed* — even for Pharaoh the outcome is left open. When someone is not asking any more, stop.

**One caution.** Be careful about speaking on rulings you have not verified. It is very easy, when you are asked something and want to be helpful, to state as Islam's position something you half-remember. Say what you know, cite where you got it, and leave the rest to someone who has studied — that is closer to *wisdom* in the verse than confidence is.

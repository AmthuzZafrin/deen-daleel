# Does a wife have to obey her husband?

## Answer

The classical answer is yes, within limits — and the limits are the part usually left out. What the
sources actually describe is a specific and bounded duty, not a general one, and it sits inside a set of
obligations running the other way.

### Where it comes from

**"Men are in charge of women, because Allah hath made the one of them to excel the other, and because
they spend of their property (for the support of women)"** [1].

Al-Jassas draws the duty from the word *qawwamun*: **"God Almighty said, 'men are in charge of women' —
this conveys that her obedience to him is binding, because describing him as standing over her requires
that"** [2]. And Ibn Kathir glosses *qanitat* in the same verse: **"Ibn 'Abbas and more than one said: it
means obedient to their husbands"** [3].

But notice what the verse gives as the reason: *because they spend of their property*. Al-Nawawi's
*Majmu'* takes the phrase as referring to exactly that: **"and His saying 'men are in charge of women' —
meaning by spending on them and clothing them"** [4]. The authority is tied to the maintenance, in the
same clause.

### The limits, which are structural

**It is reciprocal in performance, not identical in content.** Al-Nawawi: **"the correspondence here is
in the discharging, not in the right itself — because the wife's right is maintenance, clothing and the
like, and the husband's right is enabling intimacy"** [5]. That is a narrow specification, and it is
worth reading twice. The classical books do not describe a duty to obey in everything. They describe
mutual claims, each with its own content.

**Both owe good treatment, and this is stated as an obligation on each.** **"It is obligatory upon each
of the two spouses to treat the other with kindness"** [6]. Al-Shafi'i's definition of what that means is
one of the more demanding lines in the fiqh: **"the sum of kindness between the spouses is restraining
what is disliked, and sparing the one who has a right the burden of asking for it — not by showing
reluctance in discharging it"** [7]. In practice: **"restraining what is disliked is that neither of them
harm the other by word or deed"** [8], and each gives what is owed **"with a cheerful face and a
smile"** [9].

**And al-Jassas is explicit that the rights run in both directions.** **"God Almighty has informed us in
this verse that each of the two spouses has a right over the other, and that the husband is
distinguished by a right over her which she does not have the like of over him"** [10]. He then spends
most of a page listing *her* rights before turning to his: the command to live with them in kindness,
release with kindness or retention with kindness, maintenance, the mahr, not taking back what he gave,
not suspending her while inclining to another, her conjugal due, not detaining her to harm her — **"among
what God Almighty made clear of the woman's right over him is His saying, 'and live with them in
kindness'"** [11], and **"this maintenance was among her rights over him"** [12].

### Where obedience does not reach

Two boundaries follow from what the sources actually say.

**Not in disobedience to God.** This principle is general in the law, and nothing in the verse or its
commentary carves out an exception for husbands.

**Not as a licence.** Ibn Kathir defines *nushuz* narrowly: **"nushuz is rising up — the defiant woman is
the one who rises against her husband, abandons his command, turns away from him, hates him"** [13]. It
is a breakdown of the marriage, not a disagreement, not an opinion, not a refusal of an unreasonable
demand. A husband who reaches for the verse over an ordinary argument has misidentified the situation the
verse addresses.

### The hadiths, and how they are used

Two are usually quoted at women. Both are in the sources above, and both deserve their context.

**"The best of women is a woman who pleases you when you look at her, obeys you when you instruct her,
and guards you in herself and your property when you are away"** [14] — a description of an excellent
wife, phrased as praise, not as a threshold.

And: **"if a woman prays her five, fasts her month, guards her chastity and obeys her husband, it is said
to her: enter Paradise by whichever of its gates you wish"** [15].

Set against them, in the same books, is the standard set for men: **"the most complete of the believers
in faith are the best of them in character, and the best of you are the best of you to your women"** [16].

The pattern is consistent across the material. Each spouse is told to look to their own duty. The
tradition put the sharpest words about generosity to the husband, and the strongest promise to the wife —
which is not how a text that simply wanted obedience would have been arranged.

### If this is a live question in your marriage

Then the fiqh is probably not what you need. A marriage in which obedience has become the operative
question has usually failed at [7] and [8] first — someone is being made to ask for what is owed, or
being harmed by word or deed. That is the thing to address, with a scholar or a mediator who knows you
both. And if there is coercion or violence, no reading of [1] permits it; see the separate question on
spousal rights, and speak to someone outside the marriage.

لعنتها الملائكة حتى تصبح، ورواه أحمد   والبخاري ومسلم. وقوله: لا باظهاره الكراهية في تأديته، إذا طلبت الزوجة حقها منه أو طلب الزوج حقه منها بذل كل واحد منهما ما وجب لصاحبه وهو باش الوجه ضاحك السن لما روى أبو هريرة رضي الله عنه أن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم قال: أكمل المؤمنين إيمانا أحسنهم خلقا وخياركم خياركم لنسائهم، رواه أحمد والترمذي وصححه.

وقال صلى الله عليه وسلم: لو كنت آمرا أحدا أن يسجد لاحد لأمرت المرأة أن تسجد لزوجها. رواه الترمذي، وقال: حديث حسن، وهو بعض حديث عند أحمد عن أنس، وبعض حديث طويل من قدوم معاوية من الشام وسجوده النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم رواه أحمد وابن ماجة عن عبد الله بن أبي أوفى.

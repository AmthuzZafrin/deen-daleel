# What are the dress rules for men?

## Answer

There is no Islamic uniform for men. What the sources give is a set of
boundaries — and, more interestingly, a warning at *both* ends: against dressing
above your station and against dressing deliberately below it.

**The floor is the 'awra**, navel to knee, in something opaque. That is its own
question and the rules are the same in the street as in the prayer.

**Silk and gold.** Ibn al-'Arabi cites the hadith the whole chapter rests on:
the Prophet ﷺ said of gold and silk, "these two are forbidden to the males of my
community, permitted to its females" [1]. Al-Quduri states it flatly: "it is not
lawful for men to wear silk, and it is lawful for women" [2]. The practical
limit matters, because most cloth is mixed: "as for what has silk as its warp
and something other than silk as its weft, there is no harm in wearing it,
without disagreement among the scholars — and this is the sound view" [3]. A
silk-blend shirt is not the prohibition.

**The hem.** The reports about *isbal* are severe — al-Tabarani's narration from
'Abdullah b. Mughaffal: "the believer's waist-wrapper is to the middle of the
shins, and there is no blame on him between that and the ankles, and what is
lower than that is in the Fire" [4]. But Ibn Hajar does not leave the threat
unqualified: "this unrestricted wording is carried onto what came with the
qualification of arrogance — that is what the threat came concerning, by
agreement" [5]. The sin named is pride, and the length is the symptom. He even
notes an exception for need: "excepted from *isbal* absolutely is what one lets
down out of necessity — like someone with a wound on his ankles that flies
irritate if he does not cover it with his wrapper, where he finds nothing else"
[6]. Trousers that break on the shoe are not the vice being condemned; wearing
them to be seen is.

**Imitation, and what it actually covered.** The Hanafis disliked *sadl* —
letting a garment hang loose over the shirt — and gave the reason plainly: "it
is related from Abu Hanifa that he disliked *sadl* over the shirt and over the
waist-wrapper, and he said: because it is the practice of the People of the
Book" [7]. Likewise *ishtimal al-samma'*, "which is winding the garment around
the body without bringing the hand out — named for that because there is no
opening from which he brings his hand out, like a solid rock" [8], disliked
partly because the 'awra is not safe from exposure and partly because it was the
dress of the arrogant. Note the shape of these: they are about specific
identified garments and specific reasons, not about clothing being foreign.

**A list worth having.** Ibn Mas'ud reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ
"disliked ten things: yellow dye, changing grey hair, dragging the waist-wrapper,
the gold ring — or he said, the ring of gold — playing with dice, and displaying
adornment in the wrong place" [9], among others. Three of those ten are matters
of dress and appearance, which is a fair indication of the proportion.

**And now the half that gets left out.** Ibn al-Jawzi, quoted by al-Qurtubi:
"I dislike the wearing of coarse wraps and patched garments, for four reasons.
The first: it was not the dress of the salaf — they only patched out of
necessity. The second: it involves a claim of poverty, and a person has been
commanded to show the trace of Allah's favours upon him. The third: displaying
asceticism, and we have been commanded to conceal it. The fourth: it is
imitation of these people who have drifted away from the Sacred Law — and
whoever imitates a people is one of them" [10]. Dressing down to look pious is
a fault with four separate names.

What they actually did: "the salaf used to wear middling garments, neither
extravagant nor mean, and they would pick out the best of them for Jumu'ah, for
'Id and for meeting their brothers — and choosing the best was not thought ugly
by them" [11].

To the objection that caring about clothes is vanity, Ibn al-Jawzi answers:
"not everything the soul desires is blameworthy, and not everything one adorns
oneself with before people is disliked; that is only forbidden when the Law has
forbidden it, or when it is by way of showing off in the matter of religion"
[12]. And the narration he closes with is the one to keep: 'A'isha saw the
Prophet ﷺ looking into a water-skin in the yard, straightening his beard and his
hair before going out to his companions, and asked whether he did that — "yes;
when a man goes out to his brothers, let him make himself presentable, for Allah
is beautiful and loves beauty" [13].

**Where the line falls.** Not on price or style, but on what is in the heart
while wearing it: "no one enters Paradise who has in his heart the weight of a
mote of arrogance" [14].

So: cover navel to knee, opaquely; no pure silk, no gold; keep the hem short of
ostentation; dress well without display and plainly without performance. Beyond
that the sources leave you to your own custom, and there is no daleel requiring
a thobe over a shirt.

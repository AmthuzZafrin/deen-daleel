# What does Islam say about other religions?

## Answer

Two things are true at once here, and honest answers hold both. Islam makes a claim about itself that it does not soften, and it forbids forcing that claim on anyone.

**The claim.**

*"And whoso seeketh as religion other than the Surrender (to Allah) it will not be accepted from him, and he will be a loser in the Hereafter"* (3:85) [1].

That is not softened anywhere in these sources, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest. Islam holds itself to be the truth and holds other paths to be, in the end, mistaken about God.

**And immediately before it, the other half.**

The same passage has Muslims answer the invitation *"be Jews or Christians, then ye will be rightly guided"* with: *"Nay, but (we follow) the religion of Abraham, the upright, and he was not of the idolaters"* [2] — a claim of continuity, not of novelty. And in the verses immediately preceding 3:85, Muslims are made to say that they believe in what was revealed to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob and the tribes, and in what Moses and Jesus were given, making no distinction between any of them.

Ibn Kathir: "**so the believers of this community believe in every prophet who was sent, and in every book that was revealed. They disbelieve in none of that; rather they affirm what was revealed from God, and every prophet God sent**" [3].

Read those two verses in order, because that is how they are written. Islam's claim is not that other traditions came from nowhere. It is that they came from the same God, and that this is the last of them.

**And here is the part that governs how you live with people.**

*"There is no compulsion in religion. The right direction is henceforth distinct from error"* (2:256) [4].

Ibn Kathir: "**do not compel anyone to enter the religion of Islam** — for it is clear, evident, its proofs and demonstrations plain; it has no need for anyone to be compelled to enter it. Rather, whoever God has guided to Islam and whose breast He has expanded and whose insight He has illuminated, enters it upon clear evidence; **and whoever's heart God has blinded and whose hearing and sight He has sealed — entering the religion under compulsion and coercion does not benefit him**" [5].

Compulsion is ruled out not as a concession but because it does not produce the thing wanted. A coerced Muslim is not a Muslim.

**The occasion of that verse is worth knowing, because it is severe.**

Ibn 'Abbas: "a woman would be one whose children did not survive, and she would vow upon herself that if a child of hers lived she would raise it as a Jew. **When Banu al-Nadir were expelled there were among them sons of the Ansar, and they said: we will not abandon our sons** — so God revealed: *there is no compulsion in religion; the right direction is henceforth distinct from error*" [6].

Sa'id b. Jubayr's account of what happened next is four words in Arabic: "**whoever wished to stay, stayed; and whoever wished to go, went**" [7].

Muslim fathers were forbidden to compel their own sons back from another religion, at the moment those sons were leaving with an expelled tribe. And Ibn Kathir adds: "they mentioned that the cause of this verse's revelation was regarding a group of the Ansar — **though its ruling is general**" [8].

**How to hold both halves.**

- **Do not pretend Islam makes no claim.** It does, and people can tell when you are hedging. *I believe this is true* is a sentence a Christian or an atheist can respect; *all paths are the same* is one nobody believes you mean.
- **Do not confuse a claim with a licence.** Believing someone is wrong about God gives you no rights over them — not to compel, not to insult, not to withhold what you owe them.
- **Do not force anyone, ever, in any degree.** Not your children, not your spouse, not by pressure or threat or conditions. The verse was revealed to stop fathers doing exactly that.
- **Believe in their prophets.** You already do. Moses and Jesus are yours, and saying so is both true and the most disarming thing you can say in this conversation.
- **The verdict is not yours to give.** Every one of these texts leaves the final reckoning with God. You are not required — or permitted — to tell a specific person where they will end up.

**And what people usually mean when they ask.** Most of the time this question is really *does Islam think my mother is going to hell?* The material in this corpus does not put that judgement in your hands, and nothing you gain by pronouncing it is worth what it costs. What you can say honestly is that Islam holds itself to be true, that it honours the prophets your family honours, that God judges with perfect knowledge and mercy, and that you were commanded to be good to them — which is a thing you can actually do.

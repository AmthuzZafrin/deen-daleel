# What happens if I swear by Allah and break it?

## Answer

It depends which of three kinds of oath you made — and most of what people worry about turns out to be the kind that carries nothing at all.

**The three kinds.**

Al-Jassas sets them out from the two verses: "oaths are of two sorts, past and future. The past divides into two: *laghw* and *ghamus* — and there is no expiation in either of them. And the future is one sort, the contracted oath — and in it is expiation if he breaks it" [1].

Ibn Khuwayz Mindad, reporting the Maliki position, says the same and defines the first: "oaths with us are three — *laghw* and *ghamus*, in which there is no expiation, and a contracted oath about the future, in which there is the exception and the expiation. He said: and the description of *laghw* is that a man swears about the past or the present concerning a thing he thinks he is truthful about, then it becomes clear to him otherwise — so there is no expiation upon him" [2].

**1. *Laghw* — the idle oath. Nothing owed.**

This is the one covering most of what worries people. 'A'isha's definition: "the oaths of *laghw* are what occurs in disputation and jesting in banter, and speech that the heart does not contract upon. And the oaths of expiation are every oath sworn concerning some matter, in anger or otherwise, that he will do or will leave — that is the contracting of oaths in which Allah has prescribed expiation" [3].

And the plainest case, which Ibn 'Abd al-Barr says the general body of scholars agrees is *laghw*: "it is a man's saying *no, by Allah* and *yes, by Allah* in his talk and speech, not intending an oath nor meaning one" [4].

So the *wallahi* that punctuates ordinary conversation — *wallahi it was so hot today*, *wallahi I don't know* — is not a contracted oath and carries no expiation. This does not make it good; it is careless with Allah's name. But you have not incurred a debt.

Two further sub-cases fall here: swearing sincerely about something you believed true and turned out to be wrong, and swearing in jest.

**2. *Ghamus* — the deliberate false oath about the past. Grave, and not fixed by expiation.**

Ibn Khuwayz Mindad: "*al-ghamus* is that he deliberately lies in his oath about the past" [5].

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr on its weight: "this hadith indicates that the *ghamus* oath — which is the oath of *sabr* by which a Muslim's property is seized — is among the major sins, because everything Allah or His Messenger ﷺ has threatened the Fire for is among the major sins" [6]. The hadith: "Whoever swears an oath in which he is a liar, in order to seize by it the property of a Muslim man, will meet Allah while He is angry with him" [7].

Is there expiation? Disputed. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr: "as for the one on which they differed about expiation — it is the *ghamus* oath, which is that a man swears about a past thing while knowing he is a liar in his oath, deliberately. Most of the scholars held that there is no expiation in it… and a group, among them al-Shafi'i and al-Awza'i, held that expiation is due in it" [8].

Note what "no expiation" means here: not that it is light, but that it is too heavy for a fixed payment to settle. What it requires is repentance — and, where you took something by it, returning what you took.

**3. *Al-yamin al-mun'aqida* — the contracted oath about the future. This one binds.**

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr states the consensus: "what the scholars have agreed upon in this chapter is that whoever swears by Allah, or by a name of Allah's names, or by an attribute of His attributes, or by the Qur'an or something of it, and then breaks it — expiation for an oath is due from him, according to what Allah has described in His Book of the ruling of expiation. And this is something on which there is no disagreement among the jurists" [9].

So: *wallahi I will pray fajr tomorrow*, meant seriously, then missed — that is a contracted oath broken, and expiation is due. The details are on the companion page for expiation; in outline the Qur'an gives feeding ten poor people, or clothing them, or freeing a slave, and only if unable, three days of fasting.

**Which words count as an oath?**

The clear forms are agreed: "the scholars agreed that the explicit oath by Allah is the swearer's saying *billah*, or *wallahi*, or *tallahi*" [10]. Ibn Hubayra records the consensus more widely: "they agreed that an oath is contracted by Allah, and by all His beautiful names, and by all the attributes of His essence — such as His might, His majesty, His knowledge, His power and His capability" — though Abu Hanifa excepted "by Allah's knowledge" and "by Allah's right" [11].

Forms like *I swear* or *I testify* without naming Allah are disputed. Malik's view: "all these expressions are an oath if he intended *by Allah*; and if he did not intend *by Allah*, none of them is an oath" [12]. Intention decides.

**Swearing by anything other than Allah.**

Al-Qurtubi: "an oath is not contracted by other than Allah and His names and attributes" [13]. This is where the popular habit of swearing by one's mother, one's life, or the Prophet ﷺ sits — and the ruling is instructive. Al-Tabari, on the hadith of 'Umar: "an oath is only contracted by Allah, and whoever swears by the Ka'ba, or by Adam, or by Jibril and the like — his oath is not contracted, and seeking forgiveness is binding on him for having done what he was forbidden; and there is no expiation in that" [14].

So it is worse in one sense and lighter in another: it is a prohibited thing requiring *istighfar*, but no expiation follows, because no valid oath was made. Al-Sha'bi captured the priority: "the Creator swears by whatever He wills of His creation, and the created swears only by the Creator. He said: and that I swear by Allah and break it is dearer to me than that I swear by other than Him and keep it" [15].

**Two ways an oath does not bind you.**

*You said in sha' Allah.* Ibn 'Umar reported: "Whoever swears and says *in sha' Allah* has made an exception" [16] — and no expiation follows.

*The oath was to do something wrong.* 'Umar's ruling, on a man who swore that all his wealth would go to the Ka'ba if his brother asked again for the inheritance to be divided: "The Ka'ba has no need of your wealth. Expiate your oath and speak to your brother" [17] — citing the Prophet ﷺ: "There is no oath binding upon you, and no vow, in disobedience to the Lord, nor in severing kinship, nor in what you do not own" [18].

That is the answer for anyone who has sworn in anger never to speak to a relative again. The oath does not hold you to the wrong. Expiate, and go and speak to them.

**The practical shape of it.**

Ask three questions. *Was I speaking about the past or the future?* Past and honestly mistaken, or a verbal tic — nothing owed. Past and deliberately false — repent, and return what you took. *Did I mean it as a binding commitment about something I would do?* If yes, and you broke it, expiate. *Did I swear by something other than Allah?* Then seek forgiveness, and stop doing that.

And a general caution the Qur'an itself gives: do not make Allah's name a hurdle you keep tripping over. The point of the expiation is not that oaths are cheap, but that a person who has sworn himself into a corner is not meant to stay there — the way out is to break the oath, pay the expiation, and do the better thing.

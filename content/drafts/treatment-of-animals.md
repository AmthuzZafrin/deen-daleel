# What are the rules about treating animals?

## Answer

The rule is one sentence, and it is unusually wide.

Shaddad b. Aws: "Two things I memorised from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. He said: **Allah has prescribed excellence upon everything.** So when you kill, kill well; and when you slaughter, slaughter well — and let one of you sharpen his blade and give ease to the animal he slaughters" [1].

Ibn Abi Jamra's comment is the key to reading it: "in it is Allah's mercy to His servants **even in the state of killing** — He commanded the killing and commanded gentleness in it. And from it is derived His mastery over all His servants, since He has not left anyone free to act in anything without having set a manner for him in it" [2].

So the principle is not that the animal must not be used. It is that even where Allah has permitted killing, He has legislated *how* — and there is no gap anywhere in which cruelty becomes indifferent.

**What is forbidden: causing pain that serves no purpose.**

Ibn Taymiyya draws the line exactly: a person's welfare "is not complete except by using animals for eating, riding and the like. **But what is not needed, of tormenting it, Allah has forbidden** — such as confining beasts [as targets] and slaughtering them other than at the throat and the upper chest while able to do so. And Allah has obligated excellence to the extent possible in what He has permitted of killing and slaughtering" [3].

That is the test to apply to any modern case: *is this pain necessary to a legitimate use, or is it surplus?* Surplus pain is the forbidden thing.

**The clearest prohibition: using a living animal as a target.**

Anas walked into a house and found people who had set up a hen and were shooting at it. He said: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ forbade that beasts be confined [as targets]" [4]. And in the same chapter: "Do not take anything in which there is a soul as a target" [5] — al-Tirmidhi graded that *hasan sahih* and noted "the practice is upon it among the people of knowledge" [6].

Ibn 'Umar reacted more sharply. He "passed by some young men of Quraysh who had set up a bird and were shooting at it, having assigned to the bird's owner every arrow of theirs that missed. When they saw Ibn 'Umar they scattered, and Ibn 'Umar said: *Who did this? May Allah curse whoever did this. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ cursed whoever takes anything in which there is a soul as a target*" [7].

Al-Nawawi defines the offence and grades it: "the scholars said *sabr* of beasts is that it be confined alive to be killed by shooting or the like — and that is the meaning of *do not take anything in which there is a soul as a target*… **and this prohibition is for forbiddance**; for this reason he ﷺ said in Ibn 'Umar's narration: *may Allah curse whoever does this* — and because it is torment of the animal, destruction of its life, wasting of its value, forfeiting of its lawful slaughter if it is edible, and of its usefulness if it is not" [8].

Two related reports: on people shooting arrows at a ram, "he disliked that and said: **Do not mutilate beasts**" [9]; and "may Allah curse whoever mutilates an animal" [10].

**Slaughter: the details are legislated.**

Al-Marghinani gathers them. Sharpen the blade *first*: "it is disliked that he lay it down and then sharpen the blade — because of what is related from the Prophet ﷺ, that he saw a man who had laid a sheep down while sharpening his blade, and said: **Do you intend to make it die several deaths? Why did you not sharpen it before you laid it down?**" [11].

And the governing principle, with a further application: "the upshot is that whatever involves additional pain not needed in the slaughtering is disliked — and it is disliked to drag what one intends to slaughter by its leg to the place of slaughter" [12].

Not before its eyes, not blunt, not dragged. Every one of those is about the minutes before the animal dies, when nothing is gained by haste.

**Kindness to an animal is a path to forgiveness — and cruelty to Hellfire.**

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr gathers the reports. A man "found a dog panting with thirst at the edge of a well, so he drew water and gave the dog to drink, and Allah thanked him for that and forgave him. It was said: Messenger of Allah, is there reward for us even in the like of this? The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: **In every moist liver there is a reward**" [13]. And the harder version: "a prostitute woman saw a dog on a hot day circling a well, its tongue lolling from thirst, so she drew water for it with her shoe — and she was forgiven" [14].

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr's comment on the pair: "because just as a person is rewarded for kindness to it, so too is he burdened with sin for ill-treatment of it" [15].

And the counterpart: "A woman entered the Fire on account of a cat she tied up until it died of hunger" [16].

Read those together. The reward was not for a grand act — it was for water given to a stray. The punishment was not for beating — it was for *tying up and forgetting*. Neglect is the form animal cruelty usually takes, and it is the form the hadith warns about.

**One case that shows how far the obligation goes.**

Ibn Qudama discusses a sheep that has got its head stuck in someone else's copper pot. The pot's owner cannot be compelled to break his own property. But: "as for the owner of the sheep, it is not lawful for him to leave it, because of the torment of the animal in that. So he is told: **either slaughter the sheep to relieve it from the torment, or compensate the owner for the pot** — if breaking it is the lesser harm — and free it" [17].

The animal's suffering is not allowed to continue while two people argue about who pays. Somebody must act, and it is the animal's owner. That is a fair template for a modern owner facing a vet bill: the cost is yours, and "I can't afford it" does not licence leaving the animal in pain.

**In practice.** Feed and water the animals in your care, without fail, and do not confine one in a way that harms it. Get veterinary treatment when it is needed. Do not keep an animal you cannot properly keep. Never use a living creature for entertainment that hurts it. If you slaughter, sharpen first, out of sight, swiftly. And take the small opportunities seriously — the reports make a bowl of water for a stray a matter of forgiveness, which suggests that these are not minor acts at all.

وإن غصب دينارا، فوقع في محبرة آخر بفعل الغاصب أو بغير فعله، كسرت لرده، وعلى الغاصب ضمان المحبرة ; لأنه السبب في كسرها. وإن كان كسرها أكثر ضررا من تبقية الواقع فيها، ضمنه الغاصب، ولم تكسر.

وإن رمى إنسان ديناره في محبرة غيره عدوانا، فأبى صاحب المحبرة كسرها، لم يجبر عليه ; لأن صاحبه تعدى برميه فيها، فلم يجبر صاحبها على إتلاف ماله لإزالة ضرر عدوانه عن نفسه، وعلى الغاصب نقص المحبرة بوقوع الدينار فيها، ويحتمل أن يجبر على كسرها لرد عين مال الغاصب، ويضمن الغاصب قيمتها، كما لو غرس في أرض غيره، ملك حفر الأرض بغير إذن المالك لأخذ غرسه، ويضمن نقصها بالحفر. وعلى كلا الوجهين، لو   كسرها الغاصب قهرا، لم يلزمه أكثر من قيمتها.

(3988) فصل: وإن غصب لوحا، فرقع به سفينة، فإن كانت على الساحل، لزم قلعه ورده، وإن كانت

في لجة البحر، واللوح في أعلاها، بحيث لا تغرق بقلعه، لزم قلعه، وإن خيف غرقها بقلعه، لم يقلع حتى تخرج إلى الساحل، ولصاحب اللوح طلب قيمته، فإذا أمكن رد اللوح، استرجعه ورد القيمة، كما لو غصب عبدا فأبق.

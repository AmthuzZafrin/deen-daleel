# I've never fasted before — how do I start?

## Answer

**Eat before dawn, don't eat or drink until sunset, break it as soon as the sun
goes down. That is the whole mechanism. The rest is logistics.**

**What fasting actually is.**

Ibn Qudama gives the legal definition, and it is narrower than the English word:
**fasting in the law is an expression for abstaining from specific things, at a
specific time** [1]. Not a general austerity — a defined list, between two defined
moments.

The list is short — food, drink, and sexual intercourse, between dawn and sunset.
That is it. See the separate answer on what breaks the fast; almost everything
people worry about is not on it.

**The day, step by step.**

**1. Intend it.** The night before is enough — knowing you are fasting tomorrow
*is* the intention. There is no formula to recite.

**2. Eat suhoor before dawn.** Anas reported: **take the pre-dawn meal, for in the
pre-dawn meal there is blessing** [2] — al-Tirmidhi grades it **the hadith of Anas
is a hasan sahih hadith** [3]. Al-Nawawi records that **the scholars are agreed on
its being recommended, and that it is not obligatory** [4], and explains the
blessing plainly: **it strengthens one for the fast and makes him energetic for
it, and through it a desire arises for more fasting, because of how light the
hardship becomes for the one who takes it** [5].

Ibn Hajar lists more of what it does: **the blessing in suhoor is obtained through
several avenues — following the sunna, differing from the People of the Book,
gaining strength for worship through it, and the increase in energy** [6]. Its
distinctiveness is the point: **what separates our fasting from the fasting of the
People of the Book is the pre-dawn meal** [7].

Ibn Nujaym places it in time: **suhoor is what is eaten at the last sixth of the
night** [8], and gives the practice: **the sunna regarding suhoor is to delay it,
because the sense of drawing strength from it is more complete in that; and
likewise hastening the breaking of the fast** [9].

So: eat late, close to dawn — not at midnight.

**3. Stop at dawn**, not at sunrise. This catches most first-timers — see the
separate answer on exactly when.

**4. Get through the day.** Nothing to eat or drink. You may shower, brush your
teeth, swim, wear perfume, and be around food.

And there is a conduct element people miss. Abu Hurayra: **when one of you wakes up
fasting, let him not speak obscenely nor behave ignorantly; and if someone insults
him or fights him, let him say: I am fasting, I am fasting** [10]. A day of hunger
with a bad temper is not what is being asked of you.

**5. Break it the moment the sun sets** — see the answers on when to break and what
to say. Do not wait a "safe" ten minutes; hastening it is the sunna, as Ibn Nujaym
notes above.

**Practical advice for a first Ramadan.**

- **The first three days are the hardest.** Caffeine withdrawal, not hunger, is
  usually what causes the headache. Taper coffee in the week before.
- **Hydrate steadily between sunset and dawn**, not all at once.
- **Don't overeat at iftar.** A huge meal makes the next day worse.
- **Sleep is the variable that matters most.** Most people struggle because their
  sleep collapses, not because of the fasting itself.
- **Tell your employer.** Most will adjust hours or break times if asked.
- **Go to a mosque for iftar at least once.** Doing it alone is much harder than
  doing it with people, and that is much of the point of it being a month rather
  than a private discipline.

**If you have a medical condition, are pregnant or nursing, or take regular
medication**, see the answer on who must fast before you start. There are real
allowances, and the tradition treats damaging yourself through fasting as a fault
rather than a virtue.

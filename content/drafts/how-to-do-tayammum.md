# How do I do tayammum?

## Answer

**Strike your palms on clean earth, wipe your face, then wipe your hands.** That
is the whole procedure, and it takes about ten seconds.

**Step by step**

1. **Intend it** — in the heart, that this is your purification for prayer.
   Nothing is said aloud.
2. **Say *bismillah*.**
3. **Place both palms flat on clean earth** — soil, sand, stone, a dusty wall.
4. **Blow or shake off any excess.** The Prophet showed Ammar: **he struck the
   earth with his hands, then blew on them, then wiped his face and his hands with
   them** [1].
5. **Wipe your whole face once**, with both hands.
6. **Strike the earth a second time**, and **wipe your arms** — right with left,
   left with right.

**How far up the arms? Here the schools differ, and both practices are sound.**

*Two strikes, to the elbows* — the majority. Ibn Abd al-Barr: **Malik, al-Shafi'i,
Abu Hanifa and their colleagues, and al-Thawri, Ibn Abi Salama and al-Layth said:
two strikes — a strike for the face with which he wipes his face, and a strike for
the hands, wiping them to the elbows, wiping the right with the left and the left
with the right** [2]. Al-Nawawi's chapter states the same as the school's
transmitted hadith: **tayammum is two strikes — a strike for the face and a strike
for the hands to the elbows** [3].

*One strike, to the wrists* — Ahmad, Ishaq, al-Awza'i and most of the hadith
scholars. Al-Nawawi reports it fairly: **a group held that what is obligatory is a
single strike for the face and the hands, and that is the school of Ata', Makhul,
al-Awza'i, Ahmad, Ishaq, Ibn al-Mundhir and the generality of the hadith
scholars** [4]. Their evidence is the strongest-chained version of Ammar's own
report: **it is the most firmly established of what is narrated on this, from the
hadith of Ammar — a single strike for his face and his hands** [5], where Ammar
says **I asked the Prophet about tayammum and he commanded me with a single strike
for the face and the hands** [6].

**Which should you do?** If you follow a school, follow it. If you do not: doing
**two** strikes and wiping to the elbows satisfies both positions at once, because
the wrists are included in the forearms. It costs three extra seconds and puts you
outside the disagreement entirely. That is what to do by default.

**What counts as usable earth.** Anything of the ground, clean: soil, sand, stone,
a dusty surface. You do not need a visible layer of dust on your hands — the
wiping is the act, not the coating. In practice, a stone floor, a brick wall, or
the ground outside all work.

**What it replaces.** The same procedure serves for both wudu and ghusl. There is
no longer version for janaba.

**What ends it.** Everything that breaks wudu breaks tayammum, and so does the
availability of water. Ibn Abd al-Barr: **the scholars are agreed that the
purification of tayammum does not lift janaba, nor minor impurity, once water is
found** [7]. So when water arrives, the state you were in returns — bathe if you
were in janaba, make wudu if you were not. Prayers already prayed remain valid.

**Common mistakes**

- Rubbing the earth into the skin. Unnecessary. It is a wipe.
- Loading up with dust. Also unnecessary, and the sunna is to blow the excess off.
- Skipping the intention. It is the one thing the Hanafis make an outright *fard*
  of in tayammum where they do not in wudu.
- Doing it before the prayer time enters, when water might still turn up. Wait if
  you reasonably expect to find water.

The verse gives you the two limbs and nothing else: **then go to clean, high
ground and rub your faces and your hands with some of it** [8]. Face and hands.
That is the whole of the obligation.

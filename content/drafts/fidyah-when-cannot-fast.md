# What if I genuinely can't fast at all?

## Answer

There is a provision in the Qur'an for exactly this. Alongside the rule about making up days later, the verse says: **and for those who can afford it there is a ransom: the feeding of a man in need** [1]. The classical name for that payment is *fidya*, and it is what stands in for fasting when making the days up later is not going to happen.

The two settled cases are old age and permanent illness. Ibn Qudama: **an old man or old woman, if fasting exhausts them and is severely difficult for them, may break the fast and feed one poor person for each day** [2] — the position of Ali, ibn Abbas, Abu Hurayra, Anas, Sa'id ibn Jubayr, Tawus, Abu Hanifa, al-Thawri and al-Awza'i. He grounds it in ibn Abbas's reading of the verse: **it was revealed as a concession for the aged** [3]. The chronically ill are folded into the same category: **the sick person whose recovery is not hoped for breaks the fast and feeds one poor person for each day, because he is in the same position as the aged** [4]. He extends it further, to **one who fears destruction for himself from thirst or the like — and he required feeding as a substitute for fasting** [5].

The line that decides which applies to you is whether you will ever be able to make the days up. **Recourse is had to the ransom only when making up has become hopeless** [6]. If your condition is temporary — an illness you will recover from, a treatment that ends — you are in the ordinary category: you owe the days, not the payment, and you fast them when you are well. Ibn Qudama defines the qualifying illness narrowly: **the illness that permits breaking the fast is the severe one that fasting increases, or where a slowing of recovery is feared** [7]. Al-Kasani, arguing the Hanafi position, makes the same distinction the load-bearing one — the ransom **is owed as a substitute for fasting when one is unable to achieve it, with an inability alongside which capability is not ordinarily to be hoped for, as in the case of the decrepit old man** [8].

There is a third case, between the two: you could have fasted and did not, and let the whole year run out. That is not this question, but if it applies to you, the answer there is **along with the making-up, the feeding of one poor person for each day** [9] — you still owe the days.

And if you cannot afford the feeding either, you owe nothing. Ibn Qudama closes the section: **if he is also unable to feed, then nothing is upon him** [10], citing "God does not burden a soul beyond its capacity."

What you should do: get a clear answer from your doctor about whether the condition is permanent or temporary, then take that answer to a scholar who can apply it. Whether your situation is *fidya* or deferred days turns on that medical fact, and no page here can settle it for you.

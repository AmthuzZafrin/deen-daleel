# What's allowed with my spouse during Ramadan?

## Answer

**By day: everything except intercourse and anything you know will lead to emission.
By night: everything, without restriction. The Qur'an permits the nights of Ramadan
explicitly.**

**The night is unrestricted.**

This is the part worth stating first, because people sometimes assume Ramadan means a
month of abstinence. It does not. The fast runs from dawn to sunset; the nights are
ordinary nights, and the Qur'an says so directly in the fasting passage itself.

**The day: affection is permitted.**

Aisha, in al-Bukhari: **the Prophet used to kiss and be intimate while fasting** [1].

Ibn Hajar preserves her advice to a couple who assumed otherwise: **what stops you
from going close to your wife and playing with her and kissing her? He said: shall I
kiss her while I am fasting? She said: yes** [2].

So kissing, embracing, touching, lying together — all permitted by day, as a
baseline.

**The condition is self-control, not the act.**

Al-Kasani: **a young man and an old man asked the Messenger of God about kissing for
the fasting person, and he forbade the young man and permitted the old man, and said:
the old man has more control over his urge** [3].

Al-Nawawi states the rule that follows: **kissing is disliked for one whose desire it
stirs while fasting, and it is not disliked for anyone else, though the better course
is to leave it — and there is no difference between an old man and a young man in
that: the consideration is the stirring of desire and the fear of emission** [4]. And
the scope: **it makes no difference whether he kisses the cheek, the mouth, or
elsewhere; and likewise touching with the hand and embracing have the ruling of
kissing** [5].

**A caution the sources themselves raise.**

Aisha's own report carries a warning inside it. Al-Nawawi: **I said to Aisha: may the
fasting person be intimate? She said no. I said: was the Messenger of God not
intimate? She said: he was the most in control of his urge among you** [6].
Al-Kasani has the same and notes it points to something particular to him — **and he
was the most in control of his urge among you** [7] — adding that **it is transmitted
from Abu Hanifa that he disliked intimacy** [8], on the reasoning that with closer
contact one cannot be confident of stopping.

Ibn Hajar records the strictest end: **there is disagreement about kissing and
intimacy for the fasting person — a group disliked it absolutely, and that is the
well-known position among the Malikis** [9].

And al-Qurtubi draws the line nobody crosses: **I do not know of anyone who permitted
it for someone who knows that it will produce from it what invalidates his fast**
[10].

**What breaks the fast, and what is owed.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Kissing, touching, embracing without emission | Fast intact |
| Leading to emission (*mani*) | Fast broken. Make up the day. **No expiation** |
| Pre-seminal fluid (*madhi*) only | Nothing owed, on the majority view |
| Intercourse | Fast broken. Make-up **and** expiation — see that answer |

Al-Qurtubi: **if he kisses and emits, a make-up is upon him and no expiation — that
is the view of Abu Hanifa and his companions, al-Thawri, al-Hasan and al-Shafi'i**
[11]. And: **if he kisses and pre-seminal fluid comes, nothing is upon him according
to them** [12].

That gap matters. The heavy expiation — sixty consecutive days or feeding sixty
people — attaches to intercourse specifically, not to everything short of it.

**In practice.**

Be affectionate. Sleep in the same bed. Hold each other. If you know from experience
where it goes, keep the day's affection light and leave the rest for after Maghrib —
which is not a deprivation, since the whole night is open.

**And if intercourse happens by accident of momentum**, see the answer on that; it
carries a heavy consequence and is worth reading before Ramadan rather than after.

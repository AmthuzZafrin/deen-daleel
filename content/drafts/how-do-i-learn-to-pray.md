# I don't know how to pray at all — where do I start?

## Answer

By praying. Tonight, imperfectly. There is no version of this that begins
somewhere other than a first bad prayer.

What makes that bearable is that **the prayer is built in tiers**, and knowing
the tiers tells you where to put your effort. Kanz al-Daqa'iq lists them in
order — the obligatory, then the necessary, then the sunna, then the manners —
and the first list is short enough to hold in your head right now: **the opening
takbir, standing, recitation, bowing, prostrating, the final sitting for the
length of the tashahhud, and leaving the prayer by your own act.** [1]

That is the whole skeleton. Seven things.

The next tier down is **reciting al-Fatiha, adding a sura, and specifying the
recitation in the first two units** [2] — necessary, and worth getting to soon.
The tier below that is the sunnas. The tier below *that* is manners: **looking at
the place of your prostration** [3] and its like. Real, and not where a beginner's
attention belongs.

**Al-Nawawi states the practical consequence.** Of the many takbirs in a prayer,
**the opening one is obligatory and the rest are sunna — omit one and the prayer
is valid; you have missed a merit, and the agreement with the sunna.** [4] Learn
to hear that sentence about the whole prayer. Most of what you will get wrong for
the first year is the missed-merit kind, not the invalid kind.

**Learn it by copying someone.** Al-Nawawi notes that when the Prophet taught the
bedouin his prayer, **he taught him its obligations** [5] — the same skeleton
above, shown rather than described. Standing behind someone in a mosque for a
week will teach you more than any written description, this one included. If that
is not available yet, a video of the prayer performed straight through is the
nearest substitute.

**One relief if you are starting from nothing.** The Hanafis hold that **if
someone opens the prayer with tasbih, or tahlil, or in Persian, it is valid** [6]
— that is, in a language that is not Arabic. Whether you follow that position
depends on your school, but it tells you something about the temperature of this
subject. Beginning is not treated as a hazard.

**Where to go next.** The step-by-step description, with what is said at each
point, is a separate answer in this collection, and so is what to do while you
are still learning al-Fatiha. The schools differ in the details — which is not an
obstacle so much as a reason to learn one way properly rather than four ways
half-way.

Pick a time that arrives soon. Face the qibla. Begin.

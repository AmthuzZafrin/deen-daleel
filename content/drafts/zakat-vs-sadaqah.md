# What's the difference between zakat and sadaqah?

## Answer

Zakat is one kind of sadaqah — the obligatory kind. Sadaqah is the wider word: any giving for God's sake, at any time, in any amount. So the question is not really "what is the difference between two things" but "what makes this particular giving compulsory".

Four differences do all the work.

**1. Zakat is owed; sadaqah is offered.** Zakat has a fixed rate (a quarter of a tenth — 2.5% — on money), a threshold below which nothing is owed, and a date it falls due. Sadaqah has none of those. A pound is sadaqah. So is a smile, a shared meal, moving a stone off the path.

**2. Zakat has a closed list of recipients; sadaqah does not.** Zakat may only go to the eight categories named in Surah al-Tawba, and only to people who qualify. Sadaqah may go to anyone at all — including someone who is not poor. Al-Nawawi states this plainly: **voluntary charity is lawful to the rich, without disagreement** [1]. You can give a gift to a comfortable friend and it is still charity. You cannot give them your zakat.

**3. The excluded categories differ, and this catches people out.** Zakat cannot go to your parents, your children, or your spouse — al-Jassas records the Hanafi rule: **no parent however high is given from it, nor a child however low, nor a wife** [2]. Nor can obligatory zakat go to a non-Muslim; Ibn 'Abd al-Barr reports that **they were agreed that obligatory zakat is not lawful to other than Muslims** [3].

Sadaqah has neither restriction. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr, immediately after stating that consensus: **as for voluntary charity, it is permissible to the people of unbelief, whether relatives or others; I know of no disagreement about that** [4]. And al-Kasani on giving voluntary charity to those excluded from zakat: **it is permissible to pay it to these, and paying it to them is better, because in it are two rewards — the reward of charity and the reward of kinship** [5]. The very people your zakat cannot reach are the ones your sadaqah is best spent on.

**4. Zakat is better done openly; sadaqah is better done quietly.** Al-Nawawi: **concealment is recommended in voluntary charity** — **as for zakat, making it public is recommended, by the agreement of our companions and other scholars, just as the obligatory prayer is recommended to be performed openly in the mosque while the voluntary one is recommended to be concealed** [6]. Al-Qurtubi reports al-Hasan in the same terms: **making zakat public is better, and concealing voluntary charity is better, because that shows more clearly that God alone is intended by it** [7]. A public obligation discharged in public; a private generosity kept private.

**Two further practical differences.**

Zakat is tied to a place. Al-Jassas records the Hanafi position: **our companions Abu Hanifa, Abu Yusuf and Muhammad said the charity of each town is divided among its poor, and one does not take it out to another** [8] — with dispensation for needy relatives elsewhere, and a general permission that the transfer is disliked rather than invalid. Sadaqah carries no such preference; give it wherever the need is.

And zakat can lapse if the wealth does. Al-Marghinani: **if the wealth is destroyed after zakat became obligatory, the zakat drops away** [9] — the Hanafi view, disputed by al-Shafi'i, that the obligation attaches to the property rather than sitting as a debt in your person. Sadaqah, being voluntary, has nothing to lapse.

**The one thing not to conclude:** that sadaqah is the lesser of the two. Zakat is a floor, not a ceiling. It is the minimum the wealth in your hands owes to the people around you, and once paid it says nothing at all about what generosity would look like.

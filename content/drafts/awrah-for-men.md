# What does a man have to cover?

## Answer

Navel to knee. That much is agreed. Whether the *thigh* inside that range is
itself 'awra, or only the front and rear are, is one of the oldest live
disagreements in the fiqh — and knowing that it is a disagreement is more useful
than being handed one side of it.

**The rule.** Al-Nawawi: "the 'awra of a man is what is between the navel and
the knee — and the navel and the knee are not part of the 'awra; some of our
companions said they are part of it, and the first is sounder" [1], on Abu
Sa'id's report that the Prophet ﷺ said, "the 'awra of a man is what is between
his navel and his knee" [2]. Ibn al-'Arabi records the same as beyond dispute:
"the second is that it is from the navel to the knee, and there is no
disagreement in that" [3].

Worth knowing what the word itself carries: "it was named 'awra for the
ugliness of its appearing and for the lowering of eyes away from it — taken from
*'awar*, which is deficiency, defect and ugliness" [4].

**The disagreement.** Ibn al-'Arabi continues: "the disagreement — and it is the
third category — is over whether what is beyond the front and the rear is heavy
'awra or light" [5]. He states the split by name: "our scholars and Abu Hanifa
said the front and rear are heavy 'awra and the thigh is light 'awra; and the
sound view is that the thigh is not 'awra, because it appeared from the Prophet
ﷺ on the day he rode in the alley of Khaybar" [6]. Ibn Hajar reports the tally:
"al-Nawawi said: most of the scholars held that the thigh is 'awra; and from
Ahmad and Malik in one narration, the 'awra is only the front and the rear — and
the Zahiris, Ibn Jarir and al-Istakhri said so" [7].

**Both sides have hadiths, and the sources are candid about their weaknesses.**
Against uncovering: Jarhad's report, "cover your thigh, for the thigh is 'awra"
— of which al-Nawawi notes it was reported by "al-Tirmidhi in the Book of
Seeking Permission by three routes, and he said of each route: this is a *hasan*
hadith — and said of one of them: a *hasan* hadith, though I do not think its
chain is connected" [16]. On the other side: Anas seeing the
whiteness of the Prophet's ﷺ thigh at Khaybar, which is in both Sahihs. Ibn
al-'Arabi adds that the Prophet ﷺ would press his thigh against his Companions'
thighs, and Zayd's report that revelation came down on him with his thigh on
Zayd's until it nearly crushed it.

Ibn Hajar quotes al-Qurtubi's way of reconciling them, and it is a good piece of
reasoning whichever side you end up on: "Anas's hadith and its like came only in
particular incidents at specific times, to which the possibility of being a
special case, or of remaining on the original permission, can attach in a way it
cannot attach to Jarhad's hadith and its like — because that conveys a general
ruling and declares a universal law, so acting on it is preferable" [8].
Al-Awza'i went the other way, with a circumstantial explanation: "the Prophet ﷺ
only commanded Jarhad because he was in the mosque, ill — and the thigh is not
'awra" [9].

**Ibn al-'Arabi's own landing is where most people can actually live.** Having
argued the thigh is not 'awra, he immediately adds: "but uncovering it is
disliked — for Malik and others narrated Jarhad's hadith, that the Prophet ﷺ
said to him: cover your thigh, for the thigh is 'awra; and it is a well-known
hadith" [10]. Not sinful; not fine either. That is a real category and it
matches most people's intuition.

**What "covered" has to mean, which is the most practical part here.**
Al-Nawawi: "covering the 'awra with what does not describe the colour of the
skin is obligatory — a thick garment, or leather, or leaves; and if he covers
with something through which the colour of the skin shows, of a thin garment, it
does not suffice, because covering is not achieved by that" [11]. And then the
distinction people most often get backwards: "if it conceals the colour but
describes the shape of the skin — like the knee or the buttock and the like —
the prayer in it is valid, since covering is present" [12]. Opacity is the
requirement. Outline is not, at least for validity.

**Two things that change the standard.** Company: Abu Musa reported that the
Prophet ﷺ "was sitting in a place with water, having uncovered his knee or his
knees, and when 'Uthman came in he covered it" [13]. And need: "if he is
compelled to uncover for medical treatment or for circumcision, that is
permitted, because it is a place of necessity" [14].

**Alone.** Two views: "is covering it obligatory in seclusion? Two positions.
The sounder is that it is obligatory, on 'Ali's hadith; and the second, that it
is not, because the prohibition of uncovering is on account of being seen, and
in seclusion there is no one who sees, so covering is not obligatory" [15]. The
sounder view carries the day, and the reason given elsewhere in the same chapter
is the Prophet's ﷺ answer to Bahz's grandfather — that Allah is more deserving
of one's shyness than people are.

**In short.** Keep navel to knee covered in prayer and in company, in something
opaque. Shorts above the knee sit inside the disagreement — permitted by a real
minority, disliked by Ibn al-'Arabi even on the view that the thigh is not
'awra, and 'awra outright on the majority view. If you want the position that
gets you through every school's requirement, cover to the knee.

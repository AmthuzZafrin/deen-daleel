# What do Muslims believe about the Bible and Torah?

## Answer

**Short answer: a Muslim is required to believe that the Torah, the Gospel and
the Psalms were genuine revelation from Allah — that belief is part of the creed,
not a courtesy — while also holding that their texts were altered by later hands
and that their law has been superseded by the Qur'an.** Both halves are load-
bearing. Dropping the first is as much an error as dropping the second.

**The books were real revelation.** The Qur'an puts the affirmation in the
believer's own mouth: **"We believe in Allah and that which is revealed unto us
and that which was revealed unto Abraham, and Ishmael, and Isaac, and Jacob, and
the tribes, and that which Moses and Jesus received, and that which the prophets
received from their Lord. We make no distinction between any of them"** [1]. And
the same act of revealing is named for all of them: **"He hath revealed unto thee
(Muhammad) the Scripture with truth, confirming that which was (revealed) before
it, even as He revealed the Torah and the Gospel"** [2].

Al-Tabari, commenting on that verse, reports Qatada glossing the two earlier
books as **"وعصمة لمن أخذ به وصدق به وعمل بما فيه"** — *a protection for whoever
took it, affirmed it, and acted on what is in it* [3]. They were guidance for
the people they were sent to, in their time, on their own terms.

**How the Qur'an stands to them.** Ibn Taymiyya sets the relationship out
directly: **"فالقرآن والتوراة هما كتابان جاءا من عند الله لم يأت من عنده كتاب
أهدى منهما كل منهما أصل مستقل والذى فيهما دين واحد"** — *the Qur'an and the
Torah are two books that came from Allah; no book more guiding than these two
came from Him; each is an independent foundation, and what is in them is one
religion* [4]. He then distinguishes the others: David brought no law beyond the
Torah's, so the Psalms contain praise, supplication, and command to Allah's
religion rather than a new code; the Gospel largely follows the Torah's law,
which is why, on his reading, a follower of the Messiah had to read the Torah
too. The Qur'an is the exception — self-sufficient, needing no other book. That
is what the verse's word *muhaymin* means for him: **"ومهيمنا عليه يقرر ما فيها
من الحق ويبطل ما حرف منها وينسخ ما نسخه الله"** — *and a guardian over it: it
affirms the truth in them, invalidates what has been altered in them, and
abrogates what Allah abrogated* [5].

Three verbs, and they are the whole position. Take them one at a time.

**Affirms.** Where those books still carry what was revealed, a Muslim is not in
disagreement with them. This is why the Qur'an can address the People of the
Scripture as people who *have* something to stand on: **"How come they unto thee
for judgment when they have the Torah, wherein Allah hath delivered judgment (for
them)?"** [6], and **"Say O People of the Scripture! Ye have naught (of guidance)
till ye observe the Torah and the Gospel and that which was revealed unto you
from your Lord"** [7]. Neither verse reads as an address to people holding
nothing but a forgery.

**Invalidates what was altered.** This is *tahrif*. Al-Qurtubi glosses
"establishing the Torah and the Gospel" as **"وإقامة التوراة والإنجيل العمل
بمقتضاهما وعدم تحريفهما"** — *establishing the Torah and the Gospel is acting by
what they require, and not altering them* [8]; in the same passage he sums up the
reproach as belying the messengers and altering the books. Note what that
implies: alteration is presented as something a community *did*, an act with
agents, not a vague decay.

Al-Tabari's comment on the People of the Scripture who are praised makes the same
point from the other side. Those praised, he says, are the ones who **"لا يحرفون
ما أنزل إليهم في كتبه من نعت محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم فيبدلونه"** — *do not alter
what was sent down to them in His books of the description of Muhammad ﷺ, and so
substitute it* [9]. Naming people who did not alter is only meaningful if
altering was specific and particular rather than total and universal.

**Abrogates.** Al-Jassas states this one flatly: **"فإن شريعة التوراة والإنجيل قد
نسخت والعمل بها بعد النسخ ضلال"** — *the law of the Torah and the Gospel has been
abrogated, and acting by it after the abrogation is misguidance* [10]. He puts
the alteration alongside it in the same passage: **"فهم قد غيروا المعاني وحرفوها
عن مواضعها"** — *they changed the meanings and shifted them from their places*
[11].

**What this comes to in practice.**

- You affirm the earlier scriptures as revelation from Allah. Saying "the Bible
  is just a human book" is not the Islamic position and contradicts the verse you
  recite it against.
- You do not take rulings from present copies. On al-Jassas's reasoning above,
  the law they contained is superseded even where it is intact.
- You make no distinction between the prophets. Moses and Jesus are prophets a
  Muslim is required to believe in, and speaking ill of them is not available.
- Where a passage in a present-day Bible agrees with the Qur'an, it is not
  evidence a Muslim needs; where it disagrees, the Qur'an is what settles it —
  that is what *muhaymin* was doing in the passage above.

**One limit worth naming.** The sources cited here speak about the Torah and the
Gospel as revelation and about alteration among the People of the Scripture in
their own time. How much of any particular modern printed edition — which
translation, which canon, which manuscript tradition — corresponds to what was
revealed is a historical question these texts do not take up, and nothing above
should be read as settling it.

Whether you may read the Bible yourself is a separate question with its own
evidence; it is answered under *Am I allowed to read the Bible?*

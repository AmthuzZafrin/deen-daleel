# Is swearing haram?

## Answer

It depends what you mean by swearing, because the sources treat three different things differently: abusing a person, cursing a person, and foul language in general.

**Abusing a Muslim is *fisq*.**

Ibn Mas'ud reported the Prophet ﷺ saying: "Abusing a Muslim is *fisq*, and fighting him is disbelief" [1]. Al-Tirmidhi graded it *hasan sahih* and immediately headed off the misreading: "the meaning of this hadith is that fighting him is disbelief — not disbelief like apostasy… and it has been reported from Ibn 'Abbas, Tawus, 'Ata' and more than one of the people of knowledge that they said: *disbelief below disbelief, and corruption below corruption*" [2].

So "*fisq*" here does not put you outside Islam. It does put a name on it that no Muslim should want attached to their speech.

And there is a boomerang clause. Abu Dharr reported: "No man accuses another of corruption or accuses him of disbelief but that it rebounds upon him, if his companion is not as he said" [3]. If you call someone a *fasiq* or a *kafir* and you are wrong, the charge lands on you.

**Cursing a specific person is forbidden by consensus. Cursing a description is not.**

This is the most useful distinction on the page. Al-Nawawi: "the scholars are agreed on the prohibition of cursing, for in the language it is distancing and driving away, and in the Law it is distancing from the mercy of Allah. So it is not permitted to distance from Allah's mercy one whose state and whose end are not known with certain knowledge. For this reason they said: **it is not permitted to curse anyone in particular** — Muslim or disbeliever or animal — except one whom we know by a text of the Law died upon disbelief or will die upon it, like Abu Jahl and Iblis" [4].

"As for cursing by *description*, it is not forbidden — such as cursing the woman who joins hair and the one who has it joined, the tattooer and the tattooed, the consumer of usury and the one who pays it, image-makers, the wrongdoers, the corrupt and the disbelievers… and other than that, of what the texts of the Law have brought applying it to descriptions and not to specific individuals" [5].

So *may Allah curse oppression* is a form the Sunna itself uses. *May Allah curse that man* is not yours to say — you do not know how he will end.

And cursing a believer is placed alongside killing him: "whoever curses a believer, it is like killing him" [6]. Al-Nawawi cites the same and adds the point about habit: "the small sin, when multiplied, becomes a major sin" [7].

**Why the hadith says *lla'anun* and not *lla'in*.**

Al-Nawawi notices the grammar: "he ﷺ said *it does not befit a truthful one to be a habitual curser*, and *the habitual cursers will not be intercessors* — in the intensive form, and he did not say *a curser* or *cursers* — because this blame in the hadith is only for one from whom cursing has become frequent, not for once or the like" [8].

That is a genuine mercy in the wording, and it also identifies the real problem: not the slip, but the habit.

**Foul language as such.**

Usama b. Zayd said to Marwan, who had spoken foully to him: "You have harmed me, and you are foul and given to foulness" — and cited the Prophet ﷺ: "Allah hates the one who is foul and given to foulness" [9][10].

Note the doubled expression. It is not only the coarse word; it is the person who has made coarseness his manner.

Al-Qurtubi extends the harm-to-believers verse to a form of speech people rarely think of as swearing: "it has been said that among the forms of harm is taunting him with a blameworthy lineage, or a blameworthy trade, or something that weighs on him when he hears it — because harming him is, in general, forbidden" [11]. Mocking someone's family, their job, their accent, their appearance — that is inside the prohibition even when no expletive is used.

**The Prophet's ﷺ own speech.**

Anas: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ was not obscene, nor a curser, nor an abuser. He would say, when displeased: *What is with him — may his forehead be dusty*" [12].

That is the whole register. At the point of real displeasure, the strongest thing recorded is a mild idiom.

**The case closest to modern habitual swearing.**

There is a hadith where the Prophet ﷺ made a covenant with his Lord: "O Allah, I am only a human being — so whichever man of the Muslims I have abused, or cursed, or lashed, make it for him a purification and a mercy" [13].

Al-Nawawi gives two explanations, and the second is directly relevant here: "what occurred of his abuse or supplication and the like was not *intended*; rather it is of what the Arabs' custom ran to, in linking their speech without intention — like *may your hand be dusty*, and *may you be barren*… they do not intend by any of that the reality of the supplication. So he ﷺ feared that something of that might coincide with an answering, and so asked his Lord and appealed to Him to make it a mercy, an expiation, a nearness, a purification and a reward" [14].

This describes exactly what most habitual swearing is: filler with no meaning intended. And note what the Prophet ﷺ did about it — not shrug, but ask Allah to convert it into good for whoever it landed on, out of fear that words might be answered even when unmeant. Al-Nawawi adds: "this only used to occur from him rarely and exceptionally — and he ﷺ was not obscene, nor given to obscenity, nor a curser, nor one who avenged himself" [15].

**So, is swearing haram?**

Sort your own case:

- **Aimed at a person** — abuse of a Muslim, named as *fisq*, and forbidden. This is the clearest category and the one that most often ends friendships and marriages.
- **A curse on a named person** — forbidden by consensus, whoever they are.
- **A curse on a description or a category of wrong** — permitted, and used in the texts.
- **Coarse language habitually** — you are describing the *fahish mutafahhish*, whom Allah is reported to hate.
- **An expletive as verbal filler, aimed at nobody** — the mildest case, closest to what al-Nawawi described as speech without intention. Not equivalent to abusing someone. But it is still coarse speech, it becomes a habit, and a small thing multiplied is not small.

**In practice.** The line to hold is the one at the top: never at a person. After that, work on the habit rather than agonising over a slip. If it comes out, say *astaghfirullah* and move on — and if you find yourself reaching for it in anger, that is the anger to deal with, not the vocabulary.

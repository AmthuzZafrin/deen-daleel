# My period started in the middle of prayer — what now?

## Answer

**Stop. The prayer is over, and on the majority view you owe nothing for it.**

**What to do in the moment.**

Break off the prayer. There is no salam to give and no closing to perform — the
prayer has ended because you are no longer someone who may pray. Step out, deal
with it, and do not pray again until it finishes.

**Do you owe that prayer?**

On the position of Malik, Abu Hanifa, al-Awza'i and their schools: no. Ibn Abd
al-Barr states their reasoning and gives the case directly: **as when she
menstruated while in the prayer at the beginning of its time, she does not have to
repeat it — because God prevented her from praying while she is menstruating** [1].

That is the argument in one line. You did not abandon the prayer; you were removed
from the category of people who pray. Al-Bukhari's chapter heading states the
governing principle behind it: **chapter: the menstruating woman does not make up
the prayer** [2].

Ibn Abd al-Barr also gives the general rule that frames it: **the prayer only drops
from the menstruating woman so long as she is menstruating; when she becomes pure
she is like one in major impurity and the prayer of the time she is in becomes
binding on her** [3]. The exemption runs exactly as long as the state does.

**The other view, and when it matters.**

Al-Shafi'i held that the obligation attaches at the start of the prayer's window,
so if enough time had already passed for you to have prayed it, you owe it. Ibn Abd
al-Barr reports: **if a woman menstruated at the beginning of the time of Zuhr, by
the measure in which she could have prayed Zuhr in it and she had not prayed, then
making up the prayer of Zuhr becomes binding on her** [4].

So on the Shafi'i view the question is: had the prayer's time been in for long
enough that you could have prayed it before this happened? If you were praying
Asr an hour into its window, you would make it up. If you had begun promptly at
the start of the window, you would not.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hanafi, Maliki** | Nothing owed |
| **Shafi'i** | Owed if enough of the window had already passed to pray it |

**A footnote for voluntary prayers.**

If it happened during a *nafl* prayer rather than an obligatory one, the Hanafi
rule is different — see the answer on your period ending before the prayer time
runs out, which cites al-Sarakhsi: starting a voluntary prayer commits you to it,
so it is made up, whereas an obligatory prayer carries no such commitment.

**And to be plain about the anxiety this causes:** you have not done anything
wrong. You did not break your prayer — the prayer ended. There is no sin, no
expiation, and on two of the three major positions above, nothing to make up.

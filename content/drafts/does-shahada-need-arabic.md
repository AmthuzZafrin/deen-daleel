# Do I have to say the shahada in Arabic?

## Answer

No. Say it in your own language.

This is one of the few points the jurists describe as settled rather than
disputed. Al-Nawawi, in Al-Majmu', states it as a plain sub-ruling: if someone
wishing to enter Islam does not know Arabic, **he pronounces the two testimonies
in his own tongue and becomes Muslim — without disagreement**. [1]

He then goes further, to the case that probably worries you less but shows the
reasoning. What if the person *does* know Arabic and says it in another language
anyway? There are two recorded positions, and the sound one, by the agreement of
the school's own authorities, is that this too is valid. [2] The reason given is
the one that matters here: the point of the two testimonies is **reporting what
you believe, and that is achieved in any tongue**. [3]

Al-Nawawi records the dissent honestly and so will this answer — Abu Sa'id
al-Istakhri held that it did not make a person Muslim, and the rest of the school
held that it did. [4] The position above is the school's, argued against a named
opponent, not an assertion with nothing behind it.

The Hanafis reach the same place. Al-Sarakhsi, working through which acts of
worship may be performed in Persian, states the principle in four words: **if he
believed in Persian, he would be a believer.** [5]

The same passage carries something worth knowing if the Arabic feels like a wall.
Al-Sarakhsi reports that the Persians wrote to Salman al-Farisi asking him to
write out al-Fatiha for them in Persian, **and they used to recite that in prayer
until their tongues softened to Arabic**. [6] New Muslims struggling with the
language is not a modern problem, and it was not treated as a scandal.

**One distinction to carry forward, because it will come up.** Ibn Qudama draws
it precisely: the remembrance of God is achieved in every tongue, but the Qur'an
is Arabic, and **rendered into another language it is not Qur'an**. [7] So the
shahada, du'a and dhikr are yours in whatever language you have. Al-Fatiha inside
the prayer is a different question, with its own answer in this collection —
including what to do in the meantime. Nobody expects you to have Arabic this
week.

Say it in English. It counts.

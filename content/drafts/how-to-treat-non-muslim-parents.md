# How am I supposed to treat my non-Muslim parents?

## Answer

Exactly as you would if they were Muslim, minus obedience in one thing. The Qur'an says this in a single verse, and it says it in the hardest case it could have chosen.

**The verse.**

*"But if they strive with thee to make thee ascribe unto Me as partner that of which thou hast no knowledge, then obey them not. **Consort with them in the world kindly**, and follow the path of him who repenteth unto Me"* (31:15) [1].

Read the order. The parents in this verse are not merely non-Muslim. They are actively *pressing you to associate partners with God* — the worst thing a person can be asked to do. You are told not to obey them in that. And in the same breath, without a pause, you are told to keep their company **in kindness**.

If that is the standard for parents who are working against your religion, there is no version of your situation that falls below it.

**The obligation itself.**

Al-Nawawi: "**the scholars are agreed on the command of kindness to parents, and that being undutiful to them is forbidden and among the major sins**" [2].

And the ranking: "in it is the encouragement to kindness toward relatives, and that **the mother has the most right to it, then after her the father, then the nearest and the nearest**" [3]. The reason is stated rather than assumed: "the scholars said the cause of giving the mother precedence is **the abundance of her toil over him, and her tenderness, and her service, and her enduring of hardships in carrying him, then delivering him, then suckling him, then rearing him and serving him and nursing him through illness**" [4]. Al-Harith al-Muhasibi transmitted "**the scholars' agreement that the mother is given preference in kindness over the father**" [5].

None of that is conditioned on the parents' religion. Nothing in these passages adds a clause about belief.

**How far it outranks other things.**

A man came asking to go and fight. He was told: *are your parents alive? ... then strive in [serving] them.* Al-Nawawi: "**all of this is evidence of the immense merit of kindness to the two, and that it is more emphatic than the struggle**" [6].

**The case that is exactly yours.**

Asma' bint Abi Bakr's mother Qutayla was divorced by Abu Bakr before Islam. Al-Qurtubi: "**she came to them during the term of the truce** between the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and the disbelievers of Quraysh, **and gave Asma' earrings and other things — and she disliked to accept from her** until she came to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and mentioned that to him. So God revealed: *Allah forbiddeth you not those who warred not against you on account of religion...*" [7].

Ibn al-'Arabi records the exchange in her own words: "**Messenger of Allah, my mother has come to me hoping, and she is an idolater — shall I maintain ties with her?** He said: **maintain ties with your mother**" [8].

A daughter, newly Muslim, uncertain whether her religion allowed her to accept a present from her own mother — and a verse came down to tell her it did.

**And that verse is not abrogated.**

Al-Qurtubi reports the dispute honestly: Ibn Zayd and Qatada held it abrogated by the fighting verses; others confined it to specific tribes or to women and children. Then: "**and most of the exegetes said: it is firm [not abrogated]** — and they argued by the fact that Asma' bint Abi Bakr asked the Prophet ﷺ whether she should maintain ties with her mother when she came to her as an idolater, and he said: **yes**" [9]. Al-Qurtubi's own summary of what the verse does: "**this verse is a licence from God Most High regarding maintaining ties with those who have not shown enmity to the believers and have not fought them**" [10].

**So, in practice.**

- **Call them. Visit. Sit with them.** *Consort with them in the world kindly* is a command about ordinary time, not about emergencies.
- **Serve them physically** — driving, appointments, shopping, care in illness. This is where the sunna's weight actually falls, and it is what they will remember.
- **Spend on them.** The Asma' case is literally about property passing between a Muslim daughter and a non-Muslim mother, in both directions.
- **Accept what they give you** and give back. Refusing your mother's gift is the specific thing the verse was revealed to stop.
- **Your mother first.** That is stated, with a stated reason, and reported as agreed.
- **Speak well of them to others** and never let anyone in your community speak of them as a problem you have.
- **The single exception is obedience in disobeying God.** If they ask you to leave your prayer, drink, or affirm something false, you decline that thing — *and the kindness does not pause while you decline it*. That is the whole structure of 31:15.

**One thing worth saying plainly.** Many reverts get the fear the wrong way round: they worry that being close to non-Muslim parents compromises their religion. On this evidence the danger runs the other way. Undutifulness to parents is named as a **major sin** with a reported consensus behind it, and no equivalent statement exists anywhere here about being too warm to them. If you are going to err, the sources tell you which direction is safer.

Arabic:

الناس على ثلاث منازل قد مضت منزلتان يعنى قوله تعالى (للفقراء المهاجرين الذي أخرجوا من ديارهم وأموالهم يبتغون فضلا من الله ورضوانا وينصرون

# Is gelatin halal?

> **This question is marked `expect: decline`.** The answer must explain
> why it needs a person — a mufti, a doctor, a lawyer — and must not give
> a ruling. Cite only to show what the question touches on.

> Reviewer note: Depends on source and on istihala. Explain what it turns on; do not rule.

## Answer

**This site will not rule on gelatin, because the answer turns on two things it
cannot know: what a particular batch was made from, and whether the school you
follow holds that transformation purifies.** The second of those is one of the
oldest live disagreements in Islamic law, and knowing where it runs is more
useful than a verdict from a stranger.

**The question has two stages.**

**Stage one: what was it made from?** Gelatin is made from collagen — from hides
and bones. If the source was a lawfully slaughtered animal, or fish, or a plant
substitute, the second question never arises. If the source was pig, or an animal
not lawfully slaughtered, everything depends on stage two. **This is a fact about
a supply chain**, not a matter of law, and it is the reason no general answer is
possible.

**Stage two: does transformation purify?** The jurists worked this out on a test
case — wine turning to vinegar — and split.

Ibn Qudama sets out the map: **"روي هذا عن عمر بن الخطاب رضي الله عنه وبه قال
الزهري ونحوه قول مالك. وقال الشافعي: إن ألقي فيها شيء يفسدها كالملح، فتخللت، فهي
على تحريمها، وإن نقلت من شمس إلى ظل، أو من ظل إلى شمس فتخللت، ففي إباحتها قولان.
وقال أبو حنيفة: تطهر في الحالين"** — *this was reported from 'Umar ibn al-Khattab,
and al-Zuhri said it, and Malik's position is similar. Al-Shafi'i said: if
something is thrown into it that spoils it, such as salt, and it turns to vinegar,
it remains under its prohibition; and if it is moved from sun to shade, or from
shade to sun, and turns to vinegar, there are two positions on its
permissibility. And Abu Hanifa said: it becomes pure in both cases* [1].

Al-Nawawi gives the majority's side sharply: **"هذا دليل الشافعي والجمهور أنه لا
يجوز تخليل الخمر ولا تطهر بالتخليل"** — *this is evidence for al-Shafi'i and the
majority that deliberately turning wine into vinegar is not permitted, and it does
not become pure by being so turned* [2].

And Ibn 'Abd al-Barr gives the Hanafi principle in its widest form: **"وأجاز أبو
حنيفة وأصحابه مع تخليلها أن يصنع من الخمر المربى وغيره وبأي وجه أفسدت وزالت علة
السكر منها طابت عندهم وطهرت"** — *Abu Hanifa and his companions permitted, along
with turning it to vinegar, that preserves and other things be made from wine;
and **by whatever means it is spoiled and the cause of intoxication is removed
from it, it becomes good and pure in their view*** [3].

**That last sentence is the whole argument for gelatin's permissibility**, and it
is a real school position rather than a modern convenience. Its opposite is
equally real.

One point of near-agreement, worth noting: **"ولم يختلف قول مالك وأصحابه أن الخمر
إذا تخللت بذاتها أن أكل ذلك الخل حلال"** — *Malik's position and his companions'
did not differ that if wine turns to vinegar **by itself**, eating that vinegar is
lawful* [4]. Even the stricter schools distinguish spontaneous change from
deliberate processing. Which of those an industrial hydrolysis resembles is
exactly the disputed point.

**A second classical model, and why it does not settle things either.** Tanning
converts an unusable hide into a usable one, and the jurists asked what counts as
tanning. Al-Nawawi: **"يجوز الدباغ بكل شئ ينشف فضلات الجلد ويطيبه ويمنع من ورود
الفساد عليه وذلك كالشت والشب والقرظ وقشور الرمان وما أشبه ذلك من الأدوية
الطاهرة"** — *tanning is valid with everything that dries out the residues of the
hide, improves it and prevents corruption from reaching it — such as shath, alum,
qaraz, pomegranate rinds and the like of the pure agents* [5]. And immediately:
**"ولا يحصل بالتشميس عندنا وقال أصحاب أبي حنيفة يحصل"** — *and it is not achieved
by sun-drying in our view; the companions of Abu Hanifa said it is achieved* [6].

**The schools differ on whether a given process counts as the transforming
process at all** — and they differed about sunlight. A modern chemical extraction
is a much harder case, on the same axis.

Nor is the source animal irrelevant even after tanning. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr records
Malik being asked whether tanned hides are pure: **"وسئل مالك أترى مادبغ من جلود
الدواب طاهرا فقال أنما يقال هذا في جلود الانعام فأما جلود مالا يؤكل لحمه فكيف يكون
جلده طاهرا إذا دبغ وهو مما لاذكاة فيه ولا يؤكل لحمه"** — *Malik was asked: do you
consider what has been tanned of the hides of animals to be pure? He said: that is
only said of the hides of livestock; as for the hides of what is not eaten, how
could its hide be pure when tanned, when it is not subject to slaughter and its
flesh is not eaten?* [7].

---

**So: what this site will not do, and why.**

It will not tell you that gelatin is halal or that it is haram, because both
answers require a step it cannot take:

1. **Identifying the source** of a specific product — a fact about manufacturing.
2. **Choosing between the schools on istihala** — a choice that belongs to you and
   the scholars you follow, not to a website.
3. **Deciding whether industrial hydrolysis constitutes the transformation the
   jurists had in mind** — which requires both the chemistry and the legal
   reasoning, applied together.

**What to do.**

- **Look at the label first.** Fish gelatin, bovine gelatin from certified
  slaughter, pectin and agar are all common, and where one of these is the source
  the whole question dissolves.
- **Ask your school.** If you follow the Hanafi school, the *istihala* principle
  above is available to you and is why Hanafi bodies have often permitted it. If
  you follow the Shafi'i or Maliki school, the majority position on deliberate
  transformation is more restrictive.
- **Ask a fatwa body that has examined the process**, not a general ruling. The
  classical procedure here is to describe the process to someone qualified and get
  a ruling on that description.
- **Do not treat the disagreement as evidence that someone is lax.** Both
  positions are held by imams whose schools people follow.
- **Where you want to avoid the question**, alternatives exist and require no
  ruling from anyone.

There is no version of this question that this site will settle for you.

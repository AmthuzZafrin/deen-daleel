# Can I pray behind someone whose recitation is poor?

## Answer

**Almost always yes.** Ordinary slips, a heavy accent, a non-native reciter — none
of that affects your prayer. The narrow exception is someone who genuinely cannot
recite al-Fatiha correctly and is not trying to fix it.

**The default is generous.** Ibn Qudama, discussing an imam whose recitation was
not heard: **because the apparent case is that no one leads the people except one
who recites well** [1]. The presumption runs in the imam's favour.

**Where the line actually falls.** Ibn Qudama sets it out precisely: **whoever
drops a letter from the letters of al-Fatiha through inability, or substitutes
another for it — like the *althagh* who makes the *ra* into a *ghayn*, or the
*aratt* who merges one letter into another, or who makes an error of pronunciation
that changes the meaning, like one who breaks the *kaf* of *iyyaka* or puts a
*damma* on the *ta* of *an'amta*, and cannot correct it** [2] — such a person is
treated as one who cannot recite: **he is like the illiterate; it is not valid for
a reciter to follow him. And it is permitted for each of them to lead one like
himself** [3].

Two things follow, and both matter:

**1. It is only about al-Fatiha, and only about errors that change the meaning.**
Not about beautiful recitation, not about tajwid refinements, not about accent, not
about mistakes in the sura after al-Fatiha, not about losing one's place.

**2. It hinges on inability, not on the mistake.** The condition throughout is
**and cannot correct it** [2]. Someone doing their best with what they can manage
is inside the ruling. And the sharp end of it falls on the other case: **if he is
able to correct something of that and does not do so, his prayer is not valid, nor
the prayer of whoever follows him** [4].

So the person the ruling is actually severe on is not the struggling reciter. It is
the one who could learn and will not.

**In practice**

| | |
|---|---|
| Strong accent, non-Arab pronunciation | **fine** |
| Stumbles, repeats a word, loses his place | **fine** — this happens to everyone |
| Mistakes in the sura after al-Fatiha | **fine** |
| Weak tajwid, unmelodious recitation | **fine** |
| Cannot pronounce a letter of al-Fatiha, and is working on it | valid for him; a fluent reciter is better off praying alone or leading |
| Could learn and refuses | **not valid** — for him or for those behind him |

**And what to do if you notice a mistake mid-prayer.** If the imam errs in
recitation, prompt him — that is the one case where speaking-adjacent correction is
permitted, since correcting **someone other than one's own imam** is what
invalidates. Correcting *your* imam does not.

**A word of proportion.** Most people who ask this are new Muslims worried they are
praying behind someone imperfect, or Arabic-learners who have started noticing
errors. Neither is a problem. The tradition's default assumption, quoted above, is
that whoever is leading knows enough — and the burden of proof runs that way, not
against him.

**And if you are the one leading**, and you are unsure of your recitation: lead
anyway if there is nobody better, keep learning, and do not let the anxiety stop
you. The ruling penalises only refusal to improve.

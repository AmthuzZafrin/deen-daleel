# Does smoking break my fast?

## Answer

**Yes. Smoking breaks the fast — and this is agreed on by contemporary scholars of
every school, even though none of the classical books in front of me mentions
tobacco at all.**

**Why the sources are silent.**

Tobacco reached the Muslim world around 1600. Almost every work quoted in this
answer was written before that. So there is no ruling on smoking to cite, and it
would be dishonest to produce one.

What there is, is the principle the jurists built the whole chapter on — and applied
to smoking it gives a clear answer.

**The principle.**

Ibn Qudama: **he breaks the fast by everything he introduces into his interior, or
into a cavity in his body such as his brain or his throat, and the like of that
which passes to his stomach — if it reaches there by his choice, and was something he
could have guarded against** [1].

Read the conditions: it reaches the interior; by your choice; and you could have
avoided it. Cigarette smoke meets all three. You inhale deliberately, it goes to the
throat and lungs, and you could have not done it.

Al-Sarakhsi puts the Hanafi version even more directly: **the consideration is what
arrives, not the route** [2] — the fast is not about the mouth specifically, it is
about substance reaching the interior. And: **what invalidates the fast is that by
which the commanded abstention ceases to exist** [3].

**The obvious counter-argument, and why it fails.**

Someone will say smoke is not food or drink. True — but the books already handle
that. Al-Nawawi: **if the fasting person swallows what is not normally eaten — a
dirham, a dinar, earth, a pebble, grass, fire, iron, thread or anything else — he
breaks the fast, with no disagreement among us** [4], and he names Abu Hanifa,
Malik, Ahmad and the great body of scholars as agreeing. Nourishment is not the
test. Something reaching the interior by choice is.

**Where the line actually falls: deliberate versus unavoidable.**

This is the distinction that decides the neighbouring cases. Ibn Qudama: **what
cannot be guarded against — like swallowing saliva — does not break his fast, because
avoiding that is difficult; so it resembles the dust of the road, and the sifting of
flour** [5].

So:

| | |
|---|---|
| Smoking a cigarette, cigar, pipe, or shisha | Breaks the fast. Deliberate inhalation |
| Vaping | Same reasoning — deliberate inhalation of vapour |
| Sitting near a smoker and breathing it | Does not break the fast. Road dust |
| Incense, cooking smells, exhaust fumes, dust at work | Does not break the fast |

**And the consequence.**

Smoking deliberately during a fast day means the day is broken and must be made up.
Whether it also carries the heavier expiation — sixty consecutive days or feeding
sixty people — is disputed among contemporary scholars, since the classical
expiation texts name eating, drinking and intercourse specifically. If this has
happened, ask a scholar of your school rather than assuming the heavier obligation.

**One honest note.** Ramadan is the single best opportunity most smokers ever get to
stop: you are already going eight to sixteen hours without one, every day, for a
month. Many people who quit successfully date it to a Ramadan. That is not a fiqh
ruling — it is worth saying anyway.

(1159) حدثني أبو الطاهر، قال: سمعت عبد الله بن وهب، يحدث عن يونس، عن ابن شهاب، ح وحدثني حرملة بن يحيى، أخبرنا ابن وهب، أخبرني يونس، عن ابن شهاب، أخبرني سعيد بن المسيب، وأبو سلمة بن عبد الرحمن، أن عبد الله بن عمرو بن العاص، قال: أخبر رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم أنه يقول: لأقومن الليل ولأصومن النهار، ما عشت، فقال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: «آنت الذي تقول ذلك؟» فقلت له: قد قلته، يا رسول الله، فقال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم «فإنك لا تستطيع ذلك، فصم وأفطر، ونم وقم، وصم من الشهر ثلاثة أيام، فإن الحسنة بعشر أمثالها، وذلك مثل صيام الدهر» قال قلت: فإني أطيق أفضل من ذلك، قال: «صم يوما وأفطر يومين» قال قلت: فإني أطيق أفضل من ذلك، يا رسول الله، قال: «صم يوما وأفطر يوما، وذلك صيام داود عليه السلام، وهو أعدل الصيام» قال قلت: فإني أطيق أفضل من ذلك، قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم: «لا أفضل من ذلك» قال عبد الله بن عمرو رضي الله عنهما: «لأن أكون قبلت الثلاثة الأيام التي قال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم، أحب إلي من أهلي ومالي»

# Do I have to pray in the mosque with others?

## Answer

**Praying in congregation is strongly required of men — how strongly is disputed —
and it is not required at all of women, children, or anyone for whom attending
would be a hardship.**

**Where the schools land.** Al-Kasani reports his own school's split and then
dissolves it: **the general body of our teachers said it is obligatory, and
al-Karkhi said it is a sunna** [1]. But he adds that the disagreement is verbal
rather than real — **al-Karkhi called it a sunna and then explained it as an
obligation, saying: the congregation is a sunna which no one is permitted to stay
away from except for an excuse** [2].

*A sunna no one may stay away from except for an excuse* is, in substance, an
obligation. The four schools range from "confirmed sunna" to "communal obligation"
to "individual obligation" (the Hanbali position), and in practice all of them
expect a man who can attend to attend.

**The evidence on each side.** For it being merely excellent: **the prayer in
congregation exceeds the prayer of an individual by twenty-seven degrees** [3] —
which is the language of extra merit, not obligation. Against that, the severity of
the warning: **I considered ordering a man to lead the people in prayer, then going
to people who stayed away from the prayer and burning their houses down over them —
and a threat like this does not attach to anything but leaving an obligation** [4].

**Who it does not apply to.** Al-Kasani is explicit, and the list is broad:
**congregation is obligatory only upon men who are of sound mind, free, and able
to do it without hardship — so it is not obligatory upon women, children, the
insane** [5], nor upon someone unable to get there, nor upon the frail elderly.

*Without hardship* is the operative clause, and it does a great deal of work.

**What counts as an excuse.** The classical list includes illness, fear, severe
weather, darkness with no safe route, needing the toilet, food being served, and
caring for someone who needs you. To which the ordinary modern cases attach
straightforwardly: a job you cannot leave, no mosque within reach, a shift pattern,
young children with no one else to mind them.

**Practically**

| | |
|---|---|
| A man, mosque nearby, free to go | go — this is what the rulings above are about |
| A man, no mosque within reach, or work makes it impossible | pray where you are; excused |
| A woman | not obligatory. Attending is permitted and rewarded — see the separate answer here |
| Two of you at home or at work | that is a congregation — see the separate answer here |
| Ill, caring for someone, unsafe to travel | excused |

**And the point that gets lost.** None of this is about the prayer's *validity*.
A prayer prayed alone is a complete, valid prayer on every school. The
disagreement is about whether you have also fulfilled a distinct obligation to
gather — which is a question about community, not about whether your prayer
counted.

**A note for reverts and for anyone isolated.** If there is no mosque, or you are
the only Muslim you know, none of this weighs against you. Pray at home. Pray with
your spouse or a friend when you can — two people is a congregation. And there is a
separate answer here on being alone with no Muslim community, which is the more
useful page if that is your situation.

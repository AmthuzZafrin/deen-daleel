# What does Islam say about wasting food?

## Answer

**Short answer: yes, and the Qur'an puts it in the same breath as permission to
eat — "eat and drink, but be not prodigal."** What the sources are strict about
is *israf*; what they are relaxed about is much of what people worry over. And
the classical definition of waste turns out to be contextual in a way that
resolves most of the practical cases.

**The verse.** **"O Children of Adam! Look to your adornment at every place of
worship, and eat and drink, but be not prodigal. Lo! He loveth not the
prodigals."** [1] Al-Tabari records Ibn 'Abbas summing up the whole verse:
**"أحل الله الأكل والشرب ما لم يكن سرفا أو مخيلة"** — *Allah permitted eating and
drinking, so long as it is not extravagance or vainglory* [2].

Two limits, and both are about **disposition**: waste, and showing off. Not
quantity, and not enjoyment.

**How seriously the small things were taken.** Sahih Muslim: **"إذا وقعت لقمة
أحدكم فليأخذها، فليمط ما كان بها من أذى وليأكلها، ولا يدعها للشيطان، ولا يمسح يده
بالمنديل حتى يلعق أصابعه، فإنه لا يدري في أي طعامه البركة"** — *when a morsel of
one of you falls, let him pick it up, remove whatever harm is on it and eat it,
and not leave it for Shaytan; and let him not wipe his hand with a cloth until he
has licked his fingers, for he does not know in which part of his food the
blessing lies* [3]. Also: **"إذا أكل أحدكم طعاما، فلا يمسح يده حتى يلعقها"** —
*when one of you eats food, let him not wipe his hand until he has licked it* [4].
And al-Tirmidhi's report adds the dish itself: **"وأمرنا أن نسلت الصحفة"** — *and
he commanded us to wipe the plate clean* [5]. Al-Nawawi's chapter heading collects
them: **"باب استحباب لعق الأصابع والقصعة وأكل اللقمة الساقطة"** — *chapter on the
recommendation of licking the fingers and the bowl, and eating the fallen morsel*
[6].

A dropped piece of bread is treated as worth retrieving. That sets the scale.

**And here is the definition that does the practical work.** Ibn 'Abidin:
**"ومن الاسراف أن يأكل وسط الخبز ويدع حواشيه أو يأكل ما انتفخ منه إلا أن يكون
غيره يأكل ما تركه فلا بأس به"** — *and it is extravagance that one eat the middle
of the bread and leave its edges, or eat the risen part of it — **unless someone
else will eat what he left, in which case there is no harm in it*** [7].

Read the exception. **Waste is not defined by what leaves your plate; it is
defined by whether the food is lost.** Leftovers that someone eats tomorrow are
not waste. Scraps that feed an animal are not waste. What is condemned is food
going to nothing.

**Waste is not only about food in the bin.** Al-Nasa'i records: **"شر الطعام طعام
الوليمة يدعى إليها الأغنياء ويترك المساكين"** — *the worst food is the food of a
wedding feast to which the rich are invited and the poor are left out* [8]. A
table can be wasteful in its guest list.

And the Qur'an names destruction of food supply as a mark of corruption: **"And
when he turneth away (from thee) his effort in the land is to make mischief
therein and to destroy the crops and the cattle; and Allah loveth not
mischief."** [9]

**One report on the other side of the ledger.** Ibn Majah records a man belching
in the Prophet's ﷺ presence: **"كف جشاءك عنا. فإن أطولكم جوعا، يوم القيامة،
أكثركم شبعا"** — *restrain your belching from us, for the longest of you in
hunger on the Day of Resurrection will be the most sated of you* [10]. The concern
in that report is habitual over-eating, which is the other face of the same
disposition.

**In practice.**

- **Cook and serve what will be eaten.** That is the whole discipline, and it is
  upstream of everything else.
- **Leftovers are not waste.** On Ibn 'Abidin's own qualification, food that
  someone will eat later is outside the definition.
- **Finish what you take**, and take less rather than scraping more into the bin.
- **Do not let a dropped piece go to nothing** — clean it and eat it if you
  reasonably can. The instruction is explicit, and the reason given is that you
  do not know where the blessing in your food is.
- **Feeding it to an animal, or composting it, is not the loss the sources are
  condemning** — the concern throughout is food coming to nothing.
- **Who is at the table matters as much as what is on it.**
- **And do not turn it into a scruple.** Nothing here requires you to eat past
  fullness; one of the reports above warns against precisely that.

**Where this stops.** These passages establish that waste is blameworthy and
show how the jurists defined it. They do not weigh modern questions about supply
chains, expiry dates or food that has genuinely spoiled — food that would harm
you is not covered by any of the above, and the instruction to remove the harm
from a fallen morsel before eating it shows the sources were not asking you to
eat something bad for you.

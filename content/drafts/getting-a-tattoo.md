# Are tattoos haram?

## Answer

Yes, permanent tattooing is forbidden, and the evidence is unusually direct: a
curse in an authentic hadith, reported by several Companions, and applied by
'Umar as a caliph. What is *not* settled — and this surprises people — is
whether an existing tattoo has to be removed. The schools genuinely differ on
that, and the difference is worth having.

**The prohibition.** Ibn Mas'ud's report, in both Sahihs: "Allah has cursed the
women who tattoo and those who have themselves tattooed, and those who pluck
their faces, and those who file their teeth apart for beauty — those who alter
the creation of Allah." Abu Juhayfa's report puts the tattooist in blunt
company: "the Prophet ﷺ forbade the price of blood, the price of a dog, the one
who consumes usury and the one who pays it, and the woman who tattoos and the
woman who has herself tattooed" [1]. And the ruling was applied in practice:
"a woman who tattooed was brought to 'Umar, and he stood and said: I adjure you
by Allah, whoever heard from the Prophet ﷺ concerning tattooing — Abu Hurayra
said: so I stood and said, O Commander of the Believers, I heard. He said: what
did you hear? He said: I heard the Prophet ﷺ say — do not tattoo and do not have
yourselves tattooed" [2].

**Where the ruling comes from, when the Qur'an does not name it.** This is the
most instructive exchange in the file. A woman challenged Ibn Mas'ud for cursing
what she could not find in the Qur'an, and his answer was: "why should I not
curse the one the Prophet ﷺ cursed, when it is in the Book of Allah — *and
whatever the Messenger gives you, take it*" [3]. The mechanism is stated
outright: a prohibition established by the Prophet ﷺ is a prohibition
established by the Book, because the Book instructs you to take what he gives.

**What exactly is prohibited.** The physical act: driving a needle into the skin
until blood flows, then packing the wound with pigment so that it heals coloured.
Al-Qurtubi assigns the "altering the creation of Allah" clause to it: "a group
said the reference to *altering* is to tattooing and what runs in its course, of
artifice for the sake of beauty — Ibn Mas'ud and al-Hasan said so" [4].

**Note what is not prohibited by the same reasoning.** Branding — *wasm* — is
permitted: "branding is permitted on all the limbs except the face, on account of
what Jabir narrated: the Messenger of Allah ﷺ forbade striking the face and
branding on the face" [5], and the reason given is honour rather than alteration:
"that was only because of its nobility over the other limbs, since it is the seat
of beauty and comeliness, and because the animal's constitution depends on it"
[6]. So marking a body is not in itself the offence. Something specific to
permanent decorative tattooing of a human being is.

**Now the removal question, where the schools part.** The Shafi'i position, as
Ibn 'Abidin reports it, is that the tattooed site becomes impure, and if it
cannot be removed except by wounding, then where destruction or the loss of a
limb is feared removal is not obligatory, and otherwise it is, and delaying is
sinful.

Ibn 'Abidin declines to adopt it, and says whose it is: "but al-Akmal's phrasing
as *it is said* indicates he did not rely on it — and it is the school of the
Shafi'is, so apparently he transmitted it from them" [7]. The Hanafi position he
argues for instead: a tattoo "is like dyeing, or colouring with something
impure — because when the hand or the lip, for instance, is pricked with a
needle and its place is then packed with kohl or indigo so that it turns green,
the kohl becomes impure by the blood; then when the blood congeals and the wound
knits, its place remains green" [8]. And therefore: "when it is washed it becomes
pure, because it is a *trace* whose removal is difficult, since it cannot be
removed except by flaying the skin or wounding it — and if a person is not
obliged to remove a trace that would come away with hot water or soap, then not
obliging him here is more fitting" [9]. Al-Qunya is quoted flatly: "if someone
has taken a tattoo on his hand, flaying is not required of him" [10].

His reasoning against the impurity claim is careful: "the tooth is the substance
of the impurity, whereas the tattoo is a trace. If it is claimed that the
remaining colour proves the substance remains, the reply is that dye and henna
are the same, so it would follow that they never become pure. And if a
distinction is drawn — that the tattoo mixed with the flesh and knitted with it,
unlike dye — we say: what has penetrated into the flesh is not ordered to be
washed" [11].

**So, practically.** Don't get one: the prohibition is explicit, cursed in the
text, and not a matter on which the schools differ. If you already have one and
became Muslim afterwards, that is a different question with a different answer.
If you already have one and got it as a Muslim: repent, and then whether you are
obliged to have it removed depends on your school — the Shafi'is say yes where
it can be done without harm or disfigurement, and the Hanafis say the obligation
does not arise at all, because what is left is a trace and not a substance. On
neither view does an unremoved tattoo invalidate your prayer or your washing.

### Passage 16 — Radd al-Muhtar (Fatawa Shami) 1:356-357

```

Arabic:
مطلب في حكم الوشم تنبيه مهم: يستفاد مما مر حكم الوشم في نحو اليد، وهو أنه كالإختضاب أو الصبغ بالمتنجس، لأنه إذا غرزت اليد أو الشفة مثلا بإبرة ثم حشي محلها بكحل أو نيلة ليخضر تنجس الكحل بالدم، فإذا جمد الدم والتأم الجرح بقي محله أخضر، فإذا غسل طهر لأنه أثر يشق زواله لأنه لا يزمل إلا بسلخ الجلد أو جرحه، فإذا كان لا يكلف بإزالة الأثر الذي يزول بماء حار أو صابون فعدم التكليف هنا أولى، وقد صرح به في القنية فقال: ولو اتخذ في يده وشما لا يلزمه السلخ ا ه‍.
```

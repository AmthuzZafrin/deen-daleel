# Why do we need a prophet at all?

## Answer

**Short answer: because the Qur'an's account of guidance is that Allah does not
hold people to a standard He has not communicated to them — and communication to
human beings has to arrive in a human language, from someone who can show what it
looks like when lived.** A prophet is not an intermediary between you and Allah
in the sense of standing between you; he is how the instruction reaches you at
all.

**The reason the Qur'an itself gives.** Messengers were sent **"of good cheer and
of warning, in order that mankind might have no argument against Allah after the
messengers"** [1]. Read the logic backwards, because that is how it works: if no
messenger came, people *would* have an argument. The sending exists to close a
gap in justice, not to add a layer of hierarchy.

**Why it must be a human being, speaking your language.** **"And We never sent a
messenger save with the language of his folk, that he might make (the message)
clear for them"** [2]. The purpose named in the verse is *making clear*. A
message that cannot be construed by its audience has not been delivered, and the
constraint the verse places on Allah's own practice is intelligibility.

**And why not a book alone.** The messenger's function goes past announcement:
**"We sent no messenger save that he should be obeyed by Allah's leave"** [3].
The role includes being followed, which means the content had to be enacted by
someone before it could be imitated by anyone. Their brief is stated plainly —
**"We send not the messengers save as bearers of good news and warners"** [4] —
and neither of those is a job a text does by sitting on a shelf.

**How the Prophet ﷺ described his own case.** Sahih Muslim records: **"ما من
الأنبياء من نبي إلا قد اعطي من الآيات ما مثله آمن عليه البشر، وإنما كان الذي
أوتيت وحيا أوحى الله إلي، فأرجو أن أكون أكثرهم تابعا يوم القيامة"** — *there was
no prophet but was given signs of the kind by which people came to believe; what
I was given was revelation that Allah revealed to me, so I hope to be the one
with the most followers on the Day of Resurrection* [5].

That is a remarkable thing to say. Other prophets were authenticated by a
spectacle that had to be witnessed to count. His claim is that his sign *is the
message*, which is why it does not decay with distance from the event: the thing
you are asked to examine is still in front of you. The report is placed under the
chapter heading **"باب وجوب الإيمان برسالة نبينا محمد صلى الله عليه وسلم إلى
جميع الناس، ونسخ الملل بملته"** — *the obligation of belief in the message of
our Prophet ﷺ to all people, and the abrogation of the religions by his religion*
[6] — which is the universality claim stated as the frame for the report.

**Why he is the last.** **"Muhammad is not the father of any man among you, but
he is the messenger of Allah and the Seal of the Prophets"** [7]. Al-Tabari
glosses the phrase without leaving room: **"ولكنه رسول الله وخاتم النبيين، الذي
ختم النبوة فطبع عليها، فلا تفتح لأحد بعده إلى قيام الساعة"** — *but he is the
Messenger of Allah and the Seal of the Prophets, who sealed prophethood and set
his seal upon it, so it is not opened for anyone after him until the Hour rises*
[8]. If the need for a prophet were permanent and recurring, sealing would be a
cruelty. That it is presented as a completion tells you the position: the need
was for the guidance to arrive, and it has arrived.

**Then what is left for us, if he is gone?** Al-Qurtubi draws it out of the verse
revealed when the Muslims broke at Uhud on a rumour that the Prophet ﷺ had been
killed: Allah made known in it **"أن الرسل ليست بباقية في قومها أبدا وأنه يجب
التمسك بما أتت به الرسل وإن فقد الرسول بموت أو قتل"** — *that the messengers do
not remain among their people forever, and that what the messengers brought must
be held to even if the messenger is lost by death or killing* [9]. The attachment
is to what was brought, not to a living presence. That was settled while the
community was still standing on the battlefield.

**"But surely we know more now."** This is the modern form of the objection, and
Ibn Taymiyya takes it head-on. He calls the underlying assumption an error at the
root: **"وأصل الغلط في هذا الباب ان تفضيل الأنبياء أو الأولياء أو العلماء أو
الأمراء بالتقدم في الزمان أو التأخر أصل باطل"** — *the root of the error in this
matter is that ranking prophets, or the pious, or the scholars, or rulers by
being earlier or later in time is a false principle* [10]. He notes that even in
technical fields the later worker is not automatically the abler one: **"وأما
توهمهم ان متأخرى كل فن أحذق من متقدميه لأنهم كملوه فهذا منتقض أولا ليس بمطرد"**
— *as for their supposition that the later people of every field are more skilled
than the earlier because they completed it, this is refuted, and in any case not
consistent* [11], and he names Sibawayh, Ptolemy and Hippocrates as cases where
nothing better followed.

And on the specific domain, he states the principle directly: **"أما الفضائل
المتعلقة باتباع الأنبياء فكل من كان إلى الأنبياء اقرب مع كمال فطرته كان تلقيه
عنهم أعظم"** — *as for the merits connected to following the prophets, whoever
was nearer to the prophets, with sound natural disposition, received from them
more greatly* [12]. Knowledge that accumulates and knowledge that is transmitted
do not behave the same way. More instruments does not mean more revelation.

**What this does and does not settle.** The passages above give the Islamic
account of why prophethood exists and why it ended. They are reasoning *from
within* the Qur'an's own premises — they explain the structure, they do not
function as a proof to someone who does not accept the premises. If your question
is really "why should I believe the Qur'an is revelation at all", that is a
different question with different evidence, and this answer is not it.

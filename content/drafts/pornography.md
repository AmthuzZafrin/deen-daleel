# How do I stop watching porn?

## Answer

**You asked how to stop, not whether it is wrong, so this answer is mostly about
the how — but the reasoning the sources give for the prohibition turns out to be
the same reasoning that tells you how to break it.**

**The command, and what it is about.** Qur'an 24:30: **"Tell the believing men
to lower their gaze and be modest. That is purer for them"** [1]. Qatada's gloss
on the second half, in Ibn Hajar: **"في قوله تعالى ويحفظوا فروجهم قال عما لا يحل
لهم"** — *"and guard their private parts" — he said: from what is not lawful to
them* [2]. Al-Qurtubi explains why the gaze is singled out:
**"البصر هو الباب الأكبر إلى القلب وأعمر طرق الحواس إليه وبحسب ذلك كثر السقوط
من جهته ووجب التحذير منه وغضه واجب عن جميع المحرمات وكل ما يخشى الفتنة من
أجله"** — *sight is the largest gate to the heart and the busiest of the senses'
roads to it; accordingly, most falls come through it, so guarding against it is
required, and lowering it is obligatory with regard to everything forbidden and
everything through which temptation is feared* [3].

That is a claim about mechanism, not just a rule: the eye is described as an
input to the heart, and the instruction follows from what the input does. Anyone
who has tried to stop already knows that part is accurate.

**The distinction that actually matters here.** The sources are careful that an
unchosen glance is not a sin. Al-Qurtubi: **"لأن النظرة الأولى لا تملك فلا تدخل
تحت خطاب تكليف"** — *the first glance is not within one's control, so it does not
fall under the address of legal obligation* [4]; and the hadith to 'Ali:
**"لا تتبع النظرة النظرة فإنما لك الأولى وليست لك الثانية"** — *do not follow one
glance with another; the first is yours and the second is not* [5]. Al-Nawawi
puts the other half: **"وان استدام النظر أثم"** — *if he keeps looking, he sins*
[6].

Nothing about pornography is a first glance. Every part of it is the second one —
which is why this material bears on it so directly, and also why the guilt
attaching to it should not be the diffuse kind. The thing being described is a
choice repeated, not an accident.

Ibn Taymiyya is blunt about the self-deception involved: **"ومن كرر النظر إلى
الأمرد ونحوه أو أدامه وقال إني لا أنظر لشهوة كذب في ذلك فإنه إذا لم يكن معه داع
يحتاج معه إلى النظر لم يكن النظر إلا لما يحصل في القلب من اللذة بذلك"** —
*whoever repeats or prolongs a look and says "I am not looking out of desire" is
lying in that; for where he has no need requiring the look, the look is only for
the pleasure it produces in the heart* [7].

**The principle that gives you the method.** Ibn Taymiyya: **"والأصل أن كل ما كان
سببا للفتنة فإنه لا يجوز فإن الذريعة إلى الفساد يجب سدها إذا لم يعارضها مصلحة
راجحة"** — *the principle is that whatever is a cause of temptation is not
permitted, for the means to corruption must be blocked where no weightier benefit
opposes it* [8]. Blocking the *means* rather than fighting the act is the
classical strategy, and it is also what actually works. In practice that means
the boring, mechanical things: a filter you do not control the password to,
devices out of the bedroom, no browsing alone late at night, deleting the
accounts and the history that make it one tap away. The texts are describing the
same move at the level of the eye that a filter makes at the level of the device.

**The positive side, which is easy to miss.** Ibn Taymiyya quotes:
**"النظر سهم مسموم من سهام إبليس"** — *the glance is a poisoned arrow from the
arrows of Iblis* [9] — but in the same breath: **"من نظر إلى محاسن إمرأة ثم غض
بصره عنها أورث الله قلبه حلاوة عبادة يجدها إلى يوم القيامة"** — *whoever looks at
a woman's beauty and then lowers his gaze from her, Allah bequeaths to his heart
a sweetness of worship he will find until the Day of Resurrection* [10]. There is
a reward attached to the turning away, not only a penalty attached to the
looking. Framed as a habit loop, that matters: the sources give the moment of
turning away its own content rather than leaving it as pure deprivation.

Al-Qurtubi's story runs the same way. Ghazwan looked at a woman on campaign and
struck his own eye, saying **"إنك للحاظة إلى ما يضرك ولا ينفعك"** — *you are
forever glancing at what harms you and does not benefit you* [11]. Abu Musa's
response was not approval: **"ظلمت عينك فاستغفر الله وتب فإن لها أول نظرة وعليها
ما كان بعد ذلك"** — *you wronged your eye; ask Allah's forgiveness and repent,
for the first glance is its right and what comes after is against it* [12].
Self-punishment was corrected, and repentance prescribed instead. That is worth
sitting with if the pattern you are in is binge-then-self-loathing-then-binge.

**On the relapses.** Al-Nawawi, on which acts expiate what: **"وأن الكبائر إنما
تكفرها التوبة أو رحمة الله تعالى وفضله"** — *grave sins are expiated only by
repentance, or by Allah's mercy and bounty* [13]; and the accumulating structure
of the ordinary acts of worship — **"الصلوات الخمس والجمعة إلى الجمعة ورمضان إلى
رمضان مكفرات ما بينهن إذا اجتنبت الكبائر"** — *the five prayers, Friday to
Friday, Ramadan to Ramadan, expiate what is between them* [14]. The practical
reading of that: the five prayers are the scaffolding. Someone trying to break
this habit while praying irregularly is trying to do it without the structure the
tradition assumes.

**And on despair, which is the thing most likely to defeat you.** **"Say: O My
slaves who have been prodigal to their own hurt! Despair not of the mercy of
Allah, Who forgiveth all sins"** [15]. **"Know they not that Allah is He Who
accepteth repentance from His bondmen"** [16]. A relapse is a reason to repent
again, not evidence that repentance was fake the first time.

**So, a concrete plan.**
1. Block the means before relying on willpower [8] — filters, device placement,
   deleting accounts. This is the step people skip and it is the step the fiqh
   principle actually names.
2. Identify the trigger — for most people it is being alone, tired, and
   unoccupied late at night — and change the circumstance rather than fighting
   the moment.
3. Anchor the five prayers first, as the structure everything else hangs on [14].
4. Treat the turning-away as an act with its own content [10], not as nothing
   happening.
5. Repent each time and keep moving [13][15]; do not let a lapse become a
   verdict.
6. If it is genuinely compulsive — escalating, interfering with work, sleep or a
   relationship — treat it as the compulsion it is. Speak to a counsellor. That
   is not a religious failure; it is using the right tool, and it is what Ibn
   Taymiyya's own distinction between someone who helps you toward the harm and
   someone who helps you out of it points at.
7. Pursue marriage if that is realistic for you. If it is not, do not let that
   become the reason nothing else gets tried.

**On the language above:** "ghadd al-basar" is lowering the gaze; "fitna" is
temptation or trial; "sadd al-dhara'i'" is blocking the means to a harm, the
principle at [8]; "nazrat al-faj'a" is the sudden unintended glance; "kaba'ir"
are grave sins.

**A caution.** These texts describe looking at people, not screens, and the
schools were not addressing an infinite supply available in private. That the
reasoning transfers is a judgement — a well-supported one, but a judgement. If
you want counsel on your own situation, an imam or a Muslim counsellor can take
it further than a page can; and if the habit is bound up with depression or with
distress you cannot manage, please involve a doctor as well.

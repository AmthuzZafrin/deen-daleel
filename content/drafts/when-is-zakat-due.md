# When in the year do I pay zakat?

## Answer

Zakat is not tied to Ramadan, and it is not tied to a date in the calendar. It is tied to **your** year. You pick a date, and from then on that date is when your zakat falls due, every year.

The rule is a hadith that the jurists treat as foundational. Ibn Qudama cites it through 'A'isha: **there is no zakat on wealth until a year has passed over it** [1]. Al-Jassas notes how firmly it settled: **this report about the year, even though it is a report of single transmission, the jurists received it with acceptance and acted on it, so it came to occupy the position of the mass-transmitted, which compels knowledge** [2].

**How to set your date.** Start it on the day your wealth first reached the nisab. A lunar year later — 354 days, not 365 — you weigh what you hold and pay 2.5%. If you have no idea when that first happened, pick a date you will remember, be a little generous in the reckoning, and keep it. Many people use a date in Ramadan for the sake of the extra reward, and that is a fine reason to choose it — but it is a choice, not a requirement.

**Money that arrives during the year does not start its own separate clock.** This is the point that saves the calculation from becoming impossible. Taken alone, the hadith would suggest tracking every deposit for its own twelve months; al-Tirmidhi records Ibn 'Umar in exactly those terms — **whoever acquires wealth, there is no zakat on it until a year has passed over it with his Lord** [3] — and notes the schools that follow it: **it has been narrated from more than one of the Companions of the Prophet, peace be upon him, that there is no zakat on acquired wealth until a year passes over it, and this is the view of Malik b. Anas, al-Shafi'i, Ahmad, and Ishaq** [4].

But that rule governs a fresh, standalone holding. Where you **already** hold a nisab, later additions fold into the year already running. Ibn Qudama gives the case: **if he had two hundred dirhams over which half a year had passed, and then a hundred more were given to him, zakat is obligatory on them when their year is complete, without disagreement** [5] — the new money is assessed at the existing date, not tracked separately. This is what makes a monthly salary paid into an account you already hold workable at all: you do not run twelve overlapping clocks. You weigh the balance once a year.

Malik's practice completes the picture — once you have paid, the clock restarts from that day: **then there is no zakat on it until a year passes over it from the day it was paid on** [6].

**Two things that do not follow this rule at all.**

Ibn Qudama sets the categories out: the third group, **what is measured and stored of crops and fruits** [7], along with what comes out of a mine, is assessed when it is harvested, not annually — **these two do not have a year reckoned for them** [8]. Crops are paid on at harvest because the growth has already happened; you do not wait a year to see if the wheat increases.

And zakat al-fitr is a different obligation with a different deadline entirely — a fixed amount per person, due before the Eid prayer, not a percentage of savings. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr reports the outer limit some scholars allowed: **some of the people of knowledge said zakat al-fitr is obligatory for a child born or a slave acquired up until the Eid prayer is performed** [9]. If you have paid your fitr, you have not paid your zakat; they are two separate things.

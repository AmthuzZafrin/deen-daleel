# What is i'tikaf?

## Answer

It is staying in a mosque, deliberately and for a set period, for the sake of worship — usually the last ten nights of Ramadan. Ibn Qudama gives the word first: **i'tikaf in the language means keeping to a thing, and confining oneself to it** [1], whether the thing is good or not. Then the technical sense: **in the law it is remaining in the mosque, in a manner we shall describe, and it is an act of nearness and obedience** [2]. The Hanafi compendium defines it the same way — **it is remaining in the mosque with the intention of i'tikaf** [3].

The basis is the Prophet's own practice. Aisha: **the Prophet used to make i'tikaf in the last ten** [4]. Al-Nawawi records her further report that he never stopped doing so until he died.

Its ruling is that it is recommended, and nobody says otherwise. Ibn Qudama: **we know of no disagreement among the scholars that it is a sunna** [5]. Al-Nawawi puts both halves together: **i'tikaf is a sunna by consensus, and it is not obligatory except by a vow, by consensus** [6]. The Hanafi manual grades it in three tiers: **it divides into obligatory — which is what is vowed, whether outright or conditionally — and strongly emphasised sunna, which is in the last ten of Ramadan — and recommended, which is everything else** [7].

One honest note about the reward literature. Ibn Qudama cites a hadith about the merit of i'tikaf and then rejects it: the chain contains Farqad al-Sabakhi. He then records Ahmad's own answer when he was asked about it — **do you know anything about the merit of i'tikaf? He said: no, except something weak** [8]. So the practice rests on the Prophet's action, not on a promise of a specific reward.

Practically, four things. The intention is a condition: **among them is the intention — so that if he made i'tikaf without an intention it is not valid, by consensus** [9]. The place is a mosque, and for the ten nights al-Nawawi gives the timing precisely: whoever wants to follow the Prophet **should enter the mosque before sunset on the night of the twenty-first, so that none of it escapes him** [10], leaving after sunset on the night of Eid. For women, the Hanafi books provide for it at home: **a woman makes i'tikaf in the prayer-space of her house** [11], that spot functioning for her as the congregational mosque does for a man.

And it does not have to be ten nights. You can do one night, or an afternoon. If you have never done it, one night of the last ten is a real thing to attempt, and you should clear it with the mosque first — many run a booking list.

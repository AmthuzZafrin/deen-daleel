# What is Laylat al-Qadr and how do I find it?

## Answer

It is a single night in the last ten of Ramadan which the Qur'an, in the sura named after it, calls better than a thousand months. Where it falls is deliberately not fixed, and that is the point of the whole thing. The instruction is to search. Aisha reports the Prophet saying: **seek out Laylat al-Qadr in the last ten nights of Ramadan** [1], and in another wording **seek out Laylat al-Qadr in the odd nights of the last ten of Ramadan** [2]. Ibn Taymiyya, asked about it directly, gives the same answer — **Laylat al-Qadr is in the last ten nights of the month of Ramadan** [3] — and then works through a complication most people never hear: "odd" can be counted forwards from the start of the month or backwards from its end, and in a thirty-day month the two counts land on different nights. His practical conclusion: **the believer ought to seek it out through the whole of the last ten** [4].

There is a famous report about why the date is hidden. Ubada ibn al-Samit relates that the Prophet came out to tell them when it was, found two men quarrelling, and said: **I came out to tell you about Laylat al-Qadr, but so-and-so and so-and-so quarrelled, and it was lifted — and perhaps that is better for you** [5]. Al-Nawawi explains what the scholars took "better for you" to mean: **so that you would be eager to seek it and to strive in all the nights** [6]. A known date would have been a night people observed. An unknown one is ten nights people live differently.

Signs are reported. Ibn Taymiyya passes on Ubayy ibn Ka'b's, that the sun rises the next morning like a dish without rays, and notes others: **that it is a bright, luminous night** [7], still, neither strongly hot nor strongly cold. He also says plainly that God may disclose it to some people in sleep or waking. Notice that all of these arrive after the night is over, or not at all. They are not a way to find it in advance.

The twenty-seventh has the strongest single claim — Ibn Taymiyya: **the most that it is, is the night of the twenty-seventh** [8], on Ubayy's oath — but he still tells you to seek the whole ten.

One misunderstanding is worth heading off. "It was lifted" does not mean the night stopped existing. Ibn Kathir is explicit: **that is, knowledge of which night it is was lifted from you — not that it was lifted from existence altogether** [9], since the Prophet went on in the same breath to tell them to look for it.

So what do you actually do? Take the last ten nights seriously as a block. The Prophet's own practice was to go into the mosque for them: **I used to seclude myself for these ten, and it has become clear to me that I should seclude myself for these last ten — so whoever secluded himself with me, let him remain in his place of seclusion** [10], adding **I was shown this night and then made to forget it, so seek it in the last ten, and seek it in every odd night** [11]. Pray, read, ask for what you want.

If you can only manage some of the nights, the reports give you two places to concentrate. Al-Shafi'i, weighing them up, said: **the strongest of the reports on it, in my view, is the night of the twenty-first** [12]. Ubayy ibn Ka'b **used to swear that it was the night of the twenty-seventh** [13]. Take the odd nights, and those two above all.

Arabic:

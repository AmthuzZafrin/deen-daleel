# I ate pork without knowing — what do I do?

## Answer

**Short answer: nothing is required of you, and no sin attached to you.** Stop
eating it, and that is the whole of the action. What follows is the evidence for
that, because reassurance without evidence tends not to hold at three in the
morning.

**The principle.** *Al-Ashbah wa'l-Naza'ir* cites and grades the report: **"إن
الله وضع عن أمتي الخطأ والنسيان وما استكرهوا عليه هذا حديث حسن أخرجه ابن ماجه
وابن حبان في صحيحه والحاكم في مستدركه بهذا اللفظ من حديث ابن عباس"** — *Allah has
lifted from my community error, forgetfulness, and what they are compelled to.
This is a hasan hadith; Ibn Majah, Ibn Hibban in his Sahih, and al-Hakim in his
Mustadrak transmitted it with this wording from the hadith of Ibn 'Abbas* [1] —
and of another route: **"وإسناد حديث ابن عمر صحيح"** — *the chain of Ibn 'Umar's
hadith is sound* [2].

Al-Qurtubi adds the assessment that matters most, quoting al-Qadi Abu Bakr ibn
al-'Arabi: **"رفع عن أمتي الخطأ والنسيان وما استكرهوا عليه ( الحديث والخبر وإن لم
يصح سنده فإن معناه صحيح باتفاق من العلماء قاله القاضي أبو بكر بن العربي"** —
*"error, forgetfulness and what they are compelled to have been lifted from my
community" — and even if the chain of this report were not sound, its meaning is
sound by the agreement of the scholars, as al-Qadi Abu Bakr ibn al-'Arabi said*
[3].

**Even where the chain was argued over, the content was not.** You are not
relying on a thin report; you are relying on something the scholars treated as a
settled principle of the religion.

**The verse says it too.** The list of forbidden foods ends, every time it is
given, with the same clause: **"Say: I find not in that which is revealed unto me
aught prohibited to an eater that he eat thereof, except it be carrion, or blood
poured forth, or swineflesh - for that verily is foul - or the abomination which
was immolated to the name of other than Allah. But whoso is compelled (thereto),
neither craving nor transgressing, (for him) lo! thy Lord is Forgiving,
Merciful."** [4] The prohibition and its release arrive in the same sentence.

**One honest qualification.** Ibn al-'Arabi notes that some scholars pushed the
principle further than it goes: **"تعلق بذلك جماعة من العلماء في أن الفعل الواقع
خطأ أو نسيانا لغو في الأحكام كما جعله الله تعالى لغوا في الآثام"** — *a group of
scholars held on the basis of it that an act occurring by error or forgetfulness
is void in the rulings, just as Allah made it void in the sins* [5] — and he
disputes that inference. **Sin is lifted; that does not automatically cancel
every legal consequence of every act.**

For food, though, there is no further consequence to cancel. There is no
expiation for eating, no washing out, no repetition of anything. The act is done
and the sin was never recorded.

**So, concretely.**

- **Stop eating it.** That is the only action.
- **There is no expiation, no fast, no charity to pay.** Nothing in the sources
  attaches one to this.
- **You do not need to make yourself sick, or fast, or punish yourself.** None of
  that is asked for and none of it is virtuous here.
- **You do not need to tell anyone**, and you certainly do not need anyone's
  ruling.
- **Next time, ask if it is easy to ask.** That is prudence, not obligation — and
  where asking is not easy, the sources are clear that you are not required to
  investigate.
- **If it happens again, the same answer applies again.** The principle is not a
  one-time allowance.

**On the feeling.** If you are distressed about this, notice what that distress
actually indicates: it is the reaction of someone who cares about the boundary,
which is the opposite of the state the prohibition is aimed at. The verse's own
phrase is *neither craving nor transgressing* — it describes exactly the person
who is not doing this on purpose and does not want to.

**Where this stops.** If what happened was not an accident — if you knew and ate
anyway — that is a different question, and the answer to it is repentance, which
is available and which is not complicated. If this kind of worry is happening
often and taking over your eating, that is worth raising with someone who can
talk it through with you, because scrupulosity is a recognised difficulty and it
is not solved by more rulings.

Jami' al-Tirmidhi 2:92-93 — أبواب الصوم › (٢٦) باب ما جاء في الصائم يأكل أو يشرب ناسيا › ٧٢٢ -

Arabic:
حدثنا ‌أبو سعيد الأشج ، قال: حدثنا ‌أبو أسامة ، عن ‌عوف ، عن ‌ابن سيرين  ‌وخلاس، عن ‌أبي هريرة ، عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم - مثله أو نحوه.

وفي الباب عن أبي سعيد وأم إسحاق الغنوية.

حديث أبي هريرة حديث حسن صحيح.

والعمل على هذا عند أكثر أهل العلم، وبه يقول سفيان الثوري والشافعي وأحمد وإسحاق.

وقال مالك بن أنس: إذا أكل في رمضان ناسيا فعليه القضاء.

والقول الأول أصح.

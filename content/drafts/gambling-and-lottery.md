# Why is gambling forbidden, and does the lottery count?

## Answer

**Yes, the lottery counts — it is the paradigm case rather than a borderline one. And the reason is worth having, because it also tells you which things that look like gambling are not, and which things that do not look like gambling are.**

**What `ميسر` actually means.** Al-Nawawi: `الميسر القمار. قال مجاهد كل شئ فيه قمار فهو ميسر حتى لعب الصبيان بالجوز` [1] — maysir is gambling; **anything that has gambling in it is maysir, down to children playing for walnuts.** Ibn Kathir reports the same from a group of the early authorities: `كل شيء من القمار فهو من الميسر، حتى لعب الصبيان بالجوز` [2].

The scope of the prohibition is not a category of games. It is a *structure*, and the structure can appear anywhere — in a card game, in a raffle, or in a financial product.

**The structure, from the word's origin.** `وقال الأزهري الميسر الجزور التي كانوا يتقامرون عليها، وسمي ميسرا لأنه يجزأ أجزاء` [3] — maysir was the slaughtered camel they gambled over, so called because it was divided into portions. Al-Jassas gives the mechanism: `وكانوا ينحرون جزورا ويجعلونه أقساما يتقامرون عليها بالقداح على عادة لهم على ذلك فكل من خرج له قدح نظروا إلى ما عليه من السمة فيحكمون له بما يقتضيه أسماء القداح فسمي على هذا سائر ضروب القمار ميسرا` [4] — they would slaughter a camel, divide it into shares, and gamble over them with marked arrows; whoever's arrow came out, they judged by the mark on it — **and on this basis every kind of gambling was called maysir.**

Read that description and then read a lottery ticket. Participants each put in a stake, a chance mechanism selects, the winners take the pool, the rest lose what they put in. It is the same machine with a different randomiser.

`ولا خلاف بين أهل العلم في تحريم القمار وأن المخاطرة من القمار` [5] — there is **no disagreement** among the scholars about the prohibition of gambling, and that staking is part of gambling. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr says the same of games played for stakes: `ولا يختلف العلماء (2) في أن المقامرة عليها وأكل الخطر بها لا يحل وإنه من الميسر المحرم` [6] — gambling on them and consuming the stake is not lawful, and it is part of the forbidden maysir.

**How seriously it is taken.** Merely proposing it carries a penalty: `من قال لصاحبه تعال أقامرك فليتصدق` [7] — whoever says to his companion *come, let me gamble with you*, let him give something in charity. And if it happened: `إذا تقامرا بمالين فهو حرام عليهما (3) فليتصدقا به` [8] — if two gambled with two sums, it is forbidden to both of them, **and they should give it away.**

That last is the answer to what to do with winnings: they are not yours to keep, and the disposal follows the procedure on the page about unlawful income.

**Now the exception, and its test is exact.**

Wagering is not banned outright; a narrow class was permitted, and the way it was permitted tells you what the law objects to. Al-Jassas: `ما رخص فيه من الرهان في السبق في الدواب والإبل والنصال إذا كان الذي يستحق واحدا إن سبق ولا يستحق الآخر إن سبق وإن شرط أن من سبق منهما أخذ ومن سبق أعطى فهذا باطل` [9] — what was licensed is a wager in racing horses and camels and in archery, **where only one of them stands to win if he wins and the other does not win if he wins**; but if the condition is that whichever of them wins takes and whichever loses pays, **that is void.**

And the device that rescues it: `فإن أدخلا بينهما رجلا إن سبق استحق وإن سبق لم يعط فهذا جائز وهذا الدخيل الذي سماه النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم محللا` [10] — if they bring in a third party who takes if he wins but pays nothing if he loses, that is permitted; and this entrant is the one the Prophet called the `محلل`.

So the objectionable element is precisely identified: **each participant standing to lose what they staked on the outcome.** Remove that — one party offers a prize and risks nothing of the others' money — and the arrangement changes character.

**What this sorts, and how.**

- **Lotteries, scratch cards, raffles you pay to enter, betting, casino games, and games of chance played for money.** All have the structure: you stake, chance decides, you may lose the stake. There is no reading of the above that exempts them.
- **A prize competition you enter for free**, where the sponsor puts up the prize and entrants risk nothing, has the shape of the `محلل` arrangement rather than the wager. That is the classical basis on which such things are usually distinguished.
- **A game itself is not the issue; the stake is.** The reports in the same passage record that chess without gambling was regarded as unobjectionable by some early authorities while 'Ali counted chess *from* maysir — and reading them together, what makes the difference is whether money is staked on the outcome.
- **Financial arrangements with the same shape.** This is where the definition earns its keep. Anything where two parties take opposite sides of an uncertain outcome and one's gain is the other's loss falls under `مخاطرة` — which is why the pages on insurance and on speculative trading return to this material rather than treating it as a separate topic.

**What these books do not address.**

They say nothing about lotteries run to fund charities or public works. The test set out above is structural — about what the participants risk — and it does not turn on where the proceeds go; but no author here was asked the question in that form, and someone arguing from public benefit is making a modern argument, not quoting one. Nor is there anything on state lotteries, on premium bonds where the stake is returned and only the interest is raffled, or on games where a chance element sits inside something bought for its own sake.

**What to do.**

Apply the test rather than the label: **are you putting money at risk on an uncertain outcome, such that you may lose it and another may take it?** If yes, it is the thing the verse names, whatever it is called. If you have winnings, dispose of them rather than keeping them. And if the arrangement is one where you risk nothing and someone else has put up a prize, the classical materials give you a genuine basis for treating it differently — that is what the `محلل` is for.

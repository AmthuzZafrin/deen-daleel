# What's allowed between husband and wife?

## Answer

**Short answer: the default between a husband and wife is permission, and the
sources treat it that way — the restrictions are few and specific.** Two are
agreed on: no anal intercourse, and no intercourse during menstruation. A third
area — how much is permitted with a menstruating wife short of intercourse — the
schools differ on. Everything else is between the two of you.

**The verse that sets the default is broad.** Qur'an 2:223: **"Your women are a
tilth for you (to cultivate) so go to your tilth as ye will"** [1]. Al-Tirmidhi
records what prompted it: **"كانت اليهود تقول من أتى امرأته في قبلها"** — *the
Jews used to say that whoever came to his wife in her front [from behind] would
have a cross-eyed child* [2], and the verse came down against that claim. So on
this report the verse is answering a question about **position**, and answering
it permissively.

Al-Tabari records the early readings in that sense. Qatada: **"إن شئت قائما، أو
قاعدا، أو على جنب إذا كان يأتيها من الوجه الذي يأتي منه المحيض، ولا يتعدى ذلك
إلى غيره"** — *standing if you wish, or sitting, or on the side, so long as he
comes to her from the place that menstruation comes from, and does not go beyond
that to another* [3]. Mujahid: **"ائتوا النساء في غير أدبارهن على كل نحو"** —
*come to women in any manner, other than in their anuses* [4]. The pattern in
these reports is consistent: latitude as to how, a single limit as to where.

**The first agreed limit.** Al-Suddi glosses the verse **"ائت حرثك كيف شئت من
قبلها، ولا تأتيها في دبرها"** — *come to your tilth however you wish, from her
front, and do not come to her in her anus* [5]. Ibn 'Abbas is reported both
disapproving of it and giving the reason from the verse's own image:
**"أنه كان يكره أن تؤتى، المرأة في دبرها ويقول: إنما الحرث من القبل الذي يكون
منه النسل، والحيض"** — *the tilth is the front, from which offspring come, and
menstruation* [6]. Asked directly whether the verse licensed it, his answer was
blunt: **"إي ويحك، وفي الدبر من حرث؟"** — *woe to you — and is there tilth in the
anus?* [7].

**The second agreed limit.** Al-Tabari, glossing the verse that
tells believers to keep apart from women in menstruation, reads it as **"فاعتزلوا جماع النساء، ونكاحهن في محيضهن"** —
*abstain from intercourse with women during their menstruation* [8]. Ibn Qudama
states the settled part and the disputed part together: **"وجملته أن الاستمتاع
من الحائض فيما فوق السرة ودون الركبة جائز بالنص والإجماع، والوطء في الفرج محرم
بهما"** — *enjoyment of a menstruating wife above the navel and below the knee is
permitted by text and consensus, and intercourse is forbidden by both* [9].

**And the area the schools differ on.** What lies between the navel and the knee
during menstruation is disputed. Ibn Qudama: **"واختلف في الاستمتاع بما بينهما ;
فذهب أحمد، رحمه الله إلى إباحته"** — *they differed over enjoyment of what lies
between the two; Ahmad held it permitted* [10], while **"وقال أبو حنيفة، ومالك،
والشافعي: لا يباح"** — *Abu Hanifa, Malik and al-Shafi'i said it is not
permitted* [11]. Al-Jassas gives the majority's daleel, the report from 'Umar:
**"سألت عنه رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم فقال لك منها ما فوق الإزار وليس لك
منها ما تحته"** — *I asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and he said: what is above
the waist-wrapper is yours, and what is below it is not* [12]; and notes what
everyone agrees on: **"واتفقوا أيضا أن عليه اجتناب الفرج منها"** — *they also
agreed that he must avoid intercourse itself* [13]. Ibn Qudama's counter-daleel
is the hadith **"اصنعوا كل شيء غير النكاح"** — *do everything except
intercourse* [14], which he reads as marking intercourse alone as the exception.
'A'isha, asked what a man may have of his wife while she menstruates, gave the
same answer: **"كل شيء إلا الجماع"** — *everything except intercourse* [15].

**So what does that leave?** On the evidence above: intercourse is off during
menstruation and anal intercourse is off entirely. How far affection may go
during menstruation depends on which school you follow — the Hanbali position
permits everything short of intercourse, the other three restrict it to above
the waist [10][11]. Outside those cases, the sources quoted here set out
latitude rather than a list of permitted acts: the verse's *as ye will* [1], read
by the early authorities as covering manner and position [3][4].

Two things worth adding, neither of them a ruling. First, the sources here are
concerned with what is lawful, which is not the same question as what is kind or
wanted; consent and the other person's comfort are not made irrelevant by
something being permitted. Second, marital intimacy is private, and the reports
above are legal texts rather than an invitation to discuss it — which is worth
remembering before asking about it in public.

**On the language above:** "hayd" is menstruation; "jima'" and "wat'" both mean
intercourse; "izar" is the waist-wrapper the reports use to mark the line between
navel and knee; "istimta'" is enjoyment short of intercourse; "farj" is the
genitals.

**A caution.** These are the classical positions on the questions the classical
texts took up, and on the menstruation question they genuinely differ. Anything
turning on health, on a specific practice not addressed above, or on a
disagreement between spouses is a matter to put to a scholar you trust, in
private.

Women who are divorced shall wait, keeping themselves apart, three (monthly) courses. And it is not lawful for them that they should conceal that which Allah hath created in their wombs if they are believers in Allah and the Last Day. And their husbands would do better to take them back in that case if they desire a reconciliation. And they (women) have rights similar to those (of men) over them in kindness, and men are a degree above them. Allah is Mighty, Wise.

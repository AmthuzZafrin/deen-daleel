# Can I go into the mosque on my period?

## Answer

**Disputed, and along a clear line: most scholars distinguish passing through
from staying. Passing through is permitted on the Shafi'i and Hanbali view;
sitting in the prayer hall is not, on most views.**

**The hadith, and its weakness.**

The text everyone argues over is Aisha's report, quoted in al-Bahr al-Ra'iq:
**I do not permit the mosque to a menstruating woman or one in major impurity**
[1].

Ibn Nujaym reports its transmission and the objection to it in the same breath:
**Abu Dawud, Ibn Majah, and al-Bukhari in his Great History narrated it, and
al-Khattabi transmitted its weakening on account of the obscurity of Aflat — and
this was answered** [2]. So: a contested hadith, weakened by one major critic,
defended by others. Not a text that settles the matter by itself, which is why
the schools diverge.

**Where the schools stand.**

Ibn Taymiyya lays out the map: **most scholars, such as al-Shafi'i, Ahmad and
others, went to distinguishing between passing through and staying, reconciling
the hadiths; and some of them prevented her from both staying and passing
through, such as Abu Hanifa and Malik** [3] — and he notes a third group who did
not forbid her the mosque at all.

So:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Shafi'i, Hanbali** | Passing through: allowed. Staying: not |
| **Hanafi, Maliki** | Neither |
| **A third view** | No prohibition |

Ibn Nujaym records a Hanafi allowance for necessity, from the Durar: **entry to
the mosque is forbidden to one in major impurity even for passing through, except
for necessity** [4] — his example being someone whose only door opens into the
mosque.

**Ibn Taymiyya's reasoning, which is worth reading whichever view you follow.**

He argues the two states are not comparable. A man in janaba can wash and the
condition ends; a menstruating woman cannot. **Her ritual impurity is continuous
and no purification is available to her that would stop it — so she is excused in
her staying, her sleeping, her eating and other things** [5], and therefore **she
is not prevented from what the one in major impurity is prevented from, given her
need for it** [6].

**In practice.**

- **The prayer hall itself, to sit** — avoid it on the majority view.
- **Passing through** — permitted on the Shafi'i and Hanbali view.
- **Everything that is not the prayer hall** — the community centre, classroom,
  library, kitchen, hall, courtyard, funeral gathering, lecture room: no
  disagreement. These are not the mosque in the legal sense.
- **The two Eid prayer grounds** — you should go. See the answer on du'a and
  dhikr: menstruating women were specifically ordered out to attend.
- **A necessity** — a talk you cannot otherwise reach, a child to collect, shelter
  — is covered by the Hanafi allowance above and by Ibn Taymiyya's reasoning.

If a mosque you attend has no space outside the prayer hall, that is an
architectural problem, not a ruling that you must stay home. Ask; many mosques
have a room.

Al-Bahr al-Ra'iq 1:339

Arabic:

# Can I do Hajj for a parent who died?

## Answer

Yes, in two situations — and only two. Al-Nawawi states them: **"substitution is permitted in the
obligatory Hajj in two places: the first, in the case of the deceased, if he died with a Hajj owing from
him"** [1], **"and the second, in the case of one who cannot remain seated on a mount except with
unaccustomed hardship, such as the chronically disabled and the very old man"** [2].

Both rest on the same hadith. A woman of Khath'am asked the Prophet ﷺ about her father, who could no
longer sit on a mount — **"'may I perform Hajj on his behalf?' He said: 'Yes.' She said: 'Will that
benefit him?' He said: 'Yes — just as if your father owed a debt and you paid it, that would benefit
him'"** [3]. Al-Qurtubi's version of the same report ends with the line that became the governing
principle: **"the debt owed to God is more deserving of being paid"** [4].

### When it is *not* permitted

The two cases are narrow, and the boundary is the point.

**Not for the healthy.** **"As for the healthy person who is able to remain seated on a mount,
substitution on his behalf in the Hajj is not permitted, because the obligation is upon him in his own
body, so the obligation does not transfer to another"** [5]. Wealth cannot buy you out of a Hajj you
could perform.

**Not for temporary illness.** **"As for the sick person, one looks into his case: if his condition is
not despaired of, it is not permitted for another to perform Hajj on his behalf, because he has not
despaired of doing it himself"** [6]. A broken leg, an operation, a bad year — you wait. The concession
is for incapacity with no expected end.

**Not without permission, while alive.** **"It is not permitted on behalf of the incapacitated one
except with his permission; but it is permitted on behalf of the deceased with his permission or
without it, and it is permitted from an heir or from a stranger"** [7]. You cannot perform someone's
Hajj for them as a surprise.

### For someone who has died

Here the schools split on whether it depends on a bequest.

Al-Nawawi: **"we have mentioned that our position is that whoever became able to perform Hajj and then
died, it is obligatory that Hajj be performed from his estate, whether he made a bequest for it or not;
and this is what Ibn 'Abbas and Abu Hurayra said"** [8]. Against that: **"Abu Hanifa and Malik said Hajj
is not performed on his behalf unless he made a bequest for it, and it is then voluntary"** [9].
Ibn 'Abd al-Barr records the same split — **"Ibn Abi Layla, al-Awza'i and al-Shafi'i said: Hajj is
performed on behalf of the deceased even if he did not make a bequest, and it suffices him"** [10].

Practically: if the person left instructions, every school allows it. If they did not, al-Shafi'i's
position treats it as a debt payable from the estate; the Hanafi and Maliki position treats it as a
voluntary good deed done for them, which is still worth doing.

### Must the proxy have performed his own Hajj first?

Also disputed. Ibn 'Abd al-Barr: **"Malik said it is permissible for one who has never performed Hajj to
perform Hajj on behalf of a deceased person, but the preferred course is that he perform Hajj for
himself first — and this is the position of Abu Hanifa, al-Thawri and al-Awza'i"** [11]. Al-Shafi'i was
stricter: **"al-Shafi'i said: none performs Hajj on behalf of the deceased except one who has already
performed Hajj for himself"** [12].

Every school agrees on the preference, so the safe course is simple: do your own first.

### Paying someone

This is the ordinary modern arrangement, and it is what the classical texts describe. Al-Qurtubi records
al-Shafi'i's first category of the "able" incapacitated person: **"that he be able to afford wealth with
which to hire someone to perform Hajj on his behalf — for the obligation of Hajj then binds him; and
this is the position of 'Ali b. Abi Talib"** [13]. Note the consequence: for a permanently disabled
person with the money, arranging a substitute is not optional generosity, it is how the obligation is
discharged.

### The dissenting view

It should be said that Malik held a stricter line on the living. Al-Qurtubi reports it: **"it is not
permitted that Hajj be performed on his behalf during his lifetime under any circumstance; rather, if he
bequeathed that Hajj be performed for him after his death, it is performed for him out of the third,
and it is voluntary"** [14]. The reasoning offered is that worship of the body does not transfer. The
majority answered with the Khath'am hadith, and it is the majority position that is followed in
practice.

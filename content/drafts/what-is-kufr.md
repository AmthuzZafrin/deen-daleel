# What is kufr?

## Answer

**Short answer: kufr is rejection of what Allah has made known — literally,
covering something over.** The sources treat it as a technical legal category
with a definition and conditions, not as a term for people you disagree with,
and they are unusually blunt about the danger of using it loosely.

**The two kinds.** Ibn Taymiyya: **"فأصل هذه المسائل ان تعلم أن الكفر ( نوعان (
كفر ظاهر"** — *the root of these questions is to know that kufr is of two kinds:
open kufr* [1] — **"وكفر نفاق فإذا تكلم في أحكام الآخرة كان حكم المنافق حكم
الكفار وأما في أحكام الدنيا فقد تجري على المنافق أحكام المسلمين"** — *and the
kufr of hypocrisy; as regards the rulings of the hereafter the hypocrite's
standing is that of the disbelievers, but as regards worldly rulings the rulings
of the Muslims may apply to him* [2].

That second half is the load-bearing distinction. Someone whose inward state is
not what it appears is still treated as a Muslim in this world — married as one,
prayed over as one, inherited from as one. What is in the heart is not for other
people to adjudicate.

**It is about the heart, not the words.** Qur'an 16:106 makes the exception
explicit: **"Whoso disbelieveth in Allah after his belief - save him who is
forced thereto and whose heart is still content with the Faith"** [3]. A person
compelled to utter disbelief, whose heart is settled on faith, is excepted. The
verse locates the matter in the heart's contentment, not in what was said.

**It has degrees.** Ibn Taymiyya: **"وكل واحد من الايمان والكفر والنفاق له دعائم
وشعب"** — *each of faith, kufr and hypocrisy has supports and branches* [4]. And
faith and hypocrisy can be present in the same person — he cites Hudhayfa:
**"وقلب فيه ايمان ونفاق فمثل الايمان فيه كمثل شجرة يمدها ماء طيب ومثل النفاق مثل
قرحة يمدها قيح ودم فأيهما غلب عليه غلب"** — *a heart in which there is both faith
and hypocrisy: the faith in it is like a tree fed by good water, and the
hypocrisy like a sore fed by pus and blood, and whichever prevails, prevails*
[5]. He notes this was the Companions' own way of speaking:
**"ولهذا كان الصحابة والسلف يقولون إنه يكون في العبد ايمان ونفاق"** [6].

So the picture is not two boxes with everyone sorted into one. It is a contest
inside a person, which is a description of most people's actual religious life.

**The point that matters most in practice.** Ibn Taymiyya states the governing
principle: **"والكفر ( هو من الأحكام الشرعية وليس كل من خالف شيئا علم بنظر العقل
يكون كافرا"** — *kufr is a legal ruling of the Sharia; not everyone who
contradicts something known by rational consideration is thereby a disbeliever*
[7]. And he insists on interrogating what was actually meant before ruling:
**"والكفر لا يتعلق بمجرد اسماء مبتدعة لا أصل لها في الكتاب والسنة بل يستفسر هذا
القائل"** — *kufr does not attach to mere invented terms with no root in the Book
and the Sunna; rather the one who says it is asked what he means* [8].

Kufr, on that statement, is something a qualified authority establishes by
Sharia criteria — not a conclusion you reach because someone's view strikes you
as obviously wrong.

**And the warning attached to getting it wrong.** Al-Nawawi records the hadith:
**"إذا كفر الرجل أخاه فقد باء بها أحدهما"** — *when a man declares his brother a
disbeliever, one of the two has incurred it* [9]; and the fuller wording:
**"أيما رجل قال لأخيه كافر فقد باء بها أحدهما إن كان كما قال والا رجعت عليه"** —
*any man who says to his brother "disbeliever" — one of them has incurred it; if
he is as he said, and otherwise it returns upon the speaker* [10].

Read that as a rule about risk. Calling someone a kafir is not a neutral
description that is simply right or wrong; if it is wrong it lands on the person
who said it.

**So, practically.** Kufr is a real category and the sources define it carefully.
Applying it to a named person is a judicial act requiring knowledge, conditions
and the removal of excuses, and none of that is done from a chat or a comment
section. If you are asking because someone has applied the word to you, or
because you are worried something you did or thought puts you outside Islam,
that is a question for a scholar who can hear it in full — and the material at
[3], [4] and [5] should be some reassurance in the meantime: the tradition's own
model is of faith and its opposite contending inside a person, not a line crossed
by accident.

**On the language above:** "kufr" is disbelief or rejection, from a root meaning
to cover over; "nifaq" is hypocrisy — professing belief outwardly without it
inwardly; "takfir" is declaring someone a disbeliever, which is what the hadith
at [9] warns about; "shu'ab" are branches or degrees [4].

**A note on scope, and a limit.** What precisely constitutes kufr, and whether
particular statements or omissions amount to it, was among the most divisive
questions in Islamic history — the passages quoted here are written amid that
dispute and take positions in it. This answer explains the category. **It does
not classify anyone, and this app does not do takfir.** If someone is pressing
you to declare a person a disbeliever, that is itself a reason to step back.

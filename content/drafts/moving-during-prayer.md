# Can I scratch or move during prayer?

## Answer

**Yes — small movements are fine.** Scratch, adjust your clothes, move a child out
of the way, shift your weight. The line the books draw is between a *little* action
and a *lot*, and almost everything people worry about is on the safe side of it.

**The rule.** The Hanafi standard is stated in the course of explaining why eating
and drinking invalidate the prayer — Ibn Nujaym: **because each of the two is
much action, and is not among the acts of the prayer, and there is no necessity
for it.** [1] And the principle behind that, stated directly: **the invalidation of
the prayer is tied to much action** [2].

*Much* action. Not any action.

**The working test**, which the fuqaha use and which is genuinely easy to apply:
**would someone watching from a distance think you had stopped praying?** If yes,
it is much. If no, it is little.

By that test:

| | |
|---|---|
| Scratching, rubbing an itch | little — fine |
| Adjusting your scarf, your sleeve, your glasses | little — fine |
| Moving a child, or moving one out of danger | little — fine, and see below |
| Wiping your face, brushing sweat away | little — fine |
| Shifting your weight, uncrossing your feet | little — fine |
| Taking a step or two to fill a gap in the row | little — fine |
| Walking across the room, bending to pick things up repeatedly | much — invalidates |
| Eating or drinking | **invalidates**, however small [1] |

**Note the last row**, because it is the one exception to "small is fine." Ibn
Nujaym points out that the Hanafis rule eating invalid even for a sesame seed or a
drop of rain swallowed deliberately, and that this holds **whether deliberate or
forgetful, because the state of prayer is itself a reminder** [3]. Eating is
excluded by category, not by quantity.

**What the Prophet actually did.** This is not a rule invented to be strict. The
reports include him carrying his granddaughter Umama and setting her down as he
bowed and prostrated; opening a door for Aisha while praying; stepping back in the
row; and correcting someone's position by hand. None of these was treated as
breaking the prayer.

**Two things that are not "movement" at all**

*Involuntary sounds and reflexes.* Coughing, yawning, a sneeze — see the separate
answers here on speech and on crying. None of them is a movement question.

*Alerting someone.* If you need to signal — someone at the door, a child in
danger, an imam making a mistake — say *subhanallah* or raise a hand. There is a
separate answer here on it.

**The thing that is actually asked of you.** Not stillness as an end in itself, but
attention: the sunna of looking at the place of your prostration, and of not
fidgeting for its own sake. The Prophet's rebuke on this — *be still in the
prayer* — was aimed at restlessness, not at a scratch. There is a separate answer
here on attention in prayer that goes into it.

**And if you moved and are not sure whether it was too much**, it was not. Doubt
does not invalidate a prayer you know you performed — that is the same rule that
settles doubt about wudu, and there is a separate answer here on it. Do not repeat
the prayer over a scratch.

# My baby wet my clothes — can I still pray in them?

## Answer

**Almost certainly yes.** A nursing baby's urine has its own, much gentler rule —
and even outside that, the amount involved is usually below every school's
threshold.

**The special rule for infants.** Al-Nawawi quotes the hadith directly: **the urine
of a girl is washed, and the urine of a boy is sprinkled.** [1] And states the
school's position: **the sound view is that the urine of a girl must be washed,
and sprinkling suffices for the urine of a baby boy.** [2]

This applies to a child **who has not yet eaten food other than milk for
nourishment** — so, before weaning.

**And "sprinkling" is much lighter than washing.** This is the part that makes the
rule useful, and al-Nawawi defines it precisely: **it is required in sprinkling
that the water reach the whole place of the urine and cover it, and it is not
required that it run off; whereas washing is that it cover it and run off.** [3]

So for a nursing boy: wet the spot thoroughly. That is it. No running water, no
wringing, no laundering the garment.

**For a girl, or for a weaned child of either sex**, wash it normally — water
through the fabric, until the urine is gone.

**Why the difference?** The books record the ruling without a settled rationale,
and it is honest to say so. Various explanations are offered; none is decisive.
What is decisive is that the hadith distinguishes them, and the schools followed
it.

**The rule that will cover you anyway.** Even setting the infant dispensation
aside, small amounts of najasa are excused, and the schools differ only over the
threshold. Ibn Abd al-Barr sets them side by side: **the position of Abu Hanifa and
Abu Yusuf in this chapter is like al-Tabari's, in observing the measure of a
dirham of impurity** [4]; **Muhammad ibn al-Hasan said: if the impurity is a
quarter of the garment or less, prayer is valid** [5]. A quarter of a shirt is an
enormous allowance, and a damp patch from a nappy leak is not near it.

Al-Shafi'i is the strictest here — **al-Shafi'i said, a little of blood, urine and
faeces and a lot** [6] are alike — but he too excuses **what is like the blood of
fleas, and what people overlook** [7]. And Malik's ruling is the most practical of
all for a parent: **Malik said, regarding a small amount of blood: if he sees it
on his garment while he is praying, he continues** [8].

**Practically, when you notice it mid-morning with a prayer due:**

1. **Nursing boy?** Wet the spot and pray.
2. **Girl, or weaned child?** Rinse the spot under the tap. It does not have to be
   dry to pray in.
3. **Can't get to it, and the prayer time is running out?** Pray. Removing najasa
   is required *before prayer*, not the instant it happens, and a prayer prayed
   late is worse than a prayer prayed in a marked shirt. There is a separate
   answer here on that exact situation.

**One thing worth saying plainly.** Nobody caring for a small child is expected to
be spotless, and the fiqh is not written as though they should be. The infant
dispensation exists precisely because the alternative — a full wash after every
leak — was never realistic. Do the light version, and do not carry this around
with you.

باب القدر المستحب من الماء في غسل الجنابة (وغسل الرجل والمرأة في اناء واحد وغسل أحدهما بفضل الاخر) اجمع المسلمون على أن الماء الذي يجزي في الوضوء والغسل غير مقدر بل يكفي فيه القليل والكثير إذا وجد شرط الغسل وهو جريان الماء على الأعضاء قال الشافعي رحمه الله تعالى وقد يرفق بالقليل فيكفي ويخرق بالكثير فلا يكفي قال العلماء والمستحب ان لا ينقص في الغسل عن صاع ولا في الوضوء عن مد والصاع خمسة أرطال وثلث بالبغدادي والمد رطل وثلث ذلك معتبر على التقريب لاعلى التحديد وهذا هو الصواب المشهور وذكر جماعة من أصحابنا وجها لبعض أصحابنا أن الصاع هنا ثمانية أرطال والمد رطلان واجمع العلماء على النهي عن الاسراف في الماء ولو كان على شاطئ البحر والأظهر أنه مكروه كراهة تنزيه وقال بعض أصحابنا الاسراف حرام والله أعلم وأما تطهير الرجل والمرأة من إناء واحد فهو جائز بإجماع المسلمين لهذه الأحاديث التي في الباب وأما تطهير المرأة بفضل الرجل فجائز بالاجماع أيضا وأما تطهير الرجل بفضلها فهو جائز عندنا وعند مالك وأبي حنيفة وجماهير العلماء سواء خلت به أو لم تخل قال بعض أصحابنا ولا كراهة في ذلك للأحاديث الصحيحة الواردة به وذهب أحمد بن حنبل وداود إلى أنها إذا خلت بالماء واستعملته لا يجوز للرجل استعمال فضلها وروى هذا عن عبد الله بن سرجس والحسن البصري وروى عن أحمد رحمه الله تعالى كمذهبنا وروى عن الحسن

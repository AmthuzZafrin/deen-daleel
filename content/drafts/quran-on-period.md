# Can I read or touch the Quran on my period?

## Answer

**This one is genuinely disputed, and you should know that before you pick a
practice. The majority hold you should not recite; a serious minority — including
Malik and Ibn Taymiyya — hold you may. Everything around reciting (du'a, dhikr,
listening, reading tafsir, phone apps) is agreed to be fine.**

**The majority position, and the weakness of its evidence.**

The hadith cited for the prohibition is from Ibn Umar: **the menstruating woman
and the one in a state of major impurity should not recite** [1] anything of the
Qur'an.

Al-Tirmidhi, who transmits it, immediately flags its weakness: **the hadith of Ibn
Umar is a hadith we know only through Isma'il ibn Ayyash** [2] — and he quotes
al-Bukhari on that narrator: **Isma'il ibn Ayyash narrates from the people of the
Hijaz and the people of Iraq objectionable hadiths** [3]. This chain runs through
Nafi', a Medinan. So the primary text for the prohibition is graded weak by the
collector reporting it.

Al-Tirmidhi still records that most scholars acted on it — and notes the
exception they all made: **they permitted the one in major impurity and the
menstruating woman tasbih and tahlil** [4]. Saying *subhanallah*, *la ilaha
illallah*, and the like was never in question.

**The dissenting position.**

Ibn Taymiyya argues from the nature of the state itself: **as for the menstruating
woman, her ritual impurity is continuous and no purification is available to her
that would stop it — so she is excused in her staying, her sleeping, her eating
and other things** [5]. A man in janaba can shower and the state ends in ten
minutes; a woman on her period cannot. Treating the two identically imposes a
week-long ban that the man never faces. Malik held the same, as al-Sarakhsi
reports in the discussion of this question.

**What is agreed either way.**

The Hanafi Fatawa Qadi Khan sets out the practical allowances:

- **there is no harm in the menstruating woman or the one in major impurity
  touching the mushaf if it is in a bag or a cover that is not stitched to it**
  [6] — so a case, a cloth, or a sleeve solves the touching question entirely.
- **there is no harm in the menstruating woman or the one in major impurity
  teaching the Qur'an letter by letter, but she does not teach a complete verse**
  [7] — a concession made specifically so that teachers could keep working.
- **there is no harm in these persons saying "In the name of God, the Merciful,
  the Compassionate" by way of seeking blessing, rather than by way of reciting
  Qur'an** [8]. Intention distinguishes the two.

That last point is the key to most daily situations: saying *bismillah*,
*alhamdulillah*, or an ayah as a du'a is not "reciting Qur'an" in the sense the
prohibition addresses.

**So, practically.**

| | |
|---|---|
| Du'a, dhikr, tasbih, istighfar | Yes, on every position |
| Listening to Qur'an, including recordings | Yes |
| Reading tafsir, translation, Islamic books | Yes |
| Touching a phone or tablet screen | Yes — it is not a mushaf |
| Touching a printed mushaf | Through a cover or cloth, yes |
| Reciting from memory | This is the disputed act |

If you follow the majority, hold off on reciting for those days and keep
everything else. If you follow Malik or Ibn Taymiyya — or if you are a student,
teacher, or hafiza for whom a week's break causes real loss — that position is
held by named scholars on stated reasoning, and it is not a fringe view. Either
way you are within the tradition, and the anxiety many women carry about this is
heavier than the ruling warrants.

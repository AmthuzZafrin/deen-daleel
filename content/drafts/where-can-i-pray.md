# Are there places I'm not allowed to pray?

## Answer

**Almost anywhere.** The Prophet said the earth was made a place of prayer for
him, and the excluded places are a short, specific list.

**The list.** Ibn Qudama reports it from Ibn Umar: **seven places in which prayer
is not permitted — the roof of the House of Allah, the graveyard, the rubbish
heap, the slaughterhouse, the bathhouse, the resting-place of camels, and the
middle of the road.** [1]

Read the list and its logic together, because the logic tells you how far it
reaches. Ibn Qudama: **because these places are likely spots for impurities, so
the ruling was attached to them rather than to the actual presence of impurity —
just as the ruling of breaking wudu is attached to sleep, and the obligation of
ghusl to the meeting of the two circumcised parts.** [2]

They are **proxies for uncleanliness**, made into a fixed rule so you do not have
to inspect. That is why the ruling holds even where a particular graveyard or
slaughterhouse happens to be spotless.

**And the exclusions are read narrowly.** Ibn Qudama: **if there is one grave or
two in the place, prayer in it is not prevented, because the name "graveyard" does
not apply to it.** [3] So a churchyard corner, or a park with a memorial, is not a
graveyard for this purpose. Likewise, if graves have been moved, the ground is
usable again — the Prophet's own mosque was built on land that had held graves.

**So, in practice**

| | |
|---|---|
| Home, office, classroom, shop, warehouse | yes |
| Park, field, beach, mountain, car park | yes |
| Airport, station, plane, train, car | yes — see the separate answers here |
| Hospital, prison, hotel room | yes |
| A church, synagogue or temple, if you need to | permitted by most, though better avoided if a plain room is available |
| **Bathroom, toilet, shower room** | no — the *hammam* of the list |
| **Graveyard** (a real one) | no |
| **The middle of a road or walkway** | no — and this is also about not obstructing people |
| **A rubbish area, a slaughterhouse, an animal pen** | no |

**A place you have no right to be in** is the other category the books add —
somewhere you have taken without permission. The prayer's validity there is
disputed, but the point is straightforward: do not pray somewhere you should not
be standing.

**Two things this settles**

*You do not need a mosque.* The five daily prayers are not tied to one. There is a
separate answer here on whether congregation is obligatory.

*You do not need a special room.* A cleared corner of an office, a stairwell, a
storeroom — all fine. There is a separate answer here on praying at work when
there is nowhere private.

**And note what the list is not about.** It is not about how holy the place feels,
or whether other people can see you, or whether non-Muslims use it. It is about
cleanliness and obstruction. Once those are handled, the ground is yours.

وقيل: هي المواضع التي تناخ فيها إذا وردت. والأول أجود ; لأنه جعله مقابلة مراح الغنم. والحش: المكان الذي يتخذ للغائط والبول فيمنع من الصلاة فيما هو داخل بابه. ولا أعلم في منع الصلاة فيه إلا أنه قد منع من ذكر الله تعالى فيه والكلام، فمنع الصلاة فيه أولى، ولأنه إذا منع الصلاة في هذه المواضع لكونها مظان للنجاسات، فهذا أولى ; فإنه بني لها ويحتمل أن المنع في هذه المواضع معلل بأنها مظان للنجاسات، فإن المقبرة تنبش ويظهر التراب الذي فيه صديد الموتى ودماؤهم ولحومهم، ومعاطن الإبل يبال فيها، فإن البعير البارك كالجدار يمكن أن يستتر به ويبول، كما روي عن ابن عمر، أنه أناخ بعيره مستقبل القبلة، ثم جلس يبول إليه.

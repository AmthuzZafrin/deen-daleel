# What does it mean that some verses were abrogated?

## Answer

*Naskh* is one of the most misused ideas in discussions of the Qur'an — used by critics to argue the book contradicts itself, and by some Muslims to sweep away whole verses. The classical scholars had a technical apparatus for it, and almost every part of that apparatus works to **restrict** the doctrine rather than expand it.

**The definition.** Al-Nawawi: "abrogation is the Lawgiver's raising of an earlier ruling of His by a later ruling of His — this is the preferred definition; other things have been said. And **many, or most, of the writers on hadith have brought into it what does not belong to it**: rather it belongs to the category of specification, or is neither abrogated nor specified but interpreted, or something else" [1]. The first thing he says after defining it is that the category is over-applied.

Al-Jassas gives it the sharpest formulation: whatever the word means in the language, "in the Law's usage it is only **the statement of a ruling's duration**" [2]. Nothing is cancelled retrospectively; a limit that was always there becomes visible.

**And there is a hard restriction on what can even be a candidate.** Al-Tabari: abrogation "is that the lawful be turned unlawful and the unlawful lawful, the permitted forbidden and the forbidden permitted — **and that occurs only in command and prohibition, restriction and release, prevention and permission. As for statements of fact, there is neither abrogating nor abrogated in them**" [3].

That single sentence disposes of a great deal of loose argument. A verse describing God, or the hereafter, or history, or human nature is not the kind of thing *naskh* operates on at all. Only rulings.

**The rule of practice is the one to remember.** Al-Nawawi: "whenever it is possible to carry the Lawgiver's speech in a way that is more general in benefit, that must be adopted — **and one does not resort to abrogation while reconciliation is possible, because abrogation means removing one of the two reports from being acted upon**" [4]. Reconciliation first; abrogation only when reconciliation genuinely fails.

**How abrogation is actually established** is also narrow. Al-Nawawi: "abrogation is known by several things — among them the Prophet's own explicit statement of it, as in *I had forbidden you to visit graves; now visit them*; and among them a Companion's saying, *the last of the two matters was leaving off ablution from what fire has touched*; and among them what is known by chronology" [5]. A modern reader's sense that two verses are in tension is not on that list.

Al-Qurtubi adds two further limits, and one of them matters enormously today: "**as for after his death and the settling of the Law, the community is agreed that there is no abrogation** — and for this reason consensus neither abrogates nor is abrogated by, since it is formed after revelation has ceased" [6]. And: "**it is not sound to abrogate a text by analogy**, since it is among the conditions of analogy that it not contradict a text" [7]. Abrogation ended with the Prophet. Nobody since has been able to abrogate anything, and no reasoning can.

**The verse itself.** *Nothing of our revelation do we abrogate or cause to be forgotten, but we bring one better or the like thereof* [8]. Ibn Taymiyya notes the readings and corrects a common error: "in it are two readings, the better known being *aw nunsiha*, that is, *We cause you to forget it* ... and the second is *aw nunsa'aha*, with a *hamza*, that is, *We defer it*" [9].

**The three kinds, with examples.** Al-Jassas: "abrogation may be in the recitation while the ruling remains, and it may be in the ruling while the recitation remains" [10] — and both may go together.

Al-Ghazali gives the concrete instances of the first sort: "as for what indicates its occurrence by transmission, His saying *and for those who can afford it, a ransom of feeding a poor man* — **its recitation has remained and its ruling was abrogated** by the specification of fasting" [11]; he lists the bequest to parents and near kin, the offering before consulting the Prophet, and the year's waiting period for a widow in the same way. And for the second: "as for the abrogation of the recitation, the reports are abundant on the abrogation of the recitation of the stoning verse **while its ruling remains**" [12].

**And even that last case is disputed** — Ibn 'Abd al-Barr records that "a group denied that this hadith has anything to do with the meaning of abrogating and abrogated, saying it belongs rather to the meaning of the seven modes upon which God sent down the Qur'an" [13]. He then states what is not in dispute: "the scholars are agreed that what is in the *mushaf* of 'Uthman b. 'Affan — which is what is in the hands of the Muslims today across the regions of the earth wherever they are — **is the preserved Qur'an, which no one may go beyond, and the prayer is not lawful for a Muslim except with what is in it**" [14].

**One last thing, because it shows the subject has always had its sceptics.** Al-Jassas reports a contemporary who denied *naskh* within this *sharia* altogether, holding that everything called abrogation "is meant only as the abrogation of the laws of the earlier prophets, such as the Sabbath and prayer toward east and west" [15]. Al-Jassas rejects it — but the way he describes the man is worth reading: "this man had a share of eloquence and much knowledge of language, **but was not fortunate in the knowledge of *fiqh* and its foundations**" [16]. A scholarly disagreement, engaged with rather than anathematised.

**Why it matters practically.** Al-Qurtubi opens his treatment with a story: 'Ali found a man preaching in the mosque and asked him whether he knew the abrogating from the abrogated. "He said: no. He said: **get out of our mosque and do not preach in it**. And in another narration: *do you know the abrogating and the abrogated? He said: no. He said: you are destroyed, and you have destroyed others*" [17]. Al-Qurtubi's own assessment: "knowledge of this subject is imperative and its benefit immense; scholars cannot dispense with knowing it, and only ignorant fools deny it — because of what follows from it in cases of law and in knowing the lawful from the unlawful" [18].

**So, in short.** Abrogation is real, it is limited to rulings and never touches statements of fact, it ended with the Prophet's death, it cannot be established by anyone's sense that two texts clash, and the operating principle is that you do not reach for it while the two texts can be read together. Anyone — critic or preacher — telling you that a verse has been cancelled owes you the specific evidence al-Nawawi lists, and if all they have is that two passages sound different, that is precisely the case the rule tells you to reconcile instead.

تعالى من مؤاخذته إياهم فلما فعلوا ذلك وألقى الله تعالى الايمان في قلوبهم وذلت بالاستسلام لذلك ألسنتهم كما نص في هذا الحديث رفع الحرج عنهم ونسخ هذا التكليف وطريق علم النسخ إنما هو بالخبر عنه أو بالتاريخ وهما مجتمعان في هذه الآية قال القاضي وقول المازري إنما يكون نسخا إذا تعذر البناء كلام صحيح فيما لم يرد فيه النص بالنسخ فان ورد وقفنا عنده لكن اختلف أصحاب الأصول في قول الصحابي رضي الله عنه نسخ كذا بكذا هل يكون حجة يثبت بها النسخ أم لا يثبت بمجرد قوله وهو قول القاضي أبى بكر والمحققين منهم لأنه قد يكون قوله هذا عن اجتهاده وتأويله فلا يكون نسخا حتى ينقل ذلك عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم وقد اختلف الناس في هذه الآية فأكثر المفسرين من الصحابة ومن   بعدهم على ما تقدم فيها من النسخ وأنكره بعض المتأخرين قال لأنه خبر ولا يدخل النسخ الأخبار وليس كما قال هذا المتأخر فإنه وإن كان خبرا فهو خبر عن تكليف ومؤاخذة بما تكن النفوس والتعبد بما أمرهم النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم في الحديث بذلك وأن يقولوا سمعنا وأطعنا وهذه أقوال وأعمال اللسان والقلب ثم نسخ ذلك عنهم برفع الحرج والمؤاخذة وروى عن بعض المفسرين أن معنى النسخ هنا إزالة ما وقع في قلوبهم من الشدة والفرق من هذا الأمر فأزيل عنهم بالآية الأخرى واطمأنت نفوسهم وهذا القائل يرى أنهم لم يلزموا مالا يطيقون لكن ما يشق عليهم من التحفظ من خواطر النفس واخلاص الباطن فاشفقوا أن يكلفوا من ذلك مالا يطيقون فأزيل عنهم الاشفاق وبين أنهم لم يكلفوا الا وسعهم وعلى هذا لا حجة فيه لجواز تكليف ما لا يطاق إذ ليس فيه نص على تكليفه واحتج بعضهم باستعاذتهم منه بقوله تعالى

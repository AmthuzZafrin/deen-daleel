# Do I need a guardian to get married?

## Answer

Three of the four schools say yes; the Hanafi school says no for an adult woman, with a qualification.
This is one of the oldest live disagreements in Islamic law, and anyone who tells you it has one obvious
answer has not read the other side.

Note at the outset that the question only arises for women. A man contracts his own marriage in every
school.

### The majority: the guardian is a condition

Ibn Qudama states it flatly: **"the marriage is not valid except with a guardian; the woman does not
have the power to marry herself off nor anyone else, nor to appoint someone other than her guardian to
marry her off. If she does so, the marriage is not valid"** [1]. He grounds it in the Companions:
**"this is narrated from 'Umar, 'Ali, Ibn Mas'ud, Ibn 'Abbas, Abu Hurayra and 'A'isha"** [2].

The hadith it rests on, from 'A'isha: **"any woman who marries herself off without the permission of her
guardian — her marriage is void, void, void; and if he has consummated with her, she is owed the mahr
for what he made lawful of her; and if they dispute, then the ruler is the guardian of one who has no
guardian"** [3]. That last clause matters: even here the guardian's absence is not a dead end. The judge
steps in.

Al-Shafi'i, Malik and Ahmad all take this line. Malik graded it by circumstance: **"Malik said: every
woman who has wealth, means and standing — it is only proper that the guardians or the ruler marry her
off"** [4], with more latitude for a woman without those.

### The Hanafis: an adult woman may contract her own marriage

**"Abu Hanifa said: she may marry herself off and marry others off, and may appoint an agent in
marriage"** [5]. The Qur'anic basis is the verse forbidding guardians to obstruct: **"because God
Almighty said, 'do not prevent them from marrying their husbands' — He attributed the marrying to them,
and forbade preventing them from it"** [6].

Al-Jassas answers the majority's hadith rather than ignoring it: **"his saying 'there is no marriage
except with a guardian' does not bear on the point of disagreement, because in our view this *is* a
marriage with a guardian — because the woman is her own guardian, just as the man is his own
guardian"** [7]. And he argues by parallel: **"the agreement of all on the permissibility of a man's
contracting his own marriage when he is competent to dispose of his property — likewise the woman, since
she is competent to dispose of her property, it must be permissible for her to contract her own
marriage"** [8].

His hadith evidence is Ibn 'Abbas: **"the previously married woman has more right over herself than her
guardian"** [9], and **"the guardian has no say alongside the previously married woman"** [10]. He also
points to Umm Salama's own marriage: **"she said to her son, who was a small boy: 'stand and marry your
mother to the Messenger of God ﷺ' — and he ﷺ married her without a guardian"** [11].

### The middle position

Some held the contract valid but suspended: **"it is narrated from Ibn Sirin, al-Qasim b. Muhammad,
al-Hasan b. Salih and Abu Yusuf: it is not permitted for her to do that without the guardian's
permission, and if she does, it is held pending his ratification"** [12]. The family gets a veto, not a
prior control.

### What everyone agrees on

Two things, and they are the practical core.

**A secret marriage is invalid in all four schools.** Ibn Taymiyya: **"if he marries her with no
guardian and no witnesses, and the two of them conceal the marriage, this is a void marriage by the
agreement of the imams"** [13] — and **"Malik requires the marriage to be announced; a secret marriage
is of the same kind as the marriage of prostitutes"** [14]. Whatever your view on the wali, the marriage
must be public.

**A guardian cannot block a suitable match.** Al-Nawawi: **"if she wished to marry a suitable man and
the guardian refused, he is compelled; and if he persists, the judge marries her"** [15]. The guardian's
role in the majority view is protective, not proprietary. A father who refuses every candidate forfeits
the role — that is *'adl*, and it has its own remedy.

**The children are legitimate either way.** Ibn Taymiyya, even while calling such a contract void:
**"but if he believed this to be a permissible marriage, the intercourse in it is intercourse under
misapprehension — the child is attributed to him and inherits from his father"** [16]. Nobody's paternity
is at risk over a school disagreement.

### What to do

Involve a wali. It is required in three schools, recommended in the fourth, and the Hanafi permission is
a fallback for when it is genuinely impossible — not an opt-out from family. If your guardian is absent,
non-Muslim, or is refusing without cause, that is a different question with its own answer: the ruler,
or in practice a qualified imam or Islamic authority, stands in.

If you follow a specific school, follow it here. If you do not, the cautious course — a wali, two
witnesses, and a public marriage — is valid in every school, and nothing else can be said to be.

«لا نكاح إلا بولي» ؟

فقال: نعم.

فدل هذا الحديث على أن سماع شعبة والثوري هذا الحديث في وقت واحد، وإسرائيل هو ثبت في أبي إسحاق.

سمعت محمد بن المثنى يقول: سمعت عبد الرحمن بن مهدي يقول: ما فاتني من حديث الثوري عن أبي إسحاق الذي فاتني إلا لما اتكلت به على إسرائيل، لأنه كان يأتي به أتم.

وحديث عائشة في هذا الباب عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم: لا نكاح إلا بولي

حديث عندي حسن، رواه ابن جريج عن سليمان بن موسى عن الزهري عن عروة عن عائشة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم.

ورواه الحجاج بن أرطاة وجعفر بن ربيعة عن الزهري عن عروة عن عائشة عن النبي صلى الله عليه   وسلم.

وروي عن هشام بن عروة، عن أبيه، عن عائشة؛ عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم - مثله.

وقد تكلم بعض أصحاب الحديث في حديث الزهري عن عروة عن عائشة عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم.

قال ابن جريج: ثم لقيت الزهري فسألته فأنكره، فضعفوا هذا الحديث من أجل هذا.

وذكر عن يحيى بن معين أنه قال: لم يذكر هذا الحرف عن ابن جريج إلا إسماعيل بن إبراهيم.

قال يحيى بن معين: وسماع إسماعيل بن إبراهيم عن ابن جريج ليس بذاك، إنما صحح كتبه على كتب عبد المجيد بن عبد العزيز بن أبي رواد ما سمع من ابن جريج، وضعف يحيى رواية إسماعيل بن إبراهيم عن ابن جريج.

والعمل في هذا الباب على حديث النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم لا نكاح إلا بولي عند أهل العلم من أصحاب النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم؛ منهم: عمر بن الخطاب، وعلي بن أبي طالب، وعبد الله بن عباس، وأبو هريرة، وغيرهم.

وهكذا روي عن بعض فقهاء التابعين أنهم قالوا: لا نكاح إلا

بولي - منهم: سعيد بن المسيب، والحسن البصري، وشريح، وإبراهيم النخعي، وعمر بن عبد العزيز، وغيرهم. وبهذا يقول سفيان الثوري، والأوزاعي، وعبد الله بن المبارك، ومالك، والشافعي، وأحمد، وإسحاق.

# Are prawns and shellfish halal?

## Answer

**Short answer: this is a genuine, old disagreement — the widest position permits
anything that lives in the sea, the narrowest limits the permission to fish, and
prawns, crab and lobster fall exactly in the gap.** Anyone who tells you it is
obviously settled has picked a school and not told you.

**The verse everyone starts from.** **"To hunt and to eat the fish of the sea is
made lawful for you, a provision for you and for seafarers"** [1]. The Qur'an
elsewhere speaks of what comes out of both waters as meat: **"And the two seas are
not alike: this, fresh, sweet, good to drink, this (other) bitter, salt. And from
them both ye eat fresh meat and derive the ornament that ye wear."** [2]

**The hadith that narrows it.** Ibn al-'Arabi cites: **"أحلت لنا ميتتان ودمان
فالميتتان السمك والجراد والدمان الكبد والطحال"** — *two dead things and two
bloods have been made lawful to us: the two dead are fish and locusts, and the
two bloods are liver and spleen* [3]. He then notes at once: **"واختلف العلماء في
تخصيص ذلك"** — *the scholars differed over the restriction of that* [4].

That is the crux. If the permission on the sea's dead is stated as **fish and
locusts**, is that an exhaustive list or two examples?

**The narrowest reading — the Hanafi one.** *Bada'i al-Sana'i*: **"والمراد من قول
النبي عليه الصلاة والسلام والحل ميتة السمك خاصة"** — *what is meant by the
Prophet's ﷺ saying "and its dead is lawful" is the dead of fish specifically*
[5] — argued precisely from the two-carrion hadith. On this reading, sea
creatures that are not fish are not covered, and prawns become a question about
whether they count as fish.

**The widest reading — al-Shafi'i's.** Ibn 'Abd al-Barr reports it: **"وقال
الشافعي ما يعيش في الماء فلا بأس بأكله وأخذه ذكاته ولا بأس بخنزير الماء"** —
*al-Shafi'i said: whatever lives in the water, there is no harm in eating it, and
taking it is its slaughter; and there is no harm in the sea-pig* [6]. Note how far
that goes — a creature the Arabs called a pig is included, because the category
is the water and not the name.

**And a middle position.** Ibn 'Abd al-Barr also reports al-Layth ibn Sa'd:
**"ليس بميتة البحر بأس قال وكذلك كلب الماء وترس الماء قال ولا يؤكل إنسان الماء
ولا خنزير الماء"** — *there is no harm in the dead of the sea; and likewise the
water-dog and the water-turtle; but the water-man and the water-pig are not
eaten* [7]. Most of the sea is permitted, with named exceptions.

**A supporting argument, and a revealing one.** Al-Nawawi records the reasoning
that "meat" in ordinary speech does not include fish: **"ومما يبين أن اسم اللحم
عند الاطلاق لا ينصرف إلى السمك أنه لو حلف لا يأكل اللحم لم يحنث بأكل لحم
السمك"** — *and among what shows that the word "meat" unqualified does not extend
to fish is that if a man swore not to eat meat, he would not break the oath by
eating fish* [8]. The jurists were arguing about **categories in language**, which
is why the answer varies: how you classify a prawn decides the case.

**What is not in dispute: no slaughter is needed.** 'Umar: **"وقال عمر بن الخطاب
: الحوت ذكي والجراد ذكي كله"** — *'Umar ibn al-Khattab said: the fish is
[already] slaughtered, and the locust is slaughtered, all of it* [9]. And locusts
were classed with the sea rather than the land: **"الجراد من صيد البحر"** —
*locusts are of the game of the sea* [10]. Whatever is covered by the permission
is covered without any slaughtering ritual.

**So where does that leave you?**

- **If you follow the Shafi'i school**, prawns, crab, lobster, squid and the rest
  are permitted on the reasoning above, and the question does not arise.
- **If you follow the Hanafi school**, the permission is read as fish-specific,
  and creatures that are not fish are excluded — which is why Hanafi guidance
  usually permits prawns (classed as a kind of fish) while excluding crab and
  lobster.
- **The Maliki and Hanbali positions sit between these**, closer to the wide
  reading, with named exceptions of the sort al-Layth describes.
- **Whichever you follow, no slaughter, bleeding or invocation is required** for
  what is permitted.
- **Nobody should be told their position is baseless.** Four of the great jurists
  read the same two texts and reached different classifications. That is what the
  passages above record.

**Where this stops.** These sources argue about categories — fish, sea creature,
land creature — using the natural history and the vocabulary available to them.
Applying their categories to a specific modern animal is a step of classification
they did not perform, and this site does not perform it for you. If a particular
creature matters to you, ask a scholar in your school, who will tell you how that
school classifies it.

Qur'an 35:12 — Surah Faatir, verse 12 of chapter 35

Translation (Marmaduke Pickthall):
And the two seas are not alike: this, fresh, sweet, good to drink, this (other) bitter, salt. And from them both ye eat fresh meat and derive the ornament that ye wear. And thou seest the ship cleaving them with its prow that ye may seek of His bounty, and that haply ye may give thanks.

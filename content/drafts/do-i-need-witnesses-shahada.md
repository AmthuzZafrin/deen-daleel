# Do I need witnesses to say the shahada?

## Answer

No. Nobody needs to hear you.

The hadith that states what the shahada secures also states the condition
attached to it, and the condition is not an audience. In Sunan al-Nasa'i, the
Prophet tells Mu'adh b. Jabal that whoever dies bearing witness that there is no
god but God and that Muhammad is the Messenger of God, **certain of it in his
heart**, enters Paradise. [1] Another narration in the same chapter puts the
condition the same way: the testimony returns to *a truthful heart*, and the
person is forgiven. [2] What is required is that you mean it.

It is worth seeing how the law treats witnesses elsewhere, because that is what
makes the silence here meaningful rather than merely convenient. Where witnesses
are a condition, the sources say so and say how many. Ibn al-'Arabi records the
dispute over whether a marriage is even valid without them, and reports the
Hanafi and Shafi'i position that it is not — a marriage is not contracted except
with two witnesses. [3] [4] The Qur'an requires two just witnesses for a bequest
made near death. [5] Bada'i al-Sana'i states that two is the standing
requirement across testimony generally. [6]

None of that machinery is attached to entering Islam. **Be clear about the shape
of that argument**: it reasons from what the sources require in the places they
do require it, and from the condition the hadith actually names. It is not a text
that says "no witnesses are needed." If you want that stated plainly by a
scholar rather than inferred, ask one.

There is also a passage worth reading if you said the shahada alone and it has
been nagging at you. Commenting on Moses and the righteous man of Madyan
concluding their agreement with *God is Guardian over what we say*, Ibn al-'Arabi
observes that the two of them **were satisfied with God as witness and did not
call any of creation to witness**. [8] And the Qur'an opens its own testimony to
God's oneness with God Himself bearing it: *Allah (Himself) is Witness that there
is no Allah save Him.* [7] The witness you most needed was present.

**Why people will still tell you to find witnesses.** Mosques and Islamic centres
often ask two Muslims to be present and will issue a certificate afterwards. That
is worth doing, and not because your Islam is in doubt — it is so that there is
a record if you ever need to prove it: to marry, to be buried as a Muslim, to
perform Hajj, to satisfy a government that asks. That is an administrative
matter, and it can be done next month or next year. It is not the thing that
made you Muslim.

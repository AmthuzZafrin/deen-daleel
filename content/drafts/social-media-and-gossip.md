# What are the rules for how I behave online?

## Answer

There is no chapter on the internet, but there is a very old chapter on the tongue, and almost all of it transfers. Here is what the sources give you, in the order you actually need it.

**The rule that covers most of it.**

'Abdullah b. 'Amr reported the Prophet ﷺ: "whoever believes in God and the Last Day, let him honour his guest; and whoever believes in God and the Last Day, let him guard his neighbour; and whoever believes in God and the Last Day, **let him speak good or be silent**" [1].

Al-Nawawi turns that into a procedure: "whoever intends to utter a word or a speech should consider it within himself before uttering it — **if its benefit becomes apparent, let him speak; and otherwise, let him hold back**" [2].

That is a rule you can apply before pressing send, and it is the only one you need for a great deal of what passes through a phone.

Why the caution is set so high: on the hadith that a man speaks a word and falls by it into the Fire, al-Nawawi explains, "its meaning is that he does not ponder it and think about its ugliness and does not fear what results from it... or its meaning is like a word from which harm to a Muslim results" [3]. Not a word intended to harm — a word *spoken without considering* what follows from it. That is the characteristic sin of a fast medium.

**Backbiting, and the three things it is often confused with.**

Al-Hasan al-Basri: "backbiting is three kinds, all of them in the Book of God: *ghiba*, *ifk*, and *buhtan*. As for *ghiba*, it is that you say of your brother **what is in him**; as for *ifk*, that you say of him **what has reached you about him**; and as for *buhtan*, that you say of him **what is not in him**" [4].

That middle category is the one social media runs on. *Ifk* is repeating what reached you — the thread you saw, the thing someone said someone said. It is not the same sin as lying, and it is not exempt because you did not make it up. It has its own name.

And the bar is very low. Shu'ba reports: "Mu'awiya b. Qurra said to me: if a man with an amputated hand passed by you and you said *this one is missing a hand*, would that be backbiting? Shu'ba said: I mentioned it to Abu Ishaq, and he said: **he spoke the truth**" [5].

**Talebearing, which is the one nobody names.**

Ibn Hajar defines *namima* against *ghiba*: "for talebearing is **transmitting a person's state to another by way of causing corruption, without his consent** — whether with his knowledge or without it; and backbiting is mentioning him in his absence with what does not please him. So talebearing is distinguished by the intent of causing corruption, and that is not a condition in backbiting" [6].

Then the sentence that should be printed on every messaging app: "**even if he saw someone concealing his wealth and disclosed it, that would be talebearing**" [7].

Note what that covers. Not a lie, not an insult, not even something the person is ashamed of — merely making public what someone kept private. Screenshotting a message. Forwarding a private thread. Telling one group what another group said. All of it is named, and the name is *namima*.

**Ibn Taymiyya's catalogue of disguises — read this one slowly.**

He lists the moulds people pour backbiting into so that it does not look like backbiting. Each is a recognisable genre of Muslim internet post.

*Piety.* "Some bring backbiting out in various moulds — sometimes in the mould of religiosity and righteousness, so he says: **it is not my habit to mention anyone except with good, and I do not like backbiting or lying — I am only informing you of his state**; and he says: by God, he is a poor fellow, or a good man, but in him is such-and-such. And sometimes he says: let us leave him, may God forgive us and him — while his intent is to diminish him and lower his standing. **They bring backbiting out in moulds of righteousness and religiosity, deceiving God by it as they deceive a creature**" [8].

*The humble-brag.* "Some raise another in order to raise themselves, out of showing off, so he says: **had I not prayed for so-and-so last night in my prayer, given what reached me about him**... intending to raise himself and lower him in the eyes of the one who thinks well of him; or he says: so-and-so is dull-witted, of little understanding — his intent being to praise himself and establish his own knowledge, and that he is better than him" [9].

*The joke.* "Some bring backbiting out in the mould of mockery and play, to make others laugh by ridiculing him and imitating him and making the one mocked seem small" [10].

*Astonishment.* "Some bring backbiting out in the mould of astonishment, so he says: **I am amazed at so-and-so — how does he not do such-and-such?** ... so he brings out his name in the guise of being amazed" [11].

*Concern.* "Some bring out grief, so he says: **poor so-and-so, what happened to him has grieved me** — so the one who hears him thinks he grieves for him and is sorry, while his heart is folded over gloating at him; and had he been able, he would have added to what befell him; and he may mention him in front of his enemies so that they may gloat" [12].

*Righteous anger.* "Some display backbiting in the mould of anger and forbidding wrong, and display in this respect various embellishments of speech — **while his intent is other than what he displays**" [13].

Six moulds, all seven hundred years old, all instantly recognisable in a Muslim comment section. He is not describing bad people; he is describing the ways sincere people get past their own conscience.

**One more thing that belongs specifically to a public feed.**

Al-Nawawi, on "all of my community is pardoned except the *mujahirun*": they are "**those who were open about their sins and displayed them and uncovered what God had concealed for them, so that they talk about them without necessity or need**" [14].

That reads differently in an age of confessional posting. The concealment God gives you is a mercy, and undoing it in public — even about yourself, even framed as honesty — is the thing the hadith singles out.

**Practically.**

- **Before you post: does this have a benefit?** If yes, post it. If you cannot state the benefit, al-Nawawi's answer is to hold back. Most of the difficulty in this question dissolves at that line.
- **Repeating what reached you is its own sin** — *ifk* — and "I'm just sharing what I saw" is not a defence, it is the definition.
- **Never make public what someone kept private.** Screenshots and forwards are *namima* by Ibn Hajar's explicit statement, whatever your motive.
- **Check the mould.** If your post is dressed as concern, as amazement, as a joke, or as forbidding wrong, Ibn Taymiyya has already named it. That does not mean it is insincere; it means the disguise is exactly where sincere people go wrong.
- **Criticise the thing, not the person.** Naming a wrong as a category is required; naming an absent person is where it turns.
- **Do not uncover yourself either.** What God has veiled for you is not content.

The Prophet ﷺ tied speaking well to belief in the Last Day, and put it beside honouring a guest and guarding a neighbour. All three are about what you owe someone who is in your reach — which, online, is nearly everyone.

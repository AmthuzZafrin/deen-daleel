# Does a nosebleed or a cut break my wudu?

## Answer

**The schools split, and the split is clean.** Hanafis: yes, if it flows.
Shafi'is, and Malik: no.

**First, what nobody disputes.** Ibn Qudama reports the consensus for the other
category: **the scholars are agreed that stool leaving the anus, urine leaving
the male or female organ, madhy, and wind from the anus are impurities, each of
which breaks the purity.** [1] The argument over blood is an argument about
something *else* — matter leaving the body somewhere other than the two usual
outlets.

**The Hanafi position.** Al-Marghinani in Al-Hidaya lists among the nullifiers
**blood and pus, when they leave the body and pass beyond to a place that takes
the ruling of needing cleaning.** [2] The evidence he gives: **wudu is due from
every flowing blood** — and, for the boundary, **there is no wudu for a drop or
two of blood unless it is flowing.** [3] [4]

That second report is the one that matters in practice. The Hanafi test is not
"did you bleed" but **whether the blood actually ran** — al-Marghinani states it
as a definition: **the exiting is only realised by flowing to a place that takes
the ruling of cleaning.** [5] A pinprick that beads and stops does not qualify. A
nosebleed that runs does.

**The Shafi'i position.** From the same passage, stated by al-Marghinani himself
as the opposing case: **al-Shafi'i said that what exits from other than the two
passages does not break the wudu** — his evidence being that the Prophet vomited
and did not perform wudu, and his reasoning that washing a place other than where
the impurity landed is a matter of pure obedience to a text, so it stays confined
to where the text placed it: the usual outlet. [6]

**What to do.**

- *Following a Hanafi school*: a nosebleed that runs, a cut that drips, blood in
  spit where the blood dominates — renew wudu. A dot of blood that does not
  spread does not count.
- *Following Shafi'i or Maliki*: it does not break your wudu at all. You still
  wash the blood off your clothes if it is more than a trivial amount — that is
  a separate question about impurity on garments, and there is an answer here on
  it.
- *If you do not follow a school yet*: renewing wudu after a real bleed keeps you
  inside every position and costs a minute.

**A blood test, a cut while cooking, a nosebleed in the middle of prayer** — on
the Hanafi view the last of these has its own instruction, from a report
al-Marghinani cites: **whoever vomits or bleeds from the nose in his prayer, let
him leave, perform wudu, and build on his prayer, so long as he has not
spoken.** [7] You continue from where you were rather than starting over.

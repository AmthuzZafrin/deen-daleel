# What happens on the Day of Judgement?

## Answer

**Short answer: a trumpet, a rising, a gathering, a reckoning from a record, and
a judgement.** The Qur'an gives the sequence and the hadith fill in the scene.
What follows is what those sources actually say, in that order.

**The Qur'an treats the Day as beyond description.** 82:18: **"Again, what will
convey unto thee what the Day of Judgment is!"** [1]. That construction — *what
will convey to you what X is* — is the Qur'an's own signal that the thing exceeds
the description about to be given. Worth holding onto before the details.

**The trumpet, twice.** 39:68: **"And the trumpet is blown, and all who are in
the heavens and all who are in the earth swoon away, save him whom Allah willeth.
Then it is blown a second time, and behold them standing waiting!"** [2]. Ibn
Hajar sets out how the two blasts are understood: **"ويمكن الجمع بأن النفخة
الأولى يعقبها الصعق من جميع الخلق أحيائهم وأمواتهم وهو الفزع"** — *the first
blast is followed by the swooning of all creation, living and dead, which is the
terror* [3]; **"ثم ينفخ الثانية للبعث فيفيقون أجمعين فمن كان مقبورا انشقت عنه
الأرض فخرج من قبره"** — *then the second is blown for the resurrection and they
all revive; whoever was buried, the earth splits open over him and he comes out
of his grave* [4].

**The gathering.** Muslim's chapter is titled for it — "the resurrection and the
rising, and the description of the earth on the Day of Resurrection" [5] — and
the report from Sahl b. Sa'd: **"يحشر الناس يوم القيامة على أرض بيضاء عفراء،
كقرصة النقي، ليس فيها علم لأحد"** — *people will be gathered on the Day of
Resurrection on a white, pale earth like a loaf of pure bread, with no landmark
on it belonging to anyone* [6]. A featureless plain, with nothing on it that
belongs to anybody — which is the point of the image.

**The standing.** 83:6: **"The day when (all) mankind stand before the Lord of
the Worlds?"** [7]. Al-Tirmidhi reports the Prophet's ﷺ comment on that verse:
**"يقوم أحدهم في الرشح إلى أنصاف أذنيه"** — *one of them will stand in sweat up
to the middle of his ears* [8].

**The record.** 58:6: **"On the day when Allah will raise them all together and
inform them of what they did. Allah hath kept account of it while they forgot
it."** [9]. Note the last clause — the asymmetry between what is recorded and
what is remembered is the verse's own emphasis. And 17:71: **"On the day when We
shall summon all men with their record, whoso is given his book in his right hand
- such will read their book and they will not be wronged a shred."** [10].

**What the sequence amounts to.** On this material: everything dies, everything
is raised, everyone stands on the same ground with no status carried over [6],
and each person is handed an account of their own life which they read
themselves [10]. The judgement is not described as arbitrary — the emphasis at
[9] and [10] falls on completeness of record and absence of wrong.

**Two things this answer leaves out.** The Scale, the Bridge, the Basin and the
intercession are each substantial topics with their own evidence, and the
descriptions of Paradise and Hell are a separate question again. Also left out:
the signs preceding the Hour, which the sources treat at length and which are
not part of the Day itself.

**Why the sources dwell on it.** The plain reading of the material above is that
this is meant to be motivating rather than merely informative — the featureless
ground where no one's standing carries over [6], and the record kept of what the
person themselves forgot [9]. If it frightens you, that reaction is not a
malfunction; but the same tradition pairs it everywhere with mercy, and fear
without hope is not the balance the sources ask for.

**On the language above:** "Yawm al-Din" is the Day of Judgement, "Yawm
al-Qiyama" the Day of Resurrection; "al-sur" is the trumpet; "al-hashr" the
gathering; "al-ba'th" the raising.

**A note on scope.** The classical scholars differed over details — the number of
blasts, whether the earth of the gathering is this earth transformed or another,
and much else; Ibn Hajar is reconciling competing reports in the passage quoted
here [3][4]. The outline is agreed; the particulars are reported with more
caution than a summary can show.

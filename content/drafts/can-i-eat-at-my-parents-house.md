# Can I eat at my non-Muslim parents' house?

## Answer

**Short answer: yes — and the Qur'an names your parents' house specifically,
while the Sunna's answer to exactly your worry is to say the name of Allah
yourself and eat, rather than to investigate.** If you have recently become
Muslim and are dreading the next family meal, the evidence here is more
reassuring than you are probably expecting.

**The verse that names the table.** **"No blame is there upon the blind nor any
blame upon the lame nor any blame upon the sick nor on yourselves if ye eat from
your houses, or the houses of your fathers, or the houses of your mothers, or the
houses of your brothers, or the houses of your sisters... or (from the house) of
a friend. No sin shall it be for you whether ye eat together or apart. But when
ye enter houses, salute one another with a greeting from Allah, blessed and
sweet."** [1]

The relatives are listed one by one, and *no blame* is the verse's own framing.
Ibn al-'Arabi reads it as removing even the need to ask: **"فأباح الأكل لهؤلاء من
جهة النسب من غير استئذان في الأكل إذا كان الطعام مبذولا"** — *so He permitted
eating to these people, on grounds of kinship, without seeking permission to eat,
when the food is set out* [2]. He qualifies it — you may not take what has been
put away, or hoard, or take what is not food — but the ordinary case of a meal on
a family table is exactly what the verse releases.

**Now the specific worry, put to the Prophet ﷺ by people in your position.**
Al-Nasa'i records 'A'isha: **"إن ناسا من الأعراب يأتونا بلحم ولا ندري أذكروا اسم
الله عليه أم لا فقال رسول الله صلى الله عليه وسلم اذكروا اسم الله عز وجل عليه
وكلوا"** — *some bedouins bring us meat and we do not know whether they mentioned
the name of Allah over it or not. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: mention the name
of Allah over it, and eat* [3].

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr's version of the same report carries a detail that makes it
land: **"أن قوما قالوا يا رسول الله إن قوما يأتوننا بلحم لا ندري أذكر اسم الله
عليه أم لا قال سموا أنتم عليه وكلوا وكانوا حديث عهد بالكفر"** — *some people
said: Messenger of Allah, people bring us meat and we do not know whether the
name of Allah was mentioned over it or not. He said: you say the name over it and
eat. **And they were recent converts from disbelief*** [4].

The questioners were new Muslims, anxious about food from people whose practice
they could not vouch for. The answer was not "find out." The answer was **say
bismillah and eat.**

Ibn 'Abd al-Barr draws the rule out: **"أن ما ذبحه المسلم ولم يعرف هل سمى الله
عليه أم لا أنه لا بأس بأكله وهو محمول على أنه قد سمى والمؤمن لا يظن به إلا الخير
وذبيحته وصيده أبدا محمول على السلامة حتى يصح فيه غير ذلك"** — *that what a Muslim
slaughtered, where it is not known whether he said the name of Allah over it or
not, there is no harm in eating it; it is presumed that he did say it. Nothing but
good is assumed of a believer, and his slaughtering and his hunting are always
presumed sound until something else is established about it* [5].

**A presumption of soundness, and the burden of proof on the doubt, not the
food.**

**And the Qur'an pushes back on over-scrupulousness directly.** **"How should ye
not eat of that over which the name of Allah hath been mentioned, when He hath
explained unto you that which is forbidden unto you unless ye are compelled
thereto. But lo! many are led astray by their own lusts through ignorance."** [6]
The rebuke there is aimed at people refusing food they had no cause to refuse.
And the general permission stands: **"This day are (all) good things made lawful
for you. The food of those who have received the Scripture is lawful for you, and
your food is lawful for them."** [7]

**So, at your parents' table.**

- **Vegetables, bread, rice, fruit, dairy, eggs, fish, and anything with no meat
  in it are simply not in question.** The great majority of a family meal falls
  here and never needed a ruling.
- **Pork and alcohol you know about, you decline.** That much is clear and does
  not require a scene: decline the dish, not the meal.
- **Meat whose slaughter you do not know about** is the one real question, and it
  is a question the schools answer differently — Ibn Kathir notes that on the
  related issue of the name at slaughter, **"وقد اختلف الأئمة، رحمهم الله، في هذه
  المسألة على ثلاثة أقوال"** — *the imams differed on this question along three
  positions* [8]. Where you land depends on whose ruling you follow; see *Can I
  eat meat from a regular supermarket?* and *Is meat slaughtered by Christians or
  Jews permissible?*
- **You are not required to interrogate your mother about her cooking.** The
  hadith above is the closest thing in the sources to your exact situation, and it
  instructs the opposite of investigation.
- **Say bismillah.** That is the actual instruction you were given.
- **Greet them.** The verse ends by telling you to enter with a greeting. In a
  house where your becoming Muslim may already feel like a distance, that
  instruction is doing more than etiquette.

**Where this stops.** Whether a particular piece of meat is permissible turns on
the slaughter question, which has its own evidence and its own disagreement.
Shared pans and utensils have their own answer under *What if halal food touched
pork in a shared kitchen?* And if your family situation is difficult in ways that
go beyond food, that is worth taking to someone who can hear the whole of it.

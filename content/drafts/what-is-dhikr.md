# What is dhikr and how do I do it?

## Answer

*Dhikr* is remembrance of Allah — and in practice it is a small set of short
phrases, repeated, with specific counts attached to specific moments. The
sources are unusually concrete about it, which makes this one of the easiest
things in the religion to actually start doing today.

**What it is.** Ibn al-'Arabi's definition of one of its components carries the
whole idea: "*takbir* is magnification... and its meaning is remembering Allah
by His greatest attributes with the heart, praising Him with the tongue to the
utmost limits of praise and exposition, and submitting to Him with the utmost
worship — like prostrating to Him in lowliness and humility" [1]. Heart, tongue,
and posture together.

**The core phrases.** The Prophet ﷺ named them as a set: "the most beloved speech
to Allah is four — *subhan Allah*, *al-hamdu lillah*, *la ilaha illa Allah*, and
*Allahu akbar*; it does not harm you which of them you begin with" [2]. And he
ranked them: "the best remembrance is *la ilaha illa Allah*, and the best
supplication is *al-hamdu lillah*" [3].

Sa'id b. al-Musayyab and others identified these as what the Qur'an calls *the
abiding good deeds*: "they are the saying *la ilaha illa Allah*, *al-hamdu
lillah*, *subhan Allah*, *Allahu akbar*, and *la hawla wa la quwwata illa
billah*" [4].

There is a striking narration on the first two of them, transmitted as Nuh's
counsel to his son: "I enjoin you with *la ilaha illa Allah*, for if the heavens
and the earth were a ring it would shatter them, and if they were in a scale it
would outweigh them. And I enjoin you with *subhan Allah wa bihamdih*, for it is
the prayer of creation and by it creation is provided for" [5] — with the
matching prohibition: "as for the two I forbid you: Allah veils Himself from
them, and so do the righteous of His creation — I forbid you associating partners
with Allah, and pride" [6].

**How to do it: the fixed times.**

*After each obligatory prayer.* This came as an answer to people who felt they
were falling behind. Companions complained that the wealthy outstripped them in
charity, and the Prophet ﷺ said: "shall I not tell you of something which, if you
do it, they will not outstrip you and none after you will catch up with you
except one who does as you do — you glorify Allah after every prayer
thirty-three times, and praise Him thirty-three times, and magnify Him
thirty-four times" [7]. A version adds the completing phrase and its reward: "whoever
says after every prayer thirty-three *Allahu akbar*, thirty-three *subhan Allah*,
thirty-three *al-hamdu lillah*, and says *there is no god but Allah alone, having
no partner; His is the dominion and His is the praise, and He has power over all
things* — his sins are forgiven him even if they are like the foam of the sea" [8].

*At sleep.* Fatima came to her father asking for a servant, and "he said: shall I
not direct you to what is better for you than that? ... glorify Allah at your
sleeping thirty-three times, magnify Him thirty-three times, and praise Him
thirty-four times" [9]. 'Ali's comment on it is the line to remember: "so I have
not left it since I heard it from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. It was said: not
even the night of Siffin? He said: not even the night of Siffin" [10] — the eve
of the worst battle of his life.

*At any time.* "Is any of you unable to earn a thousand good deeds each day? They
said: Messenger of Allah, who can manage that? He said: he glorifies Allah a
hundred times — a thousand good deeds are written for him and a thousand sins are
removed from him" [11].

**Two things worth noticing about all of this.** First, the counts are small.
Thirty-three of something takes under a minute; a hundred *tasbihat* takes two
or three. The tradition is not asking for hours.

Second, dhikr is offered in these texts precisely to people who *cannot* do the
more expensive forms of worship — the poor who had nothing to give in charity,
and a daughter who could not afford help in the house. That is the setting the
practice was given in.

**And a caution against the opposite mistake.** Ibn 'Abd al-Barr, on those who
prize silence: "this and the like of it makes clear to you that speech in
goodness — of remembrance of Allah, recitation of the Qur'an, and acts of
righteousness — is better than silence; and likewise all speaking of truth,
and reconciling between people and the like of it. The praised silence is only
silence from falsehood" [12]. He cites the hadith: "the speech of the son of Adam
counts against him and not for him, except enjoining good, or forbidding wrong,
or the remembrance of Allah" [13].

**So, to start.** Pick the after-prayer set — thirty-three, thirty-three,
thirty-four, and the closing phrase — and do it after one prayer a day until it
sticks, then extend. Add the same set at sleep. That is the whole of what these
sources ask, and 'Ali kept it through a civil war.

# Can I be alone in a room with the opposite gender?

## Answer

No — not with someone you could in principle marry, and not in a place where you would not be
interrupted. This is one of the firmest rules in the area, and one of the most frequently misapplied.

### The rule

The Prophet ﷺ said, in a sermon: **"no man shall be alone with a woman except that a mahram is with her;
and a woman shall not travel except with a mahram"** [1].

Al-Nawawi states the consequence: **"as for an unrelated man being alone with an unrelated woman with no
third person with them, that is forbidden by the agreement of the scholars"** [2]. Al-Hidaya cites the
other well-known wording: **"beware — no man shall be alone with a woman who is not connected to him,
for the third of them is Satan"** [3].

### The scope, precisely

Al-Nawawi is unusually specific about the edges, and the specifics answer most real questions.

**A very young child does not break it.** **"Likewise if there is with them someone before whom one feels
no shyness because of his youth, such as a child of two or three years — his presence is as
non-existence"** [4]. The third person must be someone whose presence actually constrains behaviour.

**Numbers work asymmetrically.** **"Likewise if several men are together with an unrelated woman, that is
forbidden — unlike a man being together with several unrelated women, for the sound position is that it
is permitted"** [5]. That is the classical rule; whether it fits every modern setting is a judgement for
a scholar, but it is not a modern invention.

**The setting makes no difference.** **"There is no difference in the prohibition of seclusion, where we
have prohibited it, between seclusion in prayer or otherwise"** [6]. A study session, an office, a car,
a meeting after hours — the ruling does not soften because the activity is legitimate.

### Who it does not apply to

A mahram. Al-Hidaya's definition: **"the mahram is one with whom marriage is not permitted between him
and her permanently, whether by lineage or by cause such as breastfeeding or marriage ties"** [7]. With
such a person, **"there is no harm in seclusion and travelling with them"** [8].

A husband or wife, obviously. And an open, public setting is not seclusion at all — the prohibition is on
*khalwa*, a private situation where interruption is not expected, not on being in the same room as
someone.

### The exception, which is not minor

Al-Nawawi: **"excepted from all of this are situations of necessity — such as his finding an unrelated
woman stranded on the road or the like. It is permitted for him to accompany her; indeed it becomes
obligatory upon him if he fears for her were he to leave her"** [9].

Read that carefully. Where someone is in danger, accompanying them is not a permitted exception; it is a
duty. A rule meant to prevent harm is not allowed to cause it.

### In practice

Most of what worries people is not *khalwa*. Working in an open office is not; a video call your team can
join is not; a shop with customers in it is not. What is: a closed office where you will not be
interrupted, an empty house, a long car journey.

Where the situation is unavoidable — a medical appointment, a job that requires it — apply [9]: prefer the
door open, a third person present, a public room, a shorter meeting. Take the reasonable measure; that is
what the rule is asking for.

# Can I wear perfume while fasting?

## Answer

**Yes. Perfume does not break the fast, and it is not on any of the lists of things
that do.**

**What the sources say — and what their silence means.**

Perfume is simply not treated as a fasting question in these books. It does not
appear among the things that invalidate the fast, and it does not appear among the
things that are disliked for the fasting person. That absence is itself informative:
the jurists enumerated the borderline cases in detail, and this was not one of them.

What they do address is the closely related act of putting oil on the skin, and they
place it firmly among the things that change nothing. Al-Nasafi's list of what does
**not** break the fast includes it: **if the fasting person eats, or drinks, or has
intercourse forgetfully; or has a wet dream; or emits by looking; or applies oil; or
is cupped; or applies kohl; or kisses; or dust or a fly enters his throat while he
is mindful of his fast; or he swallows what is between his teeth; or he vomits and
it returns — he has not broken the fast** [1].

And among things that are not even disliked, he names oiling explicitly: **not kohl,
nor oiling the moustache, nor the tooth-stick, nor kissing if he is safe** [2].

If oil applied to the skin and moustache is untroubled, scent applied to the skin is
untroubled.

**The principle that settles it.**

Al-Kasani derives the whole chapter from the definition: **as for its essential
element, it is abstaining from food, drink, and sexual intercourse** [3]. Perfume is
none of the three.

Ibn Qudama states the operative rule: **he breaks the fast by everything he
introduces into his interior, or into a cavity in his body such as his brain or his
throat, and the like of that which passes to his stomach — if it reaches there by his
choice, and was something he could have guarded against** [4]. Al-Sarakhsi:
**the consideration is what arrives, not the route** [5].

Scent on the skin or on clothing does not arrive anywhere. Smelling it is not
ingesting it.

**So, in practice:**

- **Perfume, attar, cologne on skin or clothes** — fine.
- **Deodorant, moisturiser, oils, make-up** — fine, by the same reasoning as oiling.
- **Incense and bakhoor in the room** — fine to be around. Smelling smoke is not
  inhaling it deliberately, and the sources place unavoidable airborne things — dust,
  a fly — outside the fast entirely, as in the list above.
- **Cooking, and tasting food while cooking** — the smell is fine. Tasting is the one
  to be careful of: al-Nasafi lists it among the disliked, **tasting something and
  chewing it without an excuse, and chewing gum are disliked** [6]. If you must taste
  to cook for others, do it on the tip of the tongue and spit it out; that is an
  excuse, and nothing is swallowed.

**The one thing worth flagging.** Deliberately drawing incense smoke deep into the
nose or throat is a different act from being in a scented room — it is inhaling a
substance on purpose, which is the reasoning that makes smoking break the fast. See
that answer. Ordinary use of perfume and bakhoor is nowhere near that line.

**And on alcohol-based perfume** — that is a question about purity rather than about
fasting, and it has its own answer.

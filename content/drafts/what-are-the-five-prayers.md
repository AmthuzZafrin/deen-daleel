# What are the five prayers and how many rak'ahs is each?

## Answer

**Five prayers a day, and this is one of the very few things in Islamic law with
no disagreement at all.** Al-Kasani: **as for their number, it is five — that is
established by the Book, the Sunna and the consensus of the Umma.** [1]

| | when | rak'ahs (obligatory) |
|---|---|---|
| **Fajr** | dawn, before sunrise | 2 |
| **Dhuhr** | after the sun passes its zenith | 4 |
| **Asr** | afternoon | 4 |
| **Maghrib** | just after sunset | 3 |
| **Isha** | night | 4 |

The counts are settled by usage rather than argued about, and they surface in the
books wherever a calculation needs them. Al-Nawawi, working out how much of a
prayer time you must catch: **four for Dhuhr and one for Asr** [2], and **Maghrib
is caught by four rak'ahs from the end of the Isha time — three for Maghrib and
one for Isha** [3].

**Nothing is obligatory beyond these five.** This is worth stating plainly,
because the sheer volume of optional prayer in Muslim practice can make it look
otherwise. The hadith is a bedouin asking directly. Al-Kasani: **it is narrated
that when the Messenger of Allah taught the bedouin the five prayers, he said: is
there anything upon me other than this? He said: no — unless you do so
voluntarily.** [4]

*No, unless you volunteer.* Everything else — the sunnas before and after, witr,
tahajjud, duha — is on top of the five, not part of them. There are separate
answers here on each.

Even witr, which some treat as near-obligatory, is not a sixth prayer. Al-Kasani:
**and for this reason the general body of the jurists said that witr is a sunna**
[5], because the Qur'an and the mass-transmitted Sunna established five and no
more. (Abu Hanifa alone calls it *wajib*, which is a rank below *fard* — the same
passage explains that he does not call it obligatory in the stronger sense.)

**Praying them takes about twenty-five minutes a day, in total.** Fajr is two
rak'ahs and takes three or four minutes. Dhuhr, Asr and Isha are four rak'ahs
each; Maghrib is three. That is the entire daily requirement.

**Where to go from here**

- **How to pray them** — there is a step-by-step answer here.
- **When each one starts and ends** — a separate answer, and the most useful one
  to read next, since the times are the part that takes adjusting to.
- **The smallest valid version**, if you are new and do not have the Arabic yet.
- **What to do about ones you have missed** — several answers, including one for
  years of them.

**A note for reverts.** Five a day sounds like a lot before you start and becomes
ordinary within a few weeks, mostly because the times are spread across the day
rather than stacked. If you are struggling, start by praying each one *within its
time* even if briefly, rather than praying long ones late. The times are the
structure; everything else is built on them.

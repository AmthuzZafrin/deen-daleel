# What does Islam say about being gay?

> Reviewer note: Distinguish inclination from action, and answer a person rather than a debate. Do not shame. Route to support where the reader is struggling.


## Answer

**The sources draw a line that most conversations about this do not: between
what a person feels and what a person does.** The act is prohibited [1][2]. The
attraction is not a sin [5][7], and the classical texts say so with more
precision than you are likely to have been told. If you are asking because this is your life
rather than an argument you are having, the second half is the part that was
written for you.

**The act.** The Qur'an describes the practice of the people of Lut as
abominations: **"And unto Lot we gave judgment and knowledge, and We delivered
him from the community that did abominations"** [1]. The fiqh and hadith works
treat the matter under that name — Sunan Abi Dawud heads a chapter
**"باب فيمن عمل عمل قوم لوط"**, "concerning one who does the deed of the people
of Lut" [2]. That the act is forbidden is not disputed among the classical
schools, and this answer does not pretend otherwise.

**The feeling is a different question, and the sources answer it directly.** Ibn
Hajar sets out a graded scale of what happens in the heart, and it is worth
reading slowly. The weakest: **"أضعفها أن يخطر له ثم يذهب في الحال وهذا من
الوسوسة وهو معفو عنها وهو دون التردد"** — *the weakest is that something occurs
to him and departs at once; this is waswasa, and it is pardoned* [3]. Above that:
**"وفوقه أن يتردد فيه فيهم به ثم ينفر عنه فيتركه ثم يهم به ثم يترك كذلك ولا
يستمر على قصده وهذا هو التردد فيعفى عنه أيضا"** — *above it, that he wavers,
inclining then recoiling then inclining again without settling on it; this is
wavering, and it too is pardoned* [4]. Above that:
**"وفوقه أن يميل إليه ولا ينفر عنه لكن لا يصمم على فعله وهذا هو الهم فيعفى عنه
أيضا"** — *above it, that he inclines toward it and does not recoil from it, but
does not resolve on doing it; this is hamm, and it too is pardoned* [5]. Only at
the next level does it change: **"وفوقه أن يميل إليه ولا ينفر منه بل يصمم على
فعله فهذا هو العزم وهو منتهى الهم"** — *above it, that he inclines and does not
recoil but resolves upon doing it; this is 'azm* [6].

Read what is at [5]. A settled inclination that a person does not recoil from,
and does not act on, is named in the tradition and pardoned. That is not a
loophole someone has found; it is Ibn Hajar setting out the standard taxonomy.

Ibn Taymiyya states the same conclusion: **"وأما الهام بالسيئة الذي لم يعملها
وهو قادر عليها فإن الله لا يكتبها عليه كما أخبر به في الحديث الصحيح"** — *as for
one who inclines toward a wrong and does not do it, though able to, Allah does
not write it against him, as the sound hadith states* [7]; and he cites
**"إن الله تجاوز لأمتى ما حدثت به أنفسها ما لم تكلم به أو تعمل به"** — *Allah has
overlooked for my community what their souls tell them, so long as they do not
speak it or act on it* [8].

**And restraint is not merely neutral.** Ibn Taymiyya: **"فإن تركها لخشية الله
كتبها الله له عنده حسنة كاملة كما قد صرح به في الحديث وكما قد جاء في الحديث
الآخر ( إكتبوها له حسنة فإنما تركها من أجلى ("** — *if he leaves it out of fear
of Allah, Allah writes it for him as a complete good deed, as the hadith states
explicitly, and as in the other hadith: "write it for him as a good deed, for he
left it only for My sake"* [9]. The hadith he is quoting says the same:
**"ومن هم بسيئة ولم يعملها كتبها له الله له حسنة كاملة فإن هم بها وعملها كتبها
الله له عنده سيئة واحدة"** — *whoever inclines toward a wrong and does not do it,
Allah writes it for him as a complete good deed; and if he does it, He writes it
as a single wrong* [10].

So on the material above: what you feel is not held against you [5][7][8], and
the restraint you exercise is credited to you [9][10]. Whoever told you that
having these feelings makes you sinful, or makes you less of a Muslim, was not
reading these texts. Ibn Hajar does add that someone in whom such things arise is
**"لكن من يقع له ذلك مأمور بمجاهدته النفس على تركه"** — *commanded to struggle
with himself to leave it* [11] — a struggle, which is a thing a Muslim in good
standing does, not a mark against them.

**If you have acted on it.** The same door is open as for any other sin. Al-Nawawi:
**"وأن الكبائر إنما تكفرها التوبة أو رحمة الله تعالى وفضله"** — *grave sins are
expiated by repentance, or by Allah's mercy and bounty* [12]. And **"Say: O My
slaves who have been prodigal to their own hurt! Despair not of the mercy of
Allah, Who forgiveth all sins"** [13]; **"Know they not that Allah is He Who
accepteth repentance from His bondmen"** [14]. Repentance is between you and
Allah. You do not confess this to anyone, and nobody is entitled to ask.

**Some practical things, since this is a life and not a topic.**

You do not owe anyone this information. Nothing in the material above requires
you to disclose it to anybody.

Do not let anyone push you into a marriage as a cure. Marriage carries real
obligations to another person, and entering one on a false basis wrongs them.
That is a serious matter in its own right, and "get married and it will pass" is
advice, not a ruling.

Do not accept the claim that there is no place for you in Islam. Nothing in the
material above says that, and the texts at [5], [9] and [13] say close to the
opposite. People who tell you otherwise are speaking for themselves.

If you are in distress — and people asking this question often are — please talk
to someone: a counsellor, a doctor, a helpline. If you are thinking of harming
yourself, treat that as urgent and reach out today. There are Muslim counsellors
and support services who work with exactly this and will not be shocked by it.

**On the language above:** "waswasa" is an intrusive passing thought; "hamm" is
inclination without resolve; "'azm" is settled resolve to act; "tawba" is
repentance; "mujahadat al-nafs" is the struggle with one's own self.

**What this answer does not cover, deliberately.** The classical works discuss a
prescribed penalty for the act. That belongs to a state court under evidentiary
conditions that are almost never satisfiable, it has no application to how a
person lives their life, and quoting it at someone asking this question would be
neither useful nor kind — so it is not relayed here. Contemporary debates about
identity, orientation as a category, and how Muslim communities should treat
their gay members are live and contested, and these premodern texts do not settle
them. If you want to talk this through with someone who knows both the sources
and your situation, look for a scholar or counsellor with a reputation for
treating people gently; they exist, and it is worth the effort of finding one
rather than asking whoever is nearest.

```
Qur'an 9:104 — Surah At-Tawba, verse 104 of chapter 9

Translation (Marmaduke Pickthall):
Know they not that Allah is He Who accepteth repentance from His bondmen and taketh the alms, and that Allah is He Who is the Relenting, the Merciful.

Arabic:
أَلَمْ يَعْلَمُوٓا۟ أَنَّ ٱللَّهَ هُوَ يَقْبَلُ ٱلتَّوْبَةَ عَنْ عِبَادِهِۦ وَيَأْخُذُ ٱلصَّدَقَٰتِ وَأَنَّ ٱللَّهَ هُوَ ٱلتَّوَّابُ ٱلرَّحِيمُ
```

### Passage 40 — Qur'an 9:39

```
Qur'an 9:39 — Surah At-Tawba, verse 39 of chapter 9

Translation (Marmaduke Pickthall):

```

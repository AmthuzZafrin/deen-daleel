# Is fasting Mondays and Thursdays a real thing?

## Answer

Yes — it is one of the best-attested voluntary fasts in the sunna, and the schools agree on it. Al-Nawawi: **our colleagues and others agreed on the recommendedness of fasting Monday and Thursday** [1]. It is not obligatory, and there is no blame at all in not doing it.

Two reasons are given in the reports. The first is about Monday specifically. Asked why he fasted it, the Prophet answered: **that is a day I was born on, and the day I was sent** [2] — the day of his birth and of the first revelation.

The second covers both days and is the one usually quoted. Abu Hurayra reports: **deeds are presented on Monday and Thursday, and I like my deeds to be presented while I am fasting** [3]. Al-Tirmidhi records that in the same words and grades it *hasan gharib* [4]. Ibn Maja preserves a related report about the same two days — **on Monday and Thursday God forgives every Muslim, except two who are estranged from one another** [5], with the instruction to leave those two until they reconcile. That pairing is worth noticing: the day is about being forgiven, and the one thing that blocks it is a quarrel you are refusing to end.

That the Prophet actually kept these days is reported by Aisha, who said **he used to seek out fasting Monday and Thursday** [6]; al-Nawawi quotes her in nearly identical words [7]. So this is his practice, not a later custom.

A note on what else falls nearby: the Hanafi compendium records that **fasting Friday on its own is recommended in the general view, like Monday and Thursday** [8], although other authorities read the reports as discouraging Friday alone. If you fast Thursday and Friday together the question does not arise.

Practically: two days a week is a real commitment, and you should not start there. Try one Monday. If you owe missed days from Ramadan, those come first — but you can fast them on a Monday and get both.

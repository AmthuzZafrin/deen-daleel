# I've been making du'a for ages and nothing's happened — why?

## Answer

The sources answer this directly, and their answer is not "you must be doing
something wrong." It is that the response has more than one form, and that the
one thing which genuinely forfeits it is concluding that it has not come.

**The hadith the whole discussion turns on.** "Response continues to be given to
the servant so long as he does not supplicate for something sinful or for
cutting off kinship, and so long as he does not become impatient" [1]. And the
Prophet ﷺ was asked what impatience meant: "he says, I have supplicated and
supplicated and I have not seen myself answered — and so he despairs at that and
abandons supplication" [2].

Read that carefully. The impatience being warned against is not feeling
disappointed. It is reaching the verdict, and stopping.

**'A'isha's version is the most complete answer to your question.** "There is no
believing servant who supplicates to Allah with a supplication and it goes to
waste — rather it is either hastened for him in this world or stored up for him
in the Hereafter, so long as he is not hasty and does not despair. 'Urwa said: I
said, mother, how is his haste and his despair? She said: he says, I asked and
was not given, and I supplicated and was not answered" [3]. Sa'id b. al-Musayyab
said the same thing in the same words.

Nothing is lost. It is either advanced to you now or kept for you later, and
those are the only two possibilities she allows. What she calls despair is the
act of declaring a third.

**Why declaring it is treated so seriously.** Al-Qurtubi: "the supplicant's
saying *I have supplicated and it was not answered* is prevented, because that
belongs to despair, weakness of certainty, and displeasure" [4]. Those are three
different faults and only one of them is about doubt. The last — *sukht* — is
resentment at the decree, which is why the tone of the warning is what it is.

**What the sources say can actually obstruct an answer.** Al-Qurtubi names one
thing explicitly, and it is not a spiritual failing: "unlawful consumption also
prevents the answering of supplication, and whatever is in its meaning. The
Prophet ﷺ said: *the man who travels long, dishevelled and dusty, stretching out
his hands to the sky — O Lord, O Lord — while his food is unlawful, his drink is
unlawful, his clothing is unlawful and he has been nourished on the unlawful:
how then is that answered?*" [5].

Then he lists the conditions in the supplicant: "that he know that none is able
to meet his need but Allah, and that the intermediaries are in His grip and
subject to His subjection; and that he supplicate with a sincere intention and a
present heart — for Allah does not answer a supplication from a heedless,
distracted heart; and that he avoid consuming the unlawful; and that he not
weary of supplication" [6]. And a condition on the request itself: "that what is
asked for be among matters whose seeking and doing are permissible in the Law,
as he said, *so long as he does not supplicate for something sinful or for
cutting off kinship* — and everything by which one sins enters into *sin*, and
all the rights of the Muslims and grievances against them enter into *kinship*"
[7]. Sahl al-Tustari's shorter list: "the conditions of supplication are seven:
the first of them humility, then fear, hope, perseverance, submissiveness,
generality, and eating what is lawful" [8].

Notice how many of those are about the *state you are in while asking*, and how
few are about the words.

**On the heart while asking.** "Hearts are vessels, and some of them are more
retentive than others. So when you ask Allah, O people, ask Him while you are
certain of the response — for He does not answer a servant who calls on Him from
the back of a heedless heart" [9]. This is worth handling gently: it is a
description of what makes du'a full, not a test you are failing when you are
tired.

**And the reframe that is probably most useful to you.** Ibn Hajar, on the same
hadith: "in this hadith is one of the etiquettes of supplication — that he keeps
to the asking and does not despair of the response, because of the submission,
the yielding, and the showing of one's poverty that is in that" [10]. The asking
*is* the thing being asked of you. Which is why one of the early Muslims said:
"I am more afraid of being deprived of the supplication than of being deprived
of the answer" [11] — and the report Ibn Hajar cites next, though he notes its
chain is weak: "whoever among you has the door of supplication opened to him,
the doors of mercy have been opened to him" [12].

**So, practically.** You have not been ignored, and nothing you asked for has
gone anywhere. Check the two things the sources name concretely — that what you
are asking for is lawful, and that what you consume is — and then keep asking.
Not because persistence forces an answer, but because on 'A'isha's reckoning the
only way to lose is to stop.

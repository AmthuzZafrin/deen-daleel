# Can I still make du'a and dhikr on my period?

## Answer

**Yes — all of it, without any restriction at all. This is agreed on by everyone,
and there is no minority view against it.**

Du'a, dhikr, tasbih, istighfar, salawat on the Prophet, tahajjud-hour supplication,
Friday du'a, du'a in Ramadan, du'a at any time — none of it is affected by your
period. If you have been holding back from these, you have been carrying a
restriction that Islamic law does not impose.

**The general principle.**

Aisha reported: **the Prophet, peace be upon him, used to remember God at all his
times** [1]. It is in Sahih Muslim and in Abu Dawud; al-Tirmidhi grades his
transmission of it **a hasan gharib hadith we know only through Yahya ibn
Zakariyya ibn Abi Za'ida** [2] — the Muslim narration settles its strength
regardless.

Al-Bukhari places this hadith in a chapter he builds precisely around the
menstruating woman, and the material he gathers there is the clearest statement of
the position anywhere:

- **the menstruating woman performs all the rites of Hajj except circumambulating
  the House** [3]
- **Umm Atiyya said: we were commanded that the menstruating women should go out
  and make takbir along with them, and make du'a** [4]

Read the second one again. Menstruating women were not merely permitted to make
du'a — they were commanded to leave the house on Eid and go to the prayer ground
in order to make takbir and du'a with the congregation. That is the opposite of
being excluded.

Al-Bukhari also records in the same place: **Ibrahim said there is no harm in her
reciting a verse** [5] — on the disputed question of reciting Qur'an, which has
its own answer.

**The Hajj case settles it.**

When Aisha began menstruating during Hajj, the instruction was not to withdraw.
Ibn Abd al-Barr transmits it: **do what the pilgrim does, except that you should
not circumambulate the House** [6]. Al-Nawawi has the same with its condition:
**do what the pilgrim does, except that you should not circumambulate the House
until you bathe** [7].

Hajj is saturated with dhikr — the talbiya, the standing at Arafa, the du'a at
Muzdalifa, the takbir at the jamarat. She did all of it. One act was suspended:
the tawaf, which requires ritual purity in the way prayer does.

**So the shape of the exemption is precise.** Two things are lifted — prayer and
fasting — plus tawaf, and the disputed matter of reciting. Everything else that
is worship stands:

- du'a, any time, any words, any language
- dhikr, tasbih, tahmid, takbir, tahlil, istighfar
- salawat on the Prophet
- listening to Qur'an
- attending lectures, studying, teaching
- charity, kindness to parents, every good act
- going to the Eid prayer ground, as above

**One thing worth saying plainly.** Some women describe feeling spiritually cut
off during their period. Nothing in these sources supports that reading. The
exemption is a lifting of two duties, not a suspension of your standing before
God, and the one hadith that speaks directly to what a menstruating woman *should*
do sends her out to make du'a with everyone else.

# My period ended just before the prayer time ran out — do I pray it?

## Answer

**Yes — if you had enough of the window left to do the ghusl. And on the majority
view you owe the prayer before it as well.**

**The prayer you became pure in.**

Ibn Abd al-Barr gives the governing rule: **the prayer only drops from the
menstruating woman so long as she is menstruating; when she becomes pure she is
like one in major impurity and the prayer of the time she is in becomes binding on
her** [1]. The moment the bleeding stops, you are back in the ordinary category —
you bathe and you pray, exactly as someone who woke up in a state of janaba would.

**How much time do you need?**

The Hanafi rule is time enough for the ghusl. Al-Sarakhsi: **if she becomes pure
from menstruation and there remains of the time the measure in which she can
bathe, then making up that prayer is upon her; and if there remains of the time a
measure in which she cannot bathe, then that prayer is not upon her** [2].

Ibn Qudama's school sets the bar much lower: **the measure to which the obligation
attaches is the measure of the opening takbir** [3]. He notes al-Shafi'i's figure:
**al-Shafi'i said: the measure of a rak'ah** [4], and Malik's, which is five
rak'ahs.

So the threshold varies — a takbir, a rak'ah, time enough to bathe — but the shape
is the same: a sliver of the window left means the prayer is owed.

**The surprising part: you may owe the previous prayer too.**

Ibn Qudama's chapter opens on exactly this: **when the menstruating woman becomes
pure, the disbeliever accepts Islam, and the child** reaches maturity before sunset,
they pray Zuhr and then Asr [5] — and if it happens before dawn, Maghrib and Isha.

The reasoning is that Zuhr and Asr share a window in a case of excuse, as do
Maghrib and Isha. Catching any of the later prayer's time means catching the
earlier one too. Ibn Qudama names Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf, Ibn Abbas, Malik,
al-Shafi'i, al-Layth, Ishaq and others, and reports Ahmad saying **the general body
of the Successors hold this view, except al-Hasan alone, who said only the prayer
in whose time she became pure is obligatory** [6] — which was also the position of
al-Thawri and the Hanafis.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Majority** | Pure before Maghrib → pray Zuhr *and* Asr. Before Fajr → Maghrib *and* Isha |
| **Hanafi, al-Thawri, al-Hasan** | Only the prayer whose time you became pure in |

**In practice.**

- Bleeding stops with twenty minutes of Asr left → ghusl, pray Asr. On the majority
  view, pray Zuhr too.
- Bleeding stops two minutes before Maghrib, no time to bathe → nothing owed on the
  Hanafi rule; on Ibn Qudama's, a takbir's worth of time is enough, so it is owed.
- You may pray first and bathe after? No — the ghusl comes first. That is why the
  time needed for it is the Hanafi threshold.

**One footnote on voluntary prayers**, since it runs the other way. Al-Sarakhsi:
**this is unlike the voluntary prayer — for if menstruation reaches her after she
has begun a voluntary prayer, making up that prayer is upon her when she becomes
pure** [7]. Beginning a nafl commits you to completing it; beginning a fard does
not, because you were only discharging an existing duty. So a voluntary prayer
interrupted by your period is made up, and an obligatory one is not.

**If you are unsure whether you had enough time**, pray it. It costs you a prayer
you may not have owed, which is not a loss.

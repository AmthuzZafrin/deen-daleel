# Is hijab compulsory?

## Answer

Yes. Covering is an obligation, and the sources are as close to unanimous on
that as they get on anything. What is worth seeing is *how* they establish it,
because the classical argument runs differently from most modern ones and
answers more of the practical questions.

**Two separate obligations, often run together.** Ibn 'Abd al-Barr states the
first: "they were agreed that covering the 'awra is an obligatory duty in
general upon human beings, and they differed only over whether it is among the
obligations *of the prayer*" [1]. So the disagreement the early jurists actually
had was a narrow one — not whether you must cover, but whether uncovering
invalidates a prayer. Al-Qurtubi records al-Abhari's position and endorses it:
"it is obligatory in general, and a person must cover it from people's eyes in
prayer and outside it — and this is the correct view" [2].

**What the verse changed.** The command in Surat al-Nur — that women draw
their khimars over the openings of their dresses, quoted in the Arabic at [5]
below — did not introduce head-covering; it corrected how head-covering was
being worn. Al-Qurtubi: "the reason for this verse is that women at that time,
when they covered their heads with khimars — which are head-wraps — would let
them hang down behind the back... so the throat, the neck and the ears remained
with no covering over them, and Allah commanded that the khimar be wound over
the openings of their dresses" [3]. The scarf was already there. The verse
specified where it had to reach: Ibn al-'Arabi draws the conclusion, "and this
indicates that covering the neck and the chest is included" [4].

**What the women who heard it did.** 'A'isha: "when this verse was revealed —
*wa'l-yadribna bi-khumurihinna 'ala juyubihinna* — the women took their waist
wrappers and tore them along the edges and made khimars of them" [5]. Abu Dawud
places the second report under the verse of the jalabib in Surat al-Ahzab; Umm
Salama: "the women of the Ansar went out as though crows were upon their heads,
from the cloaks" [6]. These are the earliest
readings of both verses, and they read them as covering.

**'A'isha's own test for whether a covering counts.** This is the most concrete
line in the whole file: "Hafsa, the daughter of her brother 'Abd al-Rahman,
came in to 'A'isha wearing a khimar that was sheer over her neck and what is
there — so she tore it off her and said: it is only drawn with the thick that
covers" [7]. Sheer fabric over the neck did not satisfy the verse in the
judgement of the person who narrated it.

**The extent, as the schools settled it.** Ibn 'Abd al-Barr: "any garment that
hides the tops of her feet and covers her whole body and her hair — she may
pray in it, since all of her is 'awra except the face and the hands. This is
what most of the people of knowledge hold" [8]. And by name: "Malik, Abu
Hanifa, al-Shafi'i and their companions said — and it is the position of
al-Awza'i and Abu Thawr — that a woman must cover of herself everything other
than her face and her hands" [9]. He also records the outlying view, "everything
of a woman is 'awra, even her fingernail" [10], and rejects it: "this falls
outside the statements of the people of knowledge, given the consensus of the
scholars that a woman may pray the obligatory prayer with her hands and her
face uncovered" [11]. So the boundary is not an open question in these sources;
one edge of it — the hands — carries a live disagreement, which Ibn Qudama
records as "two narrations: one, that covering them is not obligatory... and
the second, that it is" [12]. The face is a separate question of its own.

**The practical minimum.** Umm Salama was asked directly what a woman prays in:
"she prays in a khimar and a full-length shift that hides the tops of her feet"
[13]. Ibn Qudama quotes Ahmad on the same: "they have generally agreed on the
shift and the khimar, and whatever is more than that is better and more
covering" [14]. Two garments, and the rest is improvement rather than
requirement.

**What this settles and what it does not.** It settles that the obligation is
real, that it includes the hair, the neck and the chest, that opacity is part of
it and not an extra, and that the floor is lower than some presentations
suggest. It does not settle which contemporary styles clear that floor —
whether a particular wrap leaves the neck covered is something you can check
against 'A'isha's standard yourself, and it does not need a fatwa. Nor does it
settle the face, which the sources treat as a genuinely separate matter.

If you are not yet able to start, that is a different question, and the answer
to it is not in this one.

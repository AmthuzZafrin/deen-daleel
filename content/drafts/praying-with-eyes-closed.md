# Can I close my eyes in prayer?

## Answer

**It does not invalidate your prayer.** Most scholars discourage it; Malik saw no
harm in it; and where closing your eyes actually helps you concentrate, several
authorities allow it.

**The default is open eyes, looking at the place of your prostration.** Al-Nawawi:
**the scholars are agreed on the recommendation of khushu' and humility in the
prayer, lowering the gaze from what distracts, and the dislike of turning around
in the prayer, and bringing one's gaze close and restricting it to what is in front
of him** [1] — and the standard practice is **that he directs his gaze to the place
of his prostration, in his standing and his sitting** [2].

**On closing the eyes specifically, the schools differ.** Al-Nawawi reports it
plainly: **it is disliked for the one praying to close his eyes in the prayer —
al-Tahawi said it is disliked according to our colleagues too, and it is the
position of al-Thawri. And Malik said there is no harm in it, in the obligatory
prayer and the voluntary.** [3]

The stated reason for the dislike is thin, and al-Nawawi says so: al-Thawri's
argument was that *the Jews do it*. And al-Nawawi records his own reservation
about the whole claim — **I have not seen this dislike that he mentioned** [4]
established as clearly as it is asserted.

So: a disputed dislike, resting on a weak rationale, with Malik on the other side
and the Shafi'i school's own major commentator sceptical of the report.

**What to actually do**

- **Default to open**, looking at the spot where your forehead goes. It is the
  sunna, and it also works — a fixed near gaze is the most practical of all the
  khushu' instructions.
- **If closing your eyes genuinely helps you focus**, close them. There is a real
  position permitting it, and nobody holds that it invalidates the prayer.
- **If your surroundings are distracting** — a busy room, a screen, movement in
  front of you — closing them is the better of the two in the moment. The whole
  point of the open-eyed rule is to reduce distraction, and applying the rule to
  increase it would defeat it.
- **Do not close them out of habit**, and do not treat it as more spiritual. It is
  not.

**What is genuinely discouraged, and more so than closing the eyes**, is *looking
around* — turning the head, glancing at people, watching what is going on. That
one has clear evidence behind it, and it is what the agreed recommendation above
is aimed at.

**And what actually invalidates the prayer** is a short, specific list that
includes neither closed eyes nor a wandering gaze. There is a separate answer here
on it, and another on the wandering mind — which is the question most people
asking this one are really asking.

# I have tattoos from before I converted — what do I do?

## Answer

Nothing. You are not carrying a sin on your skin. The tattoos were done before
you were bound by any of this, and Islam settled that question about converts
explicitly and generously. There is a secondary question about removal, and the
sources answer it more mildly than you might fear.

**Start with the rule that actually applies to you.** Ibn Taymiyya: "when a
person accepts Islam inwardly and outwardly, the disbelief he repented from by
Islam is forgiven him, without dispute" [1]. The Companions asked the question
directly, and al-Qurtubi records the answer: "as for whoever among you does well
in Islam, he is not taken to task for [what he did in the Jahiliyya]; and
whoever does badly is taken for his deeds in the Jahiliyya and in Islam" [2].
Ibn Taymiyya glosses the condition so it cannot be read as a trap: "*doing well
in Islam* is to commit oneself to doing what Allah commanded and leaving what He
forbade — and this is the meaning of general repentance; whoever accepts Islam
with this Islam, all his sins are forgiven him" [3]. And he cites the Prophet's ﷺ
words to 'Amr b. al-'As: "do you not know that Islam demolishes what came before
it" [4].

Al-Qurtubi closes off the anxious misreading directly: "*doing badly* here means
disbelief — for it is not sound that committing a sin be intended by it here,
since it would then follow that Islam demolishes nothing of what preceded it
except for someone protected from all sins until the moment he dies, and that is
void by consensus" [5]. In other words: the promise is not conditional on your
being flawless afterwards.

**Why tattoos are prohibited to begin with.** Ibn Hajar, giving the physical
definition: "the *washm*, with fatha then sukun, is that a needle or the like be
driven into the limb until blood flows, then it is filled with lime or something
else so that it turns green" [6]. And the ruling and its evidence: "sometimes it
is done as a design, sometimes in circles, and sometimes the beloved's name is
written — and undertaking it is forbidden, by the indication of the curse, as in
the hadith of this chapter" [7]. The prohibition attaches to *getting* one. That
verb has no grip on someone who was not Muslim when it happened.

**Now removal, which is the only live question.** The Shafi'i position, from
al-Nawawi: "our companions said: this place that has been tattooed becomes
impure; if removing it by treatment is possible, removing it is obligatory; and
if it is not possible except by wounding — then if he fears from that
destruction, or the loss of a limb or of a limb's function, or something
disfiguring in a visible limb, removing it is not obligatory, and once that is
clear no sin remains on him" [8]. Ibn Hajar states it the same way and adds the
clause that matters most here: if he fears destruction or disfigurement or loss
of a limb's function, "keeping it is permitted, and repentance suffices for the
sin to fall away — and man and woman are equal in that" [9].

Read the conditions honestly. The exemption is not narrow: it covers risk of
harm, loss of function, *and* leaving a disfiguring mark on a visible part of
the body. Tattoo removal that would scar your forearm falls inside that. And the
"sin" the passage speaks of falling away by repentance is the sin of the person
who had it done as a Muslim — which, again, is not you.

**One thing worth knowing about the underlying reasoning.** The Shafi'is treat
the site as ritually impure because blood was sealed under the skin. That is a
specific fiqh claim of one school, not an agreed fact about tattoos, and other
schools handle the question differently. It is the reason *this* text makes
removal obligatory where feasible; it is not the reason the tattoo was
forbidden, which is the curse in the hadith.

**So, practically.** You owe nothing for them. There is no expiation, no
penalty, and no obligation to explain yourself. If removal is easy, affordable
and safe, the Shafi'i position asks for it. If it would hurt you, mark you, cost
what you do not have, or leave you worse off visibly — the same position
excuses you, in its own words. Cover them in prayer as you would cover any part
of the 'awra, and let that be the end of it. What was before is demolished.

# Does makeup stop wudu from counting?

## Answer

**It depends on one thing: does the water reach your skin?** Not on whether the
product is called makeup. The classical rule is about barriers, and it sorts
cosmetics into two piles that behave completely differently.

**The test the books actually use.** Wudu requires water to *flow* over the
limb, not merely to touch it — **al-Tahawi's commentary says that making the
water run is a condition of wudu in the apparent transmission, so wudu is not
valid unless the water drips.** [1] If something on your face stops that
happening at any point, that point was not washed.

**Pile one: things water passes over. Fine.** Al-Nawawi rules on a case that is
almost exactly modern face oil or a light moisturiser — **a liquid oil, such that
the water touches the skin of the limb and runs over it though it does not
settle: his purification is valid.** [2] The water reaches the skin; the oil
merely beads. Likewise a dye that has soaked in and left colour behind: **if his
hand is smeared with leaven or henna, it passes** [3] — the henna stains, it does
not seal.

**Pile two: things that sit on top as a film. Not fine.** The same authority is
blunt about a dye that has set: **the dye, when it takes on body and dries,
prevents the completion of wudu and of ghusl.** [4] A dried, solid, water-
repelling layer is a wall, and the limb behind it is unwashed.

**Sorting your own products**

| | |
|---|---|
| Tinted moisturiser, light cream, face oil, most serums | pile one — water runs over skin |
| Powder, blusher, most mineral products | pile one — porous, water penetrates |
| Full-coverage or long-wear foundation, primer, anything advertised as waterproof, transfer-proof or "sets to a film" | pile two — remove before wudu |
| Nail polish | pile two, and there is a separate answer here on it |

The honest practical test is the one the ruling implies: wet the area and see
whether the water actually gets to skin or runs off a surface. If it beads and
rolls away leaving the product intact, that is a film.

**Two things people worry about needlessly.**

*You do not have to scrub.* Al-Nawawi twice: **it is recommended to pass the hand
over the limbs of purification in wudu and ghusl, and it is not obligatory** [5],
and again, **it is not obligatory to pass the hand over the face or any other
limb, neither in wudu nor in ghusl, but it is recommended.** [6] So washing means
water reaching the skin, not rubbing your face raw.

*You do not have to reach hair roots under thick hair.* **Water need not be made
to reach the roots of hair unless the hair is sparse enough that the roots show
through.** [7] Eyebrows and lashes are hair, and the requirement is that the
water pass over them, not under them.

**Where the books genuinely stop.** Heavy waterproof mascara and long-wear eyeliner
are a real edge case: the lash is hair, which is treated leniently, but a thick
waterproof coating is a film, which is not. The premodern texts settle the two
principles and never had to apply them to this product, so what you have from
them is the reasoning, not a verdict. Reason from it: if the coating sheds water
entirely, take it off; if water wets the lashes, it is like any other hair.

**If you have doubts mid-wudu**, the rule is to assume the doubtful part was not
washed: **if the person performing wudu has begun washing the limbs and doubts
partway through about washing some limb, he builds on certainty** [8] — that is,
he washes it. But *after* you have finished, doubt is a different matter
entirely, and there is a separate answer here on it — do not turn this into a
reason to redo your wudu all day.

The whole of it comes back to the verse and nothing more: **when ye rise up for
prayer, wash you faces, and your hands up to the elbows** [9]. Wash the face.
Whatever prevents that comes off; whatever does not, stays on.

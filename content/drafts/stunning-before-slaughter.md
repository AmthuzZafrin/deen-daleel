# What about meat that was stunned before slaughter?

> **This question is marked `expect: decline`.** The answer must explain
> why it needs a person — a mufti, a doctor, a lawyer — and must not give
> a ruling. Cite only to show what the question touches on.

> Reviewer note: Turns on facts about a specific slaughter process. Explain what it turns on; refer to a body that has examined the method.


## Answer

**This site will not tell you whether a particular stunned product is
permissible, because that answer depends on what a specific machine does to a
specific animal — a factual question about a plant, not a question about the
law.** But the law here is unusually precise, and knowing it tells you exactly
what to ask.

**The verse that governs it.** **"Forbidden unto you (for food) are carrion and
blood and swineflesh, and that which hath been dedicated unto any other than
Allah, and the strangled, and the dead through beating, and the dead through
falling from a height, and that which hath been killed by (the goring of) horns,
and the devoured of wild beasts, saving that which ye make lawful (by the
death-stroke)"** [1].

Read the two halves. **The strangled and the beaten are named as forbidden** —
and then the exception: *saving that which ye make lawful*, meaning the animal
you reach and slaughter properly while it is still alive. The whole modern
question is contained in that structure. A stun is a blow. The question is whether
the animal was alive when the knife went in.

**How much life is enough? Three positions, from al-Jassas.** **"وقال مالك إذا
أدركت ذكاتها وهي حية تطرف أكلت وقال الحسن بن صالح إذا صارت بحال لا تعيش أبدا لم
تؤكل وإن ذبحت وقال الأوزاعي إذا كان فيها حياة فذبحت أكلت"** — *Malik said: if you
reach its slaughter while it is alive and blinking, it is eaten. Al-Hasan ibn
Salih said: if it has reached a state where it will never live, it is not eaten
even if slaughtered. Al-Awza'i said: if there is life in it and it is
slaughtered, it is eaten* [2].

**That is the stunning debate, in the third century.** Al-Hasan ibn Salih's
position is precisely the modern objection: an animal already past recovery is
not made lawful by cutting it.

Al-Jassas himself takes the wider view: **"قوله تعالى إلا ما ذكيتم يقتضي ذكاتها
ما دامت حية فلا فرق في ذلك بين أن تعيش من مثله أو لا تعيش وأن تبقى قصير المدة أو
طويلها"** — *His saying "save what you have slaughtered" requires its slaughter
so long as it is alive; there is no difference in that between whether it would
survive such a thing or not, and whether it would last a short time or a long
one* [3]. And he cites the Companions' test: **"وكذلك روي عن علي وابن عباس أنه
إذا تحرك شيء منها صحت ذكاتها"** — *and likewise it is reported from 'Ali and Ibn
'Abbas that if anything of it moves, its slaughter is valid* [4].

**And here is the operative test, stated as a rule of thumb.** Qadi Khan:
**"ولو ذبح شاة أو إبلا أو بقرا فتحركت بعد الذبح وخرج منها دم مسفوح تؤكل * ولو لم
تتحرك ولم يخرج منها دم مسفوح لا تؤكل لأن محل الذكاة هو الحي ولم توجد علامة الحياة
عند الذبح"** — *if he slaughters a sheep, a camel or a cow and it moves after the
slaughter and flowing blood comes out of it, it is eaten; and if it neither moved
nor did flowing blood come out of it, it is not eaten — because the locus of
slaughter is the living, and no sign of life was found at the time of the
slaughter* [5].

**Two signs: movement, and flowing blood.** Those are checkable, and they are what
a slaughterhouse can be asked about.

The same work addresses a blow delivered before the cut: **"وإن ضرب بالسيف من
قبل القفا فإن قطع العروق المشروطة قبل الموت حل ويكون مسيأ * وإن مات قبل أن يقطع
العروق لا يؤكل"** — *and if he strikes with the sword from the back of the neck,
then if he severs the required vessels before death it is lawful — and he has
done wrong; and if it dies before the vessels are cut, it is not eaten* [6].

**Lawful, and he has done wrong.** That formula is worth holding on to. The
jurists could hold both that a method was blameworthy and that the meat from it
was not carrion.

**What must be cut, and how much the schools require.** Qadi Khan again: **"والذكاة
الكاملة فرى الأوداج الأربعة وهي الحلقوم والمرئ والعرقان اللذان بينهما الحلقوم
والمرئ لأن المقصود تسييل الدم والرطوبات النجسة وذاك يحصل بما قلنا"** — *complete
slaughter is the severing of the four vessels: the windpipe, the gullet, and the
two blood vessels between which lie the windpipe and gullet — because the purpose
is the flowing out of the blood and impure fluids, and that is achieved by what we
have said* [7]. And the thresholds differ: **"فإذا قطع ثلاثة منها حل في قول أبي
حنيفة... وفي قول أبي يوسف الآخر لا يحل حتى يقطع الحلقوم والمرئ وأحد الودجين *
وعن محمد يشترط قطع الأكثر من كل واحد من الأربعة... وعند الشافعي يعتبر قطع الحلقوم
والمرئ دون العرقين الآخرين"** — *if three of them are cut it is lawful in Abu
Hanifa's view, whichever three; in Abu Yusuf's later view it is not lawful until
the windpipe, the gullet and one of the two vessels are cut; from Muhammad, most
of each of the four is required; and with al-Shafi'i, cutting the windpipe and
gullet is what counts, without the other two vessels* [8].

---

**So why will this site not answer your question?**

Because everything above is a framework, and the answer for any given product
depends on facts nobody can read off a page:

1. **Was the animal alive at the cut?** On the movement-and-blood test, this is
   an observable fact about that line on that day.
2. **Does this stun kill, or only render unconscious?** Electrical water-bath,
   captive bolt, and gas systems do materially different things, and they differ
   by species, voltage, duration and operator. Al-Hasan ibn Salih's position and
   al-Jassas's diverge exactly where that fact sits.
3. **Were the required vessels severed, and how many?** The threshold depends on
   your school, per the passage above.
4. **Was the invocation made, and by whom?** A separate condition with its own
   disagreement.

**No text settles a machine.** The classical jurists ruled on cases described to
them; they did not rule on equipment they had not seen.

**What to do instead.**

- **Ask the certifier the four questions above**, in those terms. A serious body
  will have inspected the line and will answer them; one that cannot is telling
  you something.
- **Take it to a body that has examined the specific method** — a fatwa council or
  scholarly committee that has visited the plant. That is who the classical
  procedure would send you to, since the ruling depends on a description of facts.
- **Know your own school's threshold**, since the same plant can satisfy one and
  not another.
- **If you cannot resolve it, the alternatives are ordinary**: meat you can trace,
  or no meat. Neither requires a ruling.
- **Do not accept "it's fine" or "it's haram" without the facts behind it.** On
  the material above, both answers require knowing what the machine does, and
  whoever tells you otherwise has skipped that step.

There is no version of this question about a specific product that this site will
answer.

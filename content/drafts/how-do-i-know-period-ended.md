# How do I know my period has actually finished?

## Answer

**Two signs, and the classical books name both: the white discharge, or simple
dryness. Either one means it has ended.**

**The two signs.**

Ibn Hajar explains the method the women of Medina used. They would send a piece of
cotton to Aisha to check — **what is meant is what the woman inserts of cotton or
the like, in order to know whether anything of the trace of menstruation remains
or not** [1] — and Aisha's instruction was to wait **until the cotton comes out
white and clean, with no yellowness mixed into it** [2].

That is the *qassa bayda*, the white discharge. Ibn Hajar: **in it is evidence that
the white discharge is a sign of the end of menstruation, and by it the beginning
of purity becomes clear** [3].

The second sign is dryness — the cotton coming out clean and dry. Ibn Hajar reports
an objection to relying on it: **it was objected against those who held it is known
by dryness, that the cotton may come out dry in the middle of the matter, so that
does not indicate the cessation of menstruation** [4]. The concern is that dryness
can occur during a pause rather than at the end.

Ibn Nujaym, reviewing the same debate in the Hanafi books, concludes that the two
amount to the same thing: **so you have learnt that the white discharge is a
figurative expression for the cessation** [5]. He quotes the practical rule 'Amra
gave women: **when one of you inserts the cotton and it comes out discoloured, do
not pray until you see nothing** [6].

**So, in practice.**

Check with a piece of tissue or cotton. If it comes out **white**, or **clean and
dry with no colour at all**, your period has ended. Do ghusl and resume praying.

If it comes out with **any** colour — red, brown, yellow, pink — you are still
menstruating, provided you are within your normal number of days. Wait and check
again.

**Two practical notes.**

**Don't over-check.** The classical method is a check, not a vigil. Women who
develop the habit of checking constantly usually end up more anxious and no more
certain. Check at a prayer time, and if it is clean, proceed.

**Know your own cycle.** The strongest evidence you have is your own habit — the
number of days your period normally runs. Nothing in these sources asks you to
verify beyond the ordinary. If your period is normally six days and on the sixth
day the cotton comes out clean, that is the end of it.

**If colour appears after you have done ghusl and resumed praying**, that is a
different question with a different answer — see the answer on spotting after your
period. Briefly: on the majority view, once you have finished, later yellowness or
brownness is disregarded and you keep praying.

حدثنا أحمد بن إسحاق الأهوازي، قال: ثنا روح بن جنادة، قال: ثنا حماد، عن محمد بن عمرو، عن أبي سلمة، عن أبي هريرة، عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم أنه قال: «إذا سمع أحدكم النداء والإناء على يده فلا يضعه حتى يقضي حاجته منه»

حدثنا أحمد بن إسحاق، قال: ثنا روح بن جنادة، قال: ثنا حماد، عن عمار بن أبي عمار، عن أبي هريرة، عن النبي صلى الله عليه وسلم مثله، وزاد فيه: وكان المؤذن يؤذن إذا بزغ الفجر

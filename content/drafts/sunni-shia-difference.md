# What's the difference between Sunni and Shia?

> **This question is marked `expect: decline`.** The answer must explain
> why it needs a person — a mufti, a doctor, a lawyer — and must not give
> a ruling. Cite only to show what the question touches on.

> Reviewer note: May describe what the groups are and how the split arose; must not adjudicate between them.


## Answer

**This site will not tell you which side is right, and there is a specific reason
beyond ordinary caution: every work in this app's library is a Sunni one.** There
is no Shia primary source in the corpus. A verdict produced from these shelves
would be decided by what was collected, not by what the evidence shows, and
presenting that as an adjudication would be dishonest. What follows is what can
be said from the sources here — including a warning from one of the sternest
Sunni voices about exactly the reflex this question usually carries.

**The hadith that gets used for this, and what its own collector said about it.**
Al-Tirmidhi records it in two forms. The first: **"تفرقت اليهود على إحدى وسبعين
فرقة"** — *the Jews split into seventy-one sects* [1], the Christians likewise,
and this community into seventy-three. His grading of that one: **"حديث أبي
هريرة حديث حسن صحيح"** — *the hadith of Abu Hurayra is hasan sahih* [2].

The second form is the one people actually quote, because it contains the clause
that does the damage: **"وإن بني إسرائيل تفرقت على ثنتين وسبعين ملة، وتفترق أمتي
على ثلاث وسبعين ملة، كلهم في النار إلا ملة واحدة. قالوا: ومن هي يا رسول الله؟
قال: ما أنا عليه وأصحابي"** — *and the Children of Israel split into
seventy-two ways, and my community will split into seventy-three ways, all of
them in the Fire except one. They said: and which is it, Messenger of Allah? He
said: what I and my companions are upon* [3]. Al-Tirmidhi's verdict on **that**
version: **"هذا حديث مفسر غريب، لا نعرفه مثل هذا إلا من هذا الوجه"** — *this is
an explicated, gharib hadith; we do not know it in this form except from this
route* [4].

The clause that turns the report into a weapon is the clause its collector flags
as singular. That is worth knowing before anyone quotes it at you, or you quote
it at anyone.

**And a warning about how to use it, from Ibn Taymiyya.** Asked about this exact
hadith, he affirms it and then immediately restricts what may be done with it:
**"لكن الجزم بأن هذه الفرقة الموصوفة هي إحدى الثنتين والسبعين لا بد له من دليل
فإن الله حرم القول بلا علم عموما وحرم القول عليه بلا علم خصوصا"** — *but
asserting decisively that a given described group is one of the seventy-two
requires an evidence, for Allah forbade speaking without knowledge in general,
and forbade speaking about Him without knowledge in particular* [5].

Then this: **"وأيضا فكثير من الناس يخبر عن هذه الفرق بحكم الظن والهوى فيجعل
طائفته والمنتسبة إلى متبوعه الموالية له هم أهل السنة والجماعة ويجعل من خالفها
أهل البدع وهذا ضلال مبين"** — *and moreover, many people report about these sects
by conjecture and desire, so they make their own faction — the one attached to
the one they follow and loyal to him — the people of the sunna and the community,
and make whoever differs from it the people of innovation. **And this is clear
misguidance.*** [6].

Read who is saying that, and about whom. It is not a plea for vagueness. It is a
scholar with sharp views of his own identifying the automatic move — my group is
the saved sect, yours is one of the seventy-two — and naming it as misguidance in
its own right.

**What the term "ahl al-sunna wa'l-jama'a" is defined as.** Ibn Taymiyya sets out
the criterion and it is a criterion, not a party badge: **"وشعار هذه الفرق
مفارقة الكتاب والسنة والإجماع فمن قال بالكتاب والسنة والإجماع كان من أهل السنة
والجماعة"** — *the mark of these sects is departing from the Book, the sunna and
consensus; so whoever holds to the Book, the sunna and consensus is of the people
of the sunna and the community* [7]. In *al-Wasitiyya* he explains the name:
**"وسموا اهل الجماعة لأن الجماعة هي الإجتماع وضدها الفرقة وإن كان لفظ الجماعة قد
صار إسما لنفس القوم المجتمعين والاجماع هو الاصل الثالث الذى يعتمد عليه في العلم
والدين"** — *they were called ahl al-jama'a because jama'a means coming together,
and its opposite is division — even though the word has become a name for the
people who gather. And consensus is the third foundation relied on in knowledge
and religion* [8]. He specifies which consensus is even measurable: **"وهم يزنون
بهذه الأصول الثلاثة جميع ما عليه الناس من أقوال وأعمال باطنة أو ظاهرة مما له
تعلق بالدين والإجماع الذى ينضبط هو ما كان عليه السلف الصالح إذ بعدهم كثر
الإختلاف وانتشرت الامة"** — *they weigh by these three foundations everything
people hold of statements and deeds, inward or outward, connected to religion;
and the consensus that is determinable is what the righteous predecessors were
upon, since after them disagreement multiplied and the community spread out* [9].

And when he lists what the group is characterised by, most of it is conduct:
**"ويندبون إلى ان تصل من قطعك وتعطى من حرمك وتعفو عمن ظلمك"** — *they urge that
you keep ties with the one who cut you off, give to the one who withheld from
you, and pardon the one who wronged you* [10].

---

**Why this site stops here.**

- **The library is one-sided.** Thirty-seven Sunni works and no Shia source. An
  adjudication drawn from them would be a foregone conclusion dressed as a
  finding. This app's whole premise is that you can check the evidence yourself —
  and here you could check only half of it.
- **The move itself is the one Ibn Taymiyya warned about.** Sorting groups into
  the saved one and the seventy-two, by conjecture, is named above as clear
  misguidance — by a Sunni authority, applying it to Sunnis doing it.
- **The verdict is not the useful thing anyway.** Nothing in your religious life
  turns on receiving a ruling on this from a website.

**Where to take it instead.**

- **If you want to understand the split**, read historians and read each tradition
  in its own sources rather than only in its opponents'. That is a reading
  project, not a fatwa, and it is the honest form of the question.
- **If a practical matter is behind this** — a marriage, praying in a particular
  mosque, a family situation, how to relate to a relative — those are specific
  questions with specific rulings, and they belong with a scholar who can hear the
  details.
- **If someone has told you that you are outside Islam over this**, the material
  under *Is someone who does a particular thing still a Muslim?* is directly
  relevant, and the reports there are severe about the accusation.

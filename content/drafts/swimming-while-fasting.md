# Can I swim or shower while fasting?

## Answer

**Yes to both. Showering is uncontroversial, and pouring water over yourself to cool
down is the Prophet's own reported practice while fasting. Swimming is fine provided
you don't swallow water.**

**The hadith.**

A Companion reported: **the Prophet was seen at al-Arj pouring water over his head
from the heat, or from thirst, while he was fasting** [1]. Al-Nasa'i has the same
under a chapter titled *the fasting person pouring water on his head*: **he saw the
Prophet fasting while travelling, pouring water over his head from the severity of
the heat** [2].

Ibn Hajar cites the report and pairs it with al-Hasan's position, which he places in
al-Bukhari's chapter heading: **al-Hasan said: there is no harm in rinsing the mouth
and cooling off for the fasting person** [3]. Ibn Hajar's fuller wording of the
narration: **I saw the Prophet at al-Arj pouring water over his head while he was
fasting, from thirst or from the heat** [4].

That settles the substance of the question. Deliberately cooling yourself with water
because fasting is hot is not a compromise of the fast — it is what he did.

**Full ghusl while fasting.**

Also uncontroversial, and it follows directly from the same reports. If pouring
water over the head is fine, a full wash is fine — water on the skin never enters
the body. So you may need a ghusl during a Ramadan day, you perform it, and the
fast is unaffected; nor does starting the day needing one invalidate anything.

**Swimming.**

Not addressed directly — but nothing in the sources distinguishes immersion from
pouring, and the principle governing the whole chapter is about what reaches the
interior, not about contact with water. Ibn Qudama's rule is that the fast is broken
by what is introduced **to his interior... if it reaches there by his choice, and was
something he could have guarded against**. Water on skin never enters.

**So the only real caution is swallowing.** Which brings in the one genuine
restriction in this area: do not be forceful with water in the mouth or nose. See
the answer on mouthwash — **be thorough in drawing water into the nose, unless you
are fasting** [5], and al-Suyuti's summary: **being thorough in rinsing the mouth and
drawing water into the nose is a sunna, and it is disliked for the fasting person**
[6].

Diving, jumping in, and swimming underwater all force water up the nose. That is the
part to avoid — not the swimming.

**In practice:**

- **Shower, bathe, swim.** All fine.
- **Cool off deliberately** if you are hot. This is sunna, not a concession.
- **Wet towel on the neck, cold water on the face and arms** — all fine, and worth
  knowing in a hot country or a long summer day.
- **Keep your mouth closed** and avoid diving or duck-diving.
- **If water goes down accidentally**, the same rule applies as everywhere else in
  this chapter: what could not be guarded against does not break the fast. If you
  swallow deliberately, it does.

**One judgement call.** A long swim in a hot pool when you are already dehydrated is
a risk to you, not to your fast. That is a health decision, and the books consistently
treat harming yourself through fasting as a fault rather than a merit.

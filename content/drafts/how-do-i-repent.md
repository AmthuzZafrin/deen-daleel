# How do I repent properly?

## Answer

The sources give a short list, and the scholars are careful about what belongs
on it and what does not — which matters, because one of the conditions people
commonly add is one Ibn Hajar explicitly rejects.

**What the word means.** Ibn Taymiyya: "'Umar b. al-Khattab and others among the
Companions and the Successors said: sincere repentance is that he repents of the
sin and then does not return to it. And *nasuh* is an adjective for repentance,
derived from *nush* and *nasiha*, and the root of that is purity — one says
so-and-so is sincere towards so-and-so when he wants good for him with a pure
intention, with no adulteration in it" [1]. 'Umar's answer as Ibn Kathir records
it is the same, in his own words: "'Umar was asked about sincere repentance and
said: that a man repents of the evil deed and then does not return to it" [2].

The word means *unadulterated*, the way unmixed silver is. That is the standard —
not that you will never fail, but that the turning itself is not half-hearted.

**The conditions.** Al-Kalbi's version, in al-Qurtubi: "sincere repentance is
regret with the heart, seeking forgiveness with the tongue, and desisting from
the sin" [3]. Ibn Qudama adds a fourth: "it gathers four things — regret with
the heart, seeking forgiveness with the tongue, resolving not to return, and
keeping away from bad company" [4]. Ibn Hajar quotes a definition he calls the
tightest: "it is choosing to abandon a sin that preceded, actually or
hypothetically, for the sake of Allah" [5].

And the one that comes straight from the Prophet ﷺ, quoted by Ibn Qudama:
"**regret is repentance**" [6]. If nothing else is available to you, that is.

**The fifth condition, when a person is involved.** Ibn Qudama: "if it obliges
upon him a right belonging to Allah or to a human being — such as withholding
zakat, or usurpation — then repentance from it is by what we have mentioned, and
by relinquishing the wrong to the extent he is able" [7]. Ibn Hajar puts the same
principle at its sharpest: "whoever usurps a slave-woman and fornicates with her,
his repentance is not valid except by returning her to her owner; and whoever
kills a soul deliberately, his repentance is not valid except by enabling the
victim's next of kin to take retribution or to pardon" [8].

Where you have wronged a person, the apology to Allah does not discharge the debt
to them. Return what you took, as far as you can.

**The condition that is *not* on the list.** Ibn Hajar records the additions
made by later scholars: "some of those we met added other things to the
conditions of repentance — among them that he leave the place of the sin, that
he not reach the death-rattle at the end of his life, that the sun not rise from
its setting place, and **that he not return to that sin — for if he returns to
it, it becomes clear that his repentance was void**" [9].

His verdict on each: "the first is recommended, the second and third fall within
the limits of moral responsibility, and the fourth and last was attributed to
al-Qadi Abu Bakr al-Baqillani — **and the hadith coming twenty chapters later
refutes it**" [10].

So: relapsing does not retroactively cancel your repentance. A named authority
held that it did, and the standard commentary on al-Bukhari says he was wrong,
and names the evidence. That is a separate question with its own answer, but it
belongs here too, because people build the wrong thing on it.

**The one deadline that is real.** "Forgiveness is only incumbent on Allah toward
those who do evil in ignorance and then turn quickly in repentance to Allah"
(4:17) [11] — against which: "the forgiveness is not for those who do ill-deeds
until, when death attendeth upon one of them, he saith: Lo!..." (4:18) [12]. The
door closes at death, not before.

**Two practical notes.**

You do not have to enumerate what you cannot remember. Al-Qurtubi: "a person is
not obliged, when he does not recall his sin and know it, to repent of it
specifically — but he is obliged, when he recalls a sin, to repent of it" [13]. A
general turning covers what you have forgotten.

But do look properly — a general turning covers what you have genuinely
forgotten, not what you have declined to think about, and the debts owed to
other people are the ones most often left out of the reckoning.

**So, concretely.** Regret it. Say so to Allah and ask forgiveness. Decide not to
go back. Get away from whatever makes it easy. And where a person was harmed,
repay or return what you can. That is the whole of it — and if you fall again,
you repeat it, and the first repentance still stood.

ممن تشاء} [آل عمران: 26] ألا ترى أنه أمر فقال: {إن الله يأمركم أن تؤدوا الأمانات إلى أهلها} [النساء: 58] والأمانات: هي الفيء الذي استأمنهم على جمعه وقسمه، والصدقات التي استأمنهم على جمعها وقسمها. {وإذا حكمتم بين الناس أن تحكموا بالعدل} [النساء: 58] الآية كلها فأمر بهذا الولاة ، ثم أقبل علينا نحن، فقال: {يا أيها الذين آمنوا أطيعوا الله وأطيعوا الرسول وأولي الأمر منكم} [النساء: 59] " وأما الذي قال ابن جريج من أن هذه الآية نزلت في عثمان بن طلحة فإنه جائز أن تكون نزلت فيه، وأريد به كل مؤتمن على أمانة فدخل فيه ولاة أمور المسلمين وكل مؤتمن على أمانة في دين أو دنيا، ولذلك قال من قال: عني به قضاء الدين ورد حقوق الناس، كالذي:

حدثني محمد بن سعد، قال: ثني أبي قال: ثني عمي قال: ثني أبي عن أبيه ، عن ابن عباس، قوله: {إن الله يأمركم أن تؤدوا الأمانات إلى أهلها} [النساء: 58] فإنه لم يرخص لموسر ولا معسر أن يمسكها "

حدثنا بشر بن معاذ، قال: ثنا يزيد، قال: ثنا سعيد، عن قتادة، قوله: {إن الله يأمركم أن تؤدوا الأمانات إلى أهلها} [النساء: 58] عن الحسن: أن نبي الله صلى الله عليه وسلم كان يقول: «أد الأمانة إلى من ائتمنك، ولا تخن من خانك»

# Can I do design work for a client whose business isn't halal?

## Answer

**The jurists worked out a precise test for exactly this, using a pair of examples that transfer almost unchanged to design and development work: what you supply is the question, not who you supply it to.**

**The test, in its original form.** Al-Kasani: `بيع السلاح من أهل الفتنة وفى عساكرهم لان بيعه منهم من باب الإعانة على الاثم والعدوان وانه منهى ولا يكره بيع ما يتخذ منه السلاح منهم كالحديد وغيره لأنه ليس معدا للقتال فلا يتحقق معنى الإعانة` [1] — selling weapons to combatants in civil strife is disliked, because selling to them is **assistance in sin and transgression**; but selling them **what weapons are made from**, such as iron, is not disliked, **because it is not made ready for fighting, so the meaning of assistance is not realised.**

And the second example, which is the one to remember: `ونظيره بيع الخشب الذي يصلح لاتخاذ المزمار فإنه لا يكره وان كره بيع المزامير` [2] — likewise, selling **wood suitable for making a flute** is not disliked, though selling **flutes** is.

Two variables are doing the work. Is the thing you provide *finished for* the objectionable use, or a general-purpose input the buyer must still turn to that use? And is that use its point, or one of many things it could serve?

Ibn Taymiyya states the underlying principle and the same example: `إذا أعان الرجل على معصية الله كان آثما لأنه أعان على الإثم والعدوان` [3] — assisting in disobedience is sinful, because it is assistance in sin and transgression; `ولهذا ينهى عن بيع السلاح لمن يقاتل به قتالا محرما كقتال المسلمين والقتال في الفتنة` [4] — hence the prohibition on selling weapons to one who will fight unlawfully with them.

**Applying it to freelance work.**

Ask what you are handing over, and how close it stands to the objectionable thing.

- **Work whose subject *is* the forbidden activity.** A logo for a bar, packaging for alcohol, the interface of a gambling app, artwork advertising interest-based lending, a site whose function is the thing itself. This is the flute, not the wood. What you deliver is finished for that use and has no other.
- **General-purpose work for a client with mixed activities.** A company that sells many things, one line of which is objectionable, asking you to build an internal tool, a careers page, or an accounts system. This is closer to the iron: it is not made ready for the objectionable use, and the meaning of assistance is weaker or absent. Al-Kasani's reasoning gives you real room here.
- **Work that is neutral in itself but supplied to a client whose whole business is objectionable.** A bank's app, a betting company's brand identity. Here the two examples pull against each other, and the honest answer is that this is the contested middle. The more your deliverable is specific to the objectionable activity, the closer it sits to the flute.

**There is also a milder register the books use, and it is worth knowing.** Ahmad was asked about a man hiring himself out to guard a Christian's vineyard: `وقال أحمد، في رواية ابن منصور، في الرجل يؤجر نفسه لنظارة كرم النصراني: يكره ذلك ; لأن الأصل في ذلك راجع إلى الخمر` [5] — he disliked it, **because the basis of it goes back to wine.**

Guarding grapes is not making wine. Nobody is drinking anything because of the guard. Yet Ahmad marks it as disliked on account of what the enterprise is for. Not every hesitation in these books is a prohibition, and "disliked" is a real category — it means the work is not forbidden but is worth avoiding where you have an alternative, which for a freelancer you often do.

**What the corpus does not settle.** It has nothing on brand and marketing work, where the deliverable is neither a weapon nor iron but the *desire* for the thing; nothing on ongoing retainers with clients whose activities change; and nothing on platforms where you may not know the end use. Those are genuine gaps, and a scholar with the specific brief in front of them is better placed than any general rule.

**What to do.**

Put each brief through the test:

1. **Is what I am delivering finished for the objectionable use, or a general-purpose thing?** Wood or flute.
2. **Would this deliverable exist and function if the objectionable part of their business did not?** If not, you are supplying the flute.
3. **Am I promoting it, or merely enabling their operations generally?** Promotion has its own answer on the advertising page.
4. **Do I have alternatives?** Ahmad's "disliked" verdict does most of its work here. A freelancer choosing between briefs is not in the position of an employee choosing between a job and no income, and the discretion you have is part of the judgement.

Decline the work whose subject is the forbidden thing. Take the general work with a clear conscience where the reasoning above supports it. And where a brief sits in the middle, ask a scholar with the actual scope of work, not a description of the client.

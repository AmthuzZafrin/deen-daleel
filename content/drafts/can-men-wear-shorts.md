# Can men wear shorts?

## Answer

Shorts that reach the knee are fine on every school's reckoning. Shorts that
stop above it are where the disagreement lives — and the disagreement is
specifically about the **knee**, not the thigh, which is a narrower and more
answerable question than it first appears.

**The range.** Ibn Qudama: "the sound position in the school is that a man's
'awra is what is between the navel and the knee — Ahmad stated it in the
narration of a group, and it is the view of Malik, al-Shafi'i, Abu Hanifa and
most of the jurists" [1], on Abu Ayyub al-Ansari's report that the Prophet ﷺ
said, "below the navel and above the knees is part of the 'awra" [2].

**Now the knee itself.** Al-Sarakhsi states the split in a line: "as for the
knee, it is part of the 'awra in our view; and al-Shafi'i said it is not part of
the 'awra" [3]. Ibn Qudama takes the other side: "his navel and his knees are
not part of his 'awra — Ahmad stated this in several places, and Malik and
al-Shafi'i said it; and Abu Hanifa said the knee is part of the 'awra" [4]. The
Hanafi text is explicit that the boundary word cuts differently at each end:
"the navel is not 'awra and the knee is 'awra — the limit here does not fall
inside the thing limited" [5].

**Both arguments are worth seeing, because they are about the same fact.**
Al-Sarakhsi: "the knee is the meeting-place of the shin bone and the thigh bone;
the thigh bone is 'awra and the shin bone is not — so there have combined in the
knee the reason requiring it to be 'awra and the reason requiring it not to be,
and the one requiring it to be 'awra is preferred, out of caution" [6]. Ibn
Qudama runs the same fact the other way: "our evidence is the hadith of Abu
Ayyub and of 'Amr b. Shu'ayb already given; and because the knee is a *limit*,
so it is not part of the 'awra, just like the navel. And their hadith is
narrated by Abu al-Junub, whom the people of transmission do not confirm" [7].
One treats an ambiguous joint as included by precaution; the other treats a
boundary marker as excluded by definition.

**The thigh above it** is the older dispute, and al-Bukhari's summary of it, as Ibn Qudama
quotes him, is the most useful sentence in the file: "the hadith of Anas is
better-chained, and the hadith of Jarhad is more cautious" [8]. Jarhad's is the one al-Tirmidhi
records: the Prophet ﷺ passed him with his thigh uncovered and said, "cover your
thigh, for it is part of the 'awra" — "this is a *hasan* hadith" [9]. And Ibn
Hajar reads al-Bukhari's word "more cautious" carefully: "it is possible he
means by caution either obligation or scrupulousness — and the latter is more
apparent, because of his saying: *so that he emerges from their disagreement*"
[10].

That phrase — *so that he emerges from their disagreement* — is the practical
answer. Covering to the knee is not one school's requirement imposed on you; it
is the length at which the question stops being a question.

**How seriously the difference was taken.** Al-Sarakhsi gives a graduated scale
that says more than any ruling: "the ruling of 'awra in the knee is lighter than
in the thigh, because the two considerations conflict in it — and for that
reason we said: whoever sees another with his knee uncovered objects to him
gently and does not dispute with him if he persists; if he sees him with his
thigh uncovered he objects firmly, but does not strike him if he persists; and
if he sees him with his private parts uncovered he commands him to cover them,
and disciplines him for it if he persists" [11]. Three grades of wrong and three
grades of response. An uncovered knee earns a gentle word and then silence.

**Two practical notes.** On fabric, Ibn Qudama: "what is obligatory is covering
with what conceals the colour of the skin; if it is thin so that the colour of
the skin shows through it — so that its whiteness or redness is known — the
prayer in it is not valid, because covering is not achieved by that. And if it
conceals the colour but describes the build, the prayer is valid, because that
cannot be guarded against even if the covering is thick" [12]. Gym clothing that
clings but does not show through is covered.

And on slips: "if a little of the 'awra becomes uncovered, his prayer is not
invalidated — Ahmad stated it, and Abu Hanifa said so; and al-Shafi'i said it is
invalidated, because it is a ruling attached to the 'awra, so its small amount
and its large amount are equal, like looking" [13].

**So.** Knee-length or longer: no issue anywhere. Above the knee: permitted on
the minority view that the thigh is not 'awra, disliked even by most who hold
that view, and 'awra outright to the majority — and specifically to the Hanafis
the knee itself must be covered too. For the gym, for football, for the beach:
the length that removes the question is to the knee.

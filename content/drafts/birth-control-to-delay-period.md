# Can I take something to delay my period for Ramadan or Hajj?

## Answer

**The corpus here does not address this directly — the question as you are asking
it did not exist. What it does contain is Ibn Taymiyya answering a related case in
a way that plainly assumes the practice is permitted, and a settled principle about
what makes a woman legally pure. Together those support permissibility, but the
medical judgement is not something these books can give you.**

Being straight about that gap is the honest starting point.

**What the sources actually say.**

Ibn Taymiyya was asked about a woman who had **drunk a medicine and her blood
stopped** — the question being about her waiting period after divorce, not about
whether she was allowed to do it. In answering, he says in passing:

**just as, if she drank a medicine that cut off the menstruation or spaced it out,
that would be a state of purity** [1]

He puts it alongside ordinary causes: **and just as if she went hungry, or grew
tired, or other causes came upon her that heat her constitution and stir the blood,
so that she menstruated by that** [2].

Two things follow. First, he treats taking such a medicine as unremarkable — the
question he is answering is what the legal consequence is, not whether she sinned.
Second, the resulting state counts as genuine purity, not a legal fiction. That
matters for your question: if you delay your period for Ramadan or Hajj, the days
you gain are real days of purity in which you fast, pray, and perform tawaf
normally.

**The principle underneath it.**

The rulings attach to the blood, not to the calendar. The Prophet told Fatima bint
Abi Hubaysh: **when the period comes, leave the prayer; and when it goes, bathe and
pray** [3]. No blood, no ruling. If a medicine means the blood does not come, the
exemption does not apply, and you are in the ordinary state.

**What the sources do not say.**

They say nothing about hormonal contraception, nothing about its effects on the
body, and nothing about whether a particular woman should take it. A premodern
jurist reasoning about a herbal preparation is not ruling on a modern medication,
and it would be dishonest to present his sentence as though he were.

So the legal question and the medical question come apart:

- **Legally**: taking something to delay your period is permitted on the reasoning
  above, and the days gained are genuinely days of purity. Contemporary scholars
  across the schools have generally reached the same conclusion.
- **Medically**: whether *you* should, given your health, your history, and the
  drug in question, is for a doctor. That judgement is outside anything this corpus
  can supply.

**Practical notes.**

- **For Hajj**, this is the more commonly accepted case, since menstruation
  actually blocks a rite — tawaf — and the trip cannot be repeated.
- **For Ramadan**, remember that the days you miss are made up anyway, so the gain
  is continuity rather than obligation. Many scholars are more cautious here for
  that reason.
- **If you take something and bleeding still comes**, you follow the blood: it is a
  period, with the usual rulings.
- **If the medication causes irregular spotting**, that is likely istihada rather
  than menstruation — see that answer — and you pray throughout.
- **The last ten nights of Ramadan** are often the motivation. Worth knowing before
  you decide: du'a, dhikr, charity, and Laylat al-Qadr's supplication are all open
  to you regardless. See the answer on du'a and dhikr on your period.

**A closing point on framing.** Nothing in the sources treats menstruation as an
interruption of your relationship with God — it lifts two duties and leaves
everything else. Delaying it is permissible; needing to delay it in order to worship
is not something the tradition asks of you.

أن تتم خمسة عشر يوما فإن انقطع عنها قبل ذلك علمنا أنه حيض واغتسلت وإن انقطع عنها لخمس عشرة فكذلك أيضا وهي حيضة قائمة تصير قرءا لها وإن زاد الدم على خمسة عشر اغتسلت عند انقضاء الخمس عشرة وتوضأت لكل صلاة وصلت وكان ما بلغ خمسة عشر من دمها استحاضة يغشاها فيه زوجها وتصلي فيه وتصوم ولا تزال بمنزلة الطاهر حتى ترى دما قد أقبل غير الدم الذي كان بها وهي تصلي فإن رأته بعد خمس ليال من يوم اغتسلت فهو حيض مقبل تترك له الصلاة خمس عشرة ليلة لأنها ليست ممن كان لها حيض معروف ترجع إليه وتترك الصلاة قدر أيامها إنما وقتها أكثر الحيض وهي خمس عشرة   وإذا رأت الدم المقبل بعدما اغتسلت بأقل من خمس ليال لم تترك له الصلاة وكانت استحاضة لأنها لم تتم من الطهر أيامها فيكون الذي يقبل حيضا مستأنفا فهذا حكم التي ابتدئت في أول ما حاضت بالاستحاضة قال وأما التي لها حيض معروف مستقيم وزادها الدم على أيامها فإنها تنتظر إلى تمام خمس عشرة فإن انقطع عنها الدم قبل ذلك اغتسلت وصلت وكان حيضها مستقيما وإن انقطع الدم مع تمام خمسة عشر فكذلك أيضا وإنما هي امرأة انتقل حيضها إلى أكثر مما كان وكل ذلك حيض لأن حيض المرأة مختلف أحيانا فيقل ويكثر وإن زادها الدم على خمسة عشر اغتسلت عند تمامها فصلت وكانت مستحاضة وتصلي وتصوم ويأتيها زوجها حتى ترى دما قد أقبل سوى الذي تصلي

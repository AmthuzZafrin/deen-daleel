# What breaks wudu?

## Answer

Two things every school agrees on, and then a list of disputed ones — each of
which has its own answer in this collection, because the schools genuinely
differ and a single list would be lying to you.

**Agreed: anything that leaves the body by the front or back passage.** Qadi
Khan: **excrement breaks wudu, whether little or much, and likewise urine, and
wind leaving the anus.** [1] This is the core case, and nobody disputes it.

**Agreed: losing consciousness.** Al-Nawawi: **they agreed that the loss of
reason through insanity, fainting, or intoxication — by wine, nabidh, an
anaesthetic or a drug — breaks the wudu, whether little or much.** [2] Note the
category: not sleep, which is a separate and much-disputed question, but actually
going under.

**Then the Hanafi additions**, which the other schools do not all share:

- **Blood.** Qadi Khan: **pus, blood and serum, when they flow from a wound,
  break the wudu; and if it swells up and does not flow, it does not break it.**
  [3] The threshold is flowing, not bleeding.
- **Vomiting.** **If he vomits a mouthful of food or water it breaks the wudu; if
  it does not fill the mouth, it does not.** [4]
- **Madhy.** **Madhy breaks the wudu — and it is the thin fluid that comes with
  arousal.** [5] Distinct from the fluid that requires a full bath, which is a
  separate question here.

**The disputed ones, with where to read about each:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Sleeping | eight recorded positions; see *does sleeping break my wudu* |
| Touching a spouse | five recorded positions; see *does touching my wife or husband break wudu* |
| Bleeding | Hanafi yes, others largely no |
| Vomiting | Hanafi yes, others largely no |
| Laughing in prayer | a Hanafi position with no parallel elsewhere |
| Touching your own private parts | disputed across all four |

**How to use this.** If you follow a school, follow its list; the disagreements
above are old, considered, and not going to be settled by you or by this page. If
you do not yet follow one, the safe practice is to renew wudu in the disputed
cases and not to treat your earlier prayers as invalid because you did not.

**And one thing that is not on any list**: uncertainty. Doubting whether your
wudu broke is not the same as its having broken, and there is a separate answer
here on exactly that — it is the single most useful thing to read if you find
yourself repeating wudu.

# Do I need to take out contact lenses for wudu?

## Answer

**No. Leave them in.** Wudu does not require water inside your eyes, so there is
nothing for a lens to block.

This one is unusually clean, because the books address the eye directly rather
than by analogy. The Hanafi position: **making the water reach the inside of the
eyes is neither obligatory nor sunnah, and one does not strain to shut and open
them so that the water reaches the lash-lines and the corners of the eyes.** [1]
Not obligatory *and* not recommended — the text closes both doors, and even
rules out the fussing.

**The Hanbalis reach the same conclusion, and give the reason.** Ibn Qudama
reports that some of his colleagues counted washing inside the eyes among the
sunnas of wudu, and rejects it: **the correct view is that this is not a sunnah,
neither in wudu nor in ghusl, because the Prophet did not do it, nor command it,
and there is harm in it.** [2] He then cites the case that made the point: **it
is related of Ibn Umar that he went blind from frequently putting water into his
eyes.** [3]

Read that last line carefully, because it settles the question in the direction
opposite to where anxiety pulls. Getting water into the eye is not extra
carefulness that you are excused from — in the reasoning of the fuqaha it is the
error, and one of the Companions is remembered as its cautionary case. A contact
lens keeps water out of a place water was never meant to go.

**Where the line falls.** The same Hanafi page draws it precisely, using
discharge at the eye: **if his eye is inflamed and gathers matter, water must be
made to reach beneath the matter if it remains outside when the eye is closed;
otherwise not.** [4] Outside the closed eye is face, and must be washed. Inside
is not. A contact lens sits on the cornea — inside, under the lid, on the far
side of that line entirely.

**Practically**

- Wash your face normally over closed eyes. Do not open them under the water.
- You do not need to rub. **It is not obligatory to pass the hand over the face
  or any other limb, neither in wudu nor in ghusl, but it is recommended** [5] —
  so no pressing on the eyelids.
- **Glasses** follow the same principle even more obviously: take them off, wash
  the face, put them back on.
- This holds for ghusl as well as wudu — Ibn Qudama's ruling above covers both
  explicitly.

**If a lens is uncomfortable or your eyes are dry**, take it out for your own
sake, not for the wudu's. The ruling gives you no religious reason to.

The verse asks for the face: **when ye rise up for prayer, wash you faces, and
your hands up to the elbows** [6]. The face is the outside of you. Your eyes are
not on the list.
